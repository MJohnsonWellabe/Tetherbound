# FIVE-CREATURE-PRESSURE — Make the five-slot rule emotionally real before the legendary

## Goal
Make the hard five-creature limit a lived gameplay/emotional system during Meadows, not a rule the player only notices at the final legendary.

Reuse current party cap, creature instances, naming, bond, Best Creature, traits/appraisal, feeding, resting, follower/party presentation, evolution, traversal and release systems.

Do not add storage or reserves.

## Core acceptance question
By the middle/later Meadows, can a normal player truthfully say:

> **I wanted at least one creature I could not easily justify adding because my five already mattered.**

If not, the central differentiator has not happened yet.

## Sources of legitimate roster pressure
Across the chapter deliberately present more than five desirable options through:
- different types/matchups;
- tank/offense/energy roles;
- rare traits/appraisal;
- evolution potential (Mudsnout/Tuskroot);
- Meadowhart/traversal;
- rare/special wild individuals;
- personal visual/behavior preference;
- early creatures with accumulated history/bond.

Do not manufacture pressure by making existing creatures arbitrarily bad.

## Creature presence
Use current allowed systems/assets to make owned creatures feel like individuals outside stat menus.

Required/desired lightweight signals where supported:
- names used consistently;
- active/follower creature presence;
- visible bed sleeping/resting;
- feeding/favorite-food response;
- bond gain feedback that is understandable but not spammy;
- Best Creature designation feedback;
- level-up feedback;
- current moves/traits/appraisal readable in party UI;
- evolution preserving identity/history;
- battle history/time-together data surfaced in release ceremony where available.

Do not build a complex pet-simulation minigame.

## Bond
Audit current bond earn sources against canon:
- fighting together;
- time together;
- resting;
- feeding;
- favorite food.

Ensure normal play produces meaningful bond progress. Avoid grind loops where the player repeats a trivial interaction solely to raise bond.

## Best Creature
Current species-specific perks should have a clear player-facing meaning. Designation should feel like a meaningful favorite/strategy decision rather than a hidden flag.

## Traversal slot pressure
A rideable creature must also remain useful in combat. The five-slot rule should create interesting compromise, not punish the player for using traversal.

## Release ceremony
Coordinate prompt 46. Functional deletion is insufficient.

The ceremony should show enough accumulated identity/history that the decision reads as releasing *this creature*, not freeing slot 4.

At minimum use current available data such as:
- nickname/name;
- species;
- level;
- bond;
- traits/appraisal;
- moves;
- Best Creature status;
- time/history where tracked.

## Legendary setup
Before the Warden/legendary, validate that a full-team player has realistic reasons to value all five. The legendary join offer should therefore create a genuine final choice, not an obvious replacement of a disposable filler creature.

## Verification
At Gate B, D2, D4 and E record:
- party composition;
- bond/levels;
- catches declined because of five-slot pressure;
- creatures released/replaced and why;
- whether player had a favorite/Best Creature;
- whether traversal/evolution changed roster decisions.

Use a fresh realistic run rather than constructing an artificial maxed save only.

## Acceptance
- roster pressure appears before the legendary;
- creatures accumulate visible identity/history;
- normal play advances bond;
- Best Creature has understandable value;
- traversal does not create a dead slot;
- release ceremony is emotionally legible;
- legendary choice can plausibly be difficult.

## Definition of done
The player’s five are a team with history, not five interchangeable combat records, and the hard cap creates meaningful choices throughout the Meadows.
