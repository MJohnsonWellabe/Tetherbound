## Headline, for a reader who reads only this paragraph

**J1 fails, J2 fails, J3 is split, J4 cannot be determined in any container.**
A fresh blind pass on the landed tree found the open world and the Meadows
Hall/stronghold reading as two different productions — different material
fidelity, different colour philosophy, different foliage libraries — meeting
at a hard seam. Worse, a defect JUDGE-6 found and `T1-HALL-4` fixed and
*measured* (the fortress silhouette separating from its own hillside,
matching the keyart's own contrast) has **regressed** since `T1-HALL-ART`
landed on top of it: this lane's own re-measurement, run on the identical rig
and boxes, finds the separation fallen from **+33.1 back to +11.6** — "no
separation," the language JUDGE-6 used for the original defect. No lane's own
evidence caught this; `T1-HALL-ART`'s own handover says outright that it did
not re-run the measurement. That regression, found by this audit rather than
inherited from a prior report, is the single most actionable finding below.

## J1 — Cohesive from opening through Stronghold, one art direction

**The test that decides it.** No automated test exists or is cheap to write
for "reads as one art direction" — this is exactly the kind of claim
`ralph/conventions.md`'s visual-judge process exists for. Decided by: Set A +
Set B contact sheets, judged blind by a subagent with the visual-judge
rubric plus one added question (full text and full verdict in
`ralph/reports/AUDIT-J/BLIND-JUDGE-2026-08-31.md`) — *"do the open-world
sheet and the fortress sheet read as the SAME game, made by the same art
team, with one consistent material/prop/lighting language?"* — cross-checked
against `JUDGE-VISUAL-2-2026-08-30.md`'s independent, wider survey (bands
1–5, Warrens, water, UI) for the subsystems this lane did not itself capture.

**Verdict: FAILS.**

**Evidence.**

The blind judge's answer, verbatim: *"NO. This does not read as one
deliberately art-directed game... It reads as a gallery: an open-world pass
and a fortress pass built to different standards, sharing a scatter library
and a skybox but not a philosophy."* Specific, frame-cited defects it found
(paths above; frame names refer to `ralph/reports/AUDIT-J/shots/`):

- Two material-fidelity tiers in one building: `H-08-wall-close`'s painted
  stone and `H-04-gate-mouth`'s barrel are hand-painted, high-frequency work;
  `H-04`'s own black braziers sit 200px away, completely untextured. The
  open world (`02-valley-floor`'s boulder, `03-rise-overlook`'s terrain) is
  low-frequency and nearly flat by comparison.
- Two colour philosophies that don't blend at the seam: fortress
  warm/high-contrast/saturated, open world cool/flat/desaturated, with
  `H-02-sigil-gate` as the transition frame showing them sitting side by
  side with no gradient.
- The same ivy asset tinted mint-green in `H-06-west-keep` and grass-green
  in `H-04`/`H-05`, minutes apart on the same wall.
- A fairytale conical turret on a military keep (`H-03-ramp-foot`,
  `H-02-sigil-gate`); a flat teal-green shingle roof (`H-05-east-flank`)
  matching nothing else in the world's palette.
- No shared silhouette language between the two systems — every vertical
  landmark in the build is at the fortress; the open world has no tower,
  windmill, standing stone, or spire, where the keyart uses exactly those as
  connective tissue.

This lane independently confirmed one of the judge's most consequential
claims by hand, without prompting it: the judge reported seeing **no Team
Tether retrofit machinery — no siphon, no boiler, no pipe run, no
scaffold — anywhere in the eleven fortress frames.** That is surprising,
because `ralph/reports/handover-T1-HALL-ART-2026-08-30.md` (§2.3, §4.4)
records 3 siphons, 2 boilers, 4 scaffolds, 68 pipe pieces and 8 banner rigs
as placed and measured on the shipped build. This lane read `H-04-gate-mouth`
and `H-05-east-flank` directly (not through the judge) and confirms the
judge is right: neither frame shows any of that machinery, only ivy and
banners. The most likely reading, stated as a hypothesis rather than a
finding this lane verified further: `T1-HALL-ART`'s placements
(courtyard/tether-approach/arena walls, per its own table) sit on faces the
JUDGE-6/`T1-HALL-4` standard exterior stands were never aimed at, so a real
committed layer is invisible on exactly the frames used to judge the
building's silhouette and identity. Worth a scene lane's placement check
against the *judging* stands specifically, not just its own fast rig.

Cross-checked against `JUDGE-VISUAL-2-2026-08-30.md` for the subsystems this
lane did not itself photograph — its findings, on a pre-landing but
same-lineage tree, independently name the identical shape of problem across
more of the world than this lane covered: **"the world speaks at least four
rock dialects"** (Warrens granite, meadow low-poly moss-top, stronghold
puffy cobble, the rise's pale facet rock), a **grass-tint discipline that
fails uniformly across every open-field sample** (blue channel a third of
green against the keyart's roughly half), and its own bar-A verdict —
**"No — but bands 1–3, the pond, the 22:00 night and the whole creature
roster individually do"** belong to the keyart's world, while built
architecture is what breaks it every time. That is the same shape of finding
this lane's fresh pass reproduces at the specific open-world/fortress
boundary: individual systems can be good; the seams between them are not
being worked by anyone.

**What would close it, in plannable units.**

1. A genuinely cheap first pass exists and is scene-level, not new-art: lock
   one sun/sky setup across capture stands (frames currently disagree about
   time of day), replace the one smeared cloud texture used identically in
   all 15 frames, cluster/vary the meadow's uniform scatter, and reconcile
   the ivy tint between `H-06` and `H-04`/`H-05`. Estimate: one capture-and-
   judge round, a few hours of scene tuning, `tools/_judge_capture_hall.gd`
   + `tools/survey.sh` to re-verify.
2. A material/colour-language unification pass between the open-world and
   built-structure asset libraries — this is the harder half, needs either
   a shared retint pass or a genuinely shared material family, and is a
   multi-day art-direction task, not a config edit.
3. **A capture round to close this lane's own coverage gap** — village at
   standing range, Warrens interior, creature roster in habitat, HUD — is
   needed before J1 can be answered with full-chapter confidence rather than
   "open world + stronghold, extrapolated from a lineage-adjacent survey for
   the rest." `tools/_capture_locations.gd`'s existing 11-site day+4-night
   plan (named in this lane's own prior checkpoint) is the right instrument;
   it was not run this round for time reasons.
4. The retrofit-machinery visibility gap above: a scene lane re-checks
   `T1-HALL-ART`'s siphon/boiler/scaffold placements against the
   `_judge_capture_hall.gd` stand list specifically, not just its own fast
   rig — likely an hour, not a rebuild.

## J2 — Blind judge bar question A: do the frames belong to the keyart's world?

**The test that decides it.** `.claude/skills/visual-judge/SKILL.md`'s bar
question A, answered by the same blind subagent pass as J1, against
`docs/reference/tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`. Independently corroborated
by a numeric re-measurement: `tools/_t1hall4_measure.py`, the exact script
and pixel boxes JUDGE-6 and `T1-HALL-4` used, run against this lane's fresh
Set B frames with `ralph/reports/T1-HALL-4/shots` as the `BEFORE` baseline.

**Verdict: NO — and regressed from the last measured state, not merely
unimproved.**

**Evidence.**

Blind judge, verbatim: *"NO... First, the stronghold in the build is a
different building from the one on the board... a clean, symmetrical,
structurally intact toy castle... with no ruin, no scaffold, and no
machinery of any kind. Second, the meadow on the board is not the meadow in
the build... no water anywhere in any of the fifteen frames, no grove
structure, and no clearings."* Full reasoning in
`ralph/reports/AUDIT-J/BLIND-JUDGE-2026-08-31.md` §B1.

The numeric finding, `ralph/reports/AUDIT-J/shots/hall/silhouette-measurement-453107fb.txt`:

| | fortress L | hill L | midground L | **separation (worst neighbour)** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| keyart panel | 72 | 104 | — | **+32.0** |
| JUDGE-6 judged frames (`T1-HALL-3`) | 137.6 | 162.5 | 143.1 | +2.9 — "no figure/ground separation" |
| `T1-HALL-4` fix, measured | 115.7 | 170.5 | 148.9 | **+33.1 — matched the keyart** |
| **this lane, `453107fb`** | 115.0 | 154.5 | 126.7 | **+11.6 — regressed to "no separation"** |

The fortress itself barely moved (115.7 → 115.0). What moved is the ground
around it: the hill dropped 170.5 → 154.5 and the midground dropped
148.9 → 126.7, closing most of the gap `T1-HALL-4` opened. This lane visually
confirmed the regression on `H-02b-sigil-gate-raised` before trusting the
number: the fortress now reads visibly close in tone to the pale hill and
haze band behind it, the same read JUDGE-6 originally flagged.

**Why this was missed.** `ralph/reports/handover-T1-HALL-ART-2026-08-30.md`
§6 states outright: *"The JUDGE-6 silhouette number is NOT re-measured, and
this lane does not claim it... Every full-world attempt this session was
lost to the `--headless` trap... by the time that was diagnosed the
remaining budget went into the four render-and-fix rounds that the fast rig
made possible."* That lane landed real changes (ivy, rubble, retrofit props)
on top of `T1-HALL-4`'s fix without the one command its own handover names
as the way to check the fix still held. This is not a criticism of that
lane's honesty — it said plainly what it did not verify — it is exactly the
kind of gap this audit exists to catch before it compounds further.

A partial positive, also numeric: D2 (bald mid-distance) improved —
bare-pale-ground share of the mid-band dropped from 36.4% to 13.2%,
consistent with `T1-HALL-ART`'s ivy/rubble reclaim layer adding real content
— even while D1 (silhouette) regressed. The two defects moved in opposite
directions from the same landing, which is itself evidence nobody looked at
both together.

**What would close it, in plannable units.**

1. **Immediate, cheap, and this lane recommends it be done before anything
   else touches the Hall's exterior:** re-run
   `tools/_judge_capture_hall.gd -- --only=H-02b` +
   `tools/_t1hall4_measure.py` after any future landing that adds exterior
   dressing, and tune the same four levers `T1-HALL-4` already identified
   and documented (`site.stone_light`/`site.stone_skirt`/fog/aerial-fade
   colour, all in `ralph/reports/handover-T1-HALL-4-2026-08-30.md` §9) to
   restore the separation. This is a known fix, already once executed
   successfully — a few hours, not a new investigation.
2. The building-vs-board gap (no ruin, no scaffold visible on the judged
   stands, no water anywhere in 15 frames) is the harder half and is mostly
   the same "needs art / needs a placement fix" list `T1-HALL-4` and
   `T1-HALL-6` already routed to the owner or to a future scene lane — see
   J1 item 4 above for the specific retrofit-visibility question.
3. A weathered/ruined stone material and true architectural asymmetry remain
   owner-blocked on reference art, per `ralph/reports/handover-T1-HALL-4-2026-08-30.md`
   §8.3 — this lane did not find a newer resolution to that block.

## J3 — Blind judge bar question B: beside `palworld-0*.jpg`, same kind of game?

**The test that decides it.** Same rubric, same blind pass, against
`docs/reference/palworld-01..05*.jpg`.

**Verdict: SPLIT, and the split is itself the finding — leans NO on the
weight of the specific defects named.**

**Evidence.**

This lane's fresh blind judge, verbatim: *"YES... The genre read is
unambiguous. Third-person over-the-shoulder camera on a small trainer figure
in a wildflower meadow with a fortified enemy landmark on the horizon;
stylised proportions... `H-03-ramp-foot` and `02-valley-floor` would both
pass as early-access screenshots of a game in Palworld's category."* But the
same verdict immediately qualifies itself: *"nobody would mistake these for
shipped. The differentiator is density and finish, not intent — and
critically, there is not a single creature in any of the fifteen frames,
while every Palworld reference puts creatures front and centre. Judged as
'same kind of game,' yes. Judged as 'same completeness,' visibly not."*

This is a harder yes than the prior round found. `JUDGE-VISUAL-2-2026-08-30.md`,
judging a wider set on a slightly earlier tree, answered bar B **"Yes for the
open world and the creatures — no overall, and the flip still happens the
moment a structure enters frame."** Both judges agree the open field and
creatures individually clear the bar and a structure entering frame is what
breaks it; they differ on whether that's enough to flip the overall answer.
This lane records both verdicts rather than picking one to report — the
disagreement is data: it means the "is this the same game" read is right on
the edge, sensitive to exactly which frames a viewer happens to see first,
which is itself a symptom of J1's inconsistency rather than a resolved
question.

Given the weight of what this lane's own pass found specifically wrong —
the fortress gatehouse measured at roughly **half the scale the keyart's own
board calls for** (a ~2.2m arch against a board that shows a 5m vaulted
entry in a four-to-five-storey structure — full reasoning and the pixel
math in `BLIND-JUDGE-2026-08-31.md` §7), the total absence of any creature
in the 15-frame set this lane captured, and the same seam J1/J2 describe —
this lane's own reading leans toward **NO** on the stricter, "would a
Palworld player call this the same tier of game" question, while agreeing
with the judge that the surface-level genre intent reads correctly.

**What would close it, in plannable units.**

1. A creature-in-habitat capture round — none of this lane's own frames show
   a single creature, and neither judge's verdict can be trusted on the
   "does the roster hold up beside Palworld's" question without one.
   `tools/preview_creatures.gd` / a habitat-scatter capture, one round.
2. The gatehouse rescale (§J2 item 2's building-vs-board gap, restated here
   because it is the single largest scale defect found) — geometry work,
   not a config edit; needs its own capture-and-judge verification after.
3. Everything named in J1's plannable list, since the same seam is what
   both bar questions are actually measuring from two angles.

## J4 — ROG Ally performance stays acceptable

**The test that decides it.** No test can decide this from any container
available to this lane or any prior lane in this repo: no container in this
project has ever had ROG Ally hardware, and `docs/PERFORMANCE_BUDGET.md` §0
and §8 say so in its own words — "No container in this project has ROG Ally
hardware... Frame time is therefore not reported as a device number anywhere
below." The only instruments available are (a) structural render-cost
numbers (draw calls, primitives, light/shadow reachability) that are
hardware-independent in shape but not in actual frame time, and (b) real
owner playtests on the actual device.

**Verdict: CANNOT BE DETERMINED in any form this audit can produce — and the
freshest real-device evidence available says it is currently failing.**

**Evidence.**

`ralph/OWNER_PLAYTEST_2026-08-30.md` OP-0830-6, the freshest real-hardware
statement in the repo: *"the game performance on a rog is pretty bad still."*
Its own text records this as **unchanged since the 2026-08-21 playtest**,
despite every visual lane in between adding geometry, lights and scatter
against a budget that (per the same entry) had never been written down until
`T1-PERF` opened on 2026-08-30. No newer owner device session exists as of
this writing.

The structural budget that does exist, from `docs/PERFORMANCE_BUDGET.md` and
the two lanes that measured against it:

- `T1-HALL-4`, measured: **3365 draw calls at `hall_approach`** (the stand
  looking at the Hall) against the 4000-draw-call ceiling
  `docs/PERFORMANCE_BUDGET.md` §0.5 derives — itself a reasoned ceiling
  bounded by the corridor's own worst already-shipping location (`band1_open`,
  ~5900, itself flagged there as needing post-rebake reconfirmation that was
  never done), not a hardware-measured limit.
- `T1-HALL-ART`, measured on its own fast rig (not the full-world stand):
  **+208 draw calls** added by the retrofit/ruin/pipe layers, giving an
  estimated **~3573** against the same 4000 ceiling.
- **This lane attempted to independently re-confirm the combined number on
  the actual `hall_approach` stand and could not.** `tools/perf_render_stats.gd
  -- --views=hall_approach` was run in this container; after over 30 minutes
  of CPU time with the process's own progress essentially stalled (roughly
  90 seconds of further CPU work across a 7-minute wall-clock window, well
  outside the 10–25 minutes `ralph/reports/handover-T1-PERF-2026-08-30.md`
  itself records as normal for one view in this exact container), it was
  killed as a probable instrument stall rather than left to consume further
  budget. This is recorded as **inconclusive, not as evidence the figure has
  moved** — the 3365 + ~208 estimate stands as the best available number,
  attributed to the two lanes that measured it, not independently verified
  by this audit.
- Zero lights reach any of the four originally-sampled open-corridor
  locations in `docs/PERFORMANCE_BUDGET.md`'s own measurement, at either
  time of day — meaning the Hall's own light budget (≤18 exterior omnis, a
  derived ≤4 shadow-casting cap from the single shared 2048² atlas under
  Compatibility) is the one place in the authored world any light-budget
  pressure exists at all, and it has never been checked against a real
  atlas allocation on a real GPU.

**What would close it, in plannable units.** Only one thing does: a real ROG
Ally playtest with a frame-time capture (not draw calls) at the current
build, ideally including the Hall approach specifically, since that is both
the newest heavy addition and the one location with the most exterior
lighting in the authored world. No container-side work — not this lane's,
not any future lane's — can substitute for that measurement; the repo's own
performance document already says as much in four separate places. This
audit adds nothing new to that fact except a fresher confirmation that the
draw-call side of the ledger is close to its self-imposed ceiling
(~3573–3573+/4000, 89–94% spent) at the exact location that keeps regressing
on the visual side too.
