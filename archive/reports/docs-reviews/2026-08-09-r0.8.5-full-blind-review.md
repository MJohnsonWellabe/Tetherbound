# R0.8.5 — Full blind visual review, against the overhauled build

The requeued item from `ralph/BACKLOG.md`: every creature, the trainer,
Grandpa, the Warden, the village, the farmhouse interior and the meadow,
reviewed with the harness in `.claude/skills/visual-judge`, per D06. A blind
sub-agent judged the frames below with no knowledge of what changed or what
this record hoped to find. Both bar questions came back **no**.

## What was captured

- `tools/survey.sh` — the five fixed meadow viewpoints (`shots/01`–`05`).
- `tools/capture_site_shots.gd` — the five staged site frames (village
  square, camp at dusk, opening bedroom, starters by the door, hero meadow;
  `shots/site/`).
- Serial Blender turntables (`tools/art_pipeline/blender/turntable.py`,
  `--size 512`, one model at a time per the OOM warning in
  `ralph/conventions.md`) for the trainer, Grandpa, the Warden, and all
  seventeen pal species — four angles each, `shots/review/<name>/`.
- Three assembled contact sheets (`shots/_sheet_world.png`,
  `shots/_sheet_creatures.png`, `shots/_sheet_creatures_front.png`) built
  with a small ad-hoc Python compositor, since `tools/contact_sheet.gd`
  only reads `shots/*.png` directly and this pass needed far more than the
  five-frame survey it was built for.

None of these are committed — `shots/` is gitignored, same as every prior
review. This document and the backlog updates below are the record.

## The full critique

A general-purpose sub-agent, told nothing about the source, the
conversation, or what had changed, was given the three sheets, four
individual full-resolution frames (`starters-by-the-door`, the Warden,
Tuskroot, Brooktail), and the six references in `docs/reference/`, and asked
to follow the `visual-judge` rubric verbatim.

### The verdict, condensed

**Do the creatures and trainer hold up against the Palworld bar?** Not as a
unified cast. More than half the roster — Brooktail, Bramblebun, Burrowback,
Duskhush, Galecrest, Meadowhart, Mosshell, Mudsnout, Reedwing, Terrapup,
Tuskroot, Veridian — is genuinely good, and the moss-and-stone material
language shared by Burrowback, Mosshell, Tuskroot and Terrapup is "the
single best piece of cohesive art direction anywhere in this set." But
Paddlenewt, Pipwing and Ripplet are a distinctly glossier, big-eyed
toy/gacha finish that doesn't match the painted-matte naturalism of the
rest — "put Pipwing next to Galecrest and they look like they're from two
different franchises." And the trainer/Grandpa read as flat, low-detail
next to the Warden's fully painted, richly textured finish — "the antagonist
looks like concept-final art and the protagonist looks like a placeholder
mascot for a mobile app." The critic called this human-pipeline gap **the
loudest single problem in the whole review**, ahead of the creature
mismatch, because the trainer is who the player looks at the entire game.

**Top three separators from the references, ranked:**

1. **No landmark, anywhere, in any outdoor frame.** Every key-art panel and
   every Palworld reference builds on a layered silhouette read at
   distance — a mountain, a tower, a standing stone. The Tetherbound frames
   have a barn and a windmill and nothing behind them but haze-washed,
   perfectly smooth green domes. Fixable by scene composition alone — a
   landmark silhouette in the mid-to-far distance and the fog pulled back
   far enough for it to survive.
2. **The trainer/Warden fidelity gap** (above). Not fixable by re-staging —
   needs the trainer rebuilt or retextured to the Warden's standard.
3. **Flat, single-band lighting with no time-of-day read.** No frame
   establishes a light direction or colour temperature the way every
   reference does. Fixable in-scene (sun angle, ambient/shadow settings,
   exposure).

**Bar A — do these frames belong to the key art's world?** **No.** The
barn-and-windmill silhouette and general palette gesture the right
direction, but the absence of depth layering, landmarks and real value
contrast means these read as a grey-box of that world, not the world
itself. Two viewpoints (below) don't read as this world at all — they read
as broken renders.

**Bar B — same kind of game as Palworld, side by side?** **No, with one
partial exception.** `starters-by-the-door` — trainer, two creatures, a
farm, a meadow, in one continuous space — gets closest to the shared
grammar. Against `palworld-02`'s foliage density and terrain relief or
`palworld-05`'s lived-in clutter, the Tetherbound frames are too empty and
flat to read as the same tier on composition alone. That gap is fixable
with scatter density and terrain sculpting; the roster/human pipeline
mismatch and the broken frames below are not.

**Full per-criterion critique**, including the readability, colour,
intentionality, lighting, depth and artefact analysis behind the verdict
above, is preserved in the session transcript this document was generated
from; the summary above is what it condensed to; nothing in it was
softened.

## Real bugs found assembling the material — not style notes

The critic was right to flag these under "Artefacts" as broken rendering
rather than mood, and independent inspection while building the sheets
confirms the harness itself is at fault, not the game world:

**`tools/survey.gd`'s fixed viewpoints 01 and 05 render the *inside* of an
unlit room** — a dark box with four picture-frame-shaped windows on one
wall and two grass tufts glowing on the floor — instead of the meadow.
Both use `eye: Vector2(0,0)`. The farmhouse interior appears to occupy
world-space coordinates at or near the origin independent of where
`playground_world.gd`'s `HOUSE_AT = Vector2(-22, -16)` places the exterior
model — plausible given `grandpa_house.gd`'s interior area is built at a
*local* offset that was never composed with the house's own world
position. This predates the indoor-opening addition (D18) reaching this
review; the survey script's authored coordinates never moved to match.

**Viewpoints 03 and 04 render what looks like the camera embedded in or
under the terrain** — scattered props and a hazy sky sit over a flat navy
plane with no ground, no horizon line. `survey.gd`'s own doc comment says
eye heights are "sampled from the same heightfield the terrain was baked
from" — textbook symptom of a stale baked-heightfield sample no longer
matching the terrain after the D18/D19 overhaul reshaped it (the village's
crater walls came down in the same window, per recent `main` history).

Neither is a scene-content problem; both are the review harness's own fixed
coordinates having drifted from the world they were authored against. Added
to `BACKLOG.md` as its own item — recalibrating five hardcoded viewpoints
is a small, mechanical task, but not this task, and PROMPT.md's honesty
rule says discovered work goes on the backlog rather than growing the one
in progress.

**`tools/preview_creatures.gd` does not render creatures at all.** It builds
each body with `BODY.new()` — attaching `pal_body.gd` to a bare
`CharacterBody3D` — instead of instantiating `scenes/pals/pal.tscn`, the
scriptless base scene with the `$Collision`/`$Model`/`$Body`/`$Head`
children every real spawn path (`encounter_director.gd`) instantiates
before attaching a body script. Every creature's `@onready` child lookup
fails in `_ready()`, so the roster line-up this tool exists to produce —
the one purpose-built check for cross-species scale agreement, added after
the largest creature in the game once rendered smaller than a frog
(`docs/reviews/MA-03`) — is seventeen empty ruler bars and no creatures.
Tried the direct fix (instantiate `pal.tscn`, `set_script()` after); the
`$Head` crash went away but a different `is_inside_tree()` failure on
`global_position` appeared right after `add_child()`, for a reason that
needed more debugging time than a secondary tool warrants inside this
task. Reverted rather than ship an unverified partial fix; added to the
backlog as its own item.

Because that tool is broken, this review has **no working cross-species
scale comparison image**. The one real-scale evidence in the whole set is
`starters-by-the-door`, where the critic's read (using the trainer's 1.80m
and the mid-ground fence rail as rulers) leans toward the two starters
reading larger than a "pocket companion" scale — consistent with, not a
contradiction of, D19's deliberate roster resize to boar-scale creatures.
Not treated as a fresh design question; it agrees with a decision already
made.

## A correction to the record, not a new defect

**Tuskroot is not still wearing `ollie-the-songbird.glb`.** This review's
own R0.8.5 backlog note listed that as a standing defect the pass would
re-find. It didn't: `assets/pals/tetherbound/tuskroot/models/pal_tuskroot_lod0.glb`
has a different hash from the songbird placeholder, and the rendered
turntable (`shots/review/tuskroot/three_quarter.png`) is a real tusked boar
carrying the same moss-and-stone mantle language as Mudsnout, its
pre-evolution piglet form — exactly the "must read as Mudsnout grown up"
brief R4.5 set. `species.json` gives it height 2.15 against Mudsnout's
1.55, matching D17's evolved-is-larger rule and R4.6's own numbers exactly.

This looks like R4.5 is done, or far closer to done than the backlog
states — but this review didn't run `smoke_art` or check rigging/animation
clip wiring, which R4.5's own `tests:` field names, so it isn't marked done
here. Flagged in `BACKLOG.md` for a firing that can verify and close it
properly, rather than silently rewritten.

**Confirmed still accurate**, unlike the Tuskroot note: Brooktail has an
ordinary tapered otter tail, not the paddle tail its aquatic profile
implies; the Warden's face is a flat painted texture, not sculpted
geometry.
