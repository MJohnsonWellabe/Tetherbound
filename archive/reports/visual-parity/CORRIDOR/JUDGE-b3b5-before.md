# VP4 Travel Corridor — Band 3→5 (river/relay → ridge → stronghold) — BEFORE judge pass

Blind review of 8 unauthored stations (`00-before-b3b5/`), calibrated against the finished
first half of the route (`round4/`, stations 01–08) and the project references
(`docs/reference/tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`, `docs/reference/palworld-0*.jpg`,
`site/img/page-board.jpg`).

Nothing has been authored on these 8 stations yet. This is a placement brief, not a
verdict on finished work — except for the two or three stations that already clear the bar
and should not be touched.

---

## Calibration note

`round4/` is a useful yardstick. Every one of its 8 frames has a tree wall or canopy
filling at least a third of the frame by the second or third ground-plane layer, a
worn dirt path with visible edge wear, and dense low scatter (flowers, reeds, stones)
right up to the camera. Sky never exceeds roughly half the frame. That is the standard
being asked of the second half.

Across the 8 Band 3–5 stations under review here, four (09, 13, 14, and to a lesser
extent 10) fall well short of that density; three (12, 15, 16) already meet or beat it.

---

## Per-station findings

### 09 — river-lock-entry-day — FAIL
**Reads:** A worn dirt path receding into open grass, with a thin, distant treeline
sitting on the horizon at roughly 40% frame height and doing almost nothing. Sky
occupies well over half the image.
**Missing:** There is no river and no lock — the station's own name promises a water
feature and neither the foreground nor the mid-ground has one. No rock, no copse, no
structure. There is also a stray, half-cropped blue winged-creature mesh poking in
from the far left edge that reads as an accident, not a composed element — it doesn't
belong in the frame and isn't doing any compositional work.
**Top defect:** Exactly the rejected shape from the rubric — player, empty grass,
sky — with the added problem that the one prop in frame (the blue creature at the
edge) looks like debris rather than an authored choice.
**Verdict:** Fail. This is the corridor's establishing shot for the river/relay band
and currently promises nothing about where the player is headed.

### 10 — relay-approach-day — WEAK PASS / BORDERLINE
**Reads:** A campsite clearing — sign post and campfire foreground-left, a dense tree
wall filling the right third of the frame, and a red banner glimpsed through the trees
ahead. This is the first station with real foreground furniture (post, fire, banner).
**Missing:** The relay itself should be starting to announce itself here — this is the
"approach," and nothing of the industrial structure (wall, tower, gate silhouette)
is visible yet even in silhouette through the trees. The left half of the frame is
still just open field and sky.
**Top defect:** The right side of the frame is doing all the work; the left half is
unclaimed, so the frame doesn't yet read as converging on a destination.
**Verdict:** Weak pass. Foreground/mid-ground furniture exists, but the "approach"
promise (a growing destination) isn't landing yet.

### 11 — relay-day — FAIL (on execution, not on concept)
**Reads:** The industrial occupation itself — plain concrete walls, a gate opening,
crates, a workbench, and three Team Tether NPCs on a bare pale-tan ground plane. This
is the one station in the batch with human figures doing visible work, which is the
right idea for "occupation."
**Missing:** Weathering, ground texture, and Team Tether colour identity. The ground
is a single flat off-white/khaki fill with none of the grass-to-dirt transition seen
everywhere else in the corridor — it reads as an unstyled construction blockout, not
an occupied industrial site. The walls are geometrically plain with hard seams and no
grime, moss, or oxblood banners/signage to mark this as Team Tether territory (compare
the ivy-and-red-banner treatment on the Hall in the key art). There's also a visible
harsh material/scale seam at the top-right where a ramp geometry meets the wall.
**Top defect:** The bare pale ground plane breaks the corridor's palette continuity —
it's the only station in the set that doesn't look like the same world as its
neighbours.
**Verdict:** Fail as shown, though the composition (structure + NPCs + props) is
already in the right shape — this is a dressing/material problem, not a layout
problem.

### 12 — old-mill-crossing-day — PASS
**Reads:** A half-timbered mill building crowning the hill dead ahead, framed by a
tree line on both sides and a glimpse of water lower-right. Foreground includes a
sign and a small gate structure. This is a genuine landmark shot with three clean
depth layers (path/grass foreground, tree walls left and right, mill + sky
background).
**Missing:** Nothing structural. Minor: the water on the right is only a sliver —
could read more clearly as a crossing if a touch more of it broke the tree line.
**Top defect:** None significant.
**Verdict:** Pass. Matches `round4` density and reads instantly at small size — leave
alone.

### 13 — band4-entry-bend-day — FAIL (worst in the set)
**Reads:** Open grass, a scattering of low weeds and flowers, and a single small
distant tree cluster sitting on the horizon. Sky is roughly 55% of the frame.
**Missing:** Everything the rubric calls out by name — no tree line, no copse, no
rock, no water edge, no structure, no landmark. This is the literal "player → empty
grass → sky" failure case the rubric bar exists to catch.
**Top defect:** Total absence of mid-ground structure; nothing in this frame tells the
player they've crossed into a new band (Band 4 / upper-meadow ironwood) rather than
still being in Band 2.
**Verdict:** Fail, and the clearest one in the batch — this is a blank canvas, not a
weak composition.

### 14 — ridge-camp-approach-day — WEAK PASS / BORDERLINE
**Reads:** A trainer NPC and the player on a dirt path with a dense tree wall filling
the right half of the frame and a signpost reading "Watchtower Spur." There's real
mid-ground content here (NPC + tree wall) that keeps it off the outright-fail list.
**Missing:** Neither "ridge" nor "camp" is legible. The terrain is flat — no rise,
ledge, or rock shelf anywhere in frame to signal elevation — and there's no camp
silhouette (tent, watchtower, smoke) visible past the trees to justify "approach."
The left half of the frame (behind the NPC) is open grass to the horizon.
**Top defect:** Nothing in the frame differentiates "ridge" from any other meadow
path — the name promises elevation and destination, the image shows neither.
**Verdict:** Weak pass on density (NPC + trees keep it off the floor), but fails the
station's own brief — needs a real fix, not left alone.

### 15 — stronghold-approach-day — BORDERLINE PASS
**Reads:** The dark Hall silhouette sits on the horizon behind a bank of storm-grey
smoke, with a red-accented gate/fence structure and a tree cluster in the mid-ground,
rolling hills either side. Three real depth layers, and the Team Tether colour
language (red gate roundels) is present and correctly reserved for this element.
**Missing:** The landmark itself is thin and low-contrast against the smoke bank — at
30% viewing size it's the part of the frame most likely to disappear, and this is
supposed to be the station where the Hall starts announcing itself as "a growing
landmark." It needs to read faster than it currently does.
**Top defect:** Landmark-to-sky contrast is too low for the shot's job.
**Verdict:** Borderline pass — good bones, but a contrast/value pass on the Hall
silhouette (not a scene rebuild) would take this the rest of the way. Light touch
only.

### 16 — hall-gate-approach-day — PASS (strongest in the set)
**Reads:** The Hall itself, now large and dark with visible towers, a bridge, and
red banners, paired with a distinct cyan-lit Team Tether pylon/relay tower on the
right connected by a visible tether cable, plus a wolf companion in the mid-ground
and the player on the path. This is the richest frame in the whole batch — landmark,
industrial hero object, companion creature, and layered terrain all in one shot, with
smoke overhead tying it back to station 15.
**Missing:** Nothing significant.
**Top defect:** None.
**Verdict:** Pass, and the best-composed station in either half of the route reviewed
so far — leave alone.

## Ranking, weakest to strongest, with the specific fix

1. **13 — band4-entry-bend** (worst). Add a mid-ground tree cluster or rock outcrop
   directly on the path 20–40m out, plus a near-camera boulder or reed clump to one
   side, so the frame has at least one authored landmark breaking the grass plain.
2. **09 — river-lock-entry**. Put a water edge (the "river lock" the name promises)
   across the lower-middle third of the frame, add a foreground rock/reed cluster
   camera-left, and remove or properly place the stray half-cropped blue creature
   currently clipping in at the far-left edge.
3. **11 — relay**. Replace the flat pale ground fill with a dirt/gravel/patchy-grass
   transition and add foreground clutter (crates, cable runs, footprints); weather the
   walls and add Team Tether oxblood signage/banners so the site reads as occupied
   territory, not a blockout.
4. **14 — ridge-camp-approach**. Add a rock shelf or raised terrain lip on one side of
   the path to sell "ridge," and put a camp silhouette (tent, watchtower, smoke)
   beyond the tree line so there's a destination past the NPC.
5. **10 — relay-approach**. Let the relay's silhouette (a wall edge or tower tip)
   show through or over the right-side tree wall so the shot reads as converging on a
   destination; claim the empty left half with a second copse or the same tree wall
   continuing across.
6. **15 — stronghold-approach**. Raise the Hall silhouette's contrast against the
   smoke bank (darker mass or a rim-light edge) so it reads at small size — everything
   else in the frame can stay as is.
7. **12 — old-mill-crossing**. Already passes. Leave alone (optional: widen the water
   sliver on the right slightly).
8. **16 — hall-gate-approach**. Already passes, and is the strongest station in the
   set. Leave alone.

**Leave alone:** 12 and 16 outright; 15 with only the one landmark-contrast tweak
above — do not touch its composition, density, or colour placement, they're already
correct.

## The three things that most separate these frames from the references

1. **Mid-ground structure that a landmark reference always has and half of this set
   doesn't.** Every reference frame (key art, all five Palworld shots, the site
   hero) has a tree line, ridge, ruin, or building claiming the middle third of the
   frame. Stations 09 and 13 give that middle third to open grass and sky — the
   single largest gap in the set.
2. **Palette continuity.** The key art and every Palworld reference stay in one
   saturated green-and-earth register from foreground to horizon. Station 11 breaks
   that with a flat, unweathered pale-tan ground plane that reads as a different
   material system entirely, not a place in the same world as station 12 or 16 next
   to it.
3. **Landmark growth as a readable sequence.** `palworld-04-plateau-landmark.jpg`
   shows a landmark that dominates its frame even at distance, through value contrast
   against the sky. Station 15's Hall is present but under-contrasted against its own
   smoke bank, so the "growing landmark" beat the band is supposed to deliver (weak at
   15, strong at 16) currently has a soft start rather than a clean escalation.

## Bar questions

**A. Do these frames read as belonging to the world in
`docs/reference/tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`?**
**No — for the batch as a whole**, though three stations individually pass. 12, 15,
and 16 have the key art's layered depth, warm dirt-path foreground, and landmark
silhouette language and would sit comfortably next to it. 09, 11, and 13 would not:
09 and 13 are missing the mid-ground layer the key art never omits, and 11's bare pale
ground has no equivalent anywhere in the key art's six vignettes, all of which keep a
green, planted, lived-in ground plane even around the Team Tether stronghold.

**B. Shown beside `docs/reference/palworld-0*.jpg`, would someone say these are
trying to be the same kind of game?**
**No, on the current average** — the same split applies. Palworld's frames are
uniformly dense: grass, scattered vegetation, and terrain incident right up to the
camera in all five references, with the horizon always claimed by hills, cliffs, or
ruins (`palworld-04-plateau-landmark.jpg` is the clearest single comparison for what
15 and 16 are reaching for and already achieving). Stations 09, 13, and 14 have the
sparse, uncluttered ground and empty horizon that reads as a different, less-finished
game sitting right next to stations that clear the bar.

**What's fixable versus what needs new art:** everything flagged above is fixable by
changing the scene — placement of existing tree/rock/water-edge kit pieces, ground
material and texture swaps at the relay, terrain elevation at the ridge, and a
value/contrast pass on the Hall's material at station 15. Nothing in this batch
requires a new asset: the strong stations (12, 15, 16) already prove the installed
kit (mill building, Hall, tether pylon, tree/rock props) reads well when placed with
density and layering — the weak stations just haven't had that placement pass done
yet, which matches the "nothing authored" framing of this before-set.
