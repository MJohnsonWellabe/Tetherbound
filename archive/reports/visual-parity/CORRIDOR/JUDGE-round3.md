# VP4 — Travel Corridor Composition — Judge Round 3

Blind visual review. Inputs: `docs/reference/tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`, `docs/reference/palworld-0*.jpg`,
`site/img/page-board.jpg`; BASELINE = `CORRIDOR/00-before/`, PREVIOUS = `CORRIDOR/round2/`, NEW = `CORRIDOR/round4/`.
No code, config, diffs, or prior report text was read before forming this judgement.

## Headline

The canopy-rendering defect that dominated both prior rounds — trees whose foliage rendered as pale
green/white **billboard shards** ("shattered glass") instead of leaf mass — is fixed across all eight
stations in NEW. This is the single biggest visible change in the set and it changes the read of nearly
every frame: canopies now have rounded, shaded, leaf-coloured mass, so "tree" reads as "tree" instead of
"broken glass sculpture," and the corridor stops looking like a rendering bug and starts looking like a
place. That fix does more for parity with the references than any compositional change in this round.

With that defect gone, composition is now the real bottleneck, and it is uneven: five of eight stations
(01, 03, 04, 05, 08) are genuinely strong and would not look out of place cut into the Palworld reference
set. Two (02, 06) are improved and land at-or-above baseline. One (07) has lost the one piece of
foreground/mid-ground structure it used to have and is now the weakest station in the corridor —
worse than baseline, not just worse than the other seven.

## Per-station findings

### 01 — village-edge-day
- **Baseline:** fence leading line, rock mound landmark center-distance, tree cluster left and along the
  horizon — but every canopy is a pale shard mass, especially the giant one clipping the top-left corner.
- **Previous:** identical composition and identical shard defect; no change.
- **New:** same composition, canopies now render as real rounded green foliage. The fence leading line,
  the rock-mound landmark, and the tree line all still read, and now the trees actually look like trees.
  Genuine win — one of the strongest frames in the set, close to Palworld-04's landmark-plus-grove
  arrangement.
- **Verdict:** better than baseline. **Top remaining defect:** the near-foreground tree at top-right is
  clipped hard by the frame edge; a slightly wider berth would read more like a designed frame.

### 02 — first-bend-day
- **Baseline:** two symmetric shard-tree clusters, hard left and hard right, with a big gap of open sky
  and empty middle-distance between them — reads as two separate stage flats, not a landscape.
- **Previous:** unchanged from baseline (right cluster if anything reads slightly denser/closer than left).
  This is the "right-heavy" previous verdict — the two masses are not the problem, their imbalance is.
- **New:** materially recomposed, not just re-shaded. A single believable grove of ~7 discrete, varied-
  height trees now sits left-of-center at path's edge, and a thinner tree line continues across the
  horizon to the right rather than sitting in a separate hard-right block. This reads as one grove the
  path skirts, not two matched props flanking a stage.
- **Verdict:** better than both baseline and previous — the right-heavy imbalance the previous round
  flagged is gone. **Top remaining defect:** the right two-thirds of the frame is still mostly open grass
  down to a thin, low tree line at the horizon — there is no mid-ground element on that side, so the
  frame is balanced in mass but still asymmetric in structural interest. A mid-distance rock or a second,
  smaller tree cluster right-of-path would finish the job.

### 03 — loop-apex-day
- **Baseline:** two oversized shard-canopy trunks dominate the right two-thirds of frame at close range;
  a further shard tree-line sits in the far background upper-left. Foliage reads as damaged geometry, not
  woodland.
- **Previous:** same composition, same shard defect — unchanged.
- **New:** canopies fixed; the near-right trunks now read as two big believable trees, and the background
  tree line resolves into an actual dense grove edge stretching left-to-right across the middle distance.
  Strong sense of depth and of standing at the edge of real woods.
- **Verdict:** better than baseline. **Top remaining defect:** the two foreground trunks are close enough
  and similar enough in scale/spacing that they read as a matched pair rather than incidental — some
  scale variety between them would help avoid a "prop pair" read.

### 04 — eastward-swing-day
- **Baseline:** dense wall of shard-canopy trees fills nearly the whole frame at close range, leaving no
  visible path forward and no depth cue beyond the wall — the single worst "wall of glass" shot in the
  set.
- **Previous:** camera/composition already recomposed — grove pulled to the right third, open path and
  scattered small trees left, a genuine tree line on the far horizon. Big structural win, but every
  canopy in it is still the pale shard.
- **New:** same strong composition, now with real foliage. Near-left scattered trees (foreground), a
  right-hand grove with clear individual canopies and visible bark texture (mid-ground), and a distant
  tree line closing the horizon (background) — all three depth layers the rubric asks for, in one frame,
  with a clear path reading through the middle.
- **Verdict:** clean win, confirmed and improved. This is the strongest single frame in the corridor and
  a genuine match for the grove-and-path language of the keyart's second and third tiles. **Top remaining
  defect:** none structural; minor — the grass/flower scatter directly under the right-hand grove is a
  touch sparser than the open field to its left, a small density seam at the tree line's edge.

### 05 — south-bridge-day
- **Baseline:** shard-canopy trees crowd both edges and overhead, a dead tree snag pokes through, and a
  small figure and a set of steps are visible but hard to parse through the foliage noise.
- **Previous:** unchanged from baseline — same crowding, same shard defect.
- **New:** canopies fixed; the frame resolves into a clean tree-flanked approach with real depth — near
  trunks left and right framing the path, a further tree cluster mid-ground, and the steps/structure at
  the vanishing point now legible instead of buried in shard noise. Reads close to the keyart's
  "wooden dock through oak canopy" tile.
- **Verdict:** better than baseline and previous. **Top remaining defect:** the dead-tree snag from
  baseline is still present center-frame and reads as a stray asset rather than a deliberate accent —
  worth confirming it's intentional set-dressing and not a leftover placeholder.

### 06 — stone-root-entry-day
- **Baseline:** already compromised — a huge pale shard slab clips the top-left corner and partially
  obscures the sky, but the signpost (left) and the tree-line/avenue in the middle distance are both
  legible.
- **Previous:** regressed hard. A near-camera trunk-and-canopy mass now fills roughly the left 40% of the
  frame at point-blank range, burying the signpost almost entirely and eating most of the tree line that
  used to read across the middle distance. This is the frame the previous round flagged as broken, and
  the screenshot confirms it: the signpost is barely a sliver at the left edge and the avenue read is gone.
- **New:** restored and then some. The near-left mass is gone; the signpost is fully legible again at
  left-of-center; a strong avenue of trees now runs the full width of the middle distance with real depth
  (near trunks right, receding tree line left-to-right); an NPC figure on the path is clearly visible.
  This is at least baseline quality and arguably the best-composed "gate" shot in the set — it reads as an
  authored entrance into the next area.
- **Verdict:** **restored to at least baseline — yes, confirmed, and it now exceeds baseline.**
  **Top remaining defect:** none structural. The right-foreground pair of trunks is close enough to camera
  that their bark texture tiling is visible at a resolution nothing else in frame is; minor texture-scale
  mismatch, not a composition problem.

### 07 — band2-mid-day
- **Baseline:** a real tree cluster occupies the left third of the frame at near-mid range (with the shard
  defect), a wildlife prop (sheep) sits right-of-path, and a thin tree line closes the horizon — modest
  but it has actual foreground/mid-ground mass on one side of the frame.
- **Previous:** the left cluster is gone. What remains is two small isolated shard-trees at the far
  horizon (left and right) and a dark rock/prop shape at right; the frame is now mostly open grass with
  very little mid-ground structure — a visible loss versus baseline, separate from and in addition to the
  shard-canopy defect.
- **New:** canopies fixed, but the structural loss from Previous carries forward unchanged. There is one
  isolated tree left-of-path at a middle distance, a small huddle of ~5 trees far upper-right, and a camp
  prop (tent + sheep) right of path — but nothing forms an actual foreground copse or a mid-ground mass
  the way every other station now has. This is functionally "player → open field → a couple of trees on
  the skyline → sky," the exact pattern the bar exists to rule out.
- **Verdict:** **worse than baseline**, and not corrected by this round's fixes — this station needed a
  scatter/placement change, not a shader fix, and didn't get one. **Top remaining defect:** no
  foreground or mid-ground tree mass on either side of the path; the frame currently reads as the
  weakest, least-authored shot in the corridor.

### 08 — band2-far-day
- **Baseline:** rock/mountain landmark left, a shard-canopy grove right, a further shard tree line closing
  the gap between them at the horizon, twin signposts in the foreground.
- **Previous:** unchanged from baseline — same composition, same shard defect.
- **New:** canopies fixed; the rock formation reads with real form and shading, the right-hand grove has
  individual leafy canopies at varied heights, and the horizon tree line behind the rocks closes the
  scene convincingly. This is a strong landmark shot and a fair match for Palworld-04's rock-plateau-plus-
  grove framing.
- **Verdict:** better than baseline. **Top remaining defect:** the left signpost's text is clipped/
  overflowing its plank ("...ten _rderal") in every round including this one — a legibility bug, not a
  composition one, but it sits in the foreground of an otherwise strong shot and undercuts it.

## Direct answers to the assignment questions

- **Is 06 restored to at least baseline quality?** Yes. It is not merely restored — it now reads as the
  best "gate/entrance" composition in the corridor: signpost fully legible, full-width avenue of trees in
  the middle distance, an NPC on the path giving it life. The near-trunk wall that buried it in Previous
  is completely gone.
- **Is 02 balanced now?** Yes, materially better. The two-separate-stage-flats problem and the
  right-heavy skew are both gone — it now reads as one grove the path skirts on its left. It is not
  perfectly balanced: the right two-thirds of the frame still has no mid-ground element, only open grass
  down to a thin horizon line, so call it "no longer lopsided" rather than "fully resolved."
- **Do canopies now read as foliage?** Yes, at all eight stations, unconditionally. This is the round's
  real achievement — the "shattered glass" billboard shard rendering that defined both prior rounds is
  gone everywhere it was checked, replaced by shaded, rounded, leaf-colored canopy mass with visible bark
  texture on trunks.
- **Any station worse than baseline?** Yes — station 07. It lost its one foreground/mid-ground tree
  cluster between Baseline and Previous and this round did not restore it; canopy-shader fixes cannot fix
  a station whose problem is scatter placement, not rendering.

## Rank: three weakest stations (this round)

1. **07 — band2-mid-day.** The only station that fails the bar outright: no foreground or mid-ground
   structure on either side of the path, just open field to a scatter of trees on the skyline. Also the
   only station that regressed relative to baseline rather than merely lagging the others.
2. **02 — first-bend-day.** Much improved and no longer lopsided, but still has a full half-frame (the
   right side) with nothing between the player and the horizon tree line — the last station where "empty
   middle distance" is still a fair criticism, even though it's no longer failing outright.
3. **08 — band2-far-day.** Strong composition, held back only by the clipped/overflowing signpost text
   in the foreground — a legibility defect rather than a compositional one, but a real one, and it's the
   most polish-limiting single defect left in the set now that 06 and 07's structural issues are (07
   aside) addressed.

## Rank: three biggest gaps vs. the references (this round)

1. **Painted light and atmosphere vs. the keyart.** Every NEW frame is flat-lit under a uniform hazy-blue
   sky with no visible sun direction, no rim light, and no atmospheric depth-fade into the distance. The
   keyart's every tile has a clear light source, warm-to-cool gradient toward the horizon, and soft
   volumetric depth; these frames have none of that, and it's the single largest remaining gap versus the
   art-direction board specifically. This is a lighting/post gap, not a scatter gap.
2. **Ground-plane detail and color variety vs. Palworld.** Palworld-02 and -03 show visibly varied ground
   cover — worn dirt, patchy grass density, colour-graded terrain blending under trees. These corridor
   frames use one flat green grass texture with sparse small-flower/blade props scattered on top, and the
   dirt path itself is a single uniform brown with no wear variation. Side by side with Palworld-02's open
   field path this reads as noticeably plainer ground.
3. **Character presence and life.** Palworld's shots are full of creatures, a bow-and-pack silhouette with
   personality, and combat/task UI implying an active world. These corridor frames show the trainer's back
   (a fairly generic silhouette) walking through empty scenery with, at most, one static NPC or a sheep
   prop. Nothing in the corridor set puts a creature in frame the way every Palworld reference does — that
   is squarely a content/population gap the corridor's job (proving the route reads) doesn't currently
   need to close, but it is real and it is why these frames read as "environment test" rather than
   "gameplay moment."

## Bar questions

**A. Do these frames read as belonging to the world in `tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`?**
**Yes, for the first time this round — with one exception (07).** With the shard-canopy defect gone,
the palette (natural greens, warm dirt path, pale sky), the landmark language (rock mounds, tree groves,
signposted paths), and the grove-and-clearing compositions at 01, 03, 04, 05, 06 and 08 are all
recognizably the same world the keyart depicts — 04, 06 and 08 in particular could sit next to the
keyart's grove/dock/landmark tiles without embarrassment. Station 02 is close but has a thin side; station
07 does not belong — it reads as an unfinished placeholder field, not the meadows in the keyart.

**B. Shown these frames beside `palworld-0*.jpg`, would someone say these are trying to be the same kind
of game?** **Directionally yes, but not yet at parity, and someone would say so immediately.** The
biggest tell is not composition anymore — it's the flat, uniform lighting and the total absence of
creatures or a populated world in every one of these eight frames; Palworld's screenshots all carry
dynamic light, atmosphere, and pals in nearly every shot. The vegetation density and grove-and-path
compositions in 01/04/05/06/08 now genuinely resemble Palworld-02 and Palworld-04's structure. What's
fixable by changing the scene: 02's empty right side, 07's missing foreground mass entirely, and the
signpost text overflow at 06/08 — all scatter/placement/UI-content fixes, no new art required. What is
not fixable by changing the scene: the flat lighting/atmosphere gap and the total absence of any creature
or lived-in detail are both bigger swings — the first needs lighting/post work, the second needs content
(creatures, props, NPCs) that a travel-corridor survey doesn't currently place in frame, not a scatter
tweak.
