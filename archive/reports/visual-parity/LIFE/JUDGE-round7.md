## Per-frame findings

**Better/worse:** No change.
**Living bodies at native size:** Two humans — the trainer and a white-haired elder NPC near

### 01-village-edge-day

**Better/worse:** No visible change.
**Living bodies:** Two humans (trainer + tan/khaki NPC) beside a boulder on a hillside path.
No creature body is visible in the *day* version of this stand at all — worth flagging since

### 01-village-edge-night — **evidence point (C)**
**Change:** Visually identical to round 6 — same framing, same faint pale smudge to the left

**Better/worse:** No change from round 6.
**Living bodies at native size:** One human (trainer) fully readable; the tan NPC readable by

### 02-mill-pond-banks-day / -night
**Change:** No visible change from round 6.
**Living bodies:** Trainer only, standing alone on the dock in both. No creatures present at
this stand in either lighting condition — fine as a landscape/water beat, but it contributes
nothing to the "living world" claim, day or night.

### 03-band1-open-meadow-day — **evidence point (A)**
**Change:** This is the one stand that visibly moved. Round 6 showed a single pale/cream
cluster on the right, low-contrast against the tan-green ground and hard to parse as anything
alive. Round 7 replaces/supplements it with a pack of grey wolf-type creatures on the right —
one animal standing alone mid-ground, a tighter cluster of three more further right — plus two
pale pinkish rounded shapes on the left that remain as ambiguous as round 6's cluster was.
**Better/worse:** Better. The right-hand pack is a real improvement: grey bodies read against
the ochre dirt path with enough silhouette (pointed ears, four legs, tails) to count.
**Living bodies at native size, strict:** The right-hand grouping gives **at least 3–4**
countable wolf bodies without zooming. The left-hand pair of pale blobs is still camouflaged —
same failure mode round 6 had, just now confined to half the frame instead of all of it.
**Verdict on (A): PASS** — ≥2 animals (in fact 3–4) are countable without zoom, on the right
side of frame. The left side is an unresolved residual of the round-6 problem.

### 04-relay-camp-day
**Change:** Round 6 showed three pale/cream shapes bunched at the left tree line, blending
into pale flowers/undergrowth beneath the canopy. Round 7 shows three grey wolf-type creatures
in roughly the same spot, more spread out, standing in a small clearing between trunks rather
than buried in undergrowth.
**Better/worse:** Marginally better — grey-on-green-shadow reads slightly more cleanly than
cream-on-cream did, and the spacing helps separate individuals. Still the single densest,
best-composed frame of the set (round 6's "best" verdict still holds), but the improvement here
is smaller than at the open meadow.
**Living bodies:** 3 countable wolves at native size, readable without zoom.

### 04-relay-camp-night — **evidence point (B)**
**Change:** As close to pixel-identical to round 6 as any pair in this set — same campfire
glow, same black silhouette trees, same faint pink/orange smudge to the right of the fire.
**Better/worse:** No change.
**Living bodies at native size, strict: FAIL.** The campfire throws light on the trainer's
silhouette and on smoke, but everything beyond a ~1m radius of the flame is solid black,
including the tree canopy, which reads as pure negative space rather than foliage. The smudge
beside the fire that might be a creature has no discernible head, legs, or edge — it cannot be
counted as a body, only guessed at. **Verdict on (B): FAIL**, unchanged from round 6. Whatever
fix landed for the open meadow and relay-camp-day did not touch night exposure/fill at this
stand at all.

### 05-ridge-camp-day
**Change:** The creatures present are different in kind from round 6, not just position —
round 6 showed two blue-white winged creatures with a grey rock-like shape further back; round
7 shows two teal/cream spotted tortoise-shelled creatures standing together mid-ground, plus a
dark rock shape and a distant wooden structure. (This may simply be a different capture/spawn
roll rather than an intentional change, but it is what's in the frame.)
**Better/worse:** Different, not clearly better or worse — the shelled creatures in round 7 are

legible as round 6's birds were.
**Living bodies:** 2 clearly countable creatures at native size, good silhouette, plausible
scale next to the trainer (roughly waist-to-chest height, consistent with a mid-size grazer).

### 06-starter-beside-trainer-day — **evidence point (D), pairing**
**Change:** Pixel-identical to round 6.
**Better/worse:** No change — it didn't need one.
**Living bodies:** Trainer plus one large brown/cream/teal-spotted quadruped, standing
shoulder-to-shoulder. Scale reads correctly: the creature is visibly bigger and heavier than
the trainer (per the rubric's "the one you fight alongside should be bigger than the one you
practise on" test), consistent with this being the starter/hero partner rather than a small
early-game catch. Clean silhouette, legible color-blocking, best-dressed creature model in the
set. **Verdict: PASS, unchanged.**

## Remaining gaps, ranked

1. **Night exposure kills every creature read.** Both night stands that are supposed to carry
   life (village-edge-night, relay-camp-night) render companions/wildlife as an unshaped smudge
   of color with no visible silhouette — while the *human* figures in the same frames stay
   legible via rim/fire light. Whatever lighting rig lights the player and NPCs at night is not
   reaching the creature meshes, or the creatures have no fill light of their own. This is the
   single biggest remaining hole in "world life, day and night," and it is untouched since
   round 6.
2. **Open-meadow left half is still the round-6 failure, just smaller.** The pale pink blobs on
   the left of 03-band1-open-meadow-day have the exact same low-contrast-against-pale-ground
   problem the whole frame had in round 6. The fix that worked on the right (grey bodies on
   warm dirt) was not applied consistently — same stand, same frame, two different outcomes.
3. **Wolves are a flat, characterless placeholder next to the roster's better creatures.** The
   grey wolf model that now populates open-meadow and relay-camp-day reads as a generic
   low-poly quadruped — no accent color, no readable texture detail, no personality — next to
   the shelled/spotted creatures at ridge-camp and the pairing frame, which have actual color
   blocking and silhouette interest. It solves "is something alive here" but not "does this
   look like a Tetherbound creature."

## The two bar questions

**A. Do these frames read as belonging to the world in `tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`?**
**No.** The keyart's hills have soft painterly grass gradients and layered canopy depth; these
frames' hills carry a uniform speckled dot-texture that reads as a repeated tileable pattern
rather than grass, and the sky is a flatter, slightly desaturated blue-grey next to the
keyart's saturated azure. The keyart's night panel (bottom-right) still reads distance,
silhouette, and a warm campfire glow with the creature fully lit beside the trainer — exactly
the shot 04-relay-camp-night is attempting and exactly what it fails to deliver; the creature
that should mirror that reference panel is invisible here. Palette family (greens, warm dirt,
blue sky) is in the right neighborhood, so it's not a wrong-world failure, but the surface
texture and night lighting gap are large enough that this is a no.

**B. Shown beside `palworld-0*.jpg`, would someone say these are trying to be the same kind of
game?** **Partially, and still no overall.** The open-meadow and relay-camp-day stands now
show small same-species animal groups the way Palworld's screenshots do — that's real, new
common ground since round 6. But Palworld's reference shots pack multiple *species* in one
frame with strong individual color design (pink whale-thing, orange fox-thing, blue dragon
bird, each instantly distinguishable), while these frames show single-species grey wolf packs
with no individual character. The human/trainer models are also far simpler and flatter-shaded
than Palworld's cast. On density of life alone the gap narrowed; on creature-art ambition and
character polish it did not. Net: **no**, but closer than round 6.

### Fixable by changing the scene vs. needs new art

**Scene-fixable (lighting/placement/composition, no new assets required):**
- Night creature visibility at relay-camp-night and village-edge-night — extend whatever key/
  fill light currently reaches the human figures at night to the creature meshes standing next
  to them (rim light, a warmer campfire falloff radius, or simply not placing the "companion" in
  full shadow).
- The open-meadow left-side blobs — reposition or relight those two creatures onto the same
  warm-dirt/high-contrast ground the right-side pack already uses, or swap them for the same
  wolf archetype that's already proven legible in this exact frame.
- Hill surface texture reads as a repeating dot pattern rather than grass at a glance in several
  day frames (03, 05, 06) — a texture/shader pass, not new geometry.

**Needs new art (not fixable by relighting or repositioning):**
- The grey wolf model itself has no distinguishing color, texture, or accessory — it is legible
  now, but it does not look like a *designed* Tetherbound creature next to the shelled/spotted
  creatures elsewhere in this same set. Matching Palworld's per-creature character bar requires
  new material/color work on that model, not a scene change.
- Multi-species variety within a single "world life" frame — every populated stand in this round
  is one species repeated 2-4 times. Reaching Palworld's reference requires more creature
  variety actually placed together, which is content, not lighting.
