# Tetherbound — full project audit and repository reset, 2026-09-02

Evidence base: `origin/main` at `cf535cce`, cloned fresh; Godot 4.7-stable headless in
the container; the full unit suite; the player-path smoke chain run one test at a time;
five freshly rendered survey frames plus a code-blind critique; seven read-only
inventory agents; the GitHub PR and Actions history. Nothing below is taken from a
ledger's say-so unless marked *(reported)*.

## 1. Executive assessment

Tetherbound is much further along as a **codebase** than as a **game**. Every chapter
system exists, is wired into one continuous world scene, is data-driven, and is
covered by 1,728 passing unit tests and a large smoke library. The source tree is
clean: one autoload, no duplicated systems, no dead scripts, four TODOs in 93k lines.
That is unusual and worth saying plainly.

What has not happened is proof by play. The chapter has never been played start to
finish by the project's own evidence process; the first thirty minutes can dead-end
(fixed this session); the continuous first-session smoke stalls on the objective chain;
the South Bridge has a hole a real player can fall in; and four owner-reported
game-breakers (interact reliability, frame rate, sleep, day/night) cannot be closed
from a container and have not been confirmed on the Ally since their fixes landed.

Visually the build is a competent stylised prototype with real strengths (sky, village
buildings, the Hall approach, faction colour discipline) and a composition problem:
from any elevated or distant viewpoint the world reads as evenly textured acreage, not
a designed place. Trees are repeated puffballs, the ground is a carpet with no
mid-layer, and no creature appears in the standard survey. The fix is scene work, not
new art, and it is the whole of Gate 2 in the roadmap.

The repository itself was the largest drag on development: 4.7 GB tracked, 60 % of it
screenshot and telemetry dumps; a control-plane directory rewritten daily; a dozen
competing "read this first" documents; and a CI that reported green over unverified
code by three separate mechanisms. This session replaced that with ten source-of-truth
documents, an archive, ignore rules for evidence payloads, and a corrected CI base.

  `smoke_modal_stacking`, `smoke_menu` all pass; the historical trader/build freeze does
  not reproduce).
- **Test depth.** 124 unit files (1,728 tests, 0 failures, 28.5 min), 90 smokes, 40
  Gate F segment scripts, a real-input harness. The tests that failed this session
  failed for real reasons and named them precisely.
- **Content exists at chapter scale.** 25 species, 29 field trainers, 283 spawn entries
  across five bands, 131 harvest nodes, 130 conversations, 33 objectives, a tournament,
  a relay, a stronghold with a Warden and a legendary chamber, riding, farming, trade,

- **The visual program learned the right lessons.** Code-blind critics, measured axes,
  one-shot rules, ceilings recorded with mechanisms named. The Hall approach and the
  relay are genuinely close to the bar.
- **Owner feedback is captured verbatim** and outranks ledgers. That precedence rule is
  correct and was the reason three false "fixed" claims were caught.

## 4. What is not going well

- **"Landed" was being treated as "done".** Three same-day fixes from the 09-01 playtest
  were still broken by play on 09-02. A config read produced a "nothing to fix" that was
  wrong. A test existed for the camp bed that never put a creature in the bed.
- **CI lied three ways** (docs-only repaint, `[skip ci]` + previous-push diff, retry
  rescue). One is fixed this session; the other two are process rules now.
- **Evidence swamped the repository.** 49 % of recent commits were report dumps; 2.8 GB
  of frames and telemetry; per-run notes for twelve S03 attempts in one afternoon.
- **The control plane churned faster than the game.** `START_HERE.md` rewritten twice in
  a day; four handovers in two days; a 4,069-line backlog deleted and rewritten;
  "Gate D" meaning two different things in two live schemes.
- **The world is not composed.** Density work happened (grass carpet on, scatter baked,
  812k placements) but the judges' consistent finding is "subject on a plain".
- **Expensive reasoning was spent on mechanical work.** Fable-tier sessions running
  capture loops and tuning rounds; twelve iterations of one harness segment.

## 5. Visual assessment

Fresh frames from the current tree (`archive/reports/reset-2026-09-02/_sheet_survey.png`),
five fixed stands, Compatibility renderer under software GL (which is also the shipped
renderer, `project.godot` `gl_compatibility`, so composition/colour/scale reads are
representative; fine lighting is not).

- **Sky and time of day:** good. Golden hour and dusk read; the moon disc and warm rim
  are coherent.
- **Village buildings:** good materials and silhouette; the strongest frame is the
  three-quarter view of Grandpa's house.
- **Ground:** dense but flat; blades are isolated spikes on a low-frequency smear; no
  mid-layer; one uniform green with no wear paths or colour breakout.
- **Trees:** repeated canopy blobs at even intervals; no groves or asymmetry.
- **Rocks:** the same featureless dark boulder is the dominant foreground object in
  three of five frames, at an ambiguous scale beside the 1.80 m trainer.
- **Distance:** from the Rise the world collapses into a pale wash; the village does not
  read as a landmark at small size.
- **Creatures:** none in the survey stands at all. Where they appear in the location set
  (VP11-final), the scale hierarchy is right but the cel-outlined material sits apart
  from the trainer and the terrain.
- **Artefacts:** a hard-edged shadow wedge across the spawn field; capture stands
  framed inside foliage in the location set.

Blind critic's bar answers on the fresh survey: **A no, B no** (`JUDGE-survey.md`).
Orchestrator's read: A yes on palette and mood, no on composition; B yes up close, no at
distance. The final visual-parity verdict on the fuller location set was 6.5/10, Bar A
yes, Bar B partial. Does it look like a cohesive stylised commercial game? Not yet. It
looks like a prototype with a good sky and a good village. The gap list with mechanisms
is `docs/VISUAL_BIBLE.md` §4.

## 9. World assessment (village → Pond → South Bridge)

Traversable, not built. Terrain is authored and rolling; the village is dressed; the
Pond pocket is dense and approved. Between them: a uniform carpet, evenly spaced trees,
one boulder model, no mid-layer, no rock lines or fences leading the eye, no groves, one
signpost family with unreadable text, a road that reads on the map but not always in
the world. Creatures exist in the practice meadow but not in the standard views; the
temptation creature and the reason-to-be-there trainer are data, not composition.

What remains before it feels fully built (Gate 2 in the roadmap): a per-stand
composition plan by the senior model; mid-layer vegetation clustered to that plan;
silhouette variety from the installed tree family; rock variety and placement;
creatures sited where the player looks and legible against the ground; one authored
discovery with a real reward; signposts and trails readable at handheld resolution;
night fill for unlit camps; and a dead-travel measurement under ~60 s.

## 15. Process assessment

What worked: verbatim owner records with precedence; `merge-base --is-ancestor` as the
landing check; code-blind critics; measured axes; the one-shot/ceiling rule; the
overnight-coordination "armed follow-up" rule; consolidation branches.

What did not: tasks landed without play confirmation; lanes self-reporting "review
ready" against fake-green runs; a coordinator watching branch heads instead of session
status; twelve sequential harness runs by expensive models; two lanes fixing the same
system in opposite directions (creature scale); a daily rewrite of the routing doc;
evidence committed by the gigabyte; visual acceptance drifting from "does it look
good" to "did the critic name a new defect".

## 16. New agent operating model

`docs/AGENT_WORKFLOW.md`. In short: Fable owns judgment (gate choice, decomposition,
architecture, composition, visual acceptance, merge, roadmap); Sonnet owns bounded
implementation with a written brief and file ownership; Haiku owns inventories,
mechanical moves and reference fixes; a code-blind Sonnet judges frames. Every task is
30–90 minutes, ends with the completion contract (files, functionality, tests with
counts, runtime validation, screenshots if visual, limitations, commit, branch), and is
verified by the orchestrator against the branch, not the summary. Parallelize on
disjoint files; serialize on shared ones; one render at a time per box; commit verdicts
not payloads; never trust a run under five minutes or a retry-rescued pass.
