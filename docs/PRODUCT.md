# Product, positioning and release scope

## 1. The product bet

Sell a **creature expedition action RPG** with four authored chapters in this pass (Meadows → Tidewake → Cloudreach → Stormwood), solo or required 1–4-player co-op, Windows/ROG Ally first. The bet is that **building the best five-creature team** is a loop worth repeating: a normal clear targets **15–25 hours**, the main path needs real preparation but no padding, and optional grinding makes the team clearly stronger (owner, 2026-09-29, RD-01, RD-02). Creature power is the spine; Grandpa's homestead (stations, feasts, gear, essence) is its required engine. The pitch stays **Five companions. A world worth reconnecting.** The working sub-line (draft) is *Build the best five.* The product is the journey with a team you grow, not owning everything that exists. Keeping the same beloved five through the ending is success. The target look is **Palworld/Animo-class creatures and world with Valheim-class light and atmosphere** (RD-24). Eight biomes remain the plan: this pass builds four and reserves the rest (four sealed arches, eight shrine pedestals, eight material and gear tiers; RD-09), with no expansion date promised.

**Status:** substantial playable code, not a launch-ready product. `project.godot`, four world scenes, species/band/biome data and network/save code prove foundations. Commercial targets, final store claims and schedule below are **plans**, not achieved sales, certification or completion. Main's status update a49783a3d preserves the earlier owner direction, "personal / friends — I just want it good." The later request for CEO positioning supports the proposal below but does not authorize a commercial release or spending. Build the approved game and invite co-op now; sales targets do not gate useful private play. Everything in this document beyond the current baseline is a target (STATE owns status). Eventual publication remains an owner decision.

## 2. Who chooses it

Primary player: someone who names a small RPG party, likes approachable action combat, enjoys making a few creatures steadily stronger, explores for consequential rewards, and wants a campaign to finish with a partner or friends. Secondary: a Palworld or Monster Hunter player who likes a prepare–hunt–upgrade loop (gather, craft, cook, level, return stronger) but not worker management; a Valheim player who enjoys expeditions and a home base but wants less survival maintenance; a creature fan who values a few companions over a complete collection.

A Palworld player will still notice no workers, base automation or mass collection. A Valheim player will notice a homestead of six purposeful stations rather than a free-form construction game, and mild survival stakes. Do not hide these differences with survival-crafting positioning. Our proposed advantage is direct creature control, remembered individuals whose growth the player chooses, a readable home-and-portal loop, authored route and fight payoffs and a completed story. This is unproven; generic fights, a grind that feels like padding or repetitive creature models would invalidate it.

The official [Palworld store description](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1623730/Palworld/) emphasizes collecting and using creatures across combat, farming and work. The [Valheim store description](https://store.steampowered.com/app/892970/Valheim/) emphasizes procedural exploration, survival, crafting and construction. These are competitive expectations and, for look and loop appeal, the quality bar (RD-24); they are not a demand to copy their scope. Do not promise their replay hours, worker economies or building breadth.

**Not the target:** completionist collectors requiring storage/breeding; factory and automation optimizers; hardcore survival players; competitive PvP players; players expecting an enormous seamless multiplayer service. Losing those audiences is a positioning consequence, not a marketing problem to solve with misleading tags.

## 3. Store page copy

This is intended **launch** copy. Publish claims only after ACCEPTANCE verifies them. Development material labels in-progress scenes and omits unsupported promises.

**Short description**

Choose five companions and make them the best team in a divided world. Pilot them in real-time creature battles, grow them at Grandpa's homestead, and open portals to four handcrafted regions in a complete adventure for one to four players.

**About this game**

Your grandfather can no longer make the journey. He gives you a companion, a patch of land and a key that always brings you home.

Leave the farm with a creature you name and a place for four more. Team Tether has locked the roads between regions and bound their legendary guardians. At the end of the village road, the Crossing Hall's great arches wait for the keys you will win.

**Become your companion in battle.** Pilot your active creature directly. Read an attack, dodge out of danger, land a charged strike, unleash its signature ultimate and switch to the companion whose style fits the fight. Your trainer backs them up with Tether Commands; your creatures do the fighting.

**Build the best five.** Meet creatures across grassland, open water, high cliffs and storm forests. Spend essence on the companion you choose, beat a Master to learn the feast that breaks their limit, forge their gear, master their moves and teach them the traits of creatures you release. There is no reserve box waiting at home: the team you raise is the team you keep.

**Go home. Get stronger. Go back.** Tap Grandpa's Home Key to return from anywhere. Refine ore, cook feasts, plant crops and upgrade your stations, then step through a portal to the last waystone you touched. Food helps without starvation. Ride, swim, dive and fly as your companions grow.

**Reconnect a world with friends.** Explore four authored chapters alone or with up to three friends; everyone earns their own keys, relics and recipes. Break Team Tether's regional hold, then come back for bounties, rematches and rare alphas after the credits.

**Campaign:** four authored chapters and a complete regional ending, with optional exploration, repeatable challenges and team building. The target is a 15–25-hour normal clear; publish a duration only after the measured route and the owner's play pass confirm it (ACCEPTANCE §6.2 F47#0). **Launch co-op lets up to three Steam friends join by invite without entering an IP address or configuring a router. LAN/direct IP remains a fallback. No PvP, public matchmaking, factory automation or creature storage.** Publish this connectivity claim only after the target integration and outside-network acceptance pass.

Suggested tags, checked against platform taxonomy at publication: Action RPG, Creature Collector, Adventure, Co-op, Exploration, Third Person, Controller. The collector tag needs the five-owned disclosure. Do not lead with Open World Survival Craft or Automation; the homestead is not a colony.

## 4. Platform, price and distribution

**Decision:** one Windows x86_64 Steam release, English text, full controller support, offline solo and required 1–4-player host/client co-op with Steam friend invite joining that needs no typed address or router configuration. The built ENet LAN/direct-IP path remains a fallback. ROG Ally is a required tested Windows device, not an assumption from controller support. Linux remains a development/headless target; no advertised Linux/Steam Deck/native console support until separately budgeted and verified. No cross-play, cross-save service, Tetherbound account service, split screen, public matchmaking, PvP, dedicated-server product or host migration at minimum launch.

**Renderer (pending the owner's Ally test):** the owner authorized a Forward+ rendering path with Low (= Compatibility), Medium and High presets to reach the visual target. Compatibility stays the default renderer until the owner's ROG Ally run passes on Medium handheld at ≥30 fps (40 preferred) on the scripted route; only then does Forward+ become the default (RD-25; F26#5, F49#2). Store screenshots and claims name the preset they were captured on.

**Proposed price: US$19.99**, with platform regional pricing reviewed at submission. This is a positioning decision, not a current comparable-price claim. No paid progression, cosmetics shop or preorder financing. The eight-biome plan is future scope: the Crossing Hall's four sealed arches and the fifth arch's stir are world canon, not a sales promise, and no paid expansion schedule is promised. Target a complete 1.0 release; a public demo covering the Meadows, the village road, the Hall and the first homestead loop can test demand after that slice passes. Do not use Early Access to sell a missing replacement loop.

Store/video production uses existing tools after the first full homestead loop works and the relevant biome passes the visual bar (ACCEPTANCE §4). Trailer target 60–90 seconds: creature piloting with real move effects first 10s; five-team decision; the Home Key → homestead → portal loop and one breakthrough (Master win, feast, cap break); later-biome glimpses; regional stakes/co-op. No cinematic implies unavailable art quality or an absent ordinary-play mechanic. Screenshots use actual camera/materials.

**Connectivity target, partly implemented:** retain the built ENet LAN/direct-IP model (MULTIPLAYER/D95), then add a controller-first Steam friend/lobby invite path using Steam networking/relay so an ordinary friend can host and join across networks without seeing an IP address or changing router settings. Steam's official documentation supports lobby invites and peer networking that relays through Valve when appropriate; GodotSteam documents a Steam-ID-based `MultiplayerPeer` compatible with Godot's high-level multiplayer model. Main now carries an optional, default-off Steam lobby/invite/transport implementation with local evidence (MULTIPLAYER, TECHNICAL); it is not accepted for internet play. Sources: [Valve Steam Networking](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/multiplayer/networking), [Valve lobby invites](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/api/ISteamMatchmaking), [GodotSteam MultiplayerPeer](https://godotsteam.com/tutorials/multiplayer_peer/).

The launch flow is `Host for Friends` → friends-only lobby → Steam invite/Join Game → portable-character selection → version/capacity handshake → host snapshot → play. It must work by controller on Windows/ROG Ally, with the game already running or launched from the invite. Full lobby, version/content mismatch, Steam unavailable, host gone, connection failure and snapshot timeout each get a specific recovery message without creating or overwriting a save. Rejoin preserves the same portable character and reconciles the host world. Host exit saves the host-owned world, preserves committed portable characters, closes the lobby and returns peers to title; there is no migration. Public lobby browsing/quick play and a new account service remain outside the product.

This direction assumes existing Steam platform facilities and an open-source integration, with no new paid relay/account/matchmaking service or purchase authorized. `steam_api64.dll` redistribution is approved; packaging stays off until a real AppID exists. The Tetherbound Steam AppID, Steamworks partner access/credentials, separate-account test entitlements, compatible GodotSteam version and matching Godot 4.7 Windows export artifacts are unresolved launch dependencies; credentials stay outside source control. If the prerequisites cannot be provided, the product has a release blocker requiring an owner decision—ENet port forwarding does not meet the confirmed launch experience. Official-source status checked 2026-09-19; implementation must recheck pinned versions and packaging before claiming support.

## 6. Minimum release and cut order

Minimum saleable version: all four chapters in the new order with the Crossing Hall, Home Key, portal keys and waystones; the resolved regional story with the Home Key homecoming after Stormwood; the five-owned model; meaningful piloted combat with impact, enemy patterns, three move slots plus ultimates and Tether Commands; the creature-power loop (type essence and chosen leveling, caps and breakthroughs with five Masters and Ascension Feasts, the four evolution lines, traits and Trait Seeds); the homestead's six stations with one attachment per biome on Forge, Kitchen, Altar and Den, material tiers, Harness/Charm and trainer gear, forward camps; the four repeatables (bounties, rematches, alpha respawns, research log); essential traversal including Ripplet swim/Dive; six meaningful optional activities per chapter; chapter-specific audio; the new visual bar; a measured 15–25 h normal clear; and 1–4-player co-op with durable saves and accepted invite joining without IP/router setup. Save/input/device reliability and truthful art are not optional polish. Every system named here is an owner decision (RD-01..RD-35), so the cuts below reduce **breadth within** a system; removing a system itself needs an owner decision.

Cut in this order when the critical path slips (proposed; the order within RD-decided systems awaits owner confirmation):

1. Fishing, resource-survey/camp checklists, extra decorative lore pickups and optional tool variants. Already outside minimum; do not build them to satisfy an old archive list.
2. Activities above six per chapter, duplicate elite variants and redundant errands, preserving one useful detour per principal region and all reward classes in PROGRESSION.
3. Breadth above each system's floor: research tasks above three per species, bounty kinds above the four named, trait count toward the Common/Rare/Epic floor TRAINING sets, extra utility moves above ten, effect-archetype parameter variants that exceed the Ally budget. Reduce bespoke vocal variants while retaining species-family signatures and readable feedback.
4. Decoration/build catalogue beyond the stations and current essentials, cosmetics and completion titles, and additional localization. Never cut a station, attachment tier, mandatory camp or traversal component, or a repeatable RD-31 requires.
5. Promotion breadth or launch date. If combat, the creature-power loop, four chapters, co-op, ending, saves or readability cannot pass, delay or stop. A one-chapter sale is a different product needing an explicit decision.

Current 57 species/12 adapters are existing content, not a requirement to commission 57 new meshes. ART_DIRECTION prioritizes the 25–30 most-seen subjects for the Meshy batch; the storm bear is the only new species. No cut invalidates owned creatures, promised starter capabilities or required routes.

## 7. Failure case

Most likely failure: competent technology with interchangeable fights and a grind that feels like padding instead of growth. Next: art that cannot sell its creatures at the Palworld/Valheim bar, then a homestead that reads as chores or a tech tree rather than one clear next upgrade per station. Co-op persistence across new transactions (payouts, feasts, keys, relic hangs) can consume the schedule if left late. A finite campaign has little tolerance for a missing ending or filler hours.

ROADMAP and `CODEX_START_HERE.md` buy evidence in that order. Accept narrower appeal openly. If someone says the fifth catch removed their reason to explore, show the traits, essence, research, bounties and Masters that turn every later creature into progress for the five. If those are not enough in play, the central bet has failed.
