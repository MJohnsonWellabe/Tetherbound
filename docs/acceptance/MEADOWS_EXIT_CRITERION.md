# The Meadows Exit Criterion — what "finished and beautiful" means

**Status:** Coordinator synthesis, 2026-08-30. Unifies the acceptance standards
that already exist in five separate owner/canon documents into one list the
lanes can work against and the owner can hold the project to.

**Sources unified here** (none of this is invented; each item cites its source):

- `docs/GAME_VISION.md` §10 Ultimate Meadows acceptance, §8 finished
  region, §5 what every system is for, §2 core loop, §6 five-creature rule, §7 rewards
- `docs/ROADMAP.md` §6 Definition of Meadows completion
- `docs/owner/TETHERBOUND_VISUAL_STUNNING_PASS.md` §25 Acceptance standard
- `docs/owner/TETHERBOUND_MEADOWS_MIDGAME_FUN_REBUILD.md` §21 Acceptance standard
- `.claude/skills/visual-judge/SKILL.md` — the two bar questions
- `docs/owner/README.md` — the three-track priority

**Why this document exists.** Gate F is a *measurement instrument*, not the
goal. A green Gate F says the chapter runs; it does not say the chapter is
beautiful, or that anyone wants to keep playing it. The owner's own priority
order is: (1) Is Tetherbound fun? (2) Does it look like a finished, deliberately
art-directed game? (3) Does the complete experience work reliably? This list is
that priority made checkable.

---

## The one-sentence test

> A fresh player wakes in Grandpa's house, plays through to freeing the
> legendary, and — through their actions, not a survey — demonstrates every
> statement in §A below.

Everything else on this page exists to make that outcome reachable.

## A. The player-voice acceptance (VISION §10)

These are the finish line. Each must be true of a real player's behaviour.

| # | The player should be able to say | Verified by |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | I understood what I was trying to become strong enough to do | continuous run: objective clarity at every band |
| A2 | I cared which creatures made my five | roster pressure observed pre-legendary |
| A3 | I repeatedly found reasons to fight, catch, explore, gather and prepare | activity cadence, no dead stretches |
| A4 | Building and rest mattered because they supported the journey | rest/injury decisions actually taken in play |
| A5 | Different parts of the Meadows felt like distinct real places | per-band identity, blind judge |
| A6 | I was tempted off the direct route | optional discoveries taken voluntarily |
| A7 | I did not spend long periods running through empty scenery | dead-travel interval measurement |
| A8 | My team at the end felt earned and different from my team at the beginning | roster diff start vs end |
| A9 | The Warden felt like the culmination of the chapter | climax difficulty/identity |
| A10 | Freeing the legendary and choosing the final five mattered | release ceremony carries real history |
| A11 | The world looked changed because of what I did | post-Warden healing visible |

## B. Beautiful creatures

*Sources: creature expansion brief; VISION §5 wild ecology; VISUAL §25.11; judge.*

- B1. Every creature reads as a **distinct designed animal**, not a retint of
  another. (Blind judge 2026-08-30: the four Aspect variants currently read as
  ONE shared decal mask hue-swapped four times — **open defect**.)
- B2. Creatures stay **visually prominent against their habitat** — no creature
  vanishing into the grass field on luminance (Bramblebun measured 1.08:1 —
  **open**).
- B3. Rarity is **legible on sight**: common / uncommon / rare / alpha differ in
  presentation, not just in a stat block.
- B4. Creatures sit **correctly in the world** — on the ground, not embedded in
  slopes; at correct depth in water; with contact shadows so they are not
  pasted on. (**Open** — judge found embedded-on-slope spawns and no contact
  shadows.)
- B5. Every species that exists in data is **reachable in play** (owner
  directive D-0830-2: built is not done).
- B6. Silhouette reads at gameplay distance, not only in a close crop — the
  guardian is the standing example (**open**).

## C. Beautiful NPCs and human cast

*Sources: HUMANOID_ASSET_INVENTORY; VISUAL pass; owner playtests.*

- C1. NPCs read as **people in clothing**, never silhouette cutouts. (Black-NPC
  defect: **closed 2026-08-30 with measurements** — grunts 13→31.5/255 day,
  15→51.7 night.)
- C2. **Rank is readable** — grunt vs officer vs captain vs Warden distinguishable
  on sight without a nameplate.
- C3. The cast is **varied enough that the Meadows feels populated** rather than
  six rigs repeated, achieved through material/variant work rather than new
  meshes.
- C4. Named characters (Grandpa, Mira, Oskar, Tam, Vance, the Warden) are
  **visually individual** and match their story weight.

## D. Good terrain and world

*Sources: VISION §4; VISUAL §25.1–.7; owner ground-plane directive.*

- D1. The Meadows **does not read as a flat green test environment** (VISUAL
  §25.1 — the single oldest standing complaint).
- D2. Ground variation looks **natural and authored**; at least a genuine second
  grass species (**in flight**).
- D3. Open fields are **attractive without clutter** — interest from terrain
  shape, silhouettes, herds, outcrops, ruins, weather, not asset spam.
- D4. Paths, settlements and water edges **integrate**; no props standing in
  ponds, no signs in travel lanes.
- D5. The river reads as a **river, not an engineered canal**, and the stream is
  visible from its own bank (**open**).
- D6. **No visible seams** — the dashed terrain seam lines are a defect (**in
  flight**).
- D7. **Aerial perspective** gives distance real depth (as a terrain-material
  gradient, not fog — fog was tried and rejected at R9.4).
- D8. Day / night / golden hour / weather are all **attractive and readable**
  (golden hour on the driven clock: **open**).

## E. Good locations

*Sources: VISION §8 finished region; VISUAL §25.5–.9.*

Every region must have: recognizable geography · a clear reason to enter ·
ordinary wild ecology · at least one team-building temptation · useful
gathering · appropriate trainer presence · at least one optional discovery ·
at least one memorable encounter · a sensible camp/recovery spot · visible
route hierarchy · a clear payoff into the next region · no long purposeless
stretch · day/night readability · acceptable ROG performance.

- E1. **Major landmarks orient the player** — they can answer "where did I come
  from / where can I go / what region is this / what looks optional" without the
  minimap.
- E2. **Important structures look deliberately authored.** Meadows Hall is the
  live test (**in flight** — rebuild from the design doc; previously judged the
  "worst-reading structure in the world").
- E3. **Team Tether presence visibly changes the land** — pylons, hardware,
  drained ground escalating toward the stronghold.
- E4. The **campsite kit is coherent** — one art family, one consistent sense of
  scale, no interpenetrating props (**open**: judge found scanned-PBR vs
  hand-painted mixed, tent smaller than the bed inside it).
- E5. Interiors and dungeons read as places, not corridors — the **Warrens
  interior is the standing GOOD example; protect it.** *Superseded 2026-09-04
  by the owner on hardware (`OWNER_PLAYTEST_2026-09-04.md` OP-0904-7, "Burrow
  warrens looks terrible"): the four prior verdicts judged the guardian, not
  the room; the room is reworked and re-judged under W07-WARRENS-0904 (CL-O7).*

## F. Good story and its delivery

*Sources: VISION §3; MEADOWS_PROGRESSION_SPEC; FUN §16.*

- F1. The opening **establishes stakes without a wall of text** and cannot be
  accidentally skipped.
- F2. Team Tether escalates **rumor → evidence → confrontation → operation →
  hierarchy → Warden**, never as unrelated fights.
- F3. **Home stays relevant** — Grandpa's dialogue changes, rescued people
  return, returning is worth it.
- F4. The Warden is **a character and an exam**, not an HP pool.
- F5. The legendary **volunteers**, and the release ceremony carries enough
  history to hurt.
- F6. The ending **shows** the world healed rather than stating it, and points
  at the next biome without entering it.

## G. Things to do / a reason to keep going

*Sources: FUN §21; VISION §2, §7; SPEC §6.*

- G1. The **next serious challenge is usually clear** (FUN §21.1).
- G2. The player **repeatedly has understandable ways to improve** — never
  forced into grinding a number (§21.2).
- G3. **Catching stays relevant to the last hour** (§21.3).
- G4. **Every major region creates genuine roster temptation** (§21.4) — and at
  least one real moment of five-slot pressure lands *before* the legendary
  (VISION §6).
- G5. **Major fights test different aspects of the five** (§21.6) — not the same
  fight scaled up.
- G6. **Major victories change capability, access or world state** (§21.7).
- G7. **Long purposeless travel is rare** (§21.8).
- G8. Optional content exists at spec density — **6–10 activities** (was 1/6;
  five more **in flight**).
- G9. **Every detour pays** into preparation: XP, bond, a catch, materials, a TM,
  a recipe, a consumable, coins, a shortcut, information, a rare individual, or
  a genuine discovery. **No empty rewards.**
- G10. **The player would voluntarily keep exploring** (§21.11) — the primary
  judgment.

## H. Good building, survival and care

*Sources: VISION §5; FUN §21.5.*

- H1. A basic shelter is **fast and pleasant** to build; modular pieces snap,
  rotate, dismantle with refund.
- H2. Building **supports adventure and never becomes a factory game**.
- H3. **Injury creates real expedition decisions** — a creature in a bed visibly
  rests, is unavailable, and recovers over meaningful time.
- H4. Satiety **influences readiness without becoming starvation punishment**.
- H5. Camp and home offer **recovery, crafting, food, storage, personalization**.
- H6. Survival systems **serve the creature journey** rather than competing with
  it (§21.5).

## I. Good systems

*Sources: VISION §5; SPEC; owner playtests.*

- I1. **Combat** is readable, responsive, spatial; the player pilots in real
  time; type matchups are legible in the moment.
- I2. **Catching** is physical and satisfying — seeing a wanted creature is
  exciting, not administrative.
- I3. **Progression** (levels/XP/moves/traits/bond) makes getting stronger
  visible and understandable.
- I4. **Gathering/crafting** — resources have known uses; a deposit is
  interesting because the player knows what it unlocks.
- I5. **Objectives** answer "what now and why it matters" without becoming a
  quest engine or a GPS trail.
- I6. **Map** records exploration and supports the mental map; it is not a
  creature radar and does not compensate for unreadable geography.
- I7. **Controller-first** everywhere, at ROG Ally scale; no menu unreachable by
  stick; no input leaking between contexts.
- I8. **Save/load** preserves position, facing and story state.
- I9. **Core verbs never fail** — no modal freezes, no lost camera control, no
  arena phase-outs, no softlocks. (Two chapter-class defects **closed
  2026-08-30**: the fainted-ally prompt lockout and the trainer dialogue
  collapse.)

## J. It looks like one deliberate game

*Sources: VISUAL §25.12–.14; judge bar questions.*

## K. It actually works, played end to end

*Sources: ACTIVE_GAME_PLAN §6; three-track README.*

- K1. **3–4 hour focused pacing** without cutting required beats.
- K2. A **continuous fresh-save run** from waking to legendary, no developer
  intervention.
- K3. **No major core-verb reliability failures** across that run.
- K4. Every change **survives integration** — the three-track rule: do not
  accumulate individually-successful changes that fail together.

## The evidence rule (added 2026-08-30, learned the hard way)

**Evidence that does not show the shipping game is worse than no evidence.**

The blind judge found that the capture harness produces frames with **no grass
geometry**, plus haze the build does not have — meaning a share of prior
"verified" visual work was judged against a game that is not the one shipping.
The owner independently said the same thing: *"some of those renders are just a
bad shot not actual game."*

Therefore: **no item on this list may be marked done on a frame that has not
been sanity-checked for real grass, real lighting and real geometry**, and the
capture tools must fail loudly rather than silently produce a lie.

The same rule applies to gameplay: config-level assertions and passing tests are
not evidence that a player can reach a thing. A played path is.

## How to use this list

1. It is **not** a task list to clear top-down. It is the standard each lane's
   work is measured against.
2. An item is closed only with **evidence of the kind named beside it** —
   a rendered frame that shows the real build, a played path, or a measured
   number.
3. `docs/ROADMAP.md` §6 and the gate flow remain the *execution*
   order. This document is the *acceptance* order. They are not in conflict:
   gates sequence the work, this list decides whether the work was worth doing.
4. When an item conflicts with a newer owner directive, the owner directive wins
   and this file gets updated rather than quietly ignored.
