# D51 — The perimeter is two long edges with a style sequence, not a ring

**Date:** 2026-08-16 · **Decided by:** `OW5A`, against
`docs/MEADOWS_PROGRESSION_SPEC.md` §1E and `docs/decisions/D50`

**Model note:** part of `OW5`, tagged `model: fable`, run at **opus** on the
owner's direction because he has no Fable usage left. Not a Fable pass.

**Revised 2026-08-16 (second commit on `ralph/OW5A`), after a blind review.**
`D50`'s footprint was not region-aligned and its width moved from 1536 m to
2048 m, so the long edges move from `x = ±768` to **`x = ±1024`** and the
perimeter from 19.5 km to **20.5 km**. The edge grammar, the per-band style
sequence and everything this decision preserves are unchanged. One thing is
added: a note on the survey tool that photographs this boundary.

## The decision

`scripts/world/world_perimeter.gd` builds a **closed circle at `RADIUS` 235**:
40 segments × 18 spans, one continuous ground-sampled polyline, with collision
boxes generated from the same points. In D50's corridor that circle is
geometrically impossible, so it is replaced by **two long authored edge
polylines** at **`x = −1024` and `x = +1024`**, plus two short ends at
`z = −512` and `z = +7680`.

The generator's *through-line* is kept exactly: a continuous earth bank and
fieldstone kerb running the whole length under every dressing style, with a
stone pier at each join, so the boundary reads at any distance as one built line
whose dressing changes rather than as scattered props. Only the **shape**
changes — an open polyline with a style **sequence** instead of a closed circle
with a style **cycle**.

The style sequence is per band, so the boundary carries regional identity
instead of fighting it:

| band | west edge | east edge |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Lower Meadows | hedgerow on bank, ranch fence at gaps | fieldstone wall — the village's own field boundary continued |
| 2 Stone & Root | quarried rock face, run as a scarp | dense impassable growth over a terrain ridge |
| 3 The River Lock | **water** — the marsh the river drains into, reed-choked and unwadeable | rock formation and scree |
| 4 Upper Meadows / Ironwood | old-growth Ironwood, impassable by density | terrain ridge climbing to the wind ridge's shoulder |
| stronghold approach | authored Team Tether barrier | authored Team Tether barrier |

The owner's phrase was "broken land or sea off the path in either direction".
Both halves are used, and the transition between them is a band boundary, so
the edge changes when the place changes.

## Why the ring cannot be adapted

It is not a matter of feeding it different points. `RADIUS` is a scalar and the
ring is generated by walking `SEGMENTS × SPANS_PER_SEGMENT` angles around it;
the style cycle indexes on segment number modulo four; the collision boxes and
the merged bank/stonework meshes all derive from that same angular walk. There
is no polyline input to hand a corridor to. The rewrite is the point of this
decision, and the header's own note — that `SA4` would later "cut openings into
this ring at specific bearings" — is exactly the work that stops being needed:
in a corridor the spokes leave laterally across the edge rather than radially
through it, so the edge is authored with the openings in it rather than having
them cut afterwards.

## What is preserved, and why it matters more than the shape

The current perimeter was itself a rebuild, because the version before it was
four unrelated prop runs standing near each other and did not read as a
boundary. Three properties came out of that and all three survive:

- **One continuous line under all dressings.** The bank and kerb never stop.
- **Real materials from the existing families.** `T_UnevenBrick` from the same
  Quaternius medieval kit the village and the player's own walls use — D24's one
  village family. Ranch fencing is that kit's `Prop_WoodenFence_*`, already a
  shipped buildable. Hedgerow and rock formation reuse the vegetation layer's
  `Bush_Common` / `Rock_Medium_*`. **No new asset and no generation**, in the
  corridor either.
- **Collision follows the visible line and never precedes it.** Spec §1E:
  invisible collision only *supports* a visible boundary, never *is* it. The
  current implementation gets this right by deriving its boxes from the polyline
  that draws the bank. Keep that derivation.

## What it costs

**20.5 km of perimeter, against the ring's 1.48 km — 14×.** The current
implementation draws the bank and stonework as two merged meshes and batches
bushes, boulders and fence panels through MultiMesh, about a dozen draw calls
for the whole boundary. Merged meshes do not stream, so at 13× that becomes a
load-time and memory item alongside the scatter.

So: **the edge is built per region and streams with the terrain.** One bank
mesh, one stonework mesh and one MultiMesh batch per region that the edge
passes through, built and freed with that region — the same residency mechanism
`MEADOWS_MACRO_LAYOUT.md` §8.3 recommends for the scatter, not a second one that
can disagree with it about which region is loaded.

## What was rejected

- **Keeping the ring and putting the corridor inside it.** A circle enclosing
  8192 × 2048 has radius 4,227, which is 56 km² of world to bake to hold 16.8
  km² of game. It also puts the boundary 3–4 km off the trail everywhere except
  the two ends, so the player never sees it — which fails §1E's actual purpose:
  the Meadows must not read as a floating level, and a boundary you never reach
  does not stop that.
- **Invisible walls at `x = ±1024` with dressing scattered near them.** This is
  precisely what §1E forbids and precisely what the pre-ring implementation did.
  It was rebuilt once already for this reason.
- **One style for the whole length.** 20.5 km of the same hedgerow is 20.5 km of
  the same hedgerow. The per-band sequence costs nothing at build time — it is a
  lookup on z — and it is the cheapest regional-identity lever on the map.
- **A river or sea down both long sides.** The owner offered "broken land **or**
  sea"; using water for both would make the Meadows an island, which no document
  says it is, and would make the seven spokes' "land still visible past it" rule
  unsatisfiable on six of the seven.

## What this breaks in the survey tooling, which nothing else records

**Added in revision 2.** `tools/capture_perimeter.gd` is the tool that
photographs this boundary, and it hard-codes the ring it is looking at:
`RADIUS := 235.0` and `SEGMENTS := 40` at `:30`, the same two constants
`world_perimeter.gd` uses, copied. It does not read them from the world; it
walks its own circle.

So this decision does not merely rewrite `world_perimeter.gd` — **it obsoletes
`capture_perimeter.gd` outright.** A tool that orbits a radius has nothing to
orbit once the boundary is two 8 km polylines and two 2 km ends. It needs
rewriting as a traverse: sample stations along each edge, one per band boundary
at minimum, so the per-band style sequence above is actually verifiable in
frames rather than asserted in this table.

Relatedly, `tools/capture_hillside.gd:50` documents its viewpoints as "MUST stay
inside the baked world (±256 m, `world_size` 512)" and records that an eye past
the edge broke Terrain3D's streaming for a **whole survey run**, not one frame.
Both tools move with the world or the first evidence that the corridor is wrong
will be a survey that renders nothing.

## The honest remainder

The seven spokes still need openings in this edge, and the current ring's design
comment records why they were kept **inside** it at 160–200 m rather than
astride it: cutting seven openings into a continuous generated line was a
rebuild of a system that had already been rebuilt once. That reasoning applied
to a *generated circle*. An authored polyline can simply be authored with the
openings in it — but that is a claim about a file that does not exist yet, and
`OW5C` should confirm it before assuming the spokes are free.

The Band 3 marsh is the one edge style with no existing implementation. Water as
an impassable boundary needs either depth the player will not enter or reeds
dense enough to collide, and there is no swimming system to make the first of
those readable. Nothing here specifies which; it is flagged rather than decided.
