# OP-MEADOWS — Core-loop purpose and encounter density

## Goal
Fix the Meadows' biggest experiential problem: too much running through space without enough reason to engage.

This is not solved by simply adding more side quests. The ordinary route itself must repeatedly feed the game's core motivation.

## Core motivation — locked
Tetherbound's strongest motivational spine is Pokemon-like:

**build the strongest five-creature team you can, because increasingly meaningful trainers, tournament fights, Team Tether encounters, special wild creatures and bosses are ahead.**

Valheim contributes the supporting expedition rhythm:
- leave home/camp;
- gather/mine useful resources;
- fight/train/catch;
- decide whether to keep pushing or stop/build/rest;
- recover and continue stronger.

Palworld contributes visible overworld creature presence and memorable special encounters, but world density should remain lower than Palworld because Tetherbound keeps five creatures rather than encouraging huge collections.

## Route experience
Across ordinary Meadows traversal, repeatedly provide reasons to stop:
- wild creatures worth fighting for XP;
- catch opportunities that improve/alter the five-creature team;
- NPC trainers naturally positioned along routes/settlements;
- occasional stronger/rare/alpha/elder wild encounters;
- mine/harvest/resource clusters that matter to current crafting/build progression;
- camp-worthy areas where distance/injury/night makes resting useful;
- optional discoveries/activities layered on top.

Avoid long sequences where the dominant activity is simply holding the movement stick until the next story trigger.

## Progression rhythm
Coordinate RG18/RG19/MQ3/PW2/RG20/CONTENT-ACTIVITIES/CONTENT-HOME rather than treating them as isolated features.

The player should feel a repeating escalation:
1. encounter/catch/train locally;
2. gather enough resources to support the expedition;
3. prepare/rest team;
4. beat a stronger trainer or special creature;
5. reach a meaningful milestone/gate;
6. discover that the next stretch asks more of the team.

Early chapter goal: build a tournament-ready team and win the village tournament.
Afterward: Team Tether route, guarded crossing, stronger trainers/special encounters, stronghold and later boss/legendary stakes.

## Density philosophy
Do not solve boredom by carpet-bombing the world with enemies or icons.

Tune encounter/activity spacing by traversal time and sightline:
- open fields may let the player spot several possible engagements at distance;
- dense regions may hide closer discoveries/nests/resources;
- there should be breathing room, but not long purposeless emptiness;
- creature fights and trainer fights should be prevalent enough that intentional leveling does not require searching the whole biome for something to battle.

## Wild creatures
Coordinate with RG20:
- singles and nests/groups;
- species/habitat variation;
- levels/traits/size/shiny individuality;
- player wanting another creature should be able to deliberately find opportunities;
- do not violate five-creature cap/storage canon.

## Trainers
Add enough route trainers that leveling through combat is an intentional path, not only random wild grinding. Reuse NPC rigs/rank systems. Trainers should have teams appropriate to the current band and reinforce local species/faction identity.

Do not make every NPC a trainer; settlements still need ordinary people/story/crafting teachers.

## Resources and camping purpose
Distribute useful resource progression so pushing into new bands gives concrete material reasons to explore. Mining/harvesting should support crafting/build unlocks from RG13.

Place natural expedition intervals where injury, distance or night make camp construction, creature beds and player sleep useful rather than tutorial-only verbs.

Do not hard-gate every rest stop; the goal is a player decision with meaningful benefit.

## Measurement
Before and after content tuning, traverse representative chapter segments at normal player speed and record:
- time/distance between visible wild opportunities;
- trainer opportunities per band;
- useful resource encounters;
- special/rare encounter frequency;
- stretches with no meaningful choice other than movement.

Use this to prevent vague 'add more stuff' work.

## Acceptance
1. Intentional creature leveling is possible through a steady mix of wild and trainer fights.
2. Each band introduces meaningful combat/resource/exploration reasons to continue.
3. Player regularly sees choices rather than a single empty road.
4. Camping/rest has natural use during progression.
5. Tournament and Team Tether milestones provide clear reasons to strengthen the team.
6. Special creatures/boss-like encounters create aspirational targets.
7. Open spaces remain visually open; density comes from meaningful opportunities, not clutter.
8. Side activities enrich the loop without replacing it.

## Definition of done
A normal Meadows session feels like a creature-training expedition rather than an empty run: the player is continually deciding what to fight, catch, gather, build/rest for, and whether their five-creature team is ready for what comes next.
