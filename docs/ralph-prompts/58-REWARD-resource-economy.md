# REWARD-ECONOMY — Make every useful detour pay into preparation

## Goal
Create one readable Meadows reward/resource economy in which the player understands why fights, exploration, harvesting, trainers and optional detours matter.

Do not create a second inventory, currency, recipe or loot system. Use current items, coins, TMs, recipes, buildables, resources, Orbs, consumables and progression flags.

## Core rule
A reward is good when the player can connect it to something they want to do next.

Avoid rewards that exist only because every activity needs a prize.

## Resource tiers
Preserve the simple chapter economy:

### Baseline
- Wood
- Stone
- Fiber
- Berries

### Progression
- Rootstone
- Ironwood (working/canonical current name unless renamed elsewhere)

### Special
- evolution item/condition material;
- bridge keys / Sigils;
- TMs;
- Orb materials/tiers;
- useful consumables;
- coins.

Every introduced material must have understandable current uses.

## Map the reward sources
Create a chapter reward map covering:

### Ordinary wild fights
Primary: XP/bond and catch opportunity. Avoid showering generic junk.

### Special wild/alpha/elder encounters
Need a reason to win even if the player does not catch the creature: rare material, TM, recipe progress, valuable consumable, evolution lead, world discovery, or another current-system reward.

### Local trainers
Useful XP plus a small memorable reward appropriate to their lesson/location.

### Team Tether trainers/captains
Increasingly meaningful money/items/TMs/progression tokens. Major victories should visibly matter.

### Exploration
Caches, TMs, useful resource concentrations, rare encounters, shortcuts, lore/world information, optional recipes/buildables.

### Quarry/Warrens
Rootstone and the first major upgrade payoff.

### Upper Meadows
Ironwood and later preparation/riding/final-assault utility.

### Tournament
Coins/reward/status that makes victory feel meaningful and transitions the chapter.

## Coins
Audit what the player can genuinely spend coins on during Meadows. If trainers/tournament give money but useful purchasing is absent or unclear, fix the existing shop/economy content rather than inventing a parallel currency.

The player should periodically want coins.

## TMs
Follow current item semantics. A found TM should feel like a meaningful team-build choice. Site them where discovery and difficulty justify their value.

## Orbs
Basic catching must remain affordable enough that experimenting with team composition is encouraged. Better Orb tiers should be understandable preparation rewards, not mandatory scarcity walls.

## Building costs
Building a first home/camp must be affordable through normal nearby gathering. Later cosmetic/expanded building may cost more. Do not make core shelter/rest infrastructure require a grind that competes with the creature journey.

## Resource siting
Coordinate regional packages. Resource formations should create visible choices and support plausible camps:
- basic materials common enough for routine construction;
- Rootstone concentrated where its fiction/gameplay belongs;
- Ironwood introduced when its recipes matter;
- rare materials in memorable places rather than uniform scatter.

## Reward audit table
For every major challenge/optional activity, record:
- activity;
- required/optional;
- reward(s);
- what the reward enables;
- whether the player understands that use when received;
- whether the reward remains useful at that chapter point.

Remove/rework rewards that are redundant, unusable or misleading.

## Verification
In a fresh run, record:
- moments the player is short of a necessary resource and why;
- materials accumulating with no known use;
- coins received/spent;
- Orb scarcity/excess;
- whether optional detours feel worth the time;
- whether Rootstone/Ironwood visibly change capability;
- whether core camp/home costs fit normal gathering.

## Acceptance
- every core material has clear use;
- optional content gives meaningful preparation value;
- money has useful sinks;
- catching is not suppressed by excessive Orb scarcity;
- Rootstone and Ironwood create visible progression;
- basic building/rest infrastructure is affordable;
- rewards reinforce team progression rather than distracting from it.

## Definition of done
When the player sees a resource, trainer, cache, special encounter or side path, they can reasonably think: **that could help my team or preparation**, not merely **that is another collectible.**
