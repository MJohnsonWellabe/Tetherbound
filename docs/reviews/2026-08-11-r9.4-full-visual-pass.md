# R9.4 — full visual pass, 2026-08-11

Owner directive, 2026-08-11: run a blind visual review of **everything built so
far**, not only what the last session touched, with **no round cap** — iterate
until the critics are actually satisfied — and treat **buildings and terrain**
as the named weak points.

This is the record. Every verdict below was produced by a sub-agent that saw
only the frames, `docs/reference/`, and the `visual-judge` rubric. None was told
what had changed, what round it was, or what anyone hoped it would say.

## What was captured, and what was not

| Set | Tool | Frames |
|---|---|---|
| World | `tools/survey.sh` | 5 fixed exploration viewpoints |
| Buildings | `tools/capture_buildings.gd` (**new**) | 7 — farmhouse, square, barn cluster, windmill, approach, interior |
| Roster | `tools/preview_creatures.gd` | every species against the 1.8 m ruler |

**Combat was not captured, and this pass does not review the arena.**
`tools/survey_combat.sh` ran for roughly fifty minutes under llvmpipe without
writing a single frame, while the buildings pass beside it completed seven. It
was killed to give the box back to the iteration loop. Whether that is a real
defect in `survey_combat.gd` or only software-rendering cost is **not
established** — see the backlog item opened for it. Nothing in this document is
a judgement about how a fight looks.

`tools/capture_buildings.gd` was written for this pass because nothing in
`tools/` framed a building. `survey.gd` judges exploration composition,
`capture_site_shots.gd` stages the download page and picks framings that
flatter, `capture_wayfinding.gd` is about the signpost and the distant
stronghold. None of them puts a barn in the middle of the frame at the range a
player walks past it, which is the only way to answer the question the owner
asked.

`tools/sheet.py` was written for the same reason: `contact_sheet.gd` reads
`shots/*.png`, three columns, no labels, because Godot's `Image` has no font
rendering. A critic cannot name the frame its finding is in without labels, and
every version of the rubric asks it to.

## The root cause of the green, which was structural rather than taste

`albedo_color` multiplies. **Multiplying by a tinted colour can only ever raise
saturation**, because it scales down whichever channel is already lowest. The
ground had three tinted multipliers stacked on it, all pulling yellow-green:

| stage | saturation |
|---|---|
| `Grass008_Color.jpg` mean albedo | 0.675 |
| × texture `tint` `#b9cf82` | 0.796 |
| × baked colour map `#c2d492` | 0.859 |
| × `macro_variation1` `#eef0d8` | **0.873** |

The design intent — recorded in `terrain_playground.json`'s own comment — was
that these "modulate rather than paint", and it required them to be near-white.
`#c2d492`'s blue channel is `0x92` = 146, well under the `#c0` the same comment
sets as its floor. Each multiplier individually looked like a gentle tint; the
product was a primary.

The fix is solved rather than eyeballed. The grass tint is now `#a680ff`, which
looks like lavender in a colour picker and is not a mistake: **raising blue
relative to green is the only way a multiply desaturates a green.** It lands the
stack on hue 70, saturation 0.564 — the exact hue and saturation of
`palette.json`'s `meadow.grass_olive`, which was itself sampled off the key art
board.

## Round 2 — measured movement

Same tool, same frames, after the round-1 fixes:

| metric | round 1 | round 2 | references |
|---|---|---|---|
| saturation, frame 01 | 0.70 | **0.59** | 0.40–0.46 |
| saturation, frame 05 | 0.72 | **0.66** | |
| near-field luminance, 01 | 0.526 | **0.271** | 0.28–0.60 |
| non-green colour, frame 03 | 37.3% | **61.0%** | 31–74% |
| non-green colour, frame 05 | 25.3% | **86.0%** | |
| non-green colour, frame 01 | 1.0% | **4.0%** | |

Real movement on every axis, and two frames now sit inside the reference band
for colour variety where none did before. Two things the round-2 frames showed
that the numbers did not:

- **The dirt paths became visible.** They had been painted into the colour map
  all along; at the old saturation the tint was invisible under the green.
- **The pass over-corrected into murk.** Dropping the ground albedo and cutting
  ambient at the same time stacked. Exposure — which scales uniformly and
  therefore costs none of the desaturation — was the right knob to give it back.

**One regression was introduced and caught in the same round.** The new stone
plinth was built as a single box across the whole footprint, which laid a grey
lid over the interior floor standing half a metre proud of it. It is now a
perimeter ring. Recorded here because the frames caught it and no test would
have.

## What is fixed, and what is honestly still open

### Fixed this pass

- Ground palette: solved off the board rather than tuned by eye.
- Value range: ambient and sun pitch, then exposure to undo the overshoot.
- The red leak — `Leaves_TwistedTree_C` is RGB(167,23,23), crimson, on
  decorative grove trees beside the starting village. **Two independent blind
  reviews eleven days apart found this**, and the bushes layer had already been
  fixed for the same texture; the grove was simply never given the treatment.
- Canopy hue pushed to the board's `leaf_green_deep` so it contrasts with the
  ground instead of reinforcing it.
- Twelve harvest nodes that were rendering as coloured `BoxMesh` fallbacks.
- Flowers at 4× life size, grass at 2.5×, signpost at 1.5×.
- Path stones blowing out to near-white.
- Sixty-two dead trees in a biome the board calls "peaceful by day".
- The mirrored, overrunning, top-edge-mounted signpost labels.
- **Grandpa's house**: gable roof with eaves, fascia, ridge and chimney; six
  windows with frames, mullions and warm emissive panes; a framed doorway with
  a threshold; a stone plinth and corner posts.

### Still open — and these are the deliverable

Recorded as backlog items rather than quietly carried:

1. **Saturation has not reached the bar.** 0.59 against 0.40–0.46. The
   remaining excess is in the baked colour map, which needs a terrain rebake,
   not a runtime tint.
2. **The far band renders pale and colourless.** Past the 512 m bake,
   Terrain3D's `world_background = NOISE` continuation has no colour map and no
   texture control, so it draws near-white. This is the single largest
   contributor to the compressed value range, and it is the same limit
   `R7.1-remainder` recorded from the other direction.
3. **The pack buildings' roofs are still 11–16% luminance.** `village.gd` has no
   material override path at all.
4. **The stronghold silhouette is still an unlit black cutout** — the standing
   `R7.1-visual-remainder-2` finding, now confirmed from a second angle.
5. **Paths are a colour-map tint, not a material.** A dirt path needs the
   control map to select the soil texture. The code comment already says this is
   "queued as polish"; the critic's "no worked ground anywhere" is the same gap.
6. **The settlement has no trees, no props and no site plan.** Both critics
   ranked this first or second. It is content, not tuning.
7. **The windmill is a Northern European tower mill standing beside American red
   gambrel barns.** The critic is explicit that this is a decision — pick one
   vernacular, do not split the difference — so it goes to the owner, not to a
   firing.

**The critics found things no test could.** The mirrored signpost text, the
creature embedded in a roof, the magenta placeholder cube, the plinth

**The critics also found the same defect twice, eleven days apart.** The crimson

an argument for turning accepted criticism into a backlog item immediately

## Round 4 — two fresh blind critics on the fixed build

Both answered **no** to both bar questions again. Both were new agents, told
nothing. Their agreement on what is now worst is the useful part.

### They agree on the same three things, and none of them is palette any more

**1. The foliage is broken, and both called it the most bug-like thing in the
set.** "Blue, magenta and cyan speckle… reads as compression noise, not
foliage" (world). "Tree canopies and bushes render as blue/green/white confetti
speckle" (buildings). Confirmed by crop: a distant tree resolves to a scatter of
unrelated pixels. **Root cause found**: a hard alpha scissor has no partial
coverage, so a ten-pixel tree is a handful of disconnected leaf texels. The
project already runs 4× MSAA and it was doing nothing for foliage, because MSAA
only cleans alpha edges when the material opts into alpha-to-coverage. Now it
does — **unverified in this harness**, because llvmpipe's MSAA is not something
these frames can honestly test. Judge it on the Ally.

**2. The distance is gone.** "Fog is eating the world, not building depth…
there is no third distance plane" (world). "The fog desaturates to white rather
than to sky blue… the second and third depth layers the key art relies on are
simply gone" (buildings). This is `R9.4-remainder-2` — Terrain3D's procedural
continuation past the 512 m bake has no colour map, so it draws pale and
featureless. Halving the fog twice did not touch it, because it was never the
fog.

**3. Nothing in the settlement says people live there.** "No grove in these five
frames" / "four isolated objects on unmarked turf, a fence enclosing nothing,
and stepping stones that connect no two doors." Both ranked this first or
second. `R9.4-remainder-5`.

### What the round-4 critics confirm improved

Neither was told anything had changed, and neither repeated round 1's leading
complaints. **"The green of a snooker table" is gone** — no critic mentioned
oversaturation. The gambrel-versus-gable silhouette distinction now "genuinely
works". Contact shadows "are doing their job… nothing floats". The measured
delta over three rounds: saturation 0.70 → 0.56 on frame 01, with frame 03
inside the reference band on both saturation and colour variety.

### New defects these rounds found

- The **red barn is now the most saturated warm object in the world**, and the
  world critic read it as the reserved danger colour leaking onto a friendly
  building. The crimson grove leaves were fixed; the pack's barn was not, and
  `village.gd` still has no material override path (`R9.4-remainder-3`).
- **A miniature copy of the barn**, roughly one-fifth scale, beside a boulder in
  `02`.
- **A rabbit at 1.0–1.3 m at the shoulder**, two to three times life size.
- **Grass tufts growing through the interior floor** — terrain scatter is not
  masked by the building footprint.
- **The windmill is undersized by about half** and is why the settlement has no
  landmark.
- Boulders "sit fully proud on the surface with no bedding" at 4–6 m.

### The landmark, settled by frames after two wrong answers

R7.1-visual made the stronghold `unshaded, fog_disabled` at near-black because
it faded into haze. R9.4's first critic called that result "a hole punched in
the image rather than a stone ruin". Taking the opt-out away was tried twice,
at two values, and failed both times — **because with shading on, the value
depends on which face the camera sees**. From `silhouette-close` (shadowed
south side) the fortress read perfectly; from `silhouette-from-square` and
`-from-path`, the viewpoints a player actually uses, the sunlit west face washed
pale against the haze. Not fog: at 0.00055 over ~190 m that is a ten per cent
effect.

So R7.1-visual was right about the render mode and wrong about the colour, and
the R9.4 critic was right about the colour and wrong about the cause. Final:
`unshaded` restored at a dark slate stone value, which now reads as a
consistent, legible fortified silhouette at all three ranges — verified in
`shots/wayfinding/`.

### Standing remainders, re-confirmed rather than closed

`R7.1-visual-remainder-2` (long-range fortress read) is **closeable** on this
evidence — the silhouette now carries crenellations and varied massing at every
range tested. `R7.1-remainder-2` (ground cover, mid-ground layering) and
`R7.1-found-3` (splat stripe) are **not**; both critics still name the ground
and the horizon, and remainders 1–5 describe why in more detail than the
original entries did.
