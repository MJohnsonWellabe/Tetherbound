## Ranking and concrete reasons

1. **b — select.** The broad cream chest is one dominant readable mass, interrupted by a single blue chevron. Large overlapping feather plates give the chest and wings understandable volume without relying on tiny strands. The short lower legs, broad thighs, substantial feet, oversized hooked beak, and readable amber eye share the tortoise and stronger installed creatures' compact, weight-bearing proportions. The raised wings create a clear V with open space around the head. Slate blue, cream, and restrained ochre remain distinct at reduced viewing size; the cream face leads back to the chest. Matte surfaces and broad beveled forms are compatible with the installed cast's sculpted, substantial shapes. The chest is somewhat armor-like and the lower wing scallops remain numerous, but neither overwhelms the creature's identity.

2. **a — usable direction, weaker selection.** Its large body, sturdy feet, strong face, and blue/cream/ochre palette are compatible with the cast. However, `a.png` has many similarly sized feather layers across the shoulders, neck, and thighs. At small size these become a continuous textured surface, whereas the tortoise keeps its major shell and body masses distinct. The narrower pale chest and pale zigzag borders create more competing lines than b's broad bib. The wing spread also places dense shoulder plumage close to the torso outline. Its detailed scales and feather grooves push its material treatment toward a more literal bird illustration than the stronger installed mascot forms.

3. **c — weakest cohesion.** `c.png` has a distinctive sweeping crest and an elegant wing silhouette, with less small surface noise than a. Those strengths do not make it the closest cast match. Its tall narrow shins, elongated neck, small head relative to its total height, long crest, and trailing tail produce a slender, ornamental bird beside the tortoise's compact bulk and the cast's large-headed proportions. The curling wing graphics and chest ornament add a separate decorative vocabulary. Those graphics also become difficult to distinguish from shading at small size. The muted palette fits the world, but the anatomy and ornament need art changes to achieve b's cohesion.

## Three largest remaining gaps from the references

1. **Fine divisions compete with major shapes — a most, b less.** Palworld's Mammorest and Grintale retain broad uninterrupted body regions with a few prominent material boundaries. The nested shoulder feathers in a and the repeated chest and lower-wing plates in b still introduce more edge activity. Preserve b's chest mass and avoid adding further feather grooves during reconstruction. This is an art/material issue, not something scene density can solve.

2. **Thin extremities can displace the creature's readable bulk — c most.** The installed tortoise and Palworld creatures communicate weight through large connected masses. The extended crest, narrow lower legs, and multiple trailing points in c make the center of the creature feel less substantial. b resolves this most convincingly. Changing the camera or scenery would not correct c's underlying proportions.

3. **The images have no evidence of integration — all candidates.** The references show creatures remaining readable against vegetation, terrain, other characters, and combat effects. Here the neutral backgrounds isolate every contour. In particular, the blue outer wings may merge with cool shadowed foliage, and reconstructed chest shading may reduce the useful cream/blue contrast. Scene lighting and exposure can address some separation problems; lost facial form, fused wings, or incorrect geometry would require asset repair. No screenshot here proves actual relative scale, flight deformation, grounded contact, or a readable rear view.

## Meshy reconstruction risks inferred from the images

- **b:** The single three-quarter view does not establish wing thickness, back anatomy, or the far side of the head. Overlapping flight feathers could become a solid corrugated slab; the pale wing bands could become raised strips instead of coherent feather regions. Talons overlap toes and may fuse. Preserve the eye socket, beak separation, and open space between head and wings. The broad body and short, sturdy legs are comparatively forgiving forms.
- **a:** Numerous feather layers and thin terminal feathers create more opportunities for fused surfaces, fragile edges, and surface shading being interpreted as relief. Scaled ankles and curled claws add small features that may become noisy or merged.
- **c:** Thin legs, long crest prongs, extended tail tips, and the outer wing fingers are the most vulnerable silhouette features. Wing curls and chest motifs are ambiguous as paint versus raised geometry. Those errors would be especially visible because the design depends on long clean curves.

These are visible geometric ambiguities, not measured failure rates or claims about a generated mesh. No reconstruction has been inspected.
