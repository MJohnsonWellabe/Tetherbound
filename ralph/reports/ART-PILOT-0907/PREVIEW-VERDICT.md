# ART-PILOT preview verdict

**REJECT_PREVIEW_1**

The preview preserves the broad raised wings and a recognizable two-legged bird body, but the accepted reference's defining raptor face does not survive clearly enough to justify the one allowed refine. This is a judgment of visible geometry, not of the absent texture.

## Evidence and limits

Compared `candidates/b.png` with `assets_raw/galecrest/pilot_preview/thumbnail.png`. Also inspected the Meadows key art, `palworld-03-field-boss-meadow.jpg`, and the installed Mosshell portrait. No generation explanation, source code, model inspection, additional render, or history informed this judgment.

Only one small, untextured thumbnail is available. Hidden surfaces, topology, articulation, and exact head orientation cannot be established. I do not claim that an unseen surface is absent. The decision rests on the visible face and body profile; it does not assume that adding color will repair their shape.

## Three largest departures, ranked

1. **The prominent hooked beak is not readable in `thumbnail.png`.** In `b.png`, the beak projects substantially forward and curves distinctly downward, separating the face from the throat even without its dark color. The preview's left-facing facial edge reads as small irregular projections around a compact head. There is no comparably legible projecting hook. The swept crest survives, but crest plus indistinct muzzle reads closer to a generic spiky bird/dragon head than the reference's eagle face. Texture can define an eye and darken a beak region; it cannot supply this missing readable contour.
2. **The head-to-chest transition is much weaker in `thumbnail.png`.** The reference has a broad cheek ruff and layered throat feathers that carry the expressive head into a substantial breast. The preview has a narrow, smooth neck above a large, pointed central chest shape. This makes the head feel perched on a stalk and the front read like one shield-shaped lump. The issue includes actual contour and surface form, not merely missing cream and blue markings.
3. **The feet lose the reference's heavy taloned stance in `thumbnail.png`.** The reference has wide feet with clearly arched, hooked claws. The preview retains two separate feet and several separated toe tips, which is useful, but the visible toes look thin and similarly pointed, with much less distinction between toe and claw. This is a secondary weakness rather than evidence of fused legs.

The strongest retained feature is the wing silhouette: both wings are present, rise into a clear open V, and retain large separated outer feathers with smaller inner feather tiers. I see no basis to reject the wings as missing or fused. The legs also remain separate. A small tail projection is visible, but the thumbnail cannot establish the full tail fan. Those successes do not resolve the facial contour failure.

## Reference bars

**A — Belongs to the Meadows key-art world: yes, at the limited level of creature shape.** A large stylized feathered companion with a readable wing silhouette is compatible with that world. This thumbnail provides no evidence for palette, environment integration, or scale beside the trainer, so this is not a scene or shipping acceptance.

**B — Beside the Palworld reference, reads as the same kind of game: no for the supplied preview's creature presentation.** The broad fantasy-creature category fits, but the face lacks the clear, deliberate anatomy that makes a creature feel authored and expressive. Mosshell's portrait also shows how decisively a face can carry creature identity. Color and scene lighting could improve presentation; the beak contour and head-to-chest shape need geometry that is not convincingly present in this preview.

**Decision:** reject this preview before refine. The strongest reason is the unreadable hooked-beak silhouette. A single thumbnail leaves uncertainty, but it does not provide sufficient positive evidence that the accepted face geometry survived; spending the refine on the expectation that texture will recover that anatomy is not supported by what is visible.
