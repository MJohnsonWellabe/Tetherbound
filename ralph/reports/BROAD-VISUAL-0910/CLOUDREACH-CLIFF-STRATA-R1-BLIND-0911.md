# Cloudreach cliff strata R1 — code-blind visual review, 2026-09-11

## Disposition

**PASS — retain the shared cliff-strata candidate.**

This is a bounded candidate pass, not a PASS grade for Cloudreach or its named
locations. Across the three views with R5 matches, the fixed-frequency horizontal
striping is materially reduced. Rock faces now carry quieter, intermittent tonal
variation rather than repeated pale bands crossing every vertical mass. The change
does not introduce visible high-frequency noise, colour speckle, or a new competing
pattern, and the matched Windscar Beacon and Cliffhold landmarks retain their prior
readability.

The candidate does not solve the larger artificial-geometry problem: many cliffs
remain broad upright drums or tables with flat turf caps. Some faces are now arguably
too smooth. Those are remaining geometry/material-polish issues, not reasons to reject
this specific removal of the repeated strata cadence.

## Evidence integrity and limit

The manifest is complete with four planned/captured 1280x800 production frames, no
reported failures, the production camera rig/player, and day at the requested clock.
Only daytime was captured. This verdict therefore accepts the visible daytime geology
improvement; it does **not** prove that the revised faces retain enough separation at
night.

The R5 comparison strip is 1280x720 and the later production world contains other
location changes, so judgment is restricted to cliff-face treatment. In particular,
the Observatory foreground is not a clean landmark A/B.

## Frame-specific evidence

### Windscar Beacon

- Against R5, the enormous left-hand pillar loses the obvious repeated horizontal
  washboard. Its value now varies broadly along the face instead of by fixed bands.
- The central and right valley walls also show fewer synchronized pale stripes.
- The timber beacon, foreground stones, player, and grass hierarchy remain readable;
  no geology pattern competes with the landmark.
- Remaining defect: the left pillar is still a very smooth vertical monolith and the
  distant cliff family repeats similar upright silhouettes.

### Windscar Flight Aerie

- The long exposed escarpment is the strongest wide-area proof. Tonal changes are
  irregular and restrained across many adjacent faces; there is no obvious realm-wide
  horizontal frequency or noisy mottling.
- Shelf breaks and vertical clefts remain legible at distance.
- Remaining defects are outside this candidate's win: repetitive column geometry,
  flat caps, bare foreground turf, and white cloud cards.

### Cliffhold

- The far upper/right ridge no longer reads as a stack of evenly spaced pale bands.
  The settlement remains the primary focal layer.
- No visible noise or colour breakup has been introduced on the large face behind the
  cottage.
- The huge dark angular mound and polygonal ground overlays remain conspicuous, but
  neither is worsened by the strata treatment.

### Old Wind Observatory

- Visible background cliffs have quieter, less synchronized face variation than the
  R5 valley view, without speckle or shimmer artifacts in the still frame.
- The foreground is dominated by the Observatory's stone uprights, so this frame
  cannot honestly establish landmark non-regression as a matched A/B. It shows no
  apparent cliff-material interference with the structure, but a dedicated exterior
  landmark view would be stronger evidence.
- White cloud cards and dense foreground grass remain dominant shared-environment
  defects unrelated to the strata candidate.

## Acceptance boundary

Retain this candidate and move on from fixed-band parameter tuning. Before treating
the shared geology work as commercially complete, capture one matched night valley
view and address macro cliff silhouette/flat-cap repetition as a separate unit. Reject
future iterations if they restore a synchronized horizontal cadence or trade the
current restraint for high-frequency stone noise.
