# Village Inn common room R10 — independent visual judgment

## Verdict: POLISH — retain, not a strict named-location PASS

R10 is a material and presentation improvement over the retained R7 set. The Inn
now reads immediately as a specific public house from outside, the plaster and wood
have visible surface response, Bram owns the far end of a legible service room, and
the runner plus two occupied table lanes establish a useful room hierarchy. It is
not yet a strict PASS because R7's two clearest authored-form blockers remain visible
in the proof: the bar is still a broad box-built counter front, and the guest bed is
still openly exposed to the common room.

### Evidence integrity: PASS

- `manifest.json` declares `complete: true`, 12 expected frames, 12 frame records,
  and zero failures.
- All 12 native PNGs are present, unique, non-empty, and 1280x720. Each manifest
  camera has a matching day/night pair; the pairs show real lighting changes rather
  than duplicated files.
- The receipt identifies the production scene as
  `res://scenes/world/meadows_playground.tscn` and discloses the bounded capture
  interventions: fixed camera, authored clock, hidden HUD/overlay, and a hidden,
  physics-disabled player used only to make production NPC tracking face the viewer.
  It discloses no art, pose, progress, weather, encounter, or lighting injection.
- The manifest does not embed a source SHA or per-source hash. Therefore it proves
  production-scene provenance and capture completeness, but not commit identity by
  itself. This does not prevent visual judgment of the supplied set.

### Strict visual findings

- **Exterior identity and day/night: PASS.** The tall half-timbered volume, large
  signed porch, masonry base, lit windows, bench, barrel, vine, and attached lower
  wing make this recognizably *The Village Inn*, not a generic cottage. The three
  exterior angles remain readable at night and the entrance stays the focal point.
  The very broad, flat green porch fascia/roof is still a simple slab and the upper
  timbers collapse heavily toward black, but neither erases the location identity.
- **Plaster/timber material depth: improved, still POLISH.** Mottled plaster and
  directional wood grain visibly answer R7's flat-wall problem in every interior
  view. However, the same strongly striated grain repeats across wainscot, beams,
  shelves, counter, and ceiling trim; several pieces read as texture-swapped boxes
  rather than distinct construction. Interior night is readable but changes mostly
  in warmth/contrast, so it remains nearly as evenly exposed as day.
- **Bar/service focal point: POLISH blocker.** Bram, stock shelves, bottles, mugs,
  ledger shelf, stool, barrel, and the `ROOMS - ALE - STOCK` board establish service
  purpose. The counter itself is still one long rectangular mass with three repeated
  rectangular face boards and little visible joinery, kick wear, curved/mitred edge,
  or working return. In the dedicated bar frames it remains the exact box-built bar
  called out by R7.
- **Lived wear and table service: improved, not complete.** Contrasting runners,
  plates, mugs, bowl, basket, stock, tools, floor runner repairs, and unequal table
  contents keep the room from being empty. Yet the tables still read more as sparse
  display lanes than accumulated inn service: large cloth areas are bare, the floor
  and travel path are exceptionally clean, and the oversized pickaxe/crate display
  competes with food-and-drink use. More freestanding clutter is not the answer; the
  remaining need is integrated service wear and construction detail.
- **Room hierarchy: PASS.** The bordered central runner provides a clear public
  aisle, the long side tables flank it without blocking circulation, and Bram/bar
  form the destination. The teal mat at the bar is subordinate and the low screen no
  longer dominates the room.
- **Bed exposure: POLISH blocker.** In `05` and `06`, the purple guest bed and its
  head/foot are plainly visible between and around the open timber screen. The screen
  separates zones but does not make the lodging feel private; the bed still reads as
  furniture placed inside the taproom rather than a convincingly screened room.

## Promotion condition

Retain R10 as the new Inn baseline. Promote to strict PASS only after fresh
production day/night evidence shows (1) a bar whose silhouette/construction is more
than repeated rectangular panels and (2) a genuinely private lodging boundary from
ordinary common-room views, while preserving the current exterior identity, central
aisle, table occupancy, Bram sightline, and route clearance.
