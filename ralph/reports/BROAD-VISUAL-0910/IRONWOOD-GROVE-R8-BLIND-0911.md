# Ironwood Grove R8 workflow — code-blind production review (2026-09-11)

## Verdict: POLISH

R8 remains **POLISH**. It is a worthwhile improvement over R7 in workyard storytelling:
the dedicated frame now contains a left-to-right material sequence rather than only an
oversized rectangular rail. The ancient-tree hero remains strong. It does not reach
PASS because the workflow is not immediately self-explanatory at gameplay distance,
the ordinary arrival is still blocked by creatures and bright saplings, and night
exposure still swings from crushed blue-black to icy clipped foliage.

The manifest is intact: eight present 1280x720 production frames, four matched day/night
stands at the same 18–55 m distances as R7, no failures, and no prop, creature,
progression or reward injection. That permits a direct R7 comparison.

## Workyard process

### 04 ironwood workyard day

R8 adds a visible process direction:

- a pale raw log/stock lies at the left edge of the yard;
- the central timber frame contains a bench-height working surface and tool/saw-like
  diagonal forms;
- lower trestles and thin pieces suggest an intermediate shaped-output stage;
- the new slatted structure on the right plausibly reads as a seasoning rack.

This is materially better than R7's single empty rail. The issue is first-read clarity.
The central station remains visually dominated by large rectangular framing, the saw or
hewing action is too small to identify instantly, and there is no strong pile of chips,
offcuts or clearly shaped beams separating “being worked” from “finished.” The right
rack can still read as another fence because its stored timber does not separate clearly
from the horizontal slats. The process becomes understandable on inspection, but is not
yet unmistakable from the normal camera.

### 04 ironwood workyard night

The added rack and stations remain visible, so the workflow survives better than it did
in R7. However, bright green and pink ground glows compete with the stations, and the
hero behind them becomes icy blue-white. The foliage tips clip into bright individual
facets, while the warm work lamps do not organize the process into a clear input-to-
output sequence.

Workyard grade: **POLISH**, promoted from R7's FAIL-quality subsystem.

## Arrival hierarchy and grove density

### 01 road arrival day/night

The blue-grey hero crown still separates from the surrounding Meadows canopy and is
recognizable at 55 m. The scene does not look unnaturally empty: ordinary trees,
grassland and roadside vegetation retain a plausible living grove around the landmark.
But hierarchy is not decisively better than R7. Three pale/mottled creatures occupy the
central approach band, one directly over the lower hero, while bright saplings continue
to break up its trunk and crown. The road leads toward a busy wildlife cluster before it
leads toward a visible ancient root mass.

At night, the hero remains only a dark crown and partial trunk shape. Creatures and
small work lights are equal-strength accents, so the destination reveal is still weak.

### 02 southwest hero day/night

This remains the cleanest ordinary-distance proof. Day shows a monumental root plate,
interlocked trunk and wide crown without clearing the surrounding biome unnaturally.
Creature density is still excessive across both flanks, including several pale bodies
whose contrast competes with the root silhouette. Night preserves the overall tree and
adds a small warm root/hollow accent, but most internal trunk planes remain dark.

Arrival grade: **POLISH**; believable density is retained, but the reveal axis remains
occluded and cluttered.

## Hero, foliage and grounding

### 03 inside old growth day/night

The Meshy tree continues to provide PASS-quality ancient scale: trainer, ordinary trees,
massive roots, fused trunks and broad canopy form a convincing size ladder. The root
plate visibly meets the terrain and no catastrophic model float is present.

The close day frame still exposes the model defects from R7. Pale root/bark values feel
bleached against the saturated green ground, small roots terminate in flat angular
shards, and the crown is composed of many folded leaf fragments. Several bright ordinary
trees overlap the hero, and a large pale creature is cropped into the lower-left corner.

Night gives the lower hollow a useful restrained warm point and avoids turning the whole
trunk white in this angle. Most trunk/root planes nonetheless remain blue-black, while
the canopy surface loses internal hierarchy.

Hero model grade: **POLISH+** overall—strong macro form, unfinished close surface/LOD.

## Night consistency

Night is not yet consistent enough for PASS:

- 01 loses the root/trunk structure almost entirely;
- 02 retains silhouette and a modest warm base accent;
- 03 keeps the hollow accent but crushes most bark planes;
- 04 swings to the opposite extreme, turning trunk and upper foliage pale blue-white
  with conspicuous icy canopy facets.

This is a real improvement over having no readable night focal at all, but it still
looks angle-dependent rather than intentionally authored. The canopy clipping noted in
R7 remains obvious in 04.

## Remaining foliage/import defects and creature clutter

There is no visible exploded mesh, missing trunk section, whole-model float or other
catastrophic import corruption. Remaining visible generated-mesh defects are the same
class as R7: disconnected-looking triangular leaf tips, sharp crown spikes, flat root
ends and shimmer-prone high-frequency foliage. They are most obvious in 03 day and 04
night.

Creature clutter remains open. Pale creatures form a repeated dotted band through 01
and 02, one blocks the hero base on arrival, and another large creature is cropped into
03. Wildlife supports life and scale, but its current placement competes with rather
than frames the landmark.

## PASS blockers

1. Strengthen workyard nouns: one unmistakable saw/hewing station, a concentrated chip
   or offcut bed, visibly squared output, and timber occupying the seasoning rack. Reduce
   the oversized-frame/fence read.
2. Clear the central arrival cone of large creatures and enough bright saplings to show
   the hero's trunk/root base, while retaining ecology on both sides.
3. Balance local night fill across all approach angles: reveal bark/root planes in 01/03
   without recreating the icy blue-white clipping in 04.
4. Prune the most obvious foliage/root shards and validate a calmer gameplay-distance
   LOD.
5. Reduce or relocate competing green/pink ground glows around the workyard.

R8 should stay **POLISH**. It closes the absence of a work sequence, but not yet the
commercial clarity and consistency required for PASS.
