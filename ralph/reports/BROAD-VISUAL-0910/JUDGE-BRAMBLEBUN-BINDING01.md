# Blind visual verdict

**Preference: X, clearly, in both close and distant views.** X1 has visible eyes, nose, mouth, pink inner ears and a distinct pale chest. Y1 has irregular surface patches across those same regions, leaving an almost blank, mask-like face without readable eyes or expression. X2 remains recognisable as an appealing antlered rabbit; Y2 becomes a mottled upright shape. This is a substantial improvement in facial readability and surface coherence, not evidence that every remaining art problem is solved.

Evidence inspected: X1, X2, Y1, Y2; the Meadows key-art board; all five supplied Palworld screenshots. No code, provenance, previous judgments or change descriptions informed this preference. These are isolated static creature fixtures, not world or gameplay evidence.

## Eight rubric dimensions

1. **Silhouette and small-size readability.** X2 and Y2 share the same recognisable long ears, branching antlers and squat body outline. X2 wins within that outline: its dark eye and pale muzzle/chest preserve a face and body hierarchy. Y2 loses those landmarks to speckled contrast. Both have thin antler twigs that disappear at small size, while the largest branches overlap the ears enough to make the upper silhouette crowded. The outlines themselves do not establish a meaningful X advantage.

2. **Colour and value structure.** X1 uses comprehensible warm brown/gold fur, cream muzzle/chest and pink inner ears. Dark eyes anchor attention. Y1 scatters black, purple, olive, cream and orange patches across anatomy without a readable pattern. X1 still has very bright forehead, ear and chest regions and dirty-looking dark flecks through the pale ruff; its surface is busier and harsher than the broad, clean colour masses of Palworld's creatures. Neither supplies evidence about world palette consistency or reserved danger colours.

3. **Intentionality.** X1 reads as a deliberately presented woodland fantasy animal. Its facial features agree with the face, and the inner-ear regions agree with the ears. Y1 reads visibly defective: the face, ears and chest are covered by disconnected shapes whose boundaries have little relation to anatomy. X1's repeated fine mottling and crowded collar points still weaken the sense of a carefully simplified creature design. A still cannot establish the asset's origin, and no origin is inferred here.

4. **Lighting.** Both pairs have a directional light and a small ground shadow that helps place the feet. X1's near-white highlights flatten parts of the forehead and chest and obscure some surface distinctions. Y1's scrambled patches make its shading especially difficult to interpret. The empty stage provides no reliable time-of-day or habitat-lighting judgment.

5. **Horizon and depth.** All four show a bare green plane meeting blue sky. This is adequate for isolating the subject but establishes no environmental depth, atmospheric integration, vista quality or terrain transition quality. Still images cannot establish LOD popping.

6. **Interface.** No gameplay interface is present. Safe area, interaction hierarchy, controller legibility and HUD readability cannot be judged.

7. **Artefacts.** Y1 has conspicuous patch discontinuities, purple fragments on ears/branches, missing readable eyes and a dark blotch across the muzzle; Y2 retains the resulting surface noise. These look like rendering or surface-assignment defects rather than intentional markings, though the cause cannot be diagnosed from images. X1 removes that dominant failure but retains harsh pale tips, noisy chest marks and visually tangled intersections where ears and branches converge. Those intersections are difficult to read; the image does not prove actual geometry penetration. Motion defects cannot be assessed.

8. **Scale agreement.** A vertical marker appears in both pairs, but no labelled measurement or trainer is visible. X and Y occupy equivalent space relative to that marker, so there is no apparent comparative scale change. Absolute creature height, agreement with the 1.80 m trainer and suitability beside other species remain unestablished. Close-view feet are cropped, further limiting that view as scale evidence.

## Three largest reference gaps, ranked

1. **Clean facial and surface design.** Palworld 01 and 03 preserve expressive faces and broad, deliberate colour regions. Y1/Y2 fail this outright. X1/X2 restore it substantially, but X1's bright mottled fur and fragmented chest remain less controlled than the references.
2. **Controlled silhouette hierarchy.** Palworld 01 separates major horns, face and leaf mantle into readable forms. Both close fixtures crowd long ears, heavy ear-edge branches, smaller antlers and collar spikes into competing points. At the distance of X2/Y2, several decorative twigs become clutter rather than character information.
3. **World integration evidence.** The key art and Palworld 02/04/05 locate creatures within layered vegetation, ground detail, landmarks and human activity. All four fixtures supply only an empty plane. This is an evidence limitation, not a claim that the unseen game world is empty.

## Acceptance answers

**A — Key-art direction: X yes, for the visible creature design; Y no.** X's warm natural palette, friendly face and woodland hybrid silhouette plausibly belong with the key art's inviting Meadows creatures. Y's broken-looking face and patchwork surface undermine that fit. Neither fixture establishes that the surrounding world matches the board.

**B — Same kind of game as the Palworld references: X yes; Y no as presented.** X visibly supports a characterful collectible-creature fantasy. Y's unreadable face is too defective to carry that presentation. **Substantial genre-reference improvement: yes, X over Y**, particularly in the face, ear regions and coherent body markings.

**Commercial visual bar: no for either set.** X is a clear recovery, but its harsh, busy surface and crowded upper silhouette remain below the references' controlled character art. These isolated views also cannot establish animation, habitat integration or presentation during play.

Scene/material adjustments can address highlight harshness, contrast and habitat presentation. The remaining fine-pattern clutter, facial/surface refinement and crowded ear/antler design require asset-level art work if they persist under balanced lighting; adding scenery will not solve them. The frames do not establish whether replacement art is necessary or whether existing assets can be refined.
