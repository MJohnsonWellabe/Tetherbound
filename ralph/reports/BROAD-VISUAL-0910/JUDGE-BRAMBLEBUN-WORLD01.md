# Blind visual verdict: Bramblebun world 01

**Preference: X, decisively in daylight and at night.** X1/X2 present a readable, appealing rabbit face with paired dark eyes, a defined muzzle and mouth, pink inner ears, and a coherent cream chest. Y1/Y2 lose those facial landmarks beneath irregular cream, brown and occasional purple patches. The head reads as a blank rounded lump with an isolated brown blotch where a muzzle should resolve. This is a substantial visible difference in the creature itself, independent of incidental grass or cloud movement.

Judged only X1/X2/Y1/Y2 and the Meadows key art plus all five named Palworld reference images. No source, change history, previous verdicts or provenance informed this comparison. These are static, front-facing views of one creature in one habitat; they cannot establish animation quality or whole-game acceptance.

## Eight rubric dimensions

1. **Silhouette and small-size readability.** All four frames preserve the broad rabbit shape and unusually tall branching ears. X1/X2 retain a face at reduced viewing size: the dark eyes and pale muzzle form an immediate focal point. Y1/Y2 retain the outline but lose the eyes and expression. Branches and ears overlap the horizontal fence behind them in all four, weakening their finer outline.
2. **Colour and value structure.** X1's tan fur, cream chest and pink ear interiors organize the body into legible regions. Y1's angular patches break those regions into distracting scraps, while Y2 turns much of the head into an undifferentiated pale area. Both night frames separate the creature strongly from the background, but its bright chest and face appear much more illuminated than the surrounding ground. The yellow-green, heavily mottled ground in X1/Y1 competes with the creature's detailed surface. No obvious friendly oxblood contamination is visible.
3. **Intentionality.** X1/X2's facial marks support a designed personality; Y1/Y2's patch boundaries do not follow a readable facial or fur structure. The fence, cottage and path give the habitat a clear village-edge identity in every frame. Repeated upright grass clumps over broad exposed ground and the nearly uninterrupted mountain backdrop still make the setting feel assembled rather than richly inhabited. No habitat improvement between X and Y is demonstrated.
4. **Lighting.** Day and night are distinct in both pairs. Ground shadows anchor the creature, and its body has readable volume. X2's face and chest nevertheless look conspicuously front-lit against the dark meadow; Y2 suffers the same mismatch while losing more facial definition. The cottage has little warm nighttime lighting to balance the cold scene or establish a welcoming destination.
5. **Horizon and depth.** Fence, cottage, trees and mountain establish successive planes in all four. The enormous mountain fills most of the middle distance with a coarse, repeatedly folded surface. The open right-hand meadow offers little intermediate structure. Compared with the references, atmospheric separation and landmark variety are limited. No still-frame LOD transition can be established.
6. **Interface.** No HUD or interface is visible. Safe area, hierarchy and gameplay legibility cannot be assessed; this dimension provides no preference.
7. **Artefacts.** Y1/Y2's fragmented surface and absent readable eyes are the most conspicuous defect: they look like a damaged appearance rather than intentional stylization. X1/X2 repair the visible facial reading, but the cream chest and cheek edges remain jagged and highly textured. Flowers obscure the feet in every frame, so precise foot contact is only partly assessable. No z-fighting or popping is demonstrated by these stills.
8. **Scale agreement.** No trainer is present, so the specified 1.80 m ruler is unavailable. The creature appears large beside the fence, but perspective prevents a reliable height comparison with the cottage. X and Y show no meaningful visible scale difference. Do not infer roster-relative scale or certify scale compliance from this set.

## Three largest remaining reference gaps

1. **Creature finish and expression.** Palworld's forest boss and meadow boss have clean, deliberate facial landmarks and broad surface regions that carry expression. Y1/Y2 fail this at the face entirely. X1/X2 are substantially better, but still have mottled fur detail and serrated chest edges that look less unified and polished than those references. Prioritize coherent facial/surface art and clean fur boundaries.
2. **Habitat surface cohesion.** Palworld's open-field path and plateau use connected grass coverage, clear path transitions and convincing contact between terrain, vegetation and occupants. X1/Y1 have isolated blade clumps over a strong blotchy ground texture, while X2/Y2 expose the same sparse structure in shadow. Change ground palette/texture scale, vegetation clustering and coverage, and foot-level composition. This is principally scene/material work.
3. **Lighting and depth composition.** The key art uses layered valleys and welcoming settlement lights, and Palworld's plateau gives landmarks room to read across several distance planes. All four frames instead place a coarse mountain wall directly behind the cottage and creature. X2/Y2 add an unusually bright creature without a similarly motivated lighting environment. Adjust scene lighting, atmospheric separation, background framing and settlement light placement; the mountain surface may also need better authored terrain/material treatment.

## Separate bar answers

**A — Key-art direction: X yes; Y no for the complete presentation.** X1/X2's friendly woodland creature, rustic cottage, fenced meadow and distinct cool night belong to the key art's pastoral fantasy direction, even though scene finish is substantially weaker. Y1/Y2 retain that setting, but the visibly disorganized central creature surface and missing facial reading break the welcoming, intentional creature presentation. This is a direction judgment, not a comparison to painted per-pixel detail.

**B — Same kind of game as the Palworld references: X yes. Y communicates the broad creature-world premise, but no as a convincing finished presentation of that genre.** X's face and botanical rabbit silhouette establish the companion appeal needed for this comparison. Y's central subject looks visually broken. These noncombat frames do not establish whether a fight reads as an event.

**Substantial genre improvement between candidates: yes, X over Y.** Restoring readable eyes, muzzle, mouth and ear interiors is a large improvement to the thing the player should care about. This is specifically creature facial/surface improvement; the habitat is effectively unchanged.

**Commercial Palworld-quality bar: no for either set.** X wins without closing the remaining creature surface, ground/foliage and lighting/depth gaps. Scene adjustments can improve integration and habitat richness. Y's facial breakdown requires repairing the creature's visible surface appearance; X's remaining finish gap may require refined texture/material art and cleaner fur edges. These images alone cannot establish which suitable art assets already exist in the build or whether a new mesh is necessary.
