# Blind visual verdict — camera03

Evidence inspected: F01.png, F02.png, F03.png and F04.png in `.artifacts/broad-visual-0910/neutral-camera03/`; the actual `tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`; and all five Palworld JPGs (forest boss, open field path, meadow boss, plateau landmark and base building). This verdict uses visible pixels only. It makes no claim about combat activation, control ownership, input response, animation, performance or camera behavior between frames.

The creatures are visually prominent but do not yet match the reference creatures' art quality. In every frame, the blue creature's intense cobalt/violet and the winged creature's nearly uniform lemon yellow expose coarse surface patterning and weak material differentiation. Their broad shapes are identifiable, but their facial expression and anatomy do not carry the personality visible in Palworld's mammoth, purple field boss and small base creatures. Showing more of these assets helps comprehension; camera framing alone cannot establish a commercial creature-art finish.

## Preference and usable view

**F03 is my preferred frame.** Both bodies and the main appendages fit, the pair remains the subject, and enough terrain surrounds them to understand their position. The yellow left wing crosses behind the blue creature's upper silhouette, so separation is not perfect, but the two are clearly distinguishable. The yellow right wing meets the story panel. This is still a high, frontal, slightly observational view; no visible trainer anchors a conventional behind-the-character viewpoint.

**F04 is substantially more usable than F01/F02, but goes somewhat too far in distance for the strongest creature-adventure presentation.** The gap between the creatures is much clearer and the field is easier to inspect. However, the blue body is noticeably smaller and loses facial interest, the large empty hills gain importance, and the interface occupies more visual area relative to the pair. The yellow feet/lower body fall behind the slot panel. It feels closer to surveying two figures in a field than being present beside large companions. It is a useful overview, not my preferred principal view. The still cannot establish whether that distance would be useful temporarily during movement or combat.

**F01 fails framing:** a huge cropped blue head/neck consumes the left side while the yellow wing blankets the center and its body disappears behind interface panels. The ground immediately ahead is difficult to understand. **F02 remains too close:** it exposes more center ground but still crops the blue head heavily and sends much of the yellow creature off the right edge. Neither close frame makes the pair's complete silhouettes or relative placement easy to read.

## Eight rubric findings

1. **Silhouette and small-size readability.** F03 retains two recognizable creature shapes at thumbnail scale. F04 provides the cleanest space between them, but the blue face reduces to a small dark mark and the yellow creature's lower outline is obscured by UI. F01/F02 reduce the foreground blue creature to a large cropped surface; strong color separation is not a substitute for a complete silhouette. Trees, rocks and creatures remain distinct categories in F03/F04, although thin grass and flower detail collapses into speckle.

2. **Color and value structure.** All four frames share a coherent yellow-green meadow, but the ground is too chromatically busy and the creatures look excessively single-hue. F03/F04 put dark green trees behind bright yellow wings effectively; the blue face and torso have weaker internal value separation. The olive/yellow ground and gray-blue sky lack the key art's fresh warm-green/cool-blue balance. No visible oxblood element establishes or violates a danger convention here.

3. **Intentionality.** The tree grove is a recognizable cluster, and the curved exposed-earth route is useful composition in F03/F04. However, widespread fine grass spikes and scattered white flowers create fairly uniform visual chatter. The distant hills are conspicuously empty. Compared with the key art's routes leading toward settlement/landmarks, and Palworld 02/04/05's terrain enclosure, ruins and occupied spaces, the field offers little destination or authored story. One local clearing cannot prove the wider world lacks these things.

4. **Lighting.** Daylight and long cast shadows are readable in F03/F04; shadows ground trees and creatures. The yellow creature is so bright and shallowly shaded that its limbs and body form are less convincing than its wing outline. F01/F02 make the blue surface's abrupt dark-to-bright patches especially distracting. Palworld 03 gives its similarly bold purple creature clearer rounded mass and integration into the meadow light.

5. **Horizon and depth.** F03/F04 reveal foreground, grove and distant rolling hills, but depth finishes at a bland, similarly textured horizon with little landmark hierarchy or atmospheric layering. Their high angle makes this absence especially visible. The key art and Palworld 04 direct attention through layered terrain toward distinct distant forms. F01/F02 obscure most of the field with creatures. No definite LOD or chunk seam is established by these stills.

6. **Interface.** Text is generally readable and panels remain within the frame, but the story card, five-slot panel and broad command strip occupy a large fraction of the right/lower image. In F01 they conceal much of the yellow body; in F04 they conceal its lower silhouette. The five large dark cells communicate little beyond tiny number keys. The bottom-left health/food lettering and top-center time have weaker contrast than the main story text. The visible `Chop` prompt does not establish what action actually occurs. This is an internal legibility judgment, not a comparison with Palworld UI design.

7. **Artifacts.** F01/F02 expose visibly faceted boundaries and coarse color patches on the enlarged blue head/neck. Across the set, very thin high-contrast vegetation produces a prickly image, and a cyan curved band on the ground reads as a conspicuous overlay with no explanation in the image. Its purpose or correctness cannot be inferred. Cropping and UI occlusion are observable; camera clipping, z-fighting, popping and motion aliasing are not proven.

8. **Scale agreement.** No clearly visible trainer provides the required 1.80 m ruler, so exact or human-relative creature size cannot be verified. F03 makes the pair feel substantial through their width, nearby rocks and small trees. F04's increased distance weakens that presence and makes them feel more like ordinary field occupants, even though it does not show an actual change in world scale. Their differing appendages make simple bounding-box comparisons misleading: the yellow wingspan is much wider, while the central bodies are more comparable. These stills cannot establish species roles or which creature should be larger.

## Three largest gaps, ranked

1. **Creature finish and expressive presence — all frames, especially F01/F02.** Palworld 01/03/05 gives creatures readable facial character, designed material/color breaks and convincing rounded form. These enlarged assets expose coarse surfaces and dominant unmodulated blue/yellow coloring. F03 improves presentation but does not remove that gap.
2. **Subject framing and relationship to the scene — F01/F02; residual concern in F04.** Palworld 02/03/04 makes the immediate actor, other creatures and surrounding space understandable together. F01/F02 sacrifice complete silhouettes and useful ground visibility; F04 recovers those at the expense of intimacy and relative subject size. F03 best balances this set.
3. **Composed, layered environment — F03/F04.** The key art uses paths, settlements, water and varied hill/landmark layers; all five Palworld images provide meaningful terrain, foliage or occupation around their subjects. Here, repeated ground detail, a single dominant grove and empty hills provide much less sense of place. No still establishes that a fight is occurring, so the absence of visible action effects is not itself a combat defect.

## Bar answers

**A. Do these frames belong to the Meadows key-art world? No.** There is a recognizable meadow-and-grove basis, but the acidic ground/creature palette, flat distant hills and lack of compositional destination miss its inviting natural color and layered pastoral mood. This judges art direction, not painted per-leaf fidelity. Palette, lighting, vegetation grouping, framing and distant composition are scene-adjustable. More authored creature surfaces and expression require art work; these images cannot establish which replacement or improved assets are already available elsewhere in the build.

**B. Would someone say these are trying to be the same kind of game as Palworld? Yes.** Colorful fantastical creatures, a traversable outdoor field and adventure interface communicate the creature-adventure genre. F03 carries that reading most convincingly; F01/F02 obstruct it, and F04 edges toward a detached overview. **Commercial visual-quality parity with those Palworld screenshots: No.** The genre resemblance must not be counted as an art-quality pass. Framing and scene composition are addressable without proving a need for new assets. The visible creature finish requires dedicated material/texture/model presentation work, and these stills do not show enough human character art to judge it at all.

Overall choice: F03. Keep the visibility gained there; F04's extra separation is useful, but its added distance weakens creature presence more than this set needs.
