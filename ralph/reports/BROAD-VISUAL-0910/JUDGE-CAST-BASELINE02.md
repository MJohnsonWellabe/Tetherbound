# Blind creature-cast assessment

Assessed F01–F16 only, as eight consecutive close/distance pairs, against `tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png` and `palworld-01` through `palworld-05`. No source, provenance, previous reports, or runtime evidence was consulted. Frame identifiers refer to `.artifacts/broad-visual-0910/judge-cast-baseline02/`.

The visible cast is recognizable as a collection of fantasy companions, but its presentation is not commercially ready beside the Palworld references. Several subjects have appealing underlying proportions and clear identities. The common weakness is blotchy, fragmented surface colour that competes with their anatomy and faces. Two subjects have particularly weak facial readability, and the long-necked subject is not fully assessable because both pictures crop its head. These are unequal failures across the cast, not evidence that every design needs replacement.

## Full eight-part rubric

1. **Silhouette and readability at small size.** F04, F06, F08, F10, and F12 preserve distinct animal categories and useful major shapes. F02 preserves the tall branching ears but little torso organization. F14 preserves bulk rather than a clear face. F16's long neck is unmistakable but incomplete. When these distance pictures are mentally reduced to roughly one third, the best survivors are large ears, canine posture, spider width, and the long pink neck stripe; fine markings largely become noise. All backgrounds are simple, so success here does not establish visibility against real foliage, rocks, or combat effects.

2. **Colour and value structure.** The cast has strong variety: warm brown, yellow/violet, white/blue, magenta/cyan, and teal/brown. F03/F04 and F09/F10 have useful dark eyes against lighter faces. F01 and F13 distribute colour changes across important facial planes without a clear focal hierarchy. F05's dark eye areas are legible shapes but do not convey the same focused gaze as F03. The near-white regions of F07 and intense magenta/blue in F11/F15 are less restrained than the key art's natural base palette. Saturation alone is not the failure: Palworld also uses vivid creatures, with clearer large colour blocks. No allegiance or danger status is supplied, so oxblood signalling cannot be judged.

3. **Intentionality.** The eight broad forms are differentiated enough to suggest a cast: branching woodland animal, boar, long-eared companion, canine, spiny quadruped, spider, heavy quadruped, and long-necked swimmer-like animal. The shared irregular mottling gives them some surface resemblance, but it often reads as residue over the designs rather than markings that explain anatomy. F01 is the most fragmented; F03 and F09 best preserve facial intention. The images cannot establish how any art was produced. Prop arrangement and environmental authorship are outside this isolated setup.

4. **Lighting.** Bodies have readable light-to-shadow form, particularly the muzzle in F03, chest in F07, and heavy underside in F13. F01's bright pale patches and F05's bright yellow face compete with their finer facial structure. F12 and F16 have clearly separated dark ovals beneath them; their physical relationship to the ground is ambiguous. Other distance subjects have more convincing local foot contact. The uniform blue/green setting supplies little time-of-day atmosphere. These stills do not identify whether confusing surface values originate in colour, shading, or another cause.

5. **Horizon and depth.** Every frame contains a simple flat ground/sky divide, with no substantive landscape depth to assess. F08's head clears that divide, helping its silhouette. F16 is so tall within the composition that it escapes the upper boundary. No claim about game-world fog, landmark quality, LOD, or terrain seams follows from these isolated views.

6. **Interface.** No game interface or labels are visible. Safe areas, hierarchy, and gameplay legibility cannot be assessed. The thin grey upright is not labelled, and F14/F16 partly hide it. It is therefore an incomplete aid to reading scale rather than a usable player reference.

7. **Artefacts.** F01 shows especially abrupt, jagged colour fragments on its ears and torso; F05 has dark, irregular ear/cheek borders; F07 has broken-looking borders between chest tufts; F13 has pale irregular strips around the mouth. These are visible finish problems, but the images alone do not establish mesh, texture, or shading faults. F15/F16 establish a definite framing defect: the head is cut off. Several close frames cut feet or body parts, but their distance partners repair that information except for F16. Static images do not establish popping, instability, or motion defects.

8. **Scale agreement.** The repeated upright suggests intentional comparison, and the cast visibly spans compact, tall, wide, and very large forms. However, there is no visible trainer, no stated length on the upright, and no shared lineup. F16 hides much of its upright and clips the subject. Exact metres, intended roles, and any claim that a creature is wrongly sized are unsupported. The required next evidence is a complete lineup beside the 1.80 m trainer or an explicitly labelled reference; that is an assessment requirement, not a finding of incorrect scale.

## Three largest reference gaps, ranked by useful next work

1. **Restore broad, deliberate colour regions before adding detail.** Palworld's forest boss and meadow boss retain clear body masses through comparatively simple green/tan and purple/cream regions. In F01, F05, F07, F11, and F13, numerous abrupt small patches compete with those masses. The most broadly useful presentation target is cleaner faces and limbs, quieter secondary surfaces, and a small number of readable identifying accents. F03/F09 already demonstrate that a face can survive within this cast. Surface appearance needs attention; these pictures cannot prescribe the technical mechanism. Scene lighting can help values, but it cannot be assumed to remove markings contained in the creature art.

2. **Give each visible face a clear focal structure.** The references retain character identity through readable eyes, mouth/muzzle shapes, and decisive silhouettes. F01 and F13 do not establish an expression; F05's eye regions are much less focused than F03's. First show these subjects with an unobstructed face and readable facial values. If a neutral, complete view still lacks eye/muzzle definition, the remaining work is subject-specific art revision, not environment density. Do not infer missing anatomy from these views.

3. **Make the cast fully reviewable at comparable framing and scale.** Palworld places creatures in a readable relationship to a human and the surroundings. F15/F16 never show the whole head, while the grey uprights provide no explicit human scale. Fix the long-necked subject's framing and supply a complete cast lineup with the trainer, retaining useful distance views. This is directly addressable through composition and evidence capture; it does not require replacing a creature design. Recheck ambiguous ground contact in F12 in a complete, clearly grounded pose.

## Separate gates

**A — Key-art world belonging: No, not established by these frames.** Woodland anatomy in F01/F03, warm natural colours in F03/F09, and appealing companion proportions fit part of the intended language. However, the fragmented surface treatment and F11/F15's dominant intense colours do not yet form a convincing match to the board's restrained natural foundation. The plain stage also cannot demonstrate inviting meadows, mystery, landmarks, settlement life, or world integration. This is a bounded rejection of the evidence presented, not a finding that the unseen environment fails. Palette, lighting, and composition can address part of the gap; persistent confusing markings or facial structure require creature-art work. A world capture is still necessary to decide full scene belonging.

**B — Same kind of game as Palworld: Yes, at the cast-concept level.** Recognizable fantasy animals, exaggerated companion proportions, colour-coded identities, and distinct large-creature silhouettes clearly suggest the same creature-collection adventure category. F04, F06, F08, F10, and F12 carry that answer. This does not establish comparable world density, lived-in quality, encounter staging, or combat spectacle because none is shown.

**Commercial visual readiness beside Palworld: No.** The issue is the uneven finish and expression of the creatures being offered as the game's attraction. Shared surface breakup, weak facial evidence for F01/F13, and incomplete presentation of F15/F16 prevent approval. F03/F09 have a more coherent basis and should not be treated as equally deficient. Scene-only work can repair framing and improve assessment; it cannot by itself guarantee clear facial design or deliberate surface markings. No performance, animation, or movement conclusion is made.
