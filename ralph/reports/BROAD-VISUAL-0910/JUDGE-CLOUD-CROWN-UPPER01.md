# Blind visual judgment: F01–F04

Judged only the supplied four images, the Meadows key art, and all five Palworld reference screenshots. No source, change description, history, or other verdicts inspected. F01/F03 are daylight; F02/F04 are night. No numeric scores.

**Pair preference: no meaningful preference.** F01/F02 and F03/F04 present effectively the same landform silhouettes, cliff faces, ridge crown, cloud obstruction, and camera composition. Minor foreground grass/flower differences do not establish a material visual improvement. I cannot identify an improved geological formation or stronger composition in either pair.

The trainer is identifiable but small and visually ordinary compared with the expressive character/creature combinations in the references. The dark hair and trousers lose separation in daylight. No creature is visible in any supplied frame; these images cannot establish creature art quality or relative creature scale, and they lack the companion presence central to the reference presentation.

## Eight criteria

1. **Silhouette and small-size readability.** In all four frames, the trainer, grass banks, cliff wall, and sky remain identifiable at a reduced reading size. The trainer's pale shirt helps, especially in F02/F04. However, the cliff becomes a row of similar vertical teeth, and the central white/lavender cloud mass becomes the strongest shape. There is no distinct destination silhouette drawing the eye beyond the player. F01/F03 merge the player's dark lower body into the path.

2. **Colour and value structure.** F01/F03 use compatible greens, gray stone, and blue sky, but olive foreground grass and gray-green cliffs dominate without enough separation between materials. Broad nearly white central shapes consume the brightest value and attention. F02/F04 cool the scene coherently, but the pale sky and light grass still suggest an overcast or twilight scene more readily than 23:00. No conspicuous oxblood misuse is visible.

3. **Intentionality and geology.** All frames show repeated rounded cliff columns with similar face width, horizontal banding, and small attached ledges. The skyline does vary, but reads as an assembled barrier rather than an eroded mountain with major buttresses, setbacks, and a few distinctive peaks. The broad smooth green crown at the top-center and the straight left climbing strip feel too simple beside the cliff detail. Foreground grass forms thick, relatively uniform blade banks; the scattered small flowers do not sufficiently break their repeated texture. Both pairs retain these defects.

4. **Lighting.** Daylight gives the cliff useful light/dark plane separation in F01/F03, and the trainer appears grounded. F02/F04 flatten distant stone into misty gray slabs while illuminating the trainer's face/shirt and grass strongly. The night sky remains very bright. The night treatment changes colour and visibility but does not deliver the key art's dark sky, concentrated warm lights, and sheltered mystery. Exact light-source causes cannot be diagnosed from the stills.

5. **Horizon and depth.** The near boulder rim, far cliff, and sky provide depth in all frames. But large opaque, smooth-edged cloud shapes occupy the middle of the vista and hide the cliff bases. In F01/F03 they read like white cutout forms; F02/F04 reveal simple overlapping rounded lobes and an especially conspicuous pale oval near upper center-right. These interrupt the feeling of geological height and a continuous valley. The cliff wall also occupies nearly one continuous distance plane. No pair improves this structure visibly.

6. **Interface.** Text is legible at native size and the main panels sit within the image. The oversized upper-right objective panel obscures a substantial part of the vista; the lower-right slot and command bars occupy much of the foreground. Tiny white keycaps become indistinct at reduced size, while the large empty slots attract attention without conveying a team. The minimap's broad blurred colour field provides little visible terrain context. These are observations about this interface, not comparisons to Palworld UI.

7. **Artefacts.** In all frames, cloud edges and the pale oval look like simple geometry rather than atmospheric volume. The left climbing landform has a hard, ruler-straight boundary against the cliff/cloud area. Grass consists of conspicuous long intersecting strips at the camera, producing a wiry lattice; the path surface has a repetitive, low-detail mottled appearance. A cyan structure is cut off by the left edge, creating an unexplained visual fragment. I see no definite z-fighting in the stills and cannot assess popping or motion aliasing.

8. **Scale agreement.** Using the trainer as the 1.80 m ruler, nearby grass reaches roughly waist height in places and its broad individual blades dominate the foreground; it reads as coarse reeds more than varied meadow cover. Cliff height is clearly monumental and not inherently implausible, but lack of visible intermediate terraces and useful scale anchors makes its distance harder to parse. The top-left trunk and nearby boulders can coexist plausibly with the trainer. No creature comparison is possible.

## Three largest gaps, ranked

1. **Atmospheric and geological credibility — F01–F04.** Palworld's plateau reference separates broken rock terraces, vegetation, distant mountains, and a memorable tower. Here, a repeated cliff barrier is partly concealed by flat white/lavender lobes. Break the barrier into larger unequal masses and setbacks, clarify the crown silhouette, and make the cloud layer preserve readable depth instead of becoming the focal object. Both pairs are equally affected.

2. **Ground-cover composition and material separation — F01–F04.** The key art and Palworld field shots alternate readable paths, short cover, dense clumps, rock, and trees. Here, similarly tall intersecting blades crowd a dark, weakly articulated path. Reduce the foreground blade dominance; establish different cover heights, stronger clusters, more deliberate exposed ground, and clearer stone/soil/grass transitions.

3. **Character-led identity and day/night mood — F01–F04.** The references make the trainer plus expressive creatures the center of the world, and the key art gives night a distinct dark, intimate mood. These frames offer one small trainer, no visible companion, and a brightly washed night background. Stage a recognizable creature relationship and an authored destination; give night a controlled value hierarchy with specific light accents. A closer character/creature view is required to judge whether the underlying hero art itself meets the reference bar.

## Direct bar answers

**A — Belongs to the key-art world: no.** The natural palette and adventurous cliffs give partial kinship, but the coarse grass, repeated cliff language, conspicuous cloud geometry, and weak night mood do not carry the board's composed, inviting landscape. This is an art-direction judgment, not a demand for painted per-leaf fidelity.

**B — Trying to be the same kind of game as Palworld: yes.** The stylized third-person trainer, explorable green paths, monumental fantasy cliffs, and team/action interface communicate that genre intent. The absence of a visible creature makes that connection less compelling than it should be.

**Comparable commercial visual quality: no, for either pair.** Genre resemblance does not establish shipping-quality execution. The atmospheric shapes, repeated geology, coarse vegetation, and limited focal hierarchy remain conspicuous beside the supplied real-time references.

Most demonstrated gaps are addressable through scene composition, terrain/rock arrangement, ground-cover distribution, lighting, cloud treatment, and staging existing characters/creatures. Better foliage forms, rock surfacing, or atmospheric assets may be necessary if the current assets cannot support those changes; these images cannot establish what is available in the build. They also do not justify declaring that new creature or trainer art is required: no creature is shown, and the trainer occupies too few pixels for a reliable close art assessment.

Static limits: no claims about performance, traversal, animation, temporal stability, unseen locations, or the complete creature roster. The day/night stills demonstrate appearance only, not transition quality.
