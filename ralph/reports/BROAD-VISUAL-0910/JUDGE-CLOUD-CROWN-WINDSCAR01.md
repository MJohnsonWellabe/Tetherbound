## All eight criteria

1. **Silhouette and readability at small size.** In F01–F04, the blue-and-white trainer remains separable from the grass, and the central tree is identifiable. The two very tall, narrow trunks become dominant vertical bars, interrupting the vista. The immense left cliff reads as a ribbed pillar with its top cropped away. Grass reduces to a repeated scratch pattern rather than distinct vegetation masses. The small green glow beyond the central tree and pale shape beside the right path are not readable as particular creatures or objects at reduced size.

2. **Colour and value structure.** F01/F03 have a coherent green, grey, and blue palette, but saturated yellow-green ground occupies most of the lower frame with little soil, stone, or muted plant variation. The foreground's chroma competes with the trainer. F02/F04 create stronger trainer separation, but near-black ground sits against almost white cliffs and a bright blue-grey sky; the night landscape lacks a graduated value hierarchy. No clearly identifiable oxblood danger treatment is visible, so its reservation cannot be judged.

3. **Intentionality.** F01–F04 show some clear space around the trainer and a path on the right, but much of the visible grass has the same isolated, angular blade language across the slope. The central leafy tree sits between two disproportionately tall bare trunk sections, making the arrangement feel assembled rather than composed around a destination. The left rock is an enormous straight-sided shaft; the right cliffs show repeated vertical ribs and thin horizontal bands. Broad weathered shoulders, broken ledges, accumulated rubble, and vegetation following geological shelves are insufficiently legible to make these formations feel convincingly eroded. The visible distant span and ravine offer a promising landmark relationship, but the trunks and quest panel obscure it.

4. **Lighting.** F01/F03 read as daylight and have useful ground shadows. Cliff shading reveals ribs, but the sheer surfaces remain broad, uniform slabs. F02/F04 visibly change to night through the moon, darker ground, and cool palette; however, the cliffs become bright chalk-like silhouettes and the trainer's white shirt and face remain unusually bright against the surrounding vegetation. That weakens a shared sense of illumination. The central glow is a small local accent, not enough to organize the night scene.

5. **Horizon and depth.** All four frames communicate exceptional height and a deep ravine. Overlapping rock formations and the distant span establish multiple planes. F01/F03 preserve more rock relief than F02/F04. At night, pale fog and pale cliffs merge, flattening the left pillar and distant right wall. The dominant cropped pillar and trunk bars do not provide an inviting visual route through the scene. No definite chunk boundary or LOD seam can be identified from these stills.

6. **Interface.** F01–F04 keep the main HUD within the visible frame, and the quest instruction is readable. The large upper-right quest card obscures a meaningful section of the cliffs and route. The empty five-slot panel and wide action bar consume much of the lower-right view. Small keyboard keycaps are weak at reduced size; the food label and some dark action text have lower contrast. The minimap is a blurred colour field with an arrow and gives little legible local terrain structure. These are observations about these frames, not comparisons with Palworld's UI.

7. **Artefacts.** F01–F04 expose broad, sharply folded grass strips, including oversized blades close to the camera, and conspicuous horizontal banding on the cliff surfaces. These reduce material believability. The huge pale disc in F02/F04 is clipped by the top border and reads as a stark flat shape. No definite z-fighting, intersecting geometry, or broken terrain seam is proven by these images. Motion-dependent popping and shimmer are unassessed.

8. **Scale agreement.** Against the 1.80 m trainer in F01–F04, nearby grass frequently reaches knee-to-waist height; the closest blades occupy a substantial fraction of the trainer's visible height, making individual grass strips unusually dominant. The central leafy tree is only a few trainer heights tall, while the adjacent trunks extend out of frame without a visible crown. That combination gives the vegetation an inconsistent apparent scale hierarchy, although perspective and cropped crowns prevent an exact height comparison. The enormous cliffs can work as fantasy geography; their defect is insufficient intermediate geological structure, not sheer size alone. No adequately readable creature beside the trainer permits a defensible creature-to-human scale verdict.

## Three largest reference gaps, ranked

1. **Geological structure and vista composition — F01–F04.** Palworld's plateau reference uses broken, stepped rock masses, vegetated shoulders, and an open sightline toward a distant landmark. These frames feature a dominant ribbed shaft, similarly vertical cliff walls, and foreground trunk bars that interrupt the ravine view. Introduce a clearer hierarchy of large rock masses, secondary shelves, and smaller erosion features; compose foreground vegetation around the landmark opening.

2. **Ground cover and landscape integration — F01–F04.** The key art organizes natural greens into patches of flowers, stone, paths, foliage, and shade; Palworld integrates finer grass with soil and terrain transitions. Here the angular grass strips and broad green ground texture dominate almost the entire foreground. More coherent plant clumps, different height layers, exposed geology, and deliberate ground transitions would make the slope feel like a habitat instead of repeated coverage.

3. **Lighting hierarchy and living focal points — especially F02/F04, with creature readability absent across F01–F04.** The key art's night panel retains blue landscape layers and selective warm lights; these night views collapse into dark grass and nearly white distant rock. Palworld's references also put expressive creatures prominently into the scene. The small glowing shapes here cannot carry that role. Improve the night value progression and stage a readable creature encounter or companion in the visible landscape.

## Bar decisions

**A — Belonging to the key-art world: No.** The natural palette and adventurous vertical landscape provide a connection, but the repeated strip grass, pillar-like geology, obstructed composition, and stark night values do not yet express the board's integrated, inviting world. This is a palette, mood, composition, and landmark-language comparison; no painted per-leaf fidelity is demanded, and a cliff biome is not being rejected for differing from a meadow.

**B — Trying to be the same kind of game as Palworld: Yes.** A stylized third-person trainer, open natural terrain, fantasy landmarks, and creature-selection/call-out interface communicate the broad genre. This is recognition of intent, not evidence of equivalent finish.

**Commercial visual quality beside the supplied Palworld references: No.** The visible landforms, grass, scene composition, and night lighting remain materially less resolved. Neither pair closes that gap.

**Scene-adjustable work:** open the landmark sightline; reorganize tree placement and vegetation scale; replace even grass coverage with designed patches and clear ground transitions; tune night brightness, fog, and distance separation; stage readable creatures. **Art/content work:** the visible rock forms need more convincing erosion and material structure, and the dominant grass blades need a more cohesive presentation. The stills do not establish whether suitable assets already exist elsewhere in the build, so they cannot justify a claim that new purchases or meshes are required. Creature asset quality itself is unproven because no creature is sufficiently visible here.
