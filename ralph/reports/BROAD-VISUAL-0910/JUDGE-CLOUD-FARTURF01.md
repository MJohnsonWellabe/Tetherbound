# Blind visual judgment: F01/F02 versus F03/F04

Judged the four supplied stills and the Meadows key art plus all five Palworld reference screenshots. No source, change description, history, previous judgments, or provenance inspected. Repository instructions and the visual-judge rubric were read. This is a still-image assessment; it makes no performance, motion, or traversal claims.

## Pair preference

**Terrain, vegetation, and environmental scene coherence: no meaningful preference between F01/F02 and F03/F04.** The large green middle-distance slope, narrow fringe of foreground grass, exposed angular rocks, repetitive cliff wall, and sparse distant trees produce essentially the same environmental result in both pairs. Fine differences in distant grass-like speckling do not turn that slope into convincingly vegetated ground or change the scene's composition at viewing size. Neither pair resolves the central environmental gap.

**Character and interface considered separately:** F02's illuminated face and pale shirt keep the trainer immediately legible; F04's darker blue treatment sits more naturally in the shadowed foreground but loses the face, hands, and trouser detail. I prefer F02 for small-size character readability, with the reservation that its bright character feels separately lit. F04 improves that local lighting agreement without establishing a convincing nighttime scene overall. F01 and F03 show no meaningful character preference. F01/F02 have a cleaner view of the sky and cliffs because F03/F04 add a broad guidance banner; that is an interface/composition difference, not an environmental improvement.

The trainer is recognizably a stylized adventurer, but the small, neutral presentation does not establish the bespoke character appeal visible in the Palworld references. No clearly readable companion or encounter creature is presented in any of the four frames, so creature art quality and comparative creature scale cannot be judged here. Their absence from the readable composition weakens the creature-adventure presentation; it does not establish that creatures are absent from the game.

## Eight criteria

1. **Silhouette and readability at small size.** In F01 and F03, the trainer's pale torso and blue jacket remain readable against green ground, and the large rocks and tower remain identifiable. The very small trees and scattered distant objects collapse into marks; there is no readable creature silhouette. F02 retains the trainer through unusually strong illumination, whereas F04 loses much of the lower body into the ground. The cliff skyline reads as a repetitive low band instead of a distinct destination silhouette. None approaches the character-and-creature silhouette hierarchy of Palworld 03/04.

2. **Colour and value structure.** F01/F03 have a broad yellow-green ground mass, grey-green cliffs, cyan sky, and a relatively warm stone wall. That is a compatible natural palette in outline, but the slope has too little local colour/value variation to read as richly inhabited terrain. F02/F04 separate dark foreground from pale distance, yet the light grey-blue sky and bleached cliffs oppose the nominal 23:00 nighttime mood. F04's darker trainer is more consistent with its foreground than F02's. No identifiable oxblood danger element is visible, so its reservation cannot be assessed.

3. **Intentionality.** All four frames place most nearby grass in a conspicuous edge strip while leaving the large central standing area and far slope broadly bare. Distant trees look isolated and undersized within the enormous rock landscape. Rocks and vegetation do not yet form convincing pockets, sheltered ledges, or a sequence of authored destinations. The thin diagonal route on the right helps orientation, but it does not have the path-edge transitions and surrounding clusters seen in Palworld 02 or the key art's village approach. The repeating cliff columns amplify the assembled appearance.

4. **Lighting.** F01/F03 clearly read as daylight: the stone wall has warm highlights, the trainer casts a ground shadow, and the foreground has a broad shadow mass. The far slope remains comparatively flat, so its curvature and surface character are weak. F02/F04 read more like dark foreground under a pale, overcast sky than the key art's moonlit night. Bright pale grass strands in the night shadow and the washed-out horizon weaken material agreement. F04 reduces the trainer's separate illumination, but it needs controlled silhouette separation rather than simply losing the body to shadow.

5. **Horizon and depth.** The foreground wall, near grass, middle slope, and distant cliffs establish distance in every frame. Daytime cliffs retain some layered form; nighttime fog collapses much of that relief into a pale band in F02/F04. The horizontal, repeated cliff shapes provide scale but little landmark hierarchy. Near the right horizon, bright sky/cloud shapes beneath the cliff edge create an abrupt, cut-out impression. The stills do not establish whether this is an intentional landform or a defect, and show no provable LOD transition.

6. **Interface.** Main-story text is legible, with good dark-panel contrast, but its large panel covers much of the already dominant tower. Bottom action and slot panels occupy a large portion of the view while showing tiny keyboard glyphs and empty slots. Food text has weak contrast, and the minimap is too blurred to communicate much geography at a glance. F03/F04's wide banner adds a second major instruction area; F03 also has a place title. The banner and story card compete for attention. All elements remain inside the image bounds. This is an internal usability assessment, not a comparison against Palworld UI.

7. **Artefacts.** All four frames contain thin dark vertical flecks in the upper-left sky that read as stray marks rather than scene content. The distant cliffs show conspicuous horizontal banding and repeated forms, and angular rock transitions interrupt otherwise smooth slopes. Nighttime pale grass against dark earth looks harsh and wiry. These are visible symptoms; the stills do not establish their implementation cause. No definite z-fighting, temporal popping, or motion aliasing can be concluded.

8. **Scale agreement.** Using the trainer as the 1.80 m ruler, nearby tall grass reaches approximately lower-leg to knee height and the masonry blocks read as large, heavy construction stones. The wall's massive scale is plausible for a gate structure. The distant trees look small beside the cliffs, but perspective and distance prevent a confident absolute-size diagnosis. There is insufficient readable creature content to compare creature size with trainer size, or one creature with another. No unsupported species-scale judgment is made.

## Three ranked reference gaps

1. **Ground cover and integration — F01–F04.** Palworld 02/03/04 combine readable grass volume, bare travel surfaces, tree cover, and rock-to-soil transitions; the key art similarly groups vegetation around routes and terrain features. These frames instead have a foreground grass border and a broad, smooth green middle slope. Addressable through scene/material work: vary ground coverage in coherent patches, integrate rock bases and path edges, and create vegetation groupings across the middle distance. More tiny scattered flecks alone would not solve the broad read.

2. **Lighting, depth, and landmark composition — F01–F04, strongest in F02/F04.** The key art organizes recognizable destinations within layered terrain and gives night a deep blue sky, moonlight, and warm habitation. Palworld 04 gives its broad vista a distinct distant landmark and intermediate tree masses. These frames offer a long repeated cliff band, sparse tiny trees, and a pale nighttime horizon without an equally clear destination. Primarily scene work: stronger landmark hierarchy, varied cliff grouping, intermediate vegetation masses, and coherent night sky/fog/ground values. Bespoke landmark art may be needed if scene arrangement cannot create a distinctive silhouette, but the available asset inventory is unknown.

3. **Character-and-creature appeal in the composition — F01–F04.** Palworld 01/03/04 make expressive creatures the unmistakable subject or companions; even the base view includes them. These frames show one small neutral trainer and no clearly readable creature. Scene work can supply a relevant companion or encounter and give it a readable placement. Whether existing creature assets meet the art bar is unjudgeable from these frames. Bespoke expressions, animation, or silhouette improvements, if required, are art work rather than ground-density fixes; these stills do not establish what new art must be acquired.

## Bar answers

**A — Belongs to the Meadows key-art world: No, for either pair.** Green terrain, stone construction, and a blue sky offer surface compatibility, but the sparse ground treatment, repetitive grey cliff language, weak destination hierarchy, and pale night mood do not carry the board's inviting, inhabited natural world. This does not demand identical Meadows vegetation in a cliff setting. Most visible failures can be addressed through palette, lighting, composition, ground cover, and scene dressing. These frames alone cannot establish an inventory-specific need to buy or make assets.

**B — Trying to be the same kind of game as the Palworld references: Yes, for either pair.** The stylized third-person adventurer, open fantasy landscape, exploration direction, and creature-related interface establish a recognizable genre relationship. This is a genre answer, not a shipping-quality pass.

**B — Comparable commercial visual quality: No, for either pair.** Sparse and weakly integrated vegetation, repetitive distant geometry, incoherent night values, and the lack of a compelling readable creature presentation leave a substantial gap. F03/F04 do not materially close the environmental gap relative to F01/F02. Scene work can address much of it; character/creature art adequacy beyond the visible trainer presentation remains insufficiently evidenced.
