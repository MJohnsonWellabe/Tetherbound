# Blind visual judgment — F01–F04

Reviewed only the four assigned game frames, the Meadows key art, all five Palworld references, and the two explicitly supplementary references. No source, change description, manifests or other reports informed this judgment. F01/F03 show day; F02/F04 show night. Findings concern visible still images, not motion or performance.

**Overall preference: F03/F04, narrowly.** The upright grass reads more readily as rooted vegetation than the tangled, almost laid-flat strands in F01/F02, and the white-and-blue creature has a more coherent silhouette and material treatment than the brilliant purple foreground creature. These are different creature compositions, so the overall preference cannot establish that the ground treatment alone caused the improvement. On ground alone, F03/F04 gains coherent clumps but retains coarse blades, relentless density and an artificial clearing edge. Neither pair is commercially ready.

The character and creature presentation is a leading weakness. In F01/F02, the purple creature's nearly featureless neck and very bright body sit beside a much more textured dark feathered creature, without convincing shared material language. In F03/F04, the white creature reads more cohesively, but its overlapping fur plates and blue patches remain conspicuously faceted. The small trainer in all four frames is recognizable, but these distant views cannot establish facial expressiveness or close-up character quality. Palworld's subjects have stronger shape hierarchy and more integrated surface shading.

## Eight rubric criteria

1. **Silhouette and small-size readability.** In all four frames the trainer remains identifiable because of the central clearing, and the large foreground creature separates strongly by color. At thumbnail scale, however, the surrounding grass collapses into a bright or dark scratch texture; tiny flowers vanish. F01/F02's purple feathers and the dark creature behind them overlap into an ambiguous right-side mass. F03/F04's pointed ears, muzzle and legs communicate a quadruped more immediately. In all four frames the story panel covers the creature grouping and the right edge crops it, diminishing the principal attraction.

2. **Color and value structure.** F01/F03 place nearly the entire ground in strong yellow-green, with only a few tiny flower accents. F01 adds an extremely saturated purple creature that feels disconnected from the subdued brown trunks and gray central structure. F03's white and blue palette is more controlled, although its bright whites compete with the pale sky. F02/F04 darken the vegetation substantially while leaving the sky, trainer and creatures pale and conspicuous: the value structure splits into dark ground and bright subjects rather than a unified night environment. There is no visible basis to judge oxblood danger-color use here.

3. **Intentionality.** F01/F02's fine curved strands create a matted, crosshatched carpet around the clearing. F03/F04 has more legible upright clumps, but repeated blade widths and similar heights give the grass a manufactured look. All four frames have a smooth, nearly empty green patch around the trainer bounded by a fairly abrupt wall of vegetation. The few scattered flowers do not establish distinct plant communities or meaningful clusters. The large central vertical slab and trunks frame the character, but provide little readable destination or environmental story. Scene composition needs more than an increase in grass coverage.

4. **Lighting.** F01/F03 clearly read as daytime, with strong shadows grounding the trainer and trunks. Their grass highlights are so widespread that individual clumps lose rounded volume. F02/F04 read as a cool lighting variant more convincingly than 23:00: the pale sky and central structure remain bright, the trainer's face and shirt look strongly illuminated, and the large creatures have little sense of being immersed in darkness. F04's white body in particular approaches a luminous cutout above the dark grass. Night needs a coherent source and value hierarchy, not simply darker ground.

5. **Horizon and depth.** All four frames terminate much of the view in a pale, nearly empty horizon behind the foreground vegetation. The large rectangular central structure blocks the main sightline without revealing enough shape to work as a distinctive landmark. F02/F04's light structure and distant haze further merge. There is little readable progression from near ground to middle-distance terrain to far landforms, unlike the linked cliffs, paths and distant landmark in Palworld 04. These views cannot demonstrate a ravine's spatial character. No definite LOD or chunk transition can be established from the stills.

6. **Interface.** The top instruction is readable and the controls remain inside the frame in all four images. The large story box obscures a substantial part of the creature presentation, while the wide slot bar covers lower creature and grass detail. The bottom-left health and food panels have comparatively weak contrast over busy foliage, especially the muted food text in F01/F03. Five tiny white key symbols in otherwise empty slots provide little at-a-glance information. The minimap is mostly an indistinct pale field. This is a direct usability judgment, not comparison with reference UI.

7. **Artifacts.** F01/F03 show pronounced horizontal banding across the central gray structure; F02/F04 also show striated surfaces and hard-edged patches there. Grass in all frames contains many crossing straight strips and sharp ends, with F01/F02 particularly resembling tangled linework near the clearing. F03/F04's enlarged white creature exposes abrupt polygonal fur layers. These are visible surface/geometry presentation defects; the images do not establish their technical cause. No claim of z-fighting, animation clipping or popping is supported by these stills.

8. **Scale agreement.** Relative to the 1.80 m trainer, all four frames present very large creatures and tall vegetation, though foreground perspective contributes substantially. The white creature in F03/F04 has readable legs and contact with the ground, making its bodily scale easier to understand than the cropped purple silhouette in F01/F02. Grass beside the clearing approaches a substantial fraction of the trainer's height, yet remains made of broad, simple strips: it reads closer to oversized stalks than fine meadow cover. Massive trunks can be plausible, but their cropped canopies and lack of smaller vegetation layers leave few intermediate scale cues. No companion-versus-practice creature comparison is visible, and exact creature heights cannot be inferred reliably from these unequal depths.

## Three largest gaps from the primary references

1. **An integrated creature and character presentation.** Palworld 01/03/04 offers instantly legible, expressive creature forms with shading and shape detail that belong together. F01/F02's purple body and dark feathered neighbor have conflicting surface treatments; F03/F04 improves cohesion but still exposes faceted fur and loses the body beneath UI and frame cropping. Composition, lighting and material calibration are scene-fixable. Stronger underlying anatomy, fur/feather construction or expressive design, where needed, requires art work; vegetation edits cannot supply it. The frames cannot establish what replacement art is already available in the build.

2. **Ground cover with varied forms and purposeful transitions.** The key art uses layered plants, exposed earth, stones and irregular patches to connect routes with surroundings; Palworld 02/03/05 similarly gives grass readable volume and integrates it into terrain. F01–F04 rely on dense repeated strips enclosing a smooth green patch. F03/F04 improves upright growth without resolving the coarse scale or abrupt boundary. Clustering, height variation, softer clearing transitions and quieter saturation are scene-fixable; higher-quality plant forms may require new or improved vegetation art.

3. **A coherent place and time of day.** The key art builds inviting distance and makes night visibly darker while preserving chosen light accents; Palworld 04 layers traversable ground, cliffs and a distinctive landmark. F01–F04 offer little middle distance beyond the slab, and F02/F04 retain a bright sky and pale subjects above dark ground. View composition, background arrangement and day/night lighting are scene-fixable. The images alone cannot determine whether suitable landmark art exists elsewhere.

## Separate acceptance answers

**A — Belongs to the key-art world: no.** Green vegetation and a fantasy companion are shared ingredients, but F01–F04 lack the key art's natural palette relationships, layered landforms and authored plant transitions. F02/F04 especially miss its mysterious, legible night mood. Scene work can address most environmental gaps; character/creature surface construction also needs art attention.

**B — Trying to be the same kind of game as Palworld: yes.** A small human with conspicuous oversized fantasy creatures in an explorable outdoor setting makes that genre ambition apparent in all four frames. This is a genre-read answer, not a shipping-quality pass. F03/F04 communicates that ambition more cohesively than F01/F02.

**Commercial readiness: no.** The vegetation treatment, incompatible night exposure, obstructed creature presentation and weak spatial composition remain visibly unfinished in both pairs.

**Supplementary comparison only:** Enshrouded demonstrates how localized light and clear creature forms can make a dark scene readable without flattening the environment. Once Human's settlement shows a readable night through restrained sky values, cast shadows and layered silhouettes. F02/F04 do not yet achieve that integration. Their genres, art direction and UI are not substitutes for bar A or bar B, and no combat-event judgment is possible from the four inactive poses supplied here.
