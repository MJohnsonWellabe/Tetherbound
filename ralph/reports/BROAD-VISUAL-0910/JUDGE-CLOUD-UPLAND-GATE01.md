# Blind visual review: cloud upland gate

Evidence: neutral F01–F04 PNGs from `.artifacts/broad-visual-0910/neutral-cloud-upland-gate01`; the Meadows key-art board and all five `palworld-0*.jpg` references. F01/F03 are daytime views; F02/F04 are nighttime views. No source, history, implementation provenance, or other verdicts were consulted. No Godot run was performed. Judgments concern these still images only.

**Preference: F01/F02, modestly.** F01's quieter green ground and F02's quieter dark ground compete less with the trainer than the broad pale mottling in F03 and blue-green mottling in F04. The latter adds surface variation, but does not convincingly describe grass, soil, wear, or terrain form; especially at night it looks like a cloudy overlay across a flat floor. This is a surface-treatment preference, not an acceptance of either pair. Terrain composition, vegetation silhouette, character presentation, and distant cliffs remain substantially the same. F04 also lacks the upper instruction banner visible in F02; that improves sky visibility but should not be mistaken for an environmental improvement.

**Character and creature assessment first:** The trainer in every frame is identifiable and has a coherent blue-and-white outfit, but is presented as a small, isolated, upright figure in a largely empty clearing. The head, hair, and clothing read as a familiar generic stylized adventure character rather than a distinctive lead comparable to the expressive character and creature ensemble in the Palworld references. The images do not establish close-up face or animation quality. No recognizable nearby creature appears in any frame, so creature art quality, relative creature scale, and the appeal of a team cannot be assessed. The lack of visible companions weakens the intended creature-adventure presentation immediately.

## Full rubric

1. **Silhouette and readability at small size.** In all four frames the trainer's light shirt separates clearly from the ground. The wall remains unmistakable masonry, and the large rocks remain readable. Small trees on the far slope dissolve into dots, while foreground grass becomes a thin spiky band. At reduced size the view is primarily a green floor, gray cliff band, and oversized wall crop; it has no readable creature or destination silhouette that can compete with the interface.

2. **Colour and value structure.** F01/F03 share natural green, gray, and blue ingredients, but the broad middle slope is a nearly single-value green slab. F03's foreground is more mottled without adding a useful focal hierarchy. F02/F04 separate dark foreground from pale distance, yet the sky and distant cliffs are so bright that the night treatment resembles a dark foreground under a daytime sky. F04 adds distracting cyan-green ground patches. No visible oxblood misuse can be established.

3. **Intentionality.** All frames have a clear open space around the trainer and a narrow rising path at right, but the open middle slope is too barren to read as deliberately composed habitat. Repeated upright cliff sections form an almost level fence across the horizon. Sparse, similarly small trees do little to organize the space. F03/F04's texture variation does not provide the authored grass-to-soil transitions, grounded rock clusters, or inhabited destinations visible in the references.

4. **Lighting.** The daytime wall shading, rock faces, and broad cast shadow in F01/F03 give objects some volume and ground contact. The large dark shadow crossing the clearing also helps break up the floor. F02/F04 retain an extremely pale trainer face/shirt and bright grass blades against a very dark floor, while the distant environment remains washed bright. Night is distinguishable from day, but its illumination is not cohesive or inviting. There is no visible warm local light or focal night landmark to balance the cold scene.

5. **Horizon and depth.** Overlap between foreground grass, rocks, path, and distant cliffs supplies basic depth in every frame. However, the repetitive gray cliff wall limits the sense of an expansive landscape. The huge pale cloud area beyond the right-hand cliffs and the unusually uniform haze in F02/F04 flatten the far view. The narrow right-hand path is a useful directional feature, but visually subordinate to the wall crop and UI. No definite chunk or LOD seam is established by these stills.

6. **Interface.** Labels generally stay inside the image and the main task is legible. However, the large upper banner duplicates the right-hand quest panel in F01–F03 and obscures the landscape. The two broad lower-right panels consume substantial space while the five party slots contain tiny numbered squares rather than readable creature identities. The food and health numbers are clearer than their dark low-contrast surrounding labels; the small time display is difficult to read at reduced size. F04 is less obstructed at the top because its banner is absent. These are independent usability observations, not comparisons with Palworld's UI.

7. **Artefacts.** All four frames show small dotted vertical streaks in the upper-left sky, which read as stray rendering marks rather than intentional sky detail. Foreground grass has brittle needle-like edges, particularly bright and conspicuous in F02/F04. Large floor texture patches in F03/F04 read as surface noise rather than discrete materials. There is no unambiguous z-fighting or intersection defect visible here. Popping and motion aliasing cannot be judged.

8. **Scale agreement.** Using the trainer as the 1.80 m reference, nearby grass appears predominantly shin-to-knee height, with some taller clumps; this is plausible. The large wall blocks and bulky foreground rocks suggest heavy architecture and geology, with no decisive metre-scale contradiction. The distant trees look undersized against the broad plateau, but depth prevents a reliable measurement. No creatures are available for the most important trainer-to-creature or creature-to-creature comparison.

## Three largest reference gaps, ranked

1. **Missing creature-led visual event and distinct cast presentation — F01–F04.** Palworld 01, 03, and 04 give expressive creatures large, immediately legible silhouettes and clear relationships with people. These frames offer only the small isolated trainer. Better scene population and framing can establish presence; distinct character design and convincing creature expression require suitable art. The unseen creature assets cannot be condemned or approved from this evidence.

2. **Ground and habitat composition — F01–F04, particularly F03/F04's mottled floor.** Palworld 02/04 and the key art connect grass, bare paths, rock bases, and vegetation into readable patches with deliberate clearings. These views leave most of the visible plateau as an undifferentiated green surface and use a narrow band of spiky grass to supply nearly all close vegetation structure. This is chiefly scene, scatter, and material work; extra texture contrast alone does not close it.

3. **Landmark depth and day/night mood — F01–F04, strongest in F02/F04.** The key art uses varied landform silhouettes and warm settlement lights within a genuinely dark blue night. Palworld 04 organizes a layered vista around a distinct distant landmark. These frames repeat similar cliff columns across the horizon and wash the night distance pale. Composition, landform grouping, sky, lighting, and haze are actionable scene changes; a stronger landmark may need additional art if suitable assets are unavailable.

**A — Belongs to the key-art world: No.** Natural colors, stone architecture, and a stylized traveler are compatible ingredients, but the bare lawn-like plateau, repetitive horizon, and bright washed-out night do not carry the board's inviting inhabited landscape or mysterious nighttime mood. This judgment concerns palette, silhouette, composition, and mood, not painted detail. Most observed environmental gaps are addressable through scene composition and lighting. Distinctive cast appeal remains an art question beyond what staging alone can resolve.

**B — Trying to be the same kind of game as Palworld: Yes, with limited evidence.** A third-person stylized traveler, traversable natural terrain, ruins, a quest, and five party slots communicate the broad adventure structure. The absent visible creatures make the creature-adventure identity much weaker than the references, but do not make it a different genre. This is a same-genre judgment only.

**Actual commercial visual-quality bar: No, for either pair.** The modest preference for F01/F02 does not address the empty habitat, weak cast presentation, repetitive cliffs, or incoherent night illumination. These supplied views do not stand beside the Palworld screenshots as comparable shipping-quality presentation. They also do not establish the quality of combat, creature animation, or the rest of the game.
