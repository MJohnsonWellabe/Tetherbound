# Blind visual verdict: Cloud Upland Upper 01

Inspected only neutral F01–F04 and the supplied Meadows key art plus all five Palworld reference images. F01/F02 is the first day/night pair; F03/F04 is the second. No implementation, history, provenance, other reports, or Godot execution informed this judgment.

**Preference: no meaningful overall preference.** F03 makes the clearing around the trainer and the right-hand ground less uniformly green through stronger mottling. In F04 that mottling becomes conspicuous cloudy patches without clearly describing landform. F01/F02 offers a calmer surface, but it reads flat. These are small competing benefits: the composition, grass structure, cloud masses, cliff repetition, trainer presentation, and interface remain essentially the same.

The trainer is recognizable, with a readable blue jacket and human proportions, but the dark daytime face and legs do not deliver the expressive character appeal of the Palworld references. There are no identifiable creatures in these frames. Creature appeal, creature-to-trainer scale, and combat spectacle therefore cannot receive a pass from this evidence.

## Eight criteria

1. **Silhouette and small-size readability.** In all four frames the human, cliff wall, and grassy clearing remain identifiable at reduced viewing size. The bright jacket carries the trainer; the dark hair, face, and trousers merge toward the shadowed ground in F01/F03. Grass becomes a uniform band of spiky visual noise. The large white or lavender cloud shapes attract attention before the trainer or a travel destination. The cropped cyan object at the left edge has no legible purpose from this framing.

2. **Colour and value structure.** F01/F03 use compatible green, ochre, grey, and blue families, but saturated grass dominates a comparatively muted trainer and rocks. The pure-looking white cloud bank breaks the natural value hierarchy. F02/F04 cool the foreground convincingly, yet retain a luminous pale sky and clouds. They read more like a cold overcast period than the displayed 23:00. F03/F04 ground variation is visible, but does not create stronger focal hierarchy. No visible oxblood misuse can be identified.

3. **Intentionality.** The central clearing and left rising path provide an understandable route composition in all frames. However, the tall grasses have consistently sharp, oversized blades and a largely continuous density band. Sparse small trees dot the ramp, while the background is a long run of similarly proportioned vertical rock stacks. There is little visible habitation, wildlife, or distinct destination to reward looking beyond the clearing. The Palworld plateau reference organizes varied vegetation, architecture, and creatures around the route.

4. **Lighting.** F01/F03 have distinguishable lit and shaded rock faces and directional ground shadow, so terrain is not wholly flat-lit. The trainer is nevertheless dim in daylight and unusually bright relative to the ground in F02/F04. The night sky remains too bright to establish the key art's mysterious nocturnal mood. Grass tips stay pale and busy at night. Still images cannot establish how these lighting relationships behave during movement.

5. **Horizon and depth.** Rock overlaps and cooler distant cliffs supply depth in all frames, particularly F02/F04. But the broad smooth cloud mounds and oval, dish-like central formation interrupt that depth: they look like solid surfaces inserted between cliffs. The repeated upright cliff forms create a wall rather than a varied sequence of landmarks. No definite terrain chunk or LOD seam is identifiable in these stills.

6. **Interface.** The quest text is readable and has safe edge spacing, but its large upper-right panel obscures the vista. The two large bottom-right bands cover substantial ground. Empty numbered creature slots and tiny input glyphs convey little at reduced size; the bottom-left health and food labels lose contrast over busy grass. The minimap is mostly a blurred colour field with a conspicuous arrow. These observations concern the submitted interface itself, not a comparison with Palworld UI design.

7. **Artefacts.** The hard-edged white cloud masses in F01/F03 and lavender domes in F02/F04 read as unfinished geometry. The central oval cloud has an especially abrupt rim and exposed-looking dark shape below it. Repeating horizontal rock bands are conspicuous across the far wall. F04's clearing has coarse mottling that reads as surface texture rather than terrain form. There is no defensible evidence here of animated popping or z-fighting.

8. **Scale agreement.** Against the 1.80 m trainer, the surrounding grass reaches approximately thigh to waist height and has unusually broad individual blades; it competes with the character rather than furnishing the ground. The huge cliffs are plausible fantasy geography, though the very small trees on the rising ramp do little to articulate their scale. Perspective prevents a precise comparison between those distant trees and the trainer. No creature scale comparison is possible.

## Ranked reference gaps

1. **Cloud and terrain composition — F01–F04.** The key art and Palworld plateau image use irregular, layered silhouettes and atmospheric transitions to invite exploration. These frames put smooth cloud solids before a repetitive cliff wall, making the primary vista feel constructed and unfinished.
2. **Ground and vegetation treatment — F01–F04.** Palworld's field and plateau images combine fine ground cover, readable bare routes, and varied vegetation masses. These frames have giant crossing blades over either flat ground (F01/F02) or conspicuous mottling (F03/F04). Density alone does not create the reference's coherence.
3. **Character appeal and inhabited-world evidence — F01–F04.** The Palworld references visibly center expressive creatures and readable characters within activities or destinations. These frames show a small, subdued trainer, no identifiable companions or wildlife, and little reason to explore the visible terrain. Combat quality remains unassessed because no fight is shown.

## Acceptance answers

**A — Belongs to the key-art world: No.** The natural daytime palette and adventurous cliffs are compatible ingredients, but the harsh grass, solid-looking clouds, repeated landforms, and bright night sky do not carry its inviting daytime and mysterious nighttime mood. This judgment concerns composition, palette, silhouette, and atmosphere, not painted detail or a requirement that this location replicate a meadow.

**B — Trying to be the same kind of game as Palworld: Yes.** A stylized human in an expansive, traversable fantasy landscape, with a quest and visible team-slot interface, reads as the same broad exploration-adventure ambition. This is genre recognition, not evidence of equivalent execution; the central creature experience is absent from these images.

**Actual commercial visual quality against the supplied Palworld bar: No, for both pairs.** Neither pair makes the vista, vegetation, trainer presentation, and nighttime atmosphere look comparably finished.

Scene-addressable work includes ground/grass proportion and clustering, lighting and night values, cliff silhouette composition, cloud placement, stronger route destinations, character framing, and interface coverage. The clouds also need a more convincing rendered form, and the foliage needs less crude blade shapes/material treatment if placement cannot solve their appearance. Those are visible asset/presentation requirements; a blind frame cannot establish whether suitable alternatives already exist in the build. These images do not justify a verdict that the trainer needs replacement art, and they provide no basis for specifying missing creature assets.
