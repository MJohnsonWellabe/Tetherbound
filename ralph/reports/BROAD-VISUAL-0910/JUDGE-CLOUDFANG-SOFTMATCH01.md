# Blind visual verdict: X / Y

Judged only X1, X2, Y1 and Y2 against `docs/reference/tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png` and all five `palworld-0*.jpg` references. X1/Y1 are close views; X2/Y2 are distant views. These are isolated frontal creature fixtures on a uniform blue background, not world or gameplay evidence. No source, provenance, other verdicts or implementation history informed the comparison.

**Preference: Y, narrowly, for surface coherence in the close view.** Y1 has cleaner pale forelegs and cheek/ruff surfaces. X1 carries more blue-grey mottling across those areas, breaking otherwise simple volumes into smaller patches. Y1 consequently gives the face a slightly quieter surrounding field. Both already have clearly separated blue eyes, a dark nose and a readable muzzle; this is not a newly readable face. The blue shoulder accents remain strong in both. Silhouette is a tie. At X2/Y2 distance the practical preference is essentially a tie: the white body, upright ears and blue flank accents dominate, and the close-view surface improvement is slight.

## Eight rubric dimensions

1. **Silhouette and small-size readability.** X1/Y1 present an immediately identifiable pointed-ear canine with a broad ruff and separated front paws. X2/Y2 retain that same outline; neither shows a meaningful silhouette advantage. At sheet-like reduced size the eyes cease to carry much expression, while the ears, white head and blue side accents survive. The frontal pose does not establish side silhouette, tail design or readability against terrain.
2. **Colour and value structure.** All four frames separate a pale creature from the muted blue background, with dark nose/eye sockets and saturated blue accents. Y1 keeps larger clean pale areas on the legs and cheeks; X1's scattered blue-grey patches compete more with form shading. The forehead remains visibly mottled in both. Natural-world palette integration and reserved danger colours cannot be judged from this fixture.
3. **Intentionality.** The blue ears, eyes and lateral ruff accents form a consistent creature palette in X1/Y1. Y1's cleaner legs look more deliberately grouped. Both retain a busy, serrated stack of chest tufts, with abrupt dark breaks between pale pieces; that complexity competes with the comparatively simple facial features. The close-view improvement does not change the underlying character design. Environment authorship and scatter are absent.
4. **Lighting.** X1/Y1 show enough light-dark variation to describe muzzle, ruff and legs. All four have a soft ground shadow. That shadow indicates contact, although the untextured background offers little other spatial support. There is no persuasive time-of-day cue. Y's cleaner surfaces should not be mistaken for evidence of a different lighting setup.
5. **Horizon and depth.** Not assessable: every frame has a uniform background, no horizon, terrain, fog layers or landmarks. The distant pair establishes screen-size readability only. No depth or LOD improvement can be claimed.
6. **Interface.** Not assessable: no HUD, menus, text or safe-area content is shown. No comparison with Palworld's interface is made.
7. **Artefacts.** X1's small mottled patches and both close views' hard-edged chest-tuft breaks read as surface busyness, but the stills do not establish whether they are texture, shading or geometry defects. Y1 reduces the patchiness without eliminating the jagged layered appearance. There is no clearly demonstrated z-fighting or major intersection. Popping and motion stability are untested.
8. **Scale agreement.** Not assessable: no trainer, other species or metric reference appears. Camera distance explains the image-size difference; it cannot establish world size or compliance with trainer-relative scale.

## Three largest gaps from the references

1. **Simplified, coherent creature forms.** In `palworld-01` the boss's face and large foliage plates remain individually readable; `palworld-03` carries a creature through broad uninterrupted purple and cream masses. X1/Y1 use many smaller pale chest facets and dark creases that compete with the face. Y improves surface grouping modestly but leaves this larger gap.
2. **Expressive presentation.** The Palworld creatures have readable expressions and action poses, particularly the open mouth and forward gesture in `palworld-01`. X1/Y1 show a neutral, nearly symmetrical frontal stance. The existing eyes and muzzle are legible, but these fixtures do not demonstrate comparable expression, movement or personality. Pose/camera/expression evidence could address this; these stills do not prove that replacement art is required.
3. **Integration into a world.** The key art and Palworld references place creatures beside terrain, foliage, people and depth layers. X1/X2/Y1/Y2 offer none of those relationships. This is a capture limitation, not evidence that the unseen world is empty. An in-world view with terrain contrast and a trainer is required to resolve it.

## Reference gates

**A — Key-art direction: Yes, narrowly for the creature's compatibility with that direction.** The recognizable animal silhouette, pale natural base and concentrated cool fantasy accents could belong within the key art's stylized world. This is not a pass for world composition, Meadows mood or landscape integration; those are not shown.

**B — Same kind of game as the genre references: Yes at the creature-design level.** A stylized fantasy canine with a prominent face and elemental colour accents fits the creature-adventure premise. **Substantial improvement toward those references: No.** Y offers a modest close-view cleanup, with little practical change in the distant view and no demonstrated change in expression, silhouette or scene integration.

**Commercial creature-art bar: No on the supplied evidence.** The repetitive, sharply segmented chest treatment in X1/Y1 and the limited expression demonstrated still fall short of the confident broad forms and character presentation in the reference creatures. Y is the better local treatment, not evidence that this bar has been crossed. Surface grouping is an art/material issue; whether the layered chest needs texture/shading revision or geometry revision cannot be decided from these images. Pose, camera and an in-world comparison can be addressed through presentation. Missing world evidence must not be converted into a demand for new assets.
