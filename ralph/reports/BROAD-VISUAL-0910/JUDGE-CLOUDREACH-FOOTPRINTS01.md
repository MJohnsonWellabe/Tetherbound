# Cloudreach Realm Gate Crag — blind visual judgment

Evidence inspected: `neutral-cloudreach-footprints01/F01.png` through `F04.png`, individually; the actual `docs/reference/tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`; all five `palworld-0*.jpg` images; supplementary `enshrouded-0.jpg` through `enshrouded-2.jpg` and `once-human-settlement.jpg`. No implementation, diffs, history, or other verdicts were inspected. Pair labels indicate comparison only, not chronology. This judgment covers this camera at the realm gate, not the whole biome.

The trainer is identifiable and has a coherent blue-and-brown adventure outfit, but its stiff, miniature-doll presentation does not yet deliver the expressive character presence of the Palworld references. No creature is identifiable closely enough to judge its design, appeal, or relative size. That absence leaves a creature-training game's central visual promise unproven here.

## Pair comparison: terrain separately from character and interface

**Terrain/grass coverage: no meaningful overall improvement established between the pairs.** F03/F04 have more visible growth against the tower base and a denser low clump at the left edge. This makes those specific edges less bare. F01/F02 instead retain conspicuous blades in the immediate foreground. Both pairs leave the same large lawn around and in front of the trainer, the same sparse grass belt behind them, and the same nearly uninterrupted flat green middle-distance shelf. Neither treatment changes the dominant read from a textured plane with scattered spikes into convincing vegetated terrain. F03/F04 have a modest advantage in wall-edge integration, not a scene-wide coverage gain.

The day comparison, F01 against F03, is especially useful: similar lighting exposes the unchanged size of the empty lawn and distant green shelf. The night comparison, F02 against F04, supports the same terrain conclusion despite the character-lighting difference.

**Character preference:** F02 is considerably more readable than F04 at night: the white shirt, face and blue jacket remain distinct. It also looks excessively illuminated relative to the ground. F04 integrates the trainer into the night palette more convincingly, but loses face, trousers and body separation. Neither resolves both integration and readability. F01/F03 show no material character-quality distinction.

**Interface preference:** no meaningful pairwise layout improvement is visible. The same large instruction banner, quest card and lower-right controls dominate both pairs. UI preference does not contribute to the terrain verdict.

## Eight criteria

1. **Silhouette and readability at small size.** In all frames, the tower, trainer and cliff wall survive a thumbnail read; distant trees become scattered dots, and the grass becomes a narrow noisy belt. F01/F03 separate the trainer adequately from the lawn. F04 loses the trainer's dark limbs into the ground, whereas F02 retains the outfit but reads unnaturally bright. No creature silhouette competes with the landscape, unlike Palworld 03/04. The right-hand tower is a strong mass but is cropped too tightly to reveal an identifying gate silhouette.

2. **Colour and value structure.** F01/F03 have useful sky/ground separation but a broad yellow-green foreground and dull green shelf dominate the palette. Sparse tiny trees do not create larger colour groupings. F02/F04 turn the terrain into dark teal while the sky remains pale enough to suggest overcast twilight rather than 23:00. Their distant cliffs merge into a light grey band. No conspicuous oxblood leakage is visible. The key art's warm daylight accents and deep blue night punctuated by warm habitation are absent.

3. **Intentionality.** F01–F04 place a trainer on a broad blank lawn beside a tower, with grass concentrated in a roughly lateral belt. The middle-distance trees are tiny and isolated; repeated cliff columns dominate without a sufficiently distinct destination. F03/F04 improve some tower-foot planting, but the smooth green clearing has no worn approach, rubble transition, or strong local use pattern to explain its boundaries. Palworld 02/04 define open ground with paths, banks and planting masses; the key art connects its buildings to paths and inhabited edges.

4. **Lighting.** F01/F03 have a strong cast shadow and readable stone relief, so objects are not uniformly flat-lit. Nevertheless, the foreground lawn is a large simple lit/shadow division with little terrain form. F02/F04 darken the ground without a convincing deep-night sky. F02's bright trainer looks detached; F04's trainer becomes difficult to read. The Enshrouded night reference uses selected cool highlights and luminous focal subjects; these frames provide no comparable local focal light. This is a visual distinction, not a demand for the same rendering technique.

5. **Horizon and depth.** All four establish elevation through the cliff wall, descending route and cloud-filled gap at right. The cliff row repeats similar rounded columns and pale material across much of the horizon, weakening landmark hierarchy. Night fog in F02/F04 flattens the far wall into a nearly continuous silhouette. Enshrouded 0 separates overlapping ridges and distinctive towers; Palworld 04 has a clear distant spire. Here the visible road is thin and the destination is not a strong visual reward. No definite chunk seam can be established from these stills.

6. **Interface.** All frames keep controls within the image and use readable major text. However, the broad upper banner, large quest card, empty numbered slots and wide command strip consume substantial view area. The main-story text wraps into short fragments, while the top instruction repeats related navigation guidance. The minimap looks blurred and offers little immediately readable route context. Tiny keycap glyphs lose usefulness before the text does at reduced size. This is an internal hierarchy judgment; Palworld UI is not used as a scoring reference.

7. **Artefacts.** All frames contain thin dark dotted vertical marks in the upper-left sky that read as stray rendering marks. Grass blades are visibly angular and needle-like, especially the close foreground blades in F01/F02 and bright night belt in F02/F04. The ground's repeated mottled texture is exposed over large empty areas. The sky has harsh flattened white cloud edges. These are visible defects in presentation; their implementation cause is unknown. Still images cannot establish popping or motion aliasing.

8. **Scale agreement.** Against the 1.80 m trainer, the tower's large masonry blocks are exaggerated but plausible for monumental construction. Nearby grass varies from low tufts to blades approaching the trainer's knee or beyond; the tallest close blades lack enough intermediate vegetation to feel naturally embedded. The distant trees' depth is uncertain, so their true size cannot be measured reliably. No nearby creature is available for the required human/creature scale comparison. This criterion is partially unverified, not passed by absence.

## Three ranked gaps from the references

1. **Ground structure and inhabited transitions — all frames.** Palworld 02/04/05 and the key art make clearings legible through paths, layered vegetation and purposeful building edges. Once Human's settlement reference likewise gives its open courtyard a distinct surface, planted margins and enclosure. F01–F04 expose a large undifferentiated green lawn and middle shelf, with a grass belt doing most of the environmental work. Address the visible open-ground composition and material transitions; simply moving isolated blades does not close this gap.

2. **Character and creature presence — all frames, particularly F04.** Palworld's trainer and creatures have expressive silhouettes, purposeful poses and complementary colour masses. These frames show a small stiff trainer and no legible companion or encounter. F04 further suppresses the only character's readability. Framing, staging and lighting can improve presence; richer silhouette/pose quality may require character-art or animation work. Screenshots do not establish what suitable assets already exist elsewhere in the build.

3. **Distinctive depth and night mood — all frames, especially F02/F04.** The key art and Palworld 04 organize the view around recognizable landmarks and layered terrain; Enshrouded 0 reinforces this with ridge separation and focal structures. Here a repeated cliff wall and pale night horizon offer limited hierarchy. The night images lack the key art's dark blue sky and warm signs of habitation. Scene composition, atmospheric values and lighting can address much of this. More distinctive landmark silhouettes may also need art work if the existing scene pieces cannot provide them.

## Direct bar answers

**A. Do these frames belong to the key-art world? No.** They share green terrain, blue sky and stone architecture, but do not carry the board's authored natural layering, inhabited approaches, inviting daylight or mysterious inhabited night. This is a palette, composition and mood judgment, not a demand for painted fidelity. The main failures are scene-addressable: terrain transitions, clustered planting, local storytelling and lighting. Character expressiveness and distinctive architectural silhouettes may require art beyond scene tuning; the available inventory is outside this blind review.

**B. Are they trying to be the same kind of game as Palworld? Yes.** The stylized adventurer, open cliff landscape, traversal road and monumental ruins plausibly belong to the same broad third-person fantasy exploration genre. That is a genre answer only. The absence of a legible creature makes the creature-collecting identity much weaker than the reference set.

**Commercial visual quality beside Palworld: not yet.** Bare terrain coverage, weak character staging and limited environmental hierarchy remain evident in both pairs. Neither pair closes those gaps. No performance, combat-event quality, animation, or whole-biome conclusion can be drawn from these four stationary gate views.
