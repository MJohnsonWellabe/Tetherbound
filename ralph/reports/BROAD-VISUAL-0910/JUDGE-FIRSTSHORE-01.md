# First Shore: independent image review

Evidence: neutral-firstshore-01/F01.png–F04.png only, compared with `docs/reference/tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png` and all five `palworld-0*.jpg` references. F01/F02 are one day/night pair; F03/F04 are the other. No supplementary references used. No source, change history or earlier verdict consulted for this comparison. No scores.

**The creature cast is not readable enough to carry either pair.** The trainer has an identifiable backpack, blue jacket and boots, but the view is from behind and cannot establish facial appeal or expressiveness. The tiny blue/pale form near the left shore cannot be confidently classified at this distance. There is no clearly readable companion or encounter. Palworld's references make distinct creatures substantial compositional subjects; these frames primarily show unoccupied terrain. This is an evidence limitation for individual creature art quality, and a visible failure of creature presence in this view.

**Weak preference: F01/F02.** Their smaller cliff pattern is less conspicuously oversized than the broad angular patches in F03/F04. F03's somewhat smoother-looking ground does not add useful terrain form; its right slope resolves into conspicuous horizontal bands. Neither pair provides a substantial improvement in habitat, composition or cast. This preference is about the supplied images, not chronology or implementation.

## Full rubric

1. **Silhouette and small-size readability.** Across F01–F04, the trainer, central-right boulder pile, coastline and sky survive a reduced-size reading. The trainer's pale collar and blue upper body separate better than the dark legs, particularly in F02/F04. Small plants collapse into dark specks or coloured flecks, and the distant shore offers no readily identifiable creature silhouette. The boulder pile is readable but is the strongest nearby environmental subject almost by default. F03/F04 do not improve this hierarchy.

2. **Colour and value.** F01/F03 share yellow-green ground, pale grey cliffs and a muted blue sky; they plainly belong to the same location. The broad pale cliff cuts the frame more strongly than any habitat feature. F03 makes that face lighter and its individual patches larger, increasing the pale cut-out impression. F02/F04 establish night through blue sky and cool cliffs, but dark ground mottling competes with the trainer's lower body. No visible oxblood leakage can be identified. The key art uses related natural hues, but allocates them to coherent meadow, canopy, water and landmark masses; these frames concentrate variation into the ground surface.

3. **Intentionality, grain and repetition.** In all four frames, the near ground resembles a mottled covering with insufficient upright grass or plant edges to explain it as vegetation. Cliff faces carry repeated polygonal islands rather than readable strata, broken ledges or weathered masses. F01/F02's fine pattern is busy; F03/F04 enlarge it without supplying corresponding silhouette structure. F03 also exposes repeated slope-aligned horizontal streaks on the right. Bushes congregate near the upper cliff lip, which is a useful start, but much of the remaining slope and far shore is empty between isolated small accents. The frame lacks a legible arrival path, landing place or other authored shore activity.

4. **Lighting.** F01/F03's trainer shadow grounds the character and establishes directional daylight. The boulders have differentiated light and dark faces. Most terrain modelling nevertheless comes from the cliff outline and surface pattern, with little broad light/shadow organisation across the grassy slope. F02/F04 are clearly night, but present largely the same view cooled and darkened, without a visible warm destination or distinctive nocturnal focal point. The key-art night panel supplies both layered blue terrain and a small warm inhabited destination; that compositional mood is absent here.

5. **Horizon and depth.** The near cliff, middle shore and far water establish basic distance in every frame. The horizon is almost empty and the water is a large low-detail pale/blue plane. There is no distant landmark to reward looking outward, unlike Palworld-04's tower and layered rock silhouettes. The abrupt straight edge across the water/sky region is visually stark, but these stills do not establish a technical seam or its cause. No LOD behaviour can be judged.

6. **Interface.** The location and main-story goal are readable in all frames. The large right-hand quest panel and lower five-slot panel occupy substantial terrain area; the slots show only tiny key labels and communicate little at a glance. Bottom-left food text is weaker than the quest text, and the top time readout is low-contrast against both skies. Elements remain within the visible frame. This is an intrinsic legibility assessment, not a Palworld UI comparison.

7. **Artefacts.** All frames have a sharp, locally stair-stepped grass/cliff boundary that can resemble a cut edge rather than an eroded shore. The cliff pattern is especially coarse and tessellated-looking in F03/F04. F03's grass bands are another visible regression in natural surface reading. These are image-level defects, not diagnoses of shaders or geometry. No definite z-fighting or character intersection is visible. Motion-dependent popping and flicker are untested.

8. **Scale agreement.** Using the trainer as the stipulated 1.80 m ruler, the near slope and central-right boulders can plausibly occupy a human-scale landscape; perspective prevents exact measurement of the more distant pile. F03/F04's cliff patches read as very large surface cells relative to the person, without correspondingly large rock relief, which weakens material scale agreement. Far trees are too distant for dependable height comparison. No identifiable creature sharing a useful depth plane with the trainer is present, so creature/trainer scale compliance cannot be established. It would be unsupported to infer species sizes from the shore speck.

## Ranked three gaps

1. **Readable cast and habitat event — F01–F04.** Palworld-02/03/04 place distinguishable creatures along the player's view, with silhouettes, colours and surrounding space making them the reason to look. Here the trainer faces essentially vacant ground. Compose a visible creature group and an intelligible shore activity area. Scene placement can address absence; these images cannot establish whether suitable expressive creature art is available.
2. **Ground and cliff form instead of surface noise — F01–F04, especially F03/F04's enlarged cliff cells and F03's bands.** Palworld-02/04 organise rock into ledges and masses and grass into a readable cover with edges. Both submitted pairs rely on conspicuous surface pattern across broad bare shapes. Improve cliff massing, material scale, transitions and clustered vegetation; material/art work may be needed if existing surfaces cannot support this treatment, but asset availability is not visible.
3. **Authored shore destination and layered outlook — F01–F04.** The key art's river scenes and Palworld-04/05 use paths, structures, canopy and landmarks to convey habitation and depth. These shores have isolated accents and an empty horizon. Compose a shore route or arrival feature, stronger vegetation groups and a distant landmark; provide a night focal point in F02/F04. This is principally scene, lighting and composition work. Stills cannot prove whether the necessary prop art exists in the build.
