# FirstShore ground and vegetation — blind visual verdict

Judged the supplied F01, F02, F03 and F04 images in `.artifacts/broad-visual-0910/neutral-firstshore-grass02/` against the actual `docs/reference/tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png` and all five `palworld-0*.jpg` images. The supplied Enshrouded image is a secondary reference for environmental layering only. No implementation, prior verdicts, or change history informed this assessment. This is a still-image judgment; it establishes neither performance nor motion quality. No numerical scores.

## Relative preference and grass reading

I narrowly prefer F01 over F03, and F02 over F04, for this hillside's ground treatment. F01/F02 give the foreground visible vegetation volume and a stronger distinction between the grassy shelf and bare slope. This is a relative preference, not acceptance of the grass asset or scatter. F03/F04 make the trainer's feet and ground contact clearer, but the exposed ground reads as a broad mottled surface rather than a convincing meadow clearing.

F01/F02 are recognizable as grass-like vegetation in context. Close to the camera, however, the dominant shapes are long, straight, pale, pointed strips with limited visible bending or tuft structure. They suggest stiff reeds or crop-like spikes more than soft meadow grass. Their fairly consistent upright gesture and near-continuous coverage form a bristly field. Palworld's open-path, meadow and base references have more connected, leafy tufts, variable edges, and localized ground exposure. Merely covering the terrain with more blades does not close that difference.

F01/F02 preserve some visible soil/ground between stems, but the transition from this tall cover to the bare cliff is abrupt. The grass does little to integrate the cliff, nearby shrubs, or rock bases. F03/F04 show small distant sprigs that disappear into fine noise; the large foreground clearing has no convincing trampled path or composed edge to explain its shape. A useful direction is the denser pair's volume with more natural tuft silhouettes, softer grouped edges, purposeful clearings, and better base integration—not simply more coverage.

## Full rubric

### Silhouette and readability at small size

The trainer remains the clearest subject in every frame through the blue jacket, pale collar and brown pack. The rear-view character is readable, but these frames do not demonstrate Palworld's strong character expression or an appealing creature partnership. The tiny distant figure near the left shore is too small to judge creature design or proportions. There is no sufficiently visible companion or encounter to assess the creature-art bar.

At overview scale, the angular rock cluster and shrubs remain distinguishable in F01–F04, but the slender distant trees become sparse specks. F01/F02's pale blade field competes with the trainer's lower silhouette; F02 is particularly busy around the legs. F03/F04 make the silhouette cleaner but leave little else of interest on the near ground. The frames communicate a person on a grassy coast more strongly than a populated creature adventure.

### Colour and value structure

F01/F03 have a coherent green, grey and blue coastal palette, but the foreground's yellow-green mottling and the large pale-grey cliff consume much of the image's visual emphasis. The sky and water are washed out relative to the saturated ground. F01 adds a conspicuous mint-white grass-tip layer that does not feel integrated with the warmer ground beneath it.

F02/F04 read immediately as night through the blue sky and darker land. F02's cool pale stems carry considerable contrast across the entire foreground. F04 is quieter but collapses much of the land into dark mottling. The key art separates night silhouettes and distant destinations with more deliberate cool/warm accents; these frames have no comparable destination light. No visible evidence establishes an oxblood danger-colour leak.

### Intentionality

F01/F02 look scattered rather than gardened by terrain or habitat: similar upright stems recur across a large uninterrupted band, while shrubs form isolated clumps beside an exposed slope. F03/F04 replace that band near the camera with an expansive unstructured clearing. Neither pair offers the reference images' clear progression from travelled ground through low vegetation to shrubs and trees. The exposed cliff is a long, unusually clean strip, and its upper rim is insufficiently broken by roots, ledges or vegetation groupings.

### Lighting

Day and night are unambiguous in the paired frames. F03 gives the most legible trainer cast shadow, which helps place the feet. F01 has dimensional grass, but its bright strips and dark bases dominate more than coherent lighting across the hillside. The big cliff face stays comparatively flat in both day and night; its mottled surface does not convincingly describe ledges or weathering. F02/F04 convey a cool night, but lack localized pools of light or a distant illuminated landmark to turn that change into mystery or invitation.

### Horizon and depth

All four frames distinguish near grass, a descending cliff, water, an opposite land strip, and a further hill. This is genuine broad spatial structure. It is weakened by a very flat sea horizon, thinly populated distant land, and a sky occupying a large part of the view without a strong landmark. The near cliff and further pale slope share so much material character that intermediate depth reads weakly.

Palworld's plateau reference overlaps trees, architecture, cliffs and distant mountains into a readable destination. The Enshrouded image likewise uses foreground foliage framing, intermediate rock shelves and repeated distant silhouettes, with atmosphere separating them. FirstShore does not need that image's dramatic mountains or orange light; it needs comparable transitions and overlap within its own coastal vocabulary. No temporal LOD claim can be made from these stills.

### Interface

The objective, location label and main controls are readable and remain inside the frame. The objective panel has strong prominence. In every frame the large empty creature-slot panel and long action strip cover a substantial part of the lower-right environment while conveying little current content. Small white keycap marks are much less legible than their labels. The time text has weaker contrast against the sky than the location label. This is assessed independently; the Palworld interface is not the benchmark here.

### Artefacts

F01/F02 show coarse pointed blade silhouettes and a sparkling-looking field of very fine distant stems in the still image; motion shimmer is unverified. F03/F04 expose coarse, enlarged ground mottling. Every frame shows a conspicuous polygonal/mottled cliff treatment and hard vegetation-to-bare-ground boundaries. These look unfinished, though screenshots alone cannot establish whether the cause is a bug or an asset choice. F02/F04's dark cloud fragments have harsh, torn-looking edges. There is no clear still-image evidence of z-fighting or floating objects.

### Scale agreement

Using the trainer as the supplied human-height ruler, F01/F02's nearby grass reaches roughly shin-to-knee height and occasionally reads taller in the immediate foreground. Tall grass is plausible, but applying that upright height character so broadly makes the shelf resemble a coarse planted field. The rocks plausibly stand several human heights; small shrubs beside them are not inherently inconsistent. Distant trees cannot be compared reliably without depth information. The trainer is not visibly disproportionate to nearby objects. Creature relative scale cannot be judged from the tiny distant figure, and no companion-versus-practice-target comparison is available.

## Largest reference gaps, ranked

- **Ground and vegetation integration.** F01/F02 substitute a continuous field of pale upright spikes for the references' varied, leafy, layered ground cover. F03/F04 expose a flat-looking mottled carpet. Palworld's path and base images make bare ground and vegetation form believable connected edges; neither FirstShore pair does.
- **Terrain composition and destination.** F01–F04 are dominated by one long bare cliff band, open sky, and lightly dressed distant terrain. The key art and Palworld plateau compose paths, tree groups, stone forms and landmarks into an inviting route. The secondary Enshrouded comparison reinforces this layering gap.
- **Light and material hierarchy.** F01/F03's pale cliff and high-contrast ground compete with the player; F02/F04 mainly cool and darken the same arrangement. The references use stronger form-defining shade, coherent vegetation values, and spatially selected accents. FirstShore lacks that deliberate distribution of attention.

## Binding bars

**A — Belonging to the Meadows key-art world: No.** There is a compatible starting palette and an understandable day/night shift, but the stiff vegetation, bare cliff strip, sparse tree structure and weak destination composition do not yet convey the board's inviting, layered natural landscape. This is a palette, composition, silhouette and mood judgment, not a demand for painted per-leaf fidelity.

**B — Trying to be the same kind of game as the Palworld screenshots: Yes.** A stylized third-person adventurer in open natural terrain, an exploration objective and a creature-party interface establish the same broad kind of game. The vegetation differences do not erase that genre relationship. The actual creature experience is underrepresented in this view.

**Commercial visual quality beside the Palworld screenshots: No.** The recognizable genre does not establish comparable finish. Both FirstShore pairs remain substantially behind the supplied real-time references in foliage shape, terrain integration, spatial layering and authored interest. The denser pair is a modestly stronger environmental starting point, not a commercial-quality pass.

**Scene-fixable gaps:** group cover around terrain and rock bases, vary heights and clearings purposefully, improve cliff/grass transitions, compose overlapping shrubs and trees, provide a readable coastal destination, reduce ground/grass-tip competition with the character, and give night selective focal light. These are visible targets, not assumptions about available controls.

**Art-dependent gaps:** the close blade silhouettes need more convincing leafy/tuft form if existing assets cannot supply it; the cliff and exposed ground need materials/forms that retain readable surface structure at this distance. Scatter alone cannot repair those shapes and surfaces. A rear-view trainer and tiny distant creature are insufficient evidence to require replacement character/creature art. This code-blind review cannot determine which alternatives are already installed, so it does not claim that purchases or new assets are necessarily required.
