# Blind visual judgment — First Shore, F01–F04

Viewed F01.png, F02.png, F03.png and F04.png at `.artifacts/broad-visual-0910/neutral-firstshore-grass03/`, the actual `docs/reference/tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`, and all five actual Palworld JPG references: boss fight forest, open field path, field boss meadow, plateau landmark and base building. This judgment uses the images only. These are one location in daylight (F01/F03) and at night (F02/F04); they do not establish the quality of a route or biome. No numeric scores.

The trainer is identifiable and reasonably coherent with the stylized world, but these rear views do not demonstrate character appeal comparable with the Palworld references. The small, distant forms near the left shoreline cannot establish creature quality, personality or relative scale. The creature-led identity so prominent in Palworld's forest fight, meadow boss, plateau and base shots is absent from this composition. That is a framing/content finding, not proof that unseen creature art is inadequate.

## Separate relative preferences

- **Grass and ground:** No convincing winner between F01 and F03, or between F02 and F04. The visible differences in fine blade pattern/brightness do not change the dominant impression. All four have a dense mat of tall, straight, pale, pointed strips over mottled green ground, alongside a very broad exposed gray bank. Daylight makes the material problems easiest to see; darkness conceals some without resolving them.
- **Character legibility:** No meaningful preference between the two daylight frames or the two night frames. Daylight F01/F03 is preferable to night F02/F04 for reading the whole trainer. At night the pale collar and forearms survive, but the dark head, trousers and feet blend into the terrain. This preference does not imply better grass.
- **UI:** F01/F02 is preferable because the shorter five-slot panel occupies less world area while conveying the same visibly empty slots and number labels. F03/F04 devotes substantially more height to blank cards. This UI advantage is separate from the ground and character judgments.

## Eight-part rubric

### 1. Silhouette and readability at small size

In all four frames the trainer, the large rock cluster and distant trees remain classifiable when mentally reduced to contact-sheet size. The backpack and blue upper garment give the trainer a recognizable central shape in F01/F03. The foreground vegetation loses individual form and becomes a scratchy field. The lower legs compete with bright vertical blades in daylight and nearly disappear into dark ground in F02/F04. The right hillside contains no strong creature silhouette or destination to balance the centered trainer. Palworld 03/04 makes creatures substantial, distinct visual subjects even at these references' modest image sizes.

### 2. Colour and value structure

F01/F03 combines a pale cyan-gray sky, a washed-out light-gray bank, and yellow-green ground covered in lighter mint grass edges. This makes the environment's largest and busiest surfaces compete with the trainer. The palette belongs broadly to green countryside, but misses the key art's stronger separation between warm paths, shaded foliage and blue distance. F02/F04 reads consistently as the same place in blue night light; however, much of the ground collapses into dark blue-green while the bank remains a large conspicuously pale mass. No visible evidence establishes oxblood leaking onto friendly elements.

### 3. Intentionality

**The vegetation reads as stiff reeds/spikes, not natural clustered meadow grass.** This is clearest across the lower foreground and the right slope in F01/F03: numerous long, narrow triangles hold similar upright directions, with isolated stalks distributed across nearly every available patch. Small gaps exist, but they do not create convincing tuft centers, soft grass masses, broad clearings or a progression from short turf to taller margins. F02/F04 preserves the same needle-like silhouette in blue. Palworld 02/03/05 uses varied grassy edges and more coherent ground masses around usable open areas; its meadow does not depend on a uniform field of tall lines. Here the grass abruptly meets the barren gray bank rather than building a convincing soil/stone/vegetation transition. The scattered shrubs and tiny distant trees offer little authored framing.

### 4. Lighting

Day and night are immediately distinguishable. F01/F03 provides some trainer volume and dark vegetation bases, but the huge gray bank and pale sky make the daylight scene feel flat and exposed. The boulders have stronger readable planes than the adjacent terrain. F02/F04 gives the rocks and bank recognizable cool illumination, but the trainer's dark lower half lacks separation and the scene has no warm visual anchor. The key art's night panel retains layered silhouettes and a warm settlement destination; the supplied night view supplies neither. Still images cannot establish shadow stability or lighting in motion.

### 5. Horizon and depth

The shoreline, intervening bank, ridge and pale sea establish near/middle/far layering in all four frames. Distance is understandable. The horizon is nevertheless a largely empty horizontal strip, with small isolated trees and no compelling far landmark. F01/F03 has a conspicuous bright band along the left water horizon; F02/F04 retains a thin straight boundary between sea and sky. These read as simplified layers rather than the rich distant landforms seen in Palworld 04 or the key art. The broad angular bank dominates the middle distance instead of directing attention toward something worth approaching. No temporal LOD claim is possible from these stills.

### 6. Interface

All four show a readable objective and location title with adequate edge clearance. The upper-right objective panel is large for its short message; together with the minimap it covers much of the hillside. The lower action strip is another broad dark rectangle. The lower-left health and food text has subdued contrast against translucent green/brown panels, and the very small key icons require close inspection. F03/F04's enlarged empty creature cards consume more scene area without adding visible information; F01/F02's compact panel is the clear relative preference. Interface is judged on its own hierarchy and legibility, not against Palworld UI.

### 7. Artefacts

F01/F03 exposes a repeated angular, patchwork surface across the gray bank; F02/F04 retains it under blue light. It reads as a coarse artificial surface pattern rather than convincing rock or earth. The transition from green hillside to exposed bank is conspicuously hard. Across all four, many foreground grass strips appear rigid and nearly edge-on, producing visual spears. The flattened cloud streaks become especially black and cutout-like at night. These are visible material/shape problems; the images alone do not establish z-fighting, animation defects, popping, or collision errors.

### 8. Scale agreement

Using the trainer as the 1.80 m ruler, much nearby grass reaches the shins/knees, with prominent blades approaching higher on the legs. Tall meadow vegetation is plausible, but making this height the dominant foreground texture reinforces the reed-field reading. The distant boulders can plausibly be several metres across; perspective does not expose an obvious size contradiction. Tiny distant trees and shoreline forms cannot be reliably measured against the trainer. No close creature is available to test companion/training-opponent scale, and no relative creature-scale pass should be claimed.

## The three largest reference gaps, ranked

1. **Ground and vegetation construction — all frames, clearest in F01/F03.** Palworld 02/03/05 combines softer grass masses, open ground and varied transitions. These frames substitute dense rigid spikes and a conspicuous bare polygon-patterned bank. More stalks will not address the shape language or the bank.
2. **A world organized around memorable subjects — all frames.** Palworld 01/03 gives the creature immediate expressive dominance; 04 supplies a landmark and distinct companion silhouettes; 05 has tangible inhabited activity. This view offers a distant rock cluster, tiny scattered vegetation and a largely empty horizon. The key art similarly gives roads, settlements and landforms a clear compositional role.
3. **Light, value and material cohesion — daylight F01/F03 and night F02/F04.** The key art's natural warm/cool separation and Palworld's grounded terrain make foreground subjects easier to read. Here pale spiky grass and gray terrain compete during the day, while night loses the trainer's lower silhouette and supplies no warm destination.

## Direct bar answers

**A. Do these frames read as belonging to the Meadows key-art world? No.** Green hills and a blue night establish a broad rural-fantasy connection, but the stiff vegetation, barren angular bank, sparse composition and missing welcoming/mysterious destinations overwhelm that connection. This is a palette, mood, silhouette and composition judgment, not a demand for painted per-leaf fidelity.

**B. Beside the Palworld references, would someone say these are trying to be the same kind of game? Yes.** A stylized third-person adventurer, outdoor exploration space and five creature slots communicate that intended genre. That answer concerns genre resemblance only.

**Do these frames meet the Palworld commercial visual-quality bar? No.** The ground/foliage treatment, environmental composition and presented character/creature appeal fall short of what the actual references demonstrate. No combat event is shown, so combat spectacle cannot be accepted or rejected from these images.

**Scene-fixable work:** Create distinct short-ground and taller-grass regions with deliberate clearings; soften and vary vegetation directions; improve the exposed-bank material and vegetation transition; frame a destination and visible creature subjects; separate trainer values at night; retain the compact slot panel and improve low-contrast HUD details. Existing assets may support some of this, but the images do not prove availability.

**Art-dependent work:** The dominant rigid blade silhouette may require better tuft geometry/material art if scatter adjustments preserve the same spear shapes. The bank needs a more coherent surface treatment, potentially new material art. These rear/distant views do not justify declaring that trainer or creature replacement is required: closer, meaningful character and creature frames are needed to identify such an art gap. The current presentation has not demonstrated the expressive creature-led appeal of the references.
