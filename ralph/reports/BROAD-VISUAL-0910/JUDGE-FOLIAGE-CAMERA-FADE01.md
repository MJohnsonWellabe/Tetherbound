# Blind visual verdict: F01–F08

Judged only the supplied neutral stills and the six binding images: `tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png` and `palworld-01` through `palworld-05`. No source, provenance, prior verdicts, or performance evidence was inspected. The paired views are F01/F05, F02/F06, F03/F07, and F04/F08. No scores are assigned. These conclusions apply to the pictured location and viewpoints, not the whole game.

The character and creature presentation falls short of the Palworld art bar. In every frame the trainer's blue shoulders remain identifiable, but the dark hair, backpack and trousers compress into the shaded ground. F08 finally supplies prominent animal silhouettes, yet the grey, relatively naturalistic wolf-like forms lack the expressive shapes and deliberate colour separation of Palworld's featured creatures. Their feet and lower bodies disappear into the tall yellow-green vegetation. Faces and animation cannot be judged here.

## Eight criteria

1. **Silhouette and readability at small size.** F01–F08 retain a legible human torso and a recognisable gateway, but the trainer's legs lose separation against the shaded soil. At thumbnail scale, the fine grass becomes busy striping rather than a coherent meadow surface. The near bush in F01/F05 is a large dark cutout; F02/F06 retain a similar obstruction on the right. F08's animals introduce useful recognisable silhouettes against the sky, though the leftmost one is cropped and their bodies are muted against the trees. Keep important actors clear of vegetation tangencies and provide stronger value separation around the trainer.

2. **Colour and value structure.** All eight frames belong to one palette: yellow-green hills, bright green crowns, rusty brown trunks, dark ochre foreground and a muted blue sky. The sunlit foliage is much more assertive than the trainer or animals. The jump from almost black foreground patches to vivid lime blades is harsher than the natural greens and warm, softly differentiated paths in the key art. Oxblood remains localised to the gateway banners; there is no visible broad leakage onto friendly scenery. Reduce the yellow/lime competition and bring the foreground shadows into a readable range.

3. **Intentionality.** The path and paired banners supply a deliberate destination in F02/F06 and F03/F07. The broader planting is less convincing: isolated tall blade clumps stand above mottled ground, and the ridge trees repeat similar narrow crown and trunk proportions. F04/F08 expose this especially clearly across the left hillside. F08's animals add activity but do not resolve the scatter's repetitive texture. More distinct plant masses, low cover and quiet clearings would help; adding the same blades everywhere would not.

4. **Lighting.** Daylight reads in every frame, and shadows place the trees and trainer on the ground. The exact morning hour is communicated mainly by the clock. Foreground shade in F01–F08 crushes ground and clothing detail while individual blades stay conspicuously bright. The nearby bushes in F01/F05 and F02/F06 read much flatter than the modelled sunlit tree crowns. Lift shadow readability and improve consistency between the foliage's light response and the surrounding scene.

5. **Horizon and depth.** The nearby dip, gate, ridge and rear plain give a usable depth sequence in F02/F06 and F03/F07. Beyond that, the distant terrain becomes a low, largely featureless strip. The extensive sky has no strong mountain, settlement or landmark counterweight of the sort carried by the key art and Palworld's plateau image. No definite chunk or LOD seam can be established in these stills. More distant landform hierarchy would make the route feel part of a larger authored world.

6. **Interface.** In all eight frames the objective is readily legible and the panels stay inside the image bounds. However, the large objective card, five empty numbered slots and broad command strip take substantial space from this landscape. The command lettering is stronger than the tiny slot key glyphs, while the clock and health/food text have weaker contrast. Empty slots communicate little beyond their existence. Improve hierarchy and reduce the occupied panel area without shrinking essential text. This is an intrinsic UI assessment, not a comparison to Palworld's UI.

7. **Artefacts.** F01–F08 show obvious long triangular blade shapes and noisy vegetation edges; the nearest soil is visibly blurry and mottled. F03/F07 and F04/F08 have blades overlapping the trainer's lower legs, obscuring the contact with the ground. The flat-looking near shrub leaf mass in F01/F05 exposes its constructed surface particularly strongly. No definite z-fighting is visible. These stills cannot establish popping, camera transition quality, motion aliasing or frame rate.

8. **Scale agreement.** Using the trainer as the 1.80 m reference, trees and the gateway broadly agree with human scale. Foreground grasses frequently reach shin or knee height, and the nearest shrubs are person-sized; that can be plausible, but these proportions overwhelm the route when repeated densely. F08's more distant wolf-like animals read as substantial creatures rather than tiny pets. Perspective and grass occlusion prevent a reliable numerical size comparison or a judgement about species roles. The provided views do not expose an obvious contradictory size hierarchy between creatures.

## Three largest reference gaps, ranked

1. **Actors do not lead the picture.** In F01–F07 the trainer is subdued and creature presence is too small to anchor the view. F08 adds animals but their grey bodies still merge with foliage. Palworld's creature images use distinctive proportions, expressive silhouettes and colour/value separation to make the creatures the reason to look.
2. **The ground lacks a cohesive meadow surface.** F01–F08 combine a mottled base with isolated, strongly visible grass triangles and sharp light/dark patches. The key art establishes continuous, varied vegetation masses; Palworld's field/path images maintain readable paths and coherent ground cover without every blade demanding attention.
3. **The scene does not open into a compelling larger place.** F02/F06 and F03/F07 offer a gateway but little meaningful silhouette beyond its ridge. The key art and Palworld plateau frame connect nearby routes to layered landforms and memorable distant destinations. F04/F08 are particularly dominated by sky and an undistinguished low hill.

## Separate bar answers

**A — Key-art world: Yes, at the broad palette and setting level.** Green rolling hills, meadow flowers, timber-and-stone construction, a rural path and restrained danger banners belong to the proposed Meadows world. The harsh shadow contrast and repetitive planting weaken its welcoming mood, but do not erase that relationship. This is not a claim of concept-art fidelity.

**B — Same kind of game as Palworld: Yes, narrowly.** A third-person adventurer, an explorable grassy landscape, an occupied gateway, creature slots and especially F08's large roaming animals communicate the broad adventure-with-creatures premise. These frames do not demonstrate a fight or its spectacle.

**Commercial visual quality beside the supplied Palworld stills: No.** The environmental materials and planting remain visibly rough, the trainer is poorly separated, and the featured animals do not show a comparable expressive art identity.

**Scene-adjustable work:** shadow/value balance; actor separation; grass height and clustering; route clearings; shrub placement; stronger distant composition; interface hierarchy. These are visible scene/presentation problems.

**Art-dependent gap:** the visible wolf-like creature design lacks the distinctive shape and colour language carried by the comparison's creatures, and the close ground/foliage surfaces expose coarse forms. Distinctive creature art and improved foliage/ground assets may be needed if placement and material changes cannot overcome those limits. A blind still review cannot establish what alternative art is already installed, and therefore cannot assert that new acquisition is required. Trainer face quality, creature expression, animation and combat presentation require closer or moving evidence.
