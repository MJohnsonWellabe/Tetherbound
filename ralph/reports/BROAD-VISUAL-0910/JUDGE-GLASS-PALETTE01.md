# Anonymous visual comparison — GLASS-PALETTE01

Evaluated F01–F04 against `tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png` and all five supplied `palworld-0*.jpg` references. No implementation files, change descriptions, or previous visual reports were inspected. Repository read-first instructions and the visual-judge rubric were read. This is a still-image judgment; motion, performance, combat behavior, and unseen content are outside the evidence.

## Pair preference

**Prefer F03/F04 overall, narrowly, for vegetation palette coherence. This is not a commercial-quality pass or an unqualified night-readability improvement.**

In F01, repeated intensely red shrubs command attention across the grove and compete with the blue creature at the left and brown creatures near the center. F02 keeps these shrubs conspicuously pink-red after dark. In F03/F04 their green counterparts sit within the vegetation family, leaving the blue creature and pale flowers as more selective accents. The brown-black trunks also belong more comfortably with the green foliage and earthy floor than the pale gray-green trunks of F01/F02.

There is a tradeoff: F03 makes the trunks heavily dark even in daylight; F04 nearly collapses their bark relief into black silhouettes. F02 retains substantially more trunk surface information. F04 nevertheless preserves the major tree outlines, player silhouette, and visible ground, so this is a loss of material/form readability rather than total navigational darkness. Bright yellow-green grass remains disconnected from the blue night sky in both F02 and F04. The pair change improves palette restraint more convincingly than it improves lighting or material integration.

## Eight criteria

1. **Silhouette and readability at small size.** Across all four frames, the rear-view trainer remains recognizable through the blue shirt, tan pack, and central position, although plants cross the lower body. The left blue creature has a distinct color but its many thin appendages compress into a tangled shape. The central brown creatures overlap and read more like rough boulders than clear, appealing creature silhouettes in F01/F03. F02/F04 lift those forms into pale speckled shapes without resolving that ambiguity. F03/F04 removes the red shrub distractions; F04 also loses trunk detail. These creatures do not match the immediate shape clarity and expressive presence of the Palworld creatures. The trainer's face and expression cannot be judged from this rear view.

2. **Colour and value structure.** F01/F02 distributes strong red through apparently ordinary vegetation; the picture supplies no reason to read each red clump as danger. F03/F04 is more consistent with a natural woodland palette. In both daylight frames, sparse, bright grass over a mottled olive-brown floor makes the ground carry too much local contrast. In both night frames, near-black ground, yellow-green blades, bright trainer clothing, and pale creatures feel separately treated. F04 darkens trunks more convincingly for night but does not unify this value structure.

3. **Intentionality.** All frames repeat similarly sized shrubs and scattered individual grass tufts over a broad, mostly undifferentiated slope. The large trees at left give some framing, but the central ground has no clear path, curated clearing, or convincing transition between thick growth and exposed earth. F03/F04's palette reduces scatter noise without changing that placement impression. The references organize ground cover around paths, rock edges, settlements, and encounter spaces; these frames mostly show a distribution of objects.

4. **Lighting.** Day versus night is unmistakable from the sky in F01/F03 versus F02/F04. Lighting does not give the scene equally persuasive form: tree bases and the creatures have weakly legible grounding shadows, and the player and grass remain bright against the night floor without a visible local source. F03/F04's dark trunks increase silhouette contrast but surrender bark volume. The key art's night view retains cool terrain planes and selective warm focal light; neither night frame offers that hierarchy.

5. **Horizon and depth.** All four frames end at a smooth grassy ridge below an enormous pale, vertically extruded structure. Its silhouette is cropped by the top edge and its broad planar surfaces provide little legible architectural identity. There is no comparable progression through middle-distance landmarks and layered landforms to that in the key art or Palworld plateau reference. The night sky separates the ridge clearly, but the near-to-far world remains shallow. No specific LOD or chunk seam can be confirmed from these stills.

6. **Interface.** In every frame, the quest is readable but takes a large upper-right block; the five-slot panel and long control strip further obscure the lower-right world. The contextual potion prompt has a clear border and hierarchy. Conversely, the small time text and subdued food label are less legible at thumbnail size. The minimap is mostly uniform green and communicates little visible terrain context. These observations concern the supplied interface itself, not comparison with Palworld's UI.

7. **Artefacts.** F02/F04's central creatures show conspicuous pale stippling and patchy surfaces, giving a ghosted or unfinished impression. F01/F03 shows darker, harshly textured versions of the same central forms. Across all four, vegetation visibly intersects the trainer's lower-body silhouette; a still cannot establish whether this is actual penetration or foreground occlusion. Bright slash-like marks appear on the distant pale structure, but their cause is not identifiable. No claims about popping, temporal aliasing, or animation follow from these frames.

8. **Scale agreement.** The 1.80 m trainer gives the foreground a usable ruler: the large tree trunks plausibly read as mature trees, and nearby low plants reach roughly shin to knee height. The distant structure is monumental but too incomplete in view to establish its intended architectural scale. Central and left creatures occupy different depths from the trainer; these shots do not support an exact height comparison or a verdict that they are undersized. Their silhouette overlap, rather than a proven scale error, is the supported defect. A companion beside the trainer would be needed to assess the roster's relative scale reliably.

## Three ranked gaps from the references

1. **Creature presentation and material integration — F01–F04, especially F02/F04.** Palworld's creatures have immediately legible bodies, deliberate color masses, smooth material transitions, and strong presence against vegetation. Here the central creatures merge into rough rock-like lumps by day and pale stippled masses by night; the blue creature reads as an appendage cluster. Separate the silhouettes and bring their surfaces into the same lighting language as the trainer and terrain. Staging and material/lighting treatment are scene-level work. If the underlying meshes cannot produce clear individual silhouettes, they require an art revision; these views cannot establish whether suitable alternative art is already installed.

2. **Ground cover and authored spatial structure — F01–F04.** The key art and Palworld field/path screenshots combine coherent grassy masses with selective bare paths, rock edges, flowers, and clearings. These frames expose broad mottled soil under similarly distributed bright blades and repeated bushes. Establish a readable route or encounter clearing, consolidate grass into fuller patches, and make vegetation transitions respond to tree bases and terrain. Placement, density, ground-material balance, and composition are scene-level fixes; additional ground-cover art is necessary only if the available assets cannot form those masses.

3. **Lighting and landmark depth — F01–F04, particularly F02/F04.** The references lead the eye through shaped terrain toward recognizable built or natural landmarks; the key art also uses selective illumination to organize night. Here a bare ridge and giant pale extrusion dominate the distance, while bright grass and creature surfaces remain disconnected from the dark floor. Give the landmark a readable silhouette and intermediate context, retain cool ground planes at night, and reserve the strongest highlights for meaningful subjects. Lighting and framing are scene-level fixes. A landmark whose actual geometry is only the visible plain extrusion would need architectural art/detail, which palette adjustments cannot supply.

## Direct bar answers

**A — Belongs to the Meadows key-art world: No.** F03/F04 moves the foliage palette closer, but the sparse noisy ground, weakly articulated monumental structure, and disconnected night values do not deliver the board's inviting, layered woodland landscape. Scene changes can address palette, density, composition, and lighting. Creature silhouette quality and the landmark's architectural identity may also need art work; the available asset inventory is outside this review.

**B — Trying to be the same kind of game as the Palworld references: Yes.** The third-person adventurer, visible fantastical creatures, gathering interaction, and outdoor wooded setting communicate the same broad creature-adventure genre. That genre recognition is separate from quality.

**Commercial visual quality beside those references: No.** Both pairs remain substantially behind in creature readability, vegetation/ground integration, authored composition, and coherent lighting. F03/F04 is a useful palette preference with a trunk-detail tradeoff, not evidence that those larger gaps are closed.
