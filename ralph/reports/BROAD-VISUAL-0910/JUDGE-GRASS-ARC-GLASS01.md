# Blind visual judgment — F01/F02 versus F03/F04

Viewed the four individual frames, the Meadows key art, and all five supplied Palworld screenshots. No implementation or provenance was considered. F01/F03 are the daylight comparison; F02/F04 are the night comparison. No scores.

## Characters and creatures first

In all four frames, the trainer is identifiable as a small backpacked adventurer, with useful blue sleeves, pale shoulder trim and a brown pack. The lower silhouette is compromised by bright foliage crossing the hips and legs. The rear view and small on-screen size do not establish facial appeal or expression. The distant purple, many-legged creature at the left has a distinct colour but reads as a busy spiky bundle. The brown paired forms ahead are difficult to distinguish from rocks or curled creatures in F01/F03; F02/F04 reveal pale outlines but not an immediately legible anatomy. These frames do not deliver the clean creature shapes and character emphasis visible in Palworld 01, 03 and 04. That is a visible presentation shortfall; these views alone cannot establish whether different posing, framing and lighting would solve all of it or whether the models need replacement.

## Pair preference

**Prefer F01/F02, modestly.** In F01, the foreground grass bends outward into recognisable tufts with curved tips; F03 has conspicuously straight, thin upright spikes scattered across the same bare ground. F02 retains that organic tuft shape against the dark ground, while F04 looks more like repeated little needles. The preference is visible in the lower-left foreground and in the open patch immediately ahead of the trainer. It does not amount to a reference-bar pass.

The tradeoff is real: the bent blades in F01/F02 add pale crossing lines and visual noise. F03/F04 have a slightly less tangled ground silhouette, but their stiff, repeated blades make the planting feel less natural. Neither pair creates a convincing continuous meadow surface or resolves the exposed mottled soil, weak creature presentation, or monumental pale slab on the horizon.

## Full rubric

1. **Silhouette and readability at small size.** In every frame, the large tree trunks, trainer's blue-and-brown torso and central pale tower survive a reduced-size read. Shrubs and brown midground forms merge into the ground texture. The purple creature is detectable by colour more readily than by anatomy. F01/F02 grass has more believable tuft silhouettes, but white flowers and bright grass edges compete with the trainer's legs. Clear a small low-detail pocket around the character and improve value separation around creature bodies.

2. **Colour and value structure.** F01/F03 have a recognisable natural green/brown/blue foundation, but acid-green blades and muddy olive ground dominate the playable area. The key art gives grass, earth, canopy shade and flowers more distinct, composed colour masses. F02/F04 darken the ground sharply while leaving much of the grass luminous green and the trainer strongly lit; the result is less cohesive than the key art's blue night landscape with selective warm lights. No visible friendly element clearly demonstrates oxblood misuse, and faction meaning cannot be assigned to the brown forms from these views.

3. **Intentionality.** All four frames have some useful clustering around tree bases and a comparatively open central patch. However, repeated grass clumps cover broad areas at similar spacing, with isolated flower stems sitting above the same mottled ground. F01/F02 improve the shape within a clump, not the larger composition. F03/F04 expose the repeated upright pattern especially clearly. Group planting into more substantial patches and give the central clearing or route an intelligible boundary.

4. **Lighting.** Day and night are unmistakably different in sky and ground values. F01/F03 tree trunks are very dark, while foliage is bright and terrain shading supplies little readable broad form. In F02/F04 the trainer and grass appear strongly illuminated without an evident nearby light source, and the brown midground forms become pale, stippled shapes. The scene needs coherent night illumination and more legible contact/form shading, particularly around creatures. These stills do not prove what causes the lighting behaviour.

5. **Horizon and depth.** All four frames stop at a low rolling ridge and an enormous cropped pale vertical structure. Trees supply foreground overlap, but there is little layered landscape behind the ridge. The pale structure dominates without enough surrounding form to communicate a distinctive architectural landmark. Compare the key art's hill/settlement/mountain layers and Palworld 04's framed tower across a valley. No definite terrain chunk seam or LOD transition can be established from these stills.

6. **Interface.** In all frames, the quest card and interaction prompt are readable, and the screen-edge margins are adequate. The large quest card, five nearly empty shortcut boxes and wide control strip claim a substantial portion of the right and lower scene. The bottom-left health and food text has weaker contrast, especially F02/F04. The minimap is mostly a uniform green field with few useful visual distinctions. The interface is assessed on its own readability, not against Palworld's UI.

7. **Artefacts.** In every frame, leafy geometry visibly overlaps the trainer's hip/leg region, compromising the body outline; a still cannot tell whether this is ordinary foreground occlusion or actual model penetration. The pale tower shows thin irregular marks, and the night brown forms have conspicuous bright speckled outlines. Both warrant close inspection but are not enough to diagnose z-fighting or a specific rendering fault. F02/F04 also show almost black, sharply broken cloud shapes against the blue sky. Motion popping, shimmer and temporal stability are unassessable.

8. **Scale agreement.** Using the trainer as the supplied 1.80 m ruler, nearby grass and flower stems read as substantial shin-to-knee-height growth, with taller foreground blades plausibly enlarged by perspective. The immense trunks and distant structure overwhelm the human scale, and the structure is cropped before its size or purpose becomes intelligible. The creature forms are farther away than the trainer; their true relative heights or companion/practice roles cannot be established reliably. These frames do not supply evidence for a specific creature-size ratio defect.

## Three largest reference gaps, ranked

1. **Creature and character appeal is not carrying the image — all frames.** Palworld 01/03/04 presents distinct, expressive creature bodies with easily recognised heads, limbs and action silhouettes. Here the trainer is small and partly obscured, the purple creature is visually busy, and the brown forms are ambiguous. Scene work can improve framing, overlap, backdrop and illumination. Whether distinctive creature anatomy and character art require new assets needs closer views; grass changes alone cannot supply it.

2. **The meadow reads as a scatter of blades over exposed mottled ground — all frames, especially F03/F04.** Palworld 02/03/04 has connected grass cover, purposeful bare paths and varied vegetation masses. The key art makes clearings and floral banks part of the composition. F01/F02 improves individual tuft shape but retains the broad ground-cover problem. Scene-level changes to coverage, patch scale, ground colour and route composition can substantially address this; better foliage/material art may be needed if visible repetition persists.

3. **The landscape lacks inviting depth and a resolved landmark — all frames, with the lighting mismatch clearest in F02/F04.** The key art and Palworld 04 provide layered destinations and landmarks integrated into terrain. These views end at a ridge with a pale cropped slab, and night adds little composed warm/cool light structure. Terrain staging, camera composition, atmospheric separation and lighting are scene work. A finished, distinctive architectural treatment requires appropriate landmark art if it is not available.

## Explicit bar answers

**A — Belongs to the Meadows key-art world: No.** The broad ingredients fit—green meadow, large trees, backpacked adventurer and blue night—but the dominant exposed ground, unresolved monumental structure, compressed depth and incoherent night lighting do not yet deliver its welcoming pastoral world or mysterious illuminated night. Scene composition, planting, palette and lighting are substantial fixable gaps; a convincing landmark and consistently appealing creature presentation may require art beyond what these frames demonstrate.

**B — Trying to be the same kind of game as the supplied Palworld shots: Yes.** The third-person adventurer, visible fantastical creatures, open natural terrain, exploration objective and inventory/call-out controls clearly communicate the same broad creature-adventure/survival direction. This is a genre-recognition answer, not a quality pass.

**Commercial visual quality beside those Palworld shots: No.** Neither pair meets that bar. Creature presentation, ground integration and landscape composition remain conspicuous gaps at ordinary viewing size. The modest F01/F02 preference does not change this answer.

## Still-image limits

One shared camera position in two lighting states cannot establish world-wide consistency, creature animation or temperament, combat spectacle, traversal quality, frame rate, popping, temporal aliasing or hardware rendering behaviour. There is no fight visible to judge as an event. Rear-facing and distant characters limit facial and anatomical assessment. No asset provenance or implementation cause is inferred.
