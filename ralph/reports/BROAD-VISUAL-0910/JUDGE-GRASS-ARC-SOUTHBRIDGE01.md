# Blind visual judgment: F01–F04

Reviewed only the visual-judge skill, the Meadows key-art board, all five supplied Palworld references, and the four individual frames. F01/F03 are day views; F02/F04 are night views. No provenance is assumed.

## Characters and creatures first

The trainer in all four frames is identifiable as a person with a backpack, pale collar and blue sleeves, but does not yet carry the expressive visual presence of the trainers in the Palworld references. Dark trousers, boots and hair merge into the dark foreground; the small costume details contribute less than the pale shoulder band. The back view provides no basis for judging facial appeal. The figure is readable, but visually subdued rather than a strong focal character.

There is no sufficiently prominent, unambiguous creature here to judge creature appeal, species identity, or companion-versus-opponent scale. Small distant figures do not provide that evidence. This matters: the Palworld references make creatures major compositional subjects with immediately distinct bodies, colours and relative sizes. These frames cannot demonstrate that part of the bar. Absence of that evidence is not a finding that unseen creature art is inadequate.

## Pair preference

**Prefer F03/F04 over F01/F02, narrowly.** In the left foreground and across the near hillside, F03 has blades that bend sideways and spread into loose clumps. F01 has many straight, narrow, nearly vertical spikes, making the meadow read more like repeated bristles. At night the same difference is clear: F04 has irregular, drooping outlines against the dark ground; F02 has upright green slivers. F03/F04 therefore reads more like grass growing in varied directions.

This is a local improvement, not a change in overall scene quality. F03/F04 still exposes thin, angular blade shapes and numerous small bright edges. The right foreground bush, terrain values, trainer, gate composition and horizon are materially alike in both pairs. The preference is not based on the one-minute day-clock difference or the changed appearance of distant small figures.

## Full visual rubric

1. **Silhouette and small-size readability.** In F01–F04, the trainer's pale shoulders keep the torso identifiable, and the bare tree and stone gate remain recognisable. The feet and lower legs are weak against the foreground. The large lower-right bush reads as a single dark, jagged mass; its leaf shapes do not resolve into convincingly lit volume. At thumbnail scale, F03/F04's grass improvement becomes subtle. All pairs retain the same larger silhouette weaknesses.

2. **Colour and value structure.** F01/F03 have useful blue-sky/green-ground separation and limited red accents at the gate. However, the broad foreground is muddy brown with near-black shadow, while the sunlit hills turn yellow-green; these large blocks overpower the smaller flower colours. F02/F04 compress much of the terrain and vegetation into black-green, with the pale trainer and moon unusually conspicuous. The gate banners carry the evident oxblood accents; no comparable friendly red accent is visible, though allegiances cannot be determined from appearance alone.

3. **Intentionality.** In F01/F03, the gate, banners, crates and winding ridge route provide some purposeful arrangement. The meadow repeatedly switches between fine grass marks and broad exposed patches without a strong foreground-to-destination composition. The large right bush blocks the gate's approach, and the cropped left trunk contributes little beyond a vertical border. F03/F04 improves the natural direction of individual grass clumps, but does not resolve that composition. This describes visible patterning, not how the scene was produced.

4. **Lighting.** Day and night are unmistakably different in F01/F03 versus F02/F04. Daylight gives the hills some form, but the foreground shadow is so deep that the terrain texture becomes a dark smear, and the right bush has little internal light variation. At night the trainer's bright collar, arms and backpack stand apart from the much darker setting; the gate offers no compelling warm light destination. The key art's night panel separates land layers and uses warm distant habitation to invite travel. F02/F04 does neither strongly.

5. **Horizon and depth.** All four frames communicate a nearby dip and a farther ridge, but the far left horizon is a thin, low strip and the right ridge largely terminates in similarly scaled tree crowns. The distant route has little visual payoff. The key art and Palworld plateau reference build several recognisable depth layers and give the eye a substantial distant landmark. No definite chunk or LOD seam is established by these stills.

6. **Interface.** F01–F04 keep major panels inside the frame and make the quest text legible. The large quest block, minimap, blank numbered action slots and long bottom command strip occupy substantial scene area. The two lower-right strips especially cover the bush and terrain approach. Tiny keycap symbols are much harder to read than command labels; the clock is low contrast against the sky. This is an independent interface judgment, not a comparison with Palworld UI design.

7. **Artefacts.** In F01/F02, near grass visibly repeats sharp upright spikes; F03/F04 replaces many with sharp bent strokes, a more natural outline but still conspicuously thin geometry. In every frame, the right bush has hard, planar-looking leaf edges and little shading separation. F02/F04 show clouds as dark, broken streaks around a very plain bright moon, which reads graphically coarse. These are visible presentation defects; stills do not establish z-fighting, temporal popping, or their technical causes. No clear geometry intersection can be confidently called here.

8. **Scale agreement.** Using the 1.80 m trainer as the stipulated ruler, nearby grass generally reaches ankle-to-knee range and the large nearby bush is plausibly substantial vegetation. The foreground trunk is very broad, but its canopy is offscreen, so total tree proportion cannot be assessed. The gate and adjacent figure are farther away; perspective prevents a reliable metre comparison with the trainer. Nothing visible establishes a creature role/size inversion. The frames simply lack an adequate creature scale comparison.

## Three largest gaps, ranked

1. **Character and creature presence — F01–F04.** Palworld's forest fight, meadow boss and plateau references make distinct creatures and expressive trainers the subjects of the image. Here the trainer is a small dark-backed figure and no creature is clearly presented. Better camera/encounter staging is scene work; whether the required expressive creature and character art exists cannot be answered from these frames. A further view with prominent subjects is needed to assess that art.

2. **Coherent vegetation and ground treatment — F01–F04, less severe in F03/F04.** Palworld's open-field and base references blend short ground cover, fuller foliage and readable bare routes. Here very thin grass strokes, a large flat-looking bush and coarse brown ground contrast sharply with one another. Distribution, route clearing and lighting are scene-adjustable. The bush's shape/shading and grass silhouette quality may require asset/material work; the images cannot prove that suitable alternatives are already available.

3. **Inviting depth and focal destinations — F01–F04.** The key art builds rolling layers, substantial landmarks and warm inhabited points; Palworld's plateau adds cliffs, buildings and a distant tower. These frames end at a modest ridge, with the gate partly occluded and night terrain largely swallowed by darkness. Framing, landmark placement and light/value separation are scene work. A richer landmark silhouette may also require art not demonstrated in the supplied views.

## Separate bar answers

**A — Belongs to the Meadows key-art world: No.** Green hills, broadleaf trees, a rustic gate and red banners share its vocabulary, but the muddy foreground, harsh foliage shapes, compressed horizon and uninviting night values do not yet deliver its welcoming, layered meadow mood. Scene composition and lighting can address much of this; foliage and landmark art quality needs separate attention.

**B — Trying to be the same kind of game as the Palworld references: Yes.** The third-person trainer, grassy explorable landscape, rustic structures and visible creature-related commands communicate the same broad exploration/companion-game ambition. This is a genre-recognition answer, not an art-quality pass. The lack of a prominent creature makes the visual case substantially weaker than any of the references.

**Commercial visual bar beside the supplied Palworld frames: No, for both pairs.** F03/F04 is preferable, but a change in grass direction does not bridge the subject appeal, foliage cohesion and world-depth gaps above.

## Limits

These are four stills of effectively one composition. They cannot establish animation appeal, combat spectacle, traversal, temporal aliasing, popping, performance, other locations, unseen art availability or capture/rendering provenance. No frame-time or hardware-quality conclusions are made. Character judgments are restricted to a rear view; creature judgments remain unproven rather than inferred.
