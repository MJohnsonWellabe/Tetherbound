# Blind visual judgment — grass glassfield01

## Scope and preference

Inspected the actual images F01.png, F02.png, F03.png and F04.png in `.artifacts/broad-visual-0910/blind-grass-glassfield01/`, plus `docs/reference/tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png` and all five `palworld-0*.jpg` reference images. Judgment uses visible image evidence only. No source, history or other visual reports were inspected. No inference is made about provenance, implementation, renderer or which pair represents a change.

**Preference: no meaningful preference between F01/F02 and F03/F04.** F01 and F03 have effectively the same full-frame impression: thin bright blades scattered over a large brown-green ground plane, a small central trainer, dark left-hand trees and a huge pale vertical landmark. F02 and F04 retain the same night problem: conspicuous bright grass strokes against very dark ground. The middle-distance brown creatures differ in arrangement between the night frames; this does not establish a ground-foliage improvement. Any subtle local difference is insufficient to select a better pair from these stills.

The creature presentation falls short of the reference first: in F01/F03 the central brown spiky forms are difficult to separate from a pair of rocks or dead bushes at a glance, while F02/F04 make them pale, speckled masses. The blue-purple creature at left has a clearer unusual outline, but these distant views do not establish expressive character quality. The trainer is identifiable from behind and separates reasonably from the ground, but this angle is insufficient for judging facial appeal.

## All eight criteria

1. **Silhouette and small-size readability.** In all four frames, tree trunks and the backpack-bearing trainer remain recognizable when considering the broad thumbnail-sized masses. Individual grass blades become fine noise, leaving the soil plane dominant. F01/F03's brown central creatures have weak limb/body separation and compete with similarly sized background vegetation. F02/F04's brighter brown forms remain hard to parse anatomically. The plants immediately around the trainer obscure the legs and weaken the feet-to-ground read. The tall landmark is visible, but its cropped, nearly rectangular silhouette conveys little identity.

2. **Colour and value structure.** F01/F03 have a natural green/brown basis, but the vivid yellow-green grass and broad mottled brown terrain dominate more than the creatures. The left trunks are very dark against bright foliage. F02/F04 shift the sky and trunks convincingly toward blue-black, yet grass remains an unusually bright green mesh of strokes and the central brown creatures become pale and speckled. This breaks the unified nighttime value structure seen in the key-art night panel. There is no identifiable Team Tether/friendly context in these frames from which to judge oxblood reservation.

3. **Intentionality.** All frames have some useful variation from broad-leaved plants, shrubs, white flowers and the left tree cluster. Nevertheless, the dominant grass treatment is widely spaced, similarly narrow upright tufts covering most of the foreground without a strong grass-to-clearing transition or authored route. Bare patches do not form an obvious path, and the rounded hill behind the creatures is particularly empty. Palworld-02 uses a clear pale path with vegetated edges; Palworld-04 uses larger continuous grass masses and landmarks to organize the view. These frames lack that hierarchy.

4. **Lighting.** Day and night are immediately distinguishable between F01/F03 and F02/F04. F01/F03's ground has mottling but weak large-scale light/shadow modeling, so the open slope reads relatively flat. F02/F04's very bright foliage and bright central trainer do not sit comfortably with the dark trunks and sky. The brown creature surfaces look especially disconnected from their surroundings at night. The images do not establish the cause of those material/light differences.

5. **Horizon and depth.** All four frames have little depth beyond the nearby rolling ridge. The giant grey structure occupies the central skyline with a broad flat face, visible vertical streaking and a cut-off top. It supplies height without the layered geography and distinct landmark silhouette of the key art or Palworld-04. Day sky/cloud separation works, but does not compensate for the shallow visible landscape. No definite chunk or LOD seam can be diagnosed from these stills.

6. **Interface.** In all four frames, the right-side quest panel is legible but large; the slots and action strip occupy a substantial lower-right block. The repeated small key labels in dark empty slots add little immediate meaning. The cyan pickup prompt clearly reads, although the relevant object is not obvious amid flowers and grass near the trainer. The low-contrast time text competes with the tall structure behind it. HUD elements remain inside the image bounds. This is an internal legibility judgment, not a comparison with Palworld UI.

7. **Artefacts.** F02/F04 show visible fine pale stippling on the brown creatures, giving their surfaces an unstable-looking grain even in a still. F01/F03's blade edges are extremely thin and high contrast, and F02/F04 amplify that effect; temporal shimmer cannot be confirmed. In every frame, foreground plant leaves visually overlap the trainer's lower legs. That weakens readability, but this single view cannot establish actual intersection. The giant grey landmark has conspicuous vertical texture streaking and sparse angular marks. No claim of z-fighting or popping is supported by these static images.

8. **Scale agreement.** The trainer provides the stated 1.80 m reference. Nearby grass generally reads below knee height, with the shrubs around the trainer reaching into the lower-body silhouette. The left trees are large but not obviously impossible beside a person. The landmark is enormous and visually underarticulated for that scale. The creatures stand at different depths from the trainer, so their projected heights cannot establish a precise relative-height defect or whether a documented size relationship is wrong. There is no defensible basis here for assigning creature roles or recommending a scale change.

## Three largest reference gaps, ranked

1. **Ground cover lacks coherent mass and composition — F01–F04.** Palworld-02/03/04 show grass as a continuous surface with readable edges, paths and thicker patches. The key art organizes flowers and grasses into meadow masses around paths and tree bases. These frames show substantial blotchy soil between thin bright stakes, making the foreground feel sparse even where blade count appears substantial. Improve clustered coverage, blade/clump shape and ground colour relationships; keep clearings deliberate. This is chiefly a scene/material/foliage treatment gap.

2. **Creature forms do not command the scene — F01–F04, most conspicuous in F02/F04.** Palworld-01/03/04 establish expressive, cleanly separated creature bodies and readable heads, limbs and colour blocks. The central brown creatures here read as spiky lumps by day and speckled pale lumps by night. Improve visible presentation, silhouette separation and material response. These distant rear/side views cannot determine whether staging and materials suffice or whether the underlying art needs replacement; a close, well-lit creature view is necessary before that art decision.

3. **Weak landscape layering and inconsistent night integration — F01/F03 for landscape, F02/F04 for night.** The key art and Palworld-04 lead the eye through foreground, routes, middle-distance landmarks and distant terrain. Here a low ridge and a giant flat grey face terminate the view. The key-art night panel holds terrain, foliage and subjects within a coherent cool value range with localized accents; these night frames leave large amounts of grass conspicuously bright. Improve composition, terrain/vegetation layering, landmark articulation and night value relationships. Scene work can address much of this, while a sufficiently articulated landmark may require better art; the images cannot establish what art is available in the build.

## Separate bar answers

**A — Do these frames read as belonging to the Meadows key-art world? No.** The green meadow, broad trees and white flowers provide partial palette/content agreement. However, the sparse mottled ground, shallow landscape, giant bare grey landmark and bright-on-black night foliage do not carry the welcoming, layered meadow composition or coherent nocturnal mystery of the board. This is not a demand for painted per-leaf fidelity. Scene, palette, lighting and foliage composition changes could close substantial parts of this gap. Whether better creature or landmark art is also required remains unproven by these views.

**B — Beside the Palworld references, would someone say these are trying to be the same kind of game? Yes.** A third-person trainer in an open field with fantastical creatures and exploration prompts communicates the same broad creature-adventure category. This answer establishes genre intent only.

**Commercial visual quality beside the Palworld references: No.** Neither pair matches the references' coherent vegetation masses, creature readability, integrated surface lighting or composed environmental depth. There is no meaningful commercial-quality improvement between the supplied pairs.

## Limits

These are static views of one location in two times of day. They cannot establish motion shimmer, popping, animation quality, combat spectacle, traversal, performance or image stability over time. No fight is shown, so fight presentation is unassessed. Creature scale comparisons are limited by differing depth, and character-expression assessment is limited by distance and the rear camera. No source or asset inventory was inspected, so any distinction between scene fixes and missing-art needs is explicitly provisional where the image cannot settle it.
