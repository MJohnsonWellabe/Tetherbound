## Eight-part rubric

1. **Silhouette and small-size readability.** Across F01–F04, trainer, trees and tower remain identifiable at reduced attention/detail. The spider-like creature's body and legs merge against the dark tree base, despite its useful purple contrast. The central brown shapes remain ambiguous. Grass becomes a repeated streak pattern rather than a convincing continuous ground layer. F03/F04 improve coverage slightly, without changing the principal silhouettes.

2. **Colour and value structure.** F01/F03 have recognizable natural greens and a blue sky, but bright lime grass, dark trunks and muddy brown-green ground feel insufficiently integrated. F02/F04 establish a blue night sky while much of the vegetation remains sharply green and the trainer remains conspicuously bright. The pale brown central objects appear lighter at night than their daytime equivalents, weakening material consistency. There is no clearly identifiable oxblood danger coding to evaluate; the reddish-brown forms are too ambiguous to classify as friendly or hostile.

3. **Intentionality.** All frames have some good local grouping at tree bases, but the main field is dominated by many similar isolated tufts and a handful of separate shrubs. There is no strong path, bank, clearing edge or occupied place organizing the central expanse. The key art uses paths, fences, water edges and settlement masses to make the meadow a destination; Palworld 02 and 05 likewise organize ground cover around readable activity areas. Taller foreground blades in F03/F04 improve texture, not that spatial organization.

4. **Lighting.** Day and night are immediately distinguishable in F01/F03 versus F02/F04. Dark tree bases and terrain variation supply some grounding, but daylight ground form is dominated by a mottled surface rather than a persuasive light-and-shadow pattern. At night, bright blade edges, a brightly readable trainer and pale central forms do not establish a convincing common light source. Night lacks the selective warm inhabited accents and quieter broad value masses of the key art's night view.

5. **Horizon and depth.** In F01–F04, a low ridge ends abruptly against the sky and a huge pale vertical structure extends out of frame. It is a landmark in size, but its nearly blank slab-like face supplies little recognizable purpose. There are few intermediate or distant layers. The key art and Palworld 04 lead from foreground activity through distinct landforms to a distant landmark; these frames do not supply comparable depth. No definite terrain chunk seam or LOD transition is established by these stills.

6. **Interface.** All four frames keep the panels inside the image and the interaction prompt is easy to locate. The large story panel and two broad lower-right command bars dominate substantial parts of the field. Tiny hotbar numerals offer little glanceable content, the top-center time sits on a similarly valued tower, and the food/health text is less distinct than the story text. This is an internal legibility observation, not a comparison to Palworld's UI.

7. **Artefacts.** F01–F04 show very thin, hard-edged vegetation with conspicuous bright striping. The tower has visible fine horizontal/diagonal marks against its mostly blank face. F02/F04 show pale speckled central forms whose finish differs strikingly from their dark daytime appearance. These are distracting visible symptoms; the stills do not prove z-fighting, shader failure or any other technical cause. F02/F04 also have hard dark cloud fragments that feel cut out against the blue sky.

8. **Scale agreement.** Using the trainer as the stated 1.80 m ruler, nearby shrubs rise into the thigh/waist area and the mature trunks are plausibly massive. F03/F04's longer grass remains below the torso and reads as tall field grass, though it contributes to lower-leg clutter. Perspective prevents a reliable height ratio for the purple creature or central distant forms. No companion-versus-practice-target hierarchy can be verified here. The enormous tower is clearly much larger than the trainer, but the blank surface provides too few architectural cues to judge its intended human scale.

## Three largest gaps, ranked

1. **Creature and character presentation — F01–F04.** Palworld 01/03/04 show bold, distinct creature bodies and expressive silhouettes, with trainers and companions clearly separated from their surroundings. These frames offer a small rear-facing trainer, a dark distant many-legged shape and ambiguous brown forms. Improve camera distance, separation and staging with scene work; expressive faces, coherent forms and convincing creature identity require art work if the available assets cannot supply them. The images cannot establish what art exists elsewhere.

2. **A composed, grounded meadow surface — F01–F04, most exposed in F01/F02.** The key art and Palworld 02/03/05 combine continuous ground cover with purposeful bare areas, clear edges and clusters. These frames spread thin bright tufts across broad mottled ground. Density grouping, height variation, ground palette, paths and local light/shadow can be addressed in the scene. Additional foliage or ground art may be needed if current assets cannot form softer, broader masses.

3. **A destination and layered world — F01–F04.** The key art and Palworld 04 connect foreground space to inhabitable landmarks and layered terrain. Here, a shallow ridge and blank towering slab make the view feel unfinished. Terrain composition, atmospheric separation and framing are scene changes; a recognizable architectural landmark needs suitable art if none is available. Night additionally needs selective light accents to convey inhabited mystery.
