# Blind visual judgment — F01–F04

Reviewed only the visual-judge skill, the Meadows key-art board, all five supplied Palworld reference images, and the four individual frames. F01/F03 show day; F02/F04 show night. No provenance is inferred.

**Characters first.** In all four frames, the trainer reads immediately as a stylized human adventurer: brown hair and backpack, pale shoulder trim, blue sleeves, dark trousers. The backpack and contrasting upper body preserve identification against the field. The outfit communicates a familiar adventurer archetype, but this rear view and small screen size do not establish distinctive personality or expressive character art comparable with the strongly characterized trainer and creatures in Palworld 01, 03 and 04. The lower legs compete with the grass, particularly in F01/F02. No creature is visibly identifiable in these four frames; creature appeal, creature silhouette and trainer-to-creature scale remain untested. The plant to the trainer's left is not sufficient evidence of a creature.

**Pair preference: F03/F04, narrowly.** In F03 the grass around the trainer's boots and the plant to the left leaves more visible ground and more separation between individual stems. F04 retains a little more leg/ground separation in the dark foreground than F02. This is a modest readability improvement, not a transformation: both pairs remain dominated by a dense, similarly shaped blade field, and the horizon, character, water and interface have essentially the same visible strengths and weaknesses. At a small viewing size the overall impression is close to a tie; the preference comes from local ground and lower-body visibility.

1. **Silhouette and readability at small size.** In F01–F04 the trainer's head, pack and arms survive reduction; boots and lower legs are weaker. F03/F04 is slightly clearer at that contact zone. The foreground becomes a busy pale-green texture by day and cyan texture by night. The broad hill and lake remain recognizable, while individual distant rocks and thin tree crowns lose identity. Palworld 02 and 04 use paths and calmer grass surfaces to preserve character and creature silhouettes. These frames need a quieter local ground shape around the subject and stronger midground tree masses.

2. **Colour and value structure.** F01/F03 have recognizable natural green, tan and blue components, but the pale, cool grass tips occupy so much of the frame that they flatten the meadow into a washed field. The opposite grassy hill is a largely uniform yellow-green mass. F02/F04 establish night with navy ground and sky, yet extensive cyan-white grass and tree highlights compete with the trainer. There is little warm visual counterweight beyond a tiny distant light. No conspicuous oxblood contamination is visible on friendly elements. The key art has stronger grouped sun/shade values and richer warm/cool relationships, including warm inhabited points at night.

3. **Intentionality.** F01–F04 read as continuous grass coverage interrupted by a few isolated objects, with similarly thin trees along the skyline. The opposite hill's broad straight-sided exposed cuts and sharply bounded green surfaces dominate the composition without becoming a persuasive natural formation or an inviting destination. The dark horizontal object near the shore does little to establish a lived-in location at this scale. The key art and Palworld 02/04/05 give paths, clearings, buildings and vegetation clusters distinct compositional jobs. Redistribute coverage into shorter open areas and denser groups; establish a visible destination and a connected route.

4. **Lighting.** Day and night are unambiguous in the paired frames. Daylight models the large exposed slopes, and nighttime retains enough terrain information for navigation. However, the grass receives extensive bright tip contrast while the broader ground is comparatively simple, making the foliage pattern more prominent than the terrain's form. In F02/F04 the pale distant tree crowns are conspicuously bright against dark hills. The trainer remains readable but feels more strongly lit than much of the immediate terrain. More coherent subject, ground and foliage value grouping would improve integration. Precise light or shadow causes cannot be diagnosed from these stills.

5. **Horizon and depth.** The lake separates near and far banks clearly in all frames, but the opposite hill blocks much of the view and offers few layers or landmark silhouettes beyond sparse trees. Its straight exposed cuts are the principal distant feature. The lake is a broad, nearly featureless blue/cyan strip rather than a convincing connector between shore, reflection and distance. Palworld 04 and the key art use foreground framing, intermediate structures and a distinct far landmark to pull the eye onward. No definite LOD or chunk seam can be established from this view alone.

6. **Interface.** The quest objective is legible, with a clear right-side hierarchy and safe margins. The large lower-right empty numbered slots and command bar consume substantial foreground space without showing much immediate content. Health and food labels have weak contrast over busy grass in F01/F03 and remain subdued at night. Several small keycap glyphs require close inspection. The day/time text is much less salient than the objective. This is an internal readability observation, not a comparison against Palworld's UI.

7. **Artefacts.** F02/F04 show sharp dark streaks and torn-looking shapes across the cloud layer; these read less like soft atmospheric clouds than the daytime sky. The large pale moon is clipped by the top edge, weakening the sky composition. F01/F03 expose conspicuous broad angular terrain cuts and hard grass-to-exposed-slope boundaries across the lake. They are visible visual defects or awkward shapes, but the stills cannot determine whether they are technical seams or authored geometry. No definite z-fighting is visible. Popping and temporal aliasing are untestable here.

8. **Scale agreement.** Using the trainer as the 1.80 m reference, the local grass is tall enough to interfere materially with the shins and sometimes the knee region; F03/F04 opens that area slightly. The nearby flowering plant reads plausibly as a substantial low plant beside a person. Distant trees look delicate relative to the landform, but their distance prevents a reliable metre estimate. No creature size hierarchy can be checked. No clear person/nearby-prop scale contradiction is established; the actionable issue is vegetation height and coverage competing with the character's grounded stance.

**The three largest reference gaps, ranked:**

1. **Character-and-creature identity is not demonstrated (F01–F04).** Palworld 01/03/04 foreground expressive, differentiated creatures and readable trainer action. These frames present one small rear-facing adventurer and no identifiable companion. A scene/camera change can provide the missing evidence; whether new character or creature art is needed cannot be decided from these frames. The current evidence does not pass that part of the commercial comparison.
2. **Landscape composition lacks convincing destinations and natural structure (F01–F04).** The opposite bank is dominated by a blunt hill and straight exposed cuts, with little inhabited context. The key art and Palworld 02/04/05 organize routes, clearings, rocks, trees and structures around distinct places. Terrain shaping, composition and placement are scene-fixable. If suitable trees, rock forms and destination structures are unavailable, those require additional art; their availability is unknown here.
3. **Ground coverage is dense but insufficiently varied and value-grouped (F01–F04, slightly better in F03/F04).** Long bright blades fill most of the lower frame. Palworld 02/03/04 gives grass calmer surfaces, open ground and purposeful height variation; the key art adds grouped flowers, shade and clear paths. Height, clustering, density and lighting are scene-fixable. A richer vegetation asset vocabulary may be needed to sustain the reference's diversity, but that cannot be established without seeing what assets exist.

**A — Key-art world: No.** Both pairs share the broad meadow/lake/day-night vocabulary, but these particular views do not yet carry the board's inviting oak-grove landscape, inhabited route structure, layered landmarks or warm nighttime mystery. The primary visible failures are scene composition, terrain shaping, vegetation grouping and value structure. More distinctive landmark and foliage art may also be necessary; the images alone cannot establish inventory.

**B — Same kind of game as the Palworld references: Yes, provisionally.** A stylized third-person backpacked trainer exploring an open grassy world reads as adjacent adventure/survival territory. That establishes broad genre resemblance. The creature-companion identity central to the references is not demonstrated by any of these frames, so this is a genre-intent answer, not an art-quality pass.

**Commercial visual bar beside the supplied shipping-game references: No.** F03/F04 is the preferable pair, but the small local readability gain does not overcome the uniform grass treatment, blunt landscape composition, limited environmental storytelling and absent creature evidence. These images do not yet present the authored environmental variety and character-led scene appeal visible in the references.

**Still-image limits.** This judgment covers one viewpoint at two times of day. It cannot assess movement, animation, combat spectacle, traversal feel, temporal foliage behavior, performance, unseen areas, or creature quality. No renderer, hardware, implementation or asset provenance is inferred. Fine lighting issues should be confirmed in play; a static pair preference does not establish a broader gameplay improvement.
