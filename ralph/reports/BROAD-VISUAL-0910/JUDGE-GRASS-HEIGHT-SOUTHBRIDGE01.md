# Blind visual judgment: F01/F02 versus F03/F04

Reviewed only the visual-judge skill, the Meadows key art, all five supplied Palworld references, and the four individual frames. F01/F03 are day; F02/F04 are night. No implementation or provenance is inferred.

## Characters first

The trainer is recognizable as a backpacked adventurer in all four frames, but this presentation does not reach the character appeal of the Palworld references. The rear view gives readable sleeves, boots, hair and equipment, yet the small, dark figure contributes little personality to the scene. Its detailed clothing also sits uneasily beside the broad, flat leaf shapes in the foreground shrub. In F02/F04 the bright sleeves and backpack separate the trainer strongly from terrain that has nearly disappeared into black; this helps identification but weakens the impression of shared lighting.

No creature is clearly presented at a size that permits a fair assessment of design, expression or trainer/companion scale. The distant figures and bright shapes cannot support a creature-art verdict. The supplied images therefore do not demonstrate the creature appeal central to the references. A rear-view still also cannot establish the trainer's facial appeal or animation quality.

## Pair preference

**Slight preference for F03/F04 overall, carried by F03 in daylight. F02/F04 are effectively tied.**

In F03 the grass immediately left of the trainer and across the left hillside is less vertically dominant than in F01. More of the undulating green ground remains visible between the spikes, and the transition from the bare foreground into the meadow is easier to follow. F01 feels marginally fuller, but its taller bright blades compete with flowers and the terrain contour. F03 retains ample vegetation while making the slope less visually busy. This is a local readability improvement, not a change in the overall quality tier.

At night, F04 has slightly less grass crossing the dark ground, but both F02 and F04 compress much of that ground into the same near-black mass. The lighting and large foreground shrub dominate the comparison; the vegetation difference is not consequential enough to choose one night frame confidently.

## Eight-part rubric

1. **Silhouette and small-size readability.** In F01–F04 the trainer, tree groups, central dead tree and gate remain identifiable when considering a reduced view. Small plants merge into stippled or striped terrain. The large lower-right shrub becomes an opaque green mass that hides the route and part of the gate approach. The trainer's legs merge with the dark foreground in the day pair, while bright upper-body accents preserve its night silhouette. F03 has the clearest daytime hillside contour, but neither pair offers a readable creature focal point.

2. **Colour and value structure.** F01/F03 have a natural green/brown starting palette, but yellow-green turf, vivid lime tree crowns and the nearly black foreground separate into harsh bands. They lack the key art's softer colour relationships between shaded grass, sunlit meadow and distant hills. F02/F04 clearly switch to blue night, yet terrain values collapse too much to preserve the inviting, layered mystery of the key art's night panel. Red banners are local accents at the gate; these stills do not establish faction meaning or reveal an obvious spread of that red across friendly foreground elements.

3. **Intentionality.** The gate, banners, barrels and hay provide a deliberate destination in F01–F04. The tree grove on the right is also a useful cluster. Against that, thin grass blades spread across broad slopes with a repeated upright rhythm, flowers are mostly small scatter, and the foreground stones look individually dotted across the bare patch. The huge shrub occupies valuable sightline space without framing the destination cleanly. F03 reduces the grass rhythm's dominance but does not replace it with richer meadow groupings, varied ground cover or a more legible route.

4. **Lighting.** Day and night are unmistakable. F01/F03 have strong foreground shade and bright hilltops, which do establish terrain form. However, the shrub has little internal value variation, and the transition into black under the left tree is abrupt. In F02/F04 the trainer remains comparatively bright while the gate and surrounding slope lose definition. The gate lacks the warm local light that makes the key art's night settlement a destination. No finer shadow correctness or hardware behaviour can be established here.

5. **Horizon and depth.** F01–F04 establish a near patch, intermediate ravine/gate and further tree line. The broad central slope is readable, especially in F03. Beyond it, the low horizon contains little distinctive massing; a large portion of the composition is sky. The references use layered cliffs, mountains, architecture or distant landmarks to extend the world and draw the eye forward. Night reduces the separation between grove and hillside further. No definite LOD or chunk seam is visible.

6. **Interface.** All frames keep the major HUD blocks within the image and the mission text is immediately legible. Its large upper-right panel competes with the distant landscape, while the two broad bottom-right strips cover much of the foreground shrub and route. The tiny white slot/key symbols provide weak item recognition at reduced size. The subdued top-center time and lower-left food/health text have less immediate clarity than the mission instruction. This is an internal readability judgment, not a comparison against Palworld's UI.

7. **Artefacts.** F01–F04 show visibly angular, isolated grass blades and a flat overlapping-leaf appearance in the foreground shrub. These look like rendering/material limitations of the presented vegetation, although the stills cannot prove a technical fault. The left tree trunk's cropped, faceted surface is conspicuous. F02/F04's moon is an extremely plain bright disc cut by the upper frame edge. No unambiguous z-fighting or broken geometry is established. Popping and motion aliasing cannot be judged.

8. **Scale agreement.** Using the trainer as the 1.80 m reference, the closest shrub reads as a large person-height bush and obstructs the scene accordingly; it is physically possible, but compositionally expensive. Much of the adjacent grass reads as substantial shin/knee-height growth, with F01 more insistent than F03. The larger left-side tree and distant gate are plausible at their respective distances. Perspective prevents exact comparison with the small figures near the gate. There is no clearly visible companion/practice-creature pair from which to verify role-relative size.

## Three largest gaps, ranked

1. **Character and creature presentation — F01–F04.** Palworld 01, 03 and 04 give characters and creatures distinctive readable masses and focal importance; these frames give a small rear-view trainer and no assessable creature centerpiece. Scene staging and camera composition can improve visibility. Whether the underlying character or creature art has the needed expression and cohesion requires closer evidence; these images cannot establish what replacement art is available or necessary.

2. **Vegetation and ground cohesion — F01–F04, strongest in F01/F03.** Palworld 02–05 blend short grass, taller clumps, ground texture, paths and shade into continuous terrain. Here, bright discrete spikes stand over coarse ground while the foreground shrub reads as a flat leaf wall. Scene changes can improve clustering, heights, sightlines and transitions. Grass/shrub form and material quality may also need art changes if the current assets cannot produce more cohesive coverage; the stills do not reveal the available asset set.

3. **Depth and destination lighting — F01–F04.** The key art and Palworld 04 layer landmarks and landforms into the distance; the key art's night scene retains cool spatial layers and warm habitation. These images terminate largely in a low tree line and empty sky, then lose the gate and ground at night. Terrain composition, camera framing, distant silhouettes, night fill and lighting around the gate are scene-level work. New landmark art would be needed only if suitable forms cannot be made from available content, which was not inspected.

## Separate bar answers

**A — Belongs to the Meadows key-art world: Yes, at the broad setting and motif level.** Rolling green hills, deciduous groves, flowers, a rustic fortified entrance and a cool-blue night are consistent with that world. Neither pair captures the board's layered depth, luminous meadow colour or inviting night destination yet. This yes concerns palette, setting and mood language, not painted-detail fidelity or acceptance of the current finish.

**B — Trying to be the same kind of game as the Palworld references: Yes.** The third-person equipped trainer, open grassy terrain and inhabitable rustic location communicate an outdoor adventure/survival setting in the same broad family. Creature collecting and creature-led encounters are not demonstrated by these four stills, so this is a limited genre-read answer.

**Commercial visual parity with the supplied Palworld shots: No, for both pairs.** Vegetation reads less cohesive, world depth and focal staging are thinner, and the character/creature presentation does not demonstrate comparable appeal. F03/F04's small improvement does not change that answer. Scene work can address grass hierarchy, route visibility, framing and night lighting. Asset-level quality cannot be cleared by these changes, and should be judged in closer character/creature views and vegetation views before deciding what art must be made or replaced.

## Still-image limits

This is a judgment of one viewpoint at two times of day, not of the whole game. The visible clock differs by one minute between F02 and F04, so fine night-light differences should not be attributed confidently to one cause. No conclusions are offered about provenance, implementation, performance, animation, combat feel, popping, temporal aliasing or traversal. The frames cannot establish the quality of creatures that are not clearly shown.
