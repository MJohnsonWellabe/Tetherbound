# Blind visual verdict — Meadows 01

Reviewed F01–F04 against the Meadows key-art board and all five supplied Palworld references. F01/F02 and F03/F04 are treated as alternative day/night pairs. This is a still-image judgment; it makes no performance, motion, implementation or asset-provenance claims.

**Character and creature adequacy:** The trainer is identifiable in all four frames, with a readable backpack, blue sleeves and dark trousers. However, the small, rear-facing presentation does not demonstrate the expressive character appeal seen in the Palworld references. The trainer's mottled surface detail and compact silhouette are visually less clear than those references' broad costume shapes. No creature is presented clearly enough to assess its design, expression or relationship to the trainer. Small pale forms in the distance, particularly at the far right in F03/F04, cannot carry a creature game's identity or establish creature adequacy. This is a failure of the supplied presentation, not evidence that an unseen creature model is defective.

**Pair preference: tie.** The terrain coverage, foreground plant masses, trainer presentation and day/night value structure are effectively the same at the supplied viewing size. Small differences in distant forms and individual foliage do not produce a persuasive, consistent improvement. Neither pair resolves the dominant weaknesses below.

## Eight criteria

1. **Silhouette and readability at small size.** In F01/F03, the trainer, trees and gate remain identifiable when mentally reduced to contact-sheet size. Fine grass and flowers merge into stippled terrain. The large lower-right bush becomes one dark, scalloped mass; it obscures the approach to the gate rather than framing it cleanly. In F02/F04, the bright sleeves preserve the trainer, but much of the ground, vegetation and gate merges into darkness. Creatures are not reliably identifiable at small size in any frame.

2. **Colour and value structure.** F01/F03 establish a consistent yellow-green meadow, brown earth and blue sky. Much of the colour emphasis falls on bright grass and treetops while the trainer stays subdued. The black foreground shadow and bright hill produce separation, but the transition is abrupt; the hill itself is dominated by similar olive values and busy mottling. F02/F04 compress most playable terrain into very dark green or black beneath a substantially brighter sky. The red banners in F01/F03 are the sole clear oxblood accent, but their narrative allegiance cannot be inferred from the picture; no visible widespread colour leakage can be established.

3. **Intentionality.** The grass-covered banks and clearing around the trainer avoid a simple uniform carpet in all four frames. Nevertheless, similar narrow grass strokes and scattered tiny flowers create repetitive surface noise. The gate, paired banners, hay and barrels suggest a placed destination, but its approach is visually interrupted by the oversized foreground bush. The distant tree group in F01/F03 has similar bright crowns and upright trunks, offering less compositional variety than the key art's groves. A stronger path-to-destination relationship would make this view feel more deliberately composed.

4. **Lighting.** Day and night are unmistakable. F01/F03 show directional shadows and illuminated banks, giving the land form. Their foreground shadow is excessively heavy relative to the bright slope. In F02/F04, the trainer remains brightly readable while the nearby ground almost disappears, weakening the sense that both share the same illumination. The gate has no strong warm light or other focal illumination to pull the player forward. The key-art night panel preserves layered landforms and a warm settlement destination; these night views lose both.

5. **Horizon and depth.** The near trunk, trainer, depression and distant tree group establish some depth in F01/F03. Beyond the low ridge, the landscape becomes a thin, comparatively featureless strip under a large sky. There is no substantial distant landform or architectural silhouette drawing the eye onward, unlike the board and Palworld plateau reference. F02/F04 retain tree silhouettes but lose most intermediate terrain separation. No definite chunk seam is visible. These stills cannot establish LOD transitions.

6. **Interface.** The story instruction is legible and has clear hierarchy. It occupies a large part of the upper-right landscape, while the five-slot panel and control panel obscure much of the lower-right view in every frame. Empty dark slots with tiny white key labels communicate little at a glance. Health and food remain readable at full size, but their labels and the small time text weaken at reduced size. Visible elements stay inside the image boundaries. This assessment is independent of Palworld's UI.

7. **Artefacts.** F01/F03 show a notably flat, dark foreground bush whose repeated leaf silhouettes do not convey the volume suggested by its size. Grass and small flowers have a busy, jagged appearance, especially where they meet dark ground in F02/F04. The foreground earth appears blurred and blotchy beside sharp isolated stones and blades. These are visible material/presentation inconsistencies; no definite z-fighting or geometry intersection can be confirmed. Popping and motion aliasing are unjudgeable from stills.

8. **Scale agreement.** Using the trainer as the 1.80 m ruler, the nearby tree trunk is plausibly a large tree, stones read as small ground debris, and the distant gate and human figure do not show an obvious relative-scale contradiction. Perspective prevents precise comparison across the depression. The foreground bush is a substantial player-sized mass, which makes its flat rendering conspicuous. No creature is close and clear enough to compare to the trainer or another creature, so the required creature-scale agreement remains unverified.

## Three largest gaps, ranked

1. **Missing creature presence and unproven character appeal — F01–F04.** Palworld's exploration and base references, not just its combat shots, make distinctive creatures immediately visible beside the player. These frames offer a rear-facing trainer and no readable creature subject. **Scene-fixable:** stage recognizable creatures in the near or middle distance, give their silhouettes separation, and capture the trainer at an informative angle. **Art-dependent:** distinctive anatomy, expressive faces and coherent costume/creature design cannot be created by grass or lighting changes if the available art lacks them. These frames cannot establish whether suitable art exists elsewhere in the build.

2. **Terrain and foliage obscure rather than support the subjects — F01/F03, more severely F02/F04.** Palworld's open-field reference organizes grass around broad readable ground shapes; the key art uses vegetation masses and varied clearings. Here fine plant noise, mottled earth and the flat foreground bush compete for attention. Night further collapses that detail into black. **Scene-fixable:** simplify the immediate visual field, reposition or reduce the obstructing bush, improve shadow fill and separate ground, plants and characters by value. **Art-dependent:** the bush's flat leaf treatment and the mismatch between blurred ground and sharp foliage may require improved materials or foliage assets if existing options cannot provide consistent volume and surface treatment.

3. **Weak destination and distant-world composition — F01–F04.** The key art and Palworld plateau view lead through terrain toward a legible landmark and layered distance. The gate here is low, partly occluded and visually overwhelmed by sky, grass and interface; at night it nearly disappears. **Scene-fixable:** clear its approach, improve camera framing, strengthen its lighting and add readable intermediate/distant layers with available assets. **Art-dependent:** a distinctive landmark would require additional art only if no suitable asset exists; the images alone do not establish that inventory gap.

## Separate bar answers

**A. Belongs to the Meadows key-art world: no.** Green rolling banks, rustic props and blue skies share the board's ingredients, but the inviting settlement composition, layered landscape, companion presence and legible night destination are not achieved in either pair. The immediate work is predominantly scene composition, lighting and material cohesion; creature and landmark art sufficiency remains unproven.

**B. Reads as the same kind of game as the Palworld references: no.** These particular frames read as a generic third-person meadow exploration scene. They do not visibly establish the expressive creature companionship that identifies the reference game even outside combat. A better creature presentation could change this answer, but this set does not supply that evidence. No fight is shown, so combat spectacle is not judged.

**Commercial visual readiness: no.** The obstructive flat foreground foliage, heavily crushed night terrain and absent readable creature showcase leave both alternatives below the supplied shipping-game bar. The tie is not an acceptance pass.
