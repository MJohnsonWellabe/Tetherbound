# Blind visual review: F01–F04

Evidence: the four supplied frames, the Meadows key-art board, and all five supplied Palworld references. Judgments below concern visible appearance only; no source, change description, other visual report, or image provenance was used. Static images cannot establish motion defects, performance, combat quality, or the contents of an unseen asset library.

**Character and creature adequacy comes first.** In F01–F04, the trainer is recognizable as a stylized backpacked adventurer, with readable brown hair, blue sleeves, and a tan pack. That establishes a plausible genre figure, but the distant rear view does not establish facial expression, distinctive character design, or the expressive art quality of the Palworld cast. The legs lose separation against the grass. No creature is identifiable in any frame. These images therefore do not demonstrate the appeal, silhouette range, or trainer/creature relationships that make the comparison images immediately recognizable as creature adventures. An unseen creature is not evidence of a bad creature mesh, but it is a substantial failure of these views to sell that premise.

**Preferred pair: tie.** F01/F02 and F03/F04 present effectively the same strengths and defects. Minor blade arrangements do not produce a clear improvement in subject readability, ground organization, depth, or atmosphere. Neither pair earns a visible acceptance advantage.

## Eight criteria

1. **Silhouette and small-size readability.** In F01 and F03, the trainer's head and pack survive a thumbnail reading, while the legs merge into the surrounding blade pattern. F02 and F04 retain the pale sleeves and pack but lose more of the lower body into dark vegetation. Trees and rocks remain distinguishable on the opposite hill, although tiny tree silhouettes contribute little canopy mass. The enormous hill cut is more legible than any destination or creature. At small size the foreground becomes one textured band rather than useful clusters and openings.

2. **Colour and value structure.** F01/F03 have a coherent yellow-green land, pale blue water, and blue-grey sky, but the pale blade edges generate near-continuous light detail across the foreground. The distant grassy hill has a broad, rather uniform yellow-green value. F02/F04 clearly switch to a blue night palette, with cyan grass edges, dark ground, and very pale trees; those pale trees look disconnected from the otherwise dark hillside. A few warm points are too small to supply the welcoming inhabited contrast in the key-art night scene. No identifiable oxblood danger treatment is visible, so its reservation cannot be judged.

3. **Intentionality.** F01–F04 concentrate dense grass almost everywhere in the foreground, then expose a much simpler opposite hillside with isolated rocks and small tree groups. This density discontinuity reads as separate surface treatments more than an authored shore. The long straight cuts and flat terraces across the water strongly suggest a constructed terrain shape, without enough natural rock structure or vegetation to integrate it. There is no clear walking line, attractive shore access, creature grouping, or focal activity to organize the view. Palworld's open-field reference uses a broad path and purposeful clearings to make similar terrain readable.

4. **Lighting.** Day and night are unmistakable in F01/F03 versus F02/F04. The large cliff shadows give the hill some form. Daylight nevertheless leaves much of the foreground equally busy and bright, while the distant grass top reads almost flat. At night, bright blade edges and pale trees compete with the trainer rather than directing attention toward an encounter or warm destination. The trainer has conspicuously readable sleeves and pack, but no clear contact shadow can be separated from the grass around the feet. This is limited evidence, not a claim that the character floats.

5. **Horizon and depth.** The near grass, water strip, opposite hill, and more distant silhouettes establish distance in all frames. However, the central hill closes the view with a conspicuous straight incision and horizontal ledge; there is little layered landscape beyond it. The key art and Palworld plateau reference use overlapping terrain, substantial tree masses, and destination silhouettes to invite exploration. Water is a broad, nearly featureless band in F01/F03 and a stronger blue band in F02/F04; its limited surface cues weaken the transition between banks. No definite LOD or chunk seam can be established from these stills.

6. **Interface.** The quest text and location title are readable, and panels stay within the frame. The upper-right quest panel and lower-right slots/prompts occupy considerable scene area despite the sparse visible activity. The five dark slots communicate their small white numerals more clearly than their purpose. Bottom-left health and food text are harder to read because bright grass shows through the translucent bars, particularly in F01/F03. The minimap arrow is clear but the map itself communicates little detail at this image size. This assessment concerns the supplied interface directly, not comparison to Palworld UI.

7. **Artefacts.** Across all four frames, the far bank shows a jagged tooth-like grass-to-cliff boundary, and the central terrain cut has severe straight walls and abrupt ledges that read unfinished. Pale grass edges produce a dense wire-like appearance in F01/F03 and a bright cyan mesh of lines in F02/F04. The sky's dark, thin cloud fragments are particularly harsh at night. The small dark horizontal object near the water is too occluded and undetailed to read confidently as a dock or another prop. None of these stills proves z-fighting, temporal popping, or a specific shader defect.

8. **Scale agreement.** Using the stated 1.80 m trainer as the ruler, grass immediately beside the trainer reaches roughly the lower leg/knee region; the much larger apparent blades at the bottom edge also reflect their proximity to the camera. The grass is visually overdominant, but the image alone does not establish an impossible physical scale. Rocks and distant trees could be plausible given perspective; exact relative dimensions cannot be recovered across the water. No identifiable creature appears, so creature-to-trainer scale and relative combat-role scale cannot be judged. A creature scale pass is unproven.

## Three largest gaps, ranked

1. **The defining cast is missing from the scene's story — F01–F04.** Palworld's meadow, path, plateau, and base images visibly pair the human with distinctive creatures; the key-art day/night panels do likewise. These frames show a lone rear-view traveler. Scene-fixable: position visible creatures and use framing/clear ground that lets their silhouettes read. Art-dependent: expressive, distinctive creature and character design cannot be supplied by grass or lighting changes. These views do not show enough of that art to establish its adequacy or whether replacement art is necessary.

2. **Ground coverage has density without hierarchy — F01–F04.** The references combine foliage with paths, short ground, shaded clumps, rocks, and clear creature staging areas. Here long pale blades blanket the near half of the image and compete with the trainer. Primarily scene/material-fixable: create connected short-grass openings and shore access, vary grass height and cluster density, reduce the prominence of pale edges, and preserve quiet ground behind subjects. A higher blade count would not address this gap.

3. **The landscape landmark and shoreline look unfinished — F01–F04.** The references integrate rock faces, foliage, water, and destination silhouettes into layered terrain. Here the strongest landmark is a straight-sided incision in a sparsely dressed hill; the water remains a simple color strip. Scene-fixable: reshape or visually integrate the cut, break up the cliff edge, add coherent vegetation/rock groups, and create a clear destination and more convincing bank-to-water transition. Art-dependent if existing assets cannot provide it: convincing rock-face forms and a coherent water surface need appropriate visual assets/materials. The frames do not reveal what is available off camera.

## Verdicts

**A — Belongs to the Meadows key-art world: no.** The green landscape, blue water, backpacked trainer, and distinct blue night are compatible ingredients, but the composition lacks the board's substantial groves, integrated natural shore, inhabited focal points, and companion presence. This is a palette resemblance without enough of the landmark language and inviting mood. Most environmental gaps are addressable through scene composition, terrain, foliage distribution, and lighting; character/creature art adequacy remains unproven.

**B — Reads as the same kind of game as the Palworld references: no, on these frames.** A third-person traveler in grass establishes broad outdoor adventure, but no identifiable creature, creature interaction, inhabited activity, or expressive cast distinguishes this from a generic landscape exploration scene. This is a claim about what these images communicate, not a claim about unseen game systems. Showing the cast is scene-fixable; proving that its art meets the reference requires views that actually show it.

**Commercial visual readiness: no.** Both pairs retain dominant terrain cuts, grass noise, weak shoreline treatment, and inadequate presentation of the defining cast. Neither establishes the authored world or character appeal demonstrated by the real-time references. The tie does not constitute a pass.
