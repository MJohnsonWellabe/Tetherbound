# Blind visual judgment — GRASS-ROOTWALK01

## Scope and preference

Inspected the actual neutral frames F01, F02, F03 and F04, the Meadows key art, and all five palworld-0*.jpg references. No implementation, change description, history, or other judgment was consulted. This is a comparison of F01/F02 with F03/F04 at the same visible daytime/nighttime viewpoint, focused on ground foliage while retaining full-frame context. Repository routing and the visual-judge rubric were read as required.

**Preference: F03/F04, modestly.** The difference is most useful at night: F04 has darker lower grass and less continuous pale coverage than F02, so the foreground reads more like vegetation attached to a dark hillside. F03 similarly gives the daytime blades slightly more base-to-tip separation than F01, but the daytime difference is subtle. This is a local material/readability improvement, not a transformation of the scene. Both pairs retain the same conspicuous carpet of long, pale, similarly shaped blades. Neither pair passes the commercial-quality comparison.

**Character and creature art:** The trainer in all four frames has a coherent backpack, clothing, and human silhouette, but the rear view is too small to establish facial appeal, expression, or cast quality. The narrow lower-body silhouette becomes entangled with the grass. No identifiable companion or wild creature is visible, so these frames do not demonstrate the creature appeal, presence, or expressive identity central to the Palworld references. Absence in this viewpoint is a composition finding, not evidence about the whole game's population or asset inventory.

## All eight criteria

1. **Silhouette and small-size readability.** In F01–F04, water, hillside, trainer torso, rocks, and distant trees remain identifiable when considering the image at thumbnail scale. The trainer's blue-and-cream shoulders and brown pack are the clearest small focal shape. The long foreground blades merge into a busy pale field; the trainer's boots and legs lose separation within it. F04 reduces the bright aggregate grass field relative to F02, a useful improvement, but does not create a deliberate quiet area around the character. The small flowering plant left of the trainer disappears into the grass at small size in every frame.

2. **Colour and value structure.** F01/F03 use an understandable meadow palette, but pale mint grass tips occupy most of the lower frame while the exposed substrate is yellow-green. This separates blade and ground colour more strongly than it separates meaningful vegetation groups. F03's darker bases slightly help. F02/F04 read blue night, but cyan-white blades and pale treetops can resemble frost or self-lit outlines against near-black ground. F04 reduces this effect; it remains visible. The trainer has useful warm/cool contrast. There is no conspicuous oxblood leakage onto friendly elements in this set.

3. **Intentionality.** F01–F04 blanket the near slope with very similar upright tapering blade forms. Small irregular holes exist, yet there is little clear structure of short grass, taller clumps, broadleaf patches, flowers, bare soil, or a walkable edge. The distant hillside's few trees and rocks sit on broad uniform green surfaces. The result reads as a dense scatter over a simple landform. The thin brown shoreline object is not sufficient to make the named location feel inhabited. F03/F04 improve shading without meaningfully changing these compositional cues.

4. **Lighting.** Day and night are unambiguous. F01/F03 show useful large-scale shadows on the far cliff, but the foreground vegetation stays evenly pale over a broad area; individual blade brightness dominates subtle ground form. F02's bright grass edges over deep black roots are especially disconnected. F04 integrates roots better while preserving readable night vegetation. In F02/F04, the trainer's light shirt remains remarkably bright relative to the adjacent ground, and distant treetops have a uniformly silver appearance. The night lacks the small warm points and selective light emphasis that give the key art a welcoming, mysterious focus. Contact detail around feet is obscured by blades, so grounding cannot be judged precisely there.

5. **Horizon and depth.** F01–F04 have a clear near hillside, lake, opposite bank, and distant sky; the lake provides welcome visual rest. Nevertheless, the dominant opposite hill has a broad straight-sided cut and a large smooth gray wall, with tiny tree groups along its top. It reads as a coarse terrain construction more readily than a memorable natural destination. Grass-covered tops meet bare rock through abrupt, repeated sawtooth edges, most apparent in F01/F03. The far-right pale mountain gives another distance layer, but neither pair creates the layered woodland and landmark progression of the references. No temporal LOD transition can be inferred from these stills.

6. **Interface.** All frames keep panels within the image boundary. The story objective is readable and the bottom actions have an understandable order. However, the large objective box consumes much of the upper-right landscape and the broad empty five-slot bar adds a substantial dark block above the action strip. Tiny white keycap labels are difficult to parse at small size. Lower-left status text has weak contrast through the vegetation. The place name and time are legible but compete for the same central sky area. These are internal hierarchy findings; UI is not compared against Palworld as a quality gate.

7. **Artifacts.** F01–F04 expose strongly repeated sharp blade silhouettes and hard grass-to-bare-rock boundaries. F02/F04 have dark broken streaks in the clouds, which make the night sky feel ragged rather than softly layered. The grass density and pale edges create visible high-frequency noise in all frames; motion shimmer is unverified. No definite z-fighting, animated popping, or collision error is established. There is no confident evidence of a new static geometry defect unique to F03/F04.

8. **Scale agreement.** Using the trainer as the stated 1.80 m reference, adjacent grass reaches roughly shin/knee height, with taller apparent foreground blades enlarged by perspective. That is plausible meadow vegetation, though its near-uniform tallness suppresses the character's lower silhouette. Nearby flowering plants are plausible beside the trainer. Distant trees and boulders cannot be reliably measured against the trainer across the lake; their small skyline silhouettes weaken the sense of a substantial wooded landscape, but do not prove incorrect metre scale. No creature-to-trainer or creature-to-creature scale comparison is available.

## Three largest gaps from the references

1. **Ground is dense but monotonous and visually sharp — F01–F04.** Palworld 02, 03 and 05 combine softer grass masses with readable dirt, short growth, clumps, flowers, and shadowed gaps. Here pale upright blades dominate almost the whole foreground, including the character's feet. F04's darker roots are a small step toward integration. Establish variation in height, coverage, broadleaf mass, and material response so density serves composition.

2. **Large landforms and vegetation do not form an authored place — F01–F04.** Palworld 04 and the key art organize substantial tree groups, broken rock faces, traversable ground, and recognizable destinations into successive depth layers. This view is dominated by a sparsely dressed, straight-cut hill and an almost featureless water strip. Break up the cliff silhouette and grass boundary, group trees and shoreline plants, and provide a visible destination with believable approach terrain.

3. **The scene does not present the game's creature identity — F01–F04.** Palworld 01, 03 and 04 make expressive creatures strong, differentiated focal silhouettes; even Palworld 05 makes the base feel occupied by distinct characters. These frames offer only the trainer's rear view in grass. A visible companion or encounter, placed with deliberate silhouette separation, would make the genre promise legible. These images cannot establish whether current creature art would then meet the reference quality.

## Separate bar answers

**A — Does this read as belonging to the Meadows key-art world? No.** Green hills, blue water, a backpacked traveler, and distinct day/night palettes point toward it, but the defining lush woodland grouping, flower-rich meadow structure, natural rocky landforms, and inhabited focal places are not convincing here. The night has the board's blue direction without its selective warmth and depth. This is a palette/composition/mood comparison, not a demand for painted per-leaf detail.

**B — Would someone say this is trying to be the same kind of game as the Palworld references? Yes.** The third-person stylized traveler, open explorable landscape, vegetation, and creature call-out controls communicate the broad creature-adventure/survival family. The lack of visible creatures makes this weakly demonstrated in the world itself. This genre resemblance does not establish art-quality parity.

**Commercial-quality parity with the supplied Palworld screenshots? No, for either pair.** Repetitive bright grass, coarse terrain transitions, sparse environmental composition, and the absence of a creature focal subject keep these frames visibly below that bar. F03/F04 are preferable within the comparison, but the size of the improvement does not change this verdict.

**Scene-fixable work:** Grass material brightness and root grounding; variation in scatter density and height; readable short-growth clearings; vegetation and rock grouping; shoreline composition; cliff/grass transition treatment; selective night lighting; a companion framed against an uncluttered value region; HUD sizing and contrast.

**Art-dependent work:** Softer and more varied foliage forms, better cliff surface/silhouette vocabulary, and expressive creature/character presentation may require improved assets if the installed set cannot supply them. These pictures establish the visible deficits but cannot establish which suitable assets are absent from the build. The small rear-facing trainer is insufficient evidence to require a new trainer model, and no unseen creature asset can be accepted or condemned.

## Static limits

This judgment covers one location, one camera, and two times of day. It does not measure performance, traversal, wind behavior, temporal aliasing, LOD popping, animation, combat readability, or the rest of the world. F02 and F04 show different minute labels, so the night comparison is not an assertion of exact temporal equality. No source-level cause is claimed for any observed difference.
