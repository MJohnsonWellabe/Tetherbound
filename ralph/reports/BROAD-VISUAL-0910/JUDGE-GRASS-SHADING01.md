# Blind visual verdict — F01 / F02 / F03

Evidence: the three neutral same-location daytime images in `.artifacts/broad-visual-0910/blind-grass-shading01/`, `docs/reference/tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`, and all five supplied `palworld-0*.jpg` images. Full visual-judge rubric applied. No source, implementation explanation, prior verdict, or performance evidence used to choose an alternative.

## Preference

**F01 is visibly preferable, modestly, for overall ground and foliage presentation.** Its grass has a less conspicuous pale edge/tip presence and sits more quietly against the green ground. F02 presents the most chalky, busy grass surface, especially across the middle-distance crest. F03 retains conspicuous pale blades with stronger dark interruptions between them; the result still reads as sharp individual strips rather than a coherent meadow surface. F01's advantage is reduced harshness, not a substantial improvement in authored landscape quality. None passes the commercial visual-quality bar.

At small viewing size, the alternatives converge considerably. The choice is useful for this view but does not establish a robust preference across other locations, weather, camera distances, or movement.

## Eight rubric domains

1. **Silhouette and small-size readability.** In F01–F03 the trainer's brown hair, blue sleeve and backpack remain identifiable against the water and ground. The lower legs compete with upright grass and dark ground marks. Individual shrubs become weak dark specks among a much louder field of slender blades. F01 is slightly less visually abrasive; none gives foliage distinct readable masses. No creature is visible, so creature appeal, silhouette separation, or suitability as the game's central attraction cannot be judged. The trainer reads as a stylized adventurer, but this small side/back view cannot establish facial expression or character-art quality against the reference cast.

2. **Colour and value structure.** All three combine yellow-green mottled ground, pale blue-green grass highlights, cyan water and a grey-blue sky. The foreground has local contrast, but much of it is tiny bright/dark marks rather than useful large value groups. F01 better contains this high-frequency contrast. F02's pale grass surface is the least cohesive with its warmer ground. F03's pale blades and dark bases remain conspicuous. These images share a palette, but the key art's natural greens organized around shadowed groves and warm clearings are absent. No visible oxblood element supplies evidence about danger-colour discipline.

3. **Intentionality.** F01–F03 have abundant blades but little readable meadow composition: broadly continuous coverage, scattered isolated plants, and few convincing transitions between low cover, taller patches and exposed soil. Ground mottling is particularly exposed around the trainer and across the lower foreground. The reference open-field path and base-building images organize vegetation around paths, activities and openings; these frames provide no comparable spatial cue. More blades alone would not solve this.

4. **Lighting.** All three clearly read as daylight. Trainer shading and dark ground marks provide some grounding, but the low mound is mainly described by its outline and texture rather than broad, legible light-and-shade changes. The grass has a brittle pale appearance, most evident in F02. F01 is gentler without developing the deeper shaded vegetation masses visible in the Palworld meadow and plateau references. No conclusion about the rendering technique follows from this observation.

5. **Horizon and depth.** F01–F03 devote most of the upper view to sky and smooth pale water. The far bank is a thin, nearly straight green strip, with a small piece of shore at right largely covered by the objective panel. Broad cyan bands and a diagonal tonal boundary in the water attract attention without clearly explaining depth or shoreline form. The scene has foreground/background separation but very little intermediate landscape. The key art and Palworld plateau image use overlapping landforms, trees and landmarks to sustain depth and invite exploration.

6. **Interface.** F01–F03 have readable objective text and a recognizable player marker. The large objective panel hides much of the only visible distant shore detail. The two broad lower panels cover substantial foreground space while the five slot labels themselves are tiny. Bottom-left health and food text has weak contrast over bright grass; the time label is small against a patterned sky. These are internal readability findings, not comparisons with Palworld's UI design.

7. **Artefacts.** In F01–F03 the far horizontal land/water strip looks unusually flat and discontinuously streaked; the water has conspicuous broad angular tonal transitions. The foreground grass exposes repeated narrow straight-edged forms with very bright edges. These are visible presentation defects or suspicions, not proof of a seam, incorrect mesh, or particular shader problem. A still does not demonstrate z-fighting, temporal shimmer, popping, or movement quality.

8. **Scale agreement.** Using the trainer as the 1.80 m ruler, much of the immediate grass appears roughly shin-to-knee high, with taller scattered plants; this is a plausible meadow scale. No obvious trainer/nearby-plant scale contradiction is established. The empty distance and lack of comparable nearby trees, rocks, buildings or creatures prevent meaningful broader scale checks. Perspective makes the distant shore unsuitable as a size ruler.

## Three largest reference gaps, ranked

1. **Landscape depth and an identifiable destination — F01–F03.** The key art's rolling terrain, tree groups and settlements, and Palworld 04's rock formations and distant landmark, create successive readable spaces. Here a grass cap ends against a large pale expanse and almost featureless horizon. Scene composition, landform staging and placement of available landscape assets could address this.

2. **Foliage and ground cohesion — F01–F03, strongest in F02.** Palworld 02, 03 and 05 integrate ground cover with softer vegetation masses, clearings and shade. These frames foreground pale blade edges, repeated upright forms and mottled exposed ground. Material/lighting balance, patch variation, clustering and ground transitions are scene/material work. If existing foliage cannot produce varied silhouettes, additional or revised foliage art may be needed; these images cannot establish what assets are available.

3. **A world with subjects and activities — F01–F03.** The Palworld references make expressive creatures, encounters or constructed places central to their compositions. The key art similarly frames companionship and inhabited destinations. These images present a solitary trainer with no visible companion, encounter, settlement or strong environmental focal point. Scene selection and content placement can improve the shown experience. Whether the build possesses suitable creature and environmental art is not knowable here; no unseen asset should be accepted or condemned.

## Bar answers

**A — Belongs to the key-art world: No.** Green vegetation, blue water and a stylized traveler provide a broad connection, but the flat, empty distance and harsh, uniform grass surface do not carry the board's rolling, sheltered, inhabited meadow identity. The missing composition, vegetation grouping and lighting hierarchy are addressable in the scene; required new art cannot be determined from these views.

**B — Same kind of game as the supplied Palworld images: Yes, narrowly.** The third-person stylized traveler, outdoor landscape, survival meters, building action and creature-call controls make an open-world creature-adventure intention recognizable. This is a genre/intention judgment, not an art-quality pass, and no visible creature substantiates the core promise.

**Commercial visual quality beside those Palworld images: No.** All three remain substantially behind in landscape staging, coherent ground/foliage presentation and visible focal content. F01's modest local preference does not change this answer.
