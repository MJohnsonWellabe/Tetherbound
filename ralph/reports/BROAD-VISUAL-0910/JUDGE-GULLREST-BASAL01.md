# Blind visual judgment: F01–F04

Evidence: neutral F01/F02 and F03/F04 day/night pairs; `tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`; all five `palworld-0*.jpg` references. Judgment is confined to visible pixels. No source, prior verdicts, change explanation, or performance evidence was used. Repository instructions and the visual-judge rubric were read.

## Pair preference

**No material visual preference between F01/F02 and F03/F04.** F01 and F03 present essentially the same daytime composition, bare sand footprint, cliff face, and grass-covered right slope. F02 and F04 likewise preserve the same night coverage and dark ground masses. Individual grass strokes differ slightly, but neither pair visibly establishes a fuller basal layer or a better transition into the bare foreground. Those small differences do not justify calling a density improvement. F03's location label and the minimap differences do not decide the environmental comparison.

The trainer is recognizable as an adventurer, but the rear view presents a fairly generic backpack-and-tunic identity; its small, busy texture detail does little to establish a memorable character at reduced size. No creature is clearly identifiable in any of F01–F04. The small green/white glow near the cliff cannot support a creature-art verdict. These frames therefore do not demonstrate the expressive creature appeal that carries the Palworld references.

## Eight criteria

1. **Silhouette and small-size readability.** In F01/F03, the trainer's dark outline reads cleanly against sand, while the cliff and right grass bank are identifiable large masses. At roughly thumbnail scale, the grass becomes pale stippling and the green/white object becomes a light fleck; neither reads as a substantial bush or recognizable creature. F02/F04 retain the blue-and-white trainer but lose much of the bank into black. There is no distinct creature silhouette or strong destination silhouette to draw the eye beyond the trainer.

2. **Colour and value structure.** F01/F03 have an understandable warm sand / olive rock / yellow-green vegetation / blue sky palette, broadly compatible with a natural fantasy setting. The huge sand and cliff blocks dominate instead of providing a varied, connected meadow. F02/F04 clearly shift to navy night, but the vegetation's very bright cyan-white strokes sit over a nearly black base and read more wiry than leafy. No visible oxblood leakage is apparent. Both pairs are internally consistent; neither shows a meaningful palette advantage.

3. **Intentionality.** In every frame, the grass ends along a conspicuous bank boundary, leaving a very large, nearly uninterrupted foreground plane. Repeated small upright grass marks across the slope provide surface noise without a convincing combination of low ground cover, thicker clumps, occasional bushes, and irregular bare pockets. The sand-to-rock edge has repeated pointed wedges. Together with the broad uninterrupted cliff texture, this reads assembled rather than a particular authored rest location. Palworld-02 and -05 retain bare usable ground while breaking its edges with irregular tufts and nearby activity.

4. **Lighting.** Day and night are unambiguous in F01/F03 versus F02/F04. The daytime trainer shadow anchors the body, and the cliff has a light-to-shade change near its upper right shoulder. Most of its enormous facing surface still has little broad relief. Night preserves player visibility and a local glow, but the trainer is much brighter than the immediately adjacent ground, and the grass tips remain unusually prominent over their dark substrate. The key art's night scene uses several depth layers and warm inhabited light to give the darkness interest; those cues are absent here.

5. **Horizon and depth.** All four frames are dominated by the near cliff, with only a narrow upper-right corridor toward a pale slope and small distant vegetation. Occlusion establishes near versus far, but there is little sequence of midground forms, inviting vista, or readable landmark. Palworld-04 and the key art use overlapping ridges, vegetation, structures, and distant focal points to make space legible. A confined path need not copy an open vista, but this view needs a readable reason to follow its bend. No definite LOD or chunk seam can be established from these stills.

6. **Interface.** In F01–F04, the objective panel and bottom command strip are legible at full frame size and respect the screen edge. They occupy substantial view area, particularly over the only open route on the right. The five empty-looking numbered slots communicate little about the creature team. Health and food use comparatively weak contrast, while the tiny key symbols become difficult at reduced size. The minimap is largely an undifferentiated mint field and supplies little local terrain context. These observations concern this interface alone, not a comparison with Palworld UI.

7. **Artefacts.** F01–F04 show a conspicuous fine repeating sand texture, an enlarged polygonal/mottled cliff surface, and a hard material/shape junction with repeated triangular tan wedges along the cliff foot. These patterns read like terrain construction showing through the scene. The narrow bright grass strokes are visually harsh, especially in F02/F04. No definite z-fighting is visible. Popping, temporal shimmer, animation problems, and traversal behaviour cannot be judged from these frames.

8. **Scale agreement.** Using the trainer as the 1.80 m ruler, the immediate cliff reads as a plausible large landform and the backpack and outfit have mutually plausible proportions. Grass is farther away and uphill, so a precise height claim would be unreliable; the visible clumps do not establish an obvious scale contradiction. There is no clearly readable creature beside the trainer, no comparable roster pair, and no substantial nearby prop or building. Creature-to-trainer and creature-role scale are therefore unverified, not passed.

## Three largest reference gaps, ranked

1. **Ground and cliff need intermediate forms, not just texture.** F01/F03 expose a huge uniformly textured foreground and mottled rock wall; F02/F04 darken the same structure without resolving it. Palworld-02/-04/-05 combine readable grass masses, irregular path margins, trees, rock relief, and smaller grounding details. Break the cliff foot with irregular stones and clustered plants; blend grass into sand through low cover and sparse outlying tufts; introduce ledges or broken rock silhouettes to interrupt the wall. Most of this is scene composition, coverage, materials, and terrain-form work. Whether suitable assets exist cannot be inferred from images.

2. **The view does not communicate a creature-centered adventure.** F01–F04 offer a small rear-view trainer and no readable creature subject. Palworld-01/-03/-04/-05 make distinctive creatures immediately identifiable through silhouette, colour blocks, expression, and scale; even Palworld-02 has visible encounter activity along its road. Put recognizable creatures into the route composition at a useful screen size and give the trainer/creature pairing a clear visual relationship. Population and placement are scene-fixable. If close views still show generic character or creature art, that requires art work; these distant frames cannot establish that missing assets are the cause.

3. **There is no visual payoff around the bend.** F01–F04 direct attention into a cliff and a narrow, mostly obscured exit. The key art connects paths to water, settlements, trees, and landmarks; Palworld-04 supplies a layered destination even during travel. Compose a midground anchor visible past the bank, vary the path edge to lead toward it, and give night a local warm or otherwise intentional focal light instead of relying on an isolated white fleck. These are principally layout, lighting, and scene-dressing changes. A bespoke landmark may need additional art, but the frames do not prove that existing art cannot serve.

## Bar answers

**A — Does this read as belonging to the key-art world? No.** The basic natural day palette and blue night are compatible, but compatibility alone does not establish the identity. The bare foreground, featureless dominating cliff, sparse visual hierarchy, and absent readable companion miss the welcoming, inhabited meadow and layered mystery of the board. This is primarily a scene, terrain-form, coverage, and lighting gap; a categorical demand for new assets is not supported by these frames.

**B — Does this appear to be trying to be the same kind of game as Palworld? Yes, at the broad genre level.** A third-person adventurer, natural terrain, satiety, building/call-out prompts, and creature slots communicate an outdoor creature adventure. **Commercial visual parity: No.** Neither pair approaches the reference ground integration, spatial richness, immediately appealing creature presence, or strong focal composition. The genre answer must not be treated as acceptance of shipping visual quality. Scene changes can address much of the environment and creature visibility; the quality of close creature art remains unassessed until an identifiable creature is actually shown.

No performance, frame-rate, or motion conclusions are made.
