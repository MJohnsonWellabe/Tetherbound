No creature is sufficiently visible to judge its design or expressive quality. ## Full eight-part rubric

1. **Silhouette and small-size readability.** In F01/F03 the trainer's head, backpack, arms, and separated legs survive a small-size reading against light sand. Hair and pack offer a basic adventurer identity, but the stationary pose and distant rear framing do not demonstrate bespoke character appeal comparable with the Palworld cast. In F02/F04 the blue sleeves remain useful while the torso and lower body collapse toward black; the player remains locatable, but costume identity is substantially poorer. The white-green object at the grass boundary cannot be confidently identified as a creature or prop in any frame. At small size the environment becomes three simple areas: wall, sand, and grass.

2. **Colour and value structure.** F01/F03 have useful contrast between warm sand, gray-green rock, and the blue-clothed trainer. However, the bright, uniformly busy sand takes most of the visual attention. F02/F04 communicate night through navy terrain and sky, but the character lacks enough local value separation to retain its design. The pale cyan grass stalks become an unusually prominent texture against the black hillside. No visible friendly oxblood misuse is established.

3. **Intentionality.** All four frames present a broad empty sand area meeting an almost continuous wall and a mechanically dense band of similarly thin grass stalks. There are few intermediate clusters, worn edges, shoreline objects, or authored points of interest. The wall-to-sand boundary has repeated angular protrusions. A narrow opening at upper right suggests a route, but the picture does little to make it a destination. This reads as terrain coverage rather than a composed beach habitat.

4. **Lighting.** Day and night are unambiguous in F01/F03 and F02/F04 respectively. The daytime trainer shadow visibly grounds the feet. Broad wall shading gives some volume, but the uniform surface pattern and nearly continuous bank dominate over geological form. The nighttime trainer needs selective readable planes on the pack, head, and legs; this is not a request to brighten the entire night. The Once Human reference keeps a dark player while preserving stronger material and silhouette cues, and the key-art night panel uses layered blue values and distant warm accents to establish mood and orientation. F02/F04 have neither comparable layering nor a strong destination accent.

5. **Horizon and depth.** The huge nearby wall obscures most depth in every frame. Only a narrow sky wedge and distant trees remain, partly covered by the objective panel. There is no visible water horizon, coastal landmark, or layered shore to establish Gull Rest's beach identity. The key art and Palworld plateau frame use several landscape distances and recognizable landmarks; these views stop at a bank. No definite LOD or chunk seam can be established from these stills.

6. **Interface.** The objective is readable and panels stay inside the image. However, the task box occupies the useful opening toward the route. The bright green minimap reveals almost no meaningful terrain information. Slot numbers are tiny white marks inside empty dark boxes; F03/F04 devote substantially more area to this absence. The bottom hint strip is large relative to the trainer, while the food label is low-contrast in F01/F03. These observations use the interface's own legibility and hierarchy, not Palworld's UI as a design standard.

7. **Artefacts.** F01/F03 expose a visibly repetitive, angular mottled rock treatment over the entire wall and uniformly fine sand texture over a large foreground area. The sand-rock join reads serrated in all four frames. F02/F04 make the very pale, thin grass edges look noisy and disconnected from the dark ground. These are visible surface/integration defects; the stills do not establish their technical causes. No definite z-fighting, animation failure, or temporal popping is demonstrated.

8. **Scale agreement.** The trainer is a usable 1.80 m reference: backpack, limbs, and feet appear internally plausible, and the wall reads as a tall landform. There is no clearly visible companion or nearby settlement prop for a reliable relative-scale comparison. The distant glowing object cannot be measured against the foreground trainer without knowing its distance. No creature-size acceptance claim is supported by these frames.

## Ranked three largest reference gaps

1. **Authored environment and beach identity — F01–F04.** Palworld's path and plateau frames surround readable travel space with layered vegetation, rock forms, creatures, and destinations. The key art gives water, banks, paths, and settlements distinct silhouettes. These images give empty textured sand and one enormous wall, with no visible shoreline identity. Recompose the route and landform, introduce clustered bank transitions and recognizable coastal points of interest, and preserve useful open walking space.

2. **Character presentation, especially at night — F02/F04; limited appeal evidence in F01/F03.** Palworld makes the cast legible and expressive; the key-art night scene retains the human/companion relationship, and the supplied dark Enshrouded scenes retain strong subject contours and lit planes. Here the nighttime trainer is mostly a black center shape with blue arms. Recover selective material and outline separation and present a clearly recognizable creature alongside the person. A rear still cannot prove facial or animation quality, and no visible creature design earns a pass here.

3. **Material and vegetation integration — F01–F04.** Palworld's ground and Enshrouded's foregrounds combine foliage masses, small plants, exposed surfaces, and relief at different scales. These frames rely on fine uniform sand noise, broad polygon-pattern rock, and pale stick-like grass. Improve scale variation, rock form, surface transitions, foliage clustering, and nighttime grass response.

## Required bar answers

**A — Key-art world: NO.** The warm ground and blue-clothed traveler in daylight, and the blue night palette, are partial matches. The barren enclosure, weak beach identity, missing destination hierarchy, and poor nighttime cast presentation do not convey the board's welcoming, inhabited, layered natural world.

**B — Trying to be the same kind of game as Palworld: YES.** The stylized third-person traveler, natural terrain, five-slot companion-style interface, and visible Call Out/Change Creature prompts communicate that intended genre. This is a statement about recognizable intent, not production quality or a creature-art pass.

**Commercial-quality comparison remains FAIL**, including against the supplemental Once Human/Enshrouded visual bar. Their dark scenes demonstrate that night can retain authored space, material distinctions, and focal subjects; darkness does not excuse the shortcomings here.

Scene-level work can address composition, ground/grass transitions, clustering, route framing, selective night lighting, and HUD footprint. The visible rock/grass treatment also needs material or art improvement if scene tuning cannot remove its repetitive and thin appearance. As a blind judge I cannot establish what art is installed elsewhere in the build; these frames do not justify claiming that a new humanoid or creature mesh is required. They also do not demonstrate adequate creature art. That needs a readable creature view before an asset-level verdict can be made.
