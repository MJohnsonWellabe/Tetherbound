But it loses clothing identity and internal form, especially when mentally reduced to contact-sheet size. F02's brighter character is somewhat isolated from its dark surroundings, whereas F04 is more uniformly dark with the scene; the substantial readability gain in F02 is more useful than that tonal integration in F04. Neither night frame makes the face assessable from this rear view.

## Eight criteria

1. **Silhouette and readability at small size.** Across F01–F04, the trainer, cliff and grass bank remain distinguishable, but nearly everything that survives reduction is a large terrain mass. F01/F03 separate the trainer cleanly against gold sand. F02 retains costume colour blocks at night; F04 mostly retains only the human outline. The tiny distant green/glowing form cannot communicate species or behaviour. Palworld's open-field and plateau frames keep several unmistakable creature shapes in the playable view.

2. **Colour and value structure.** F01/F03 have clear light-sand/dark-cliff separation, with teal clothing fitting the board's natural palette. However, the dominant yellow sand and grey-green wall overpower the small vegetation strip; this is a narrow palette sample rather than the board's green, flower-accented, layered landscape. F02/F04 clearly share one blue night world, but the cyan-white grass tips are conspicuously bright against near-black vegetation bases. F04 compresses the trainer's internal values too far. No visible oxblood contamination can be established here.

3. **Intentionality.** In all four frames the enormous bare foreground, uninterrupted rounded cliff and grass terminating in a fairly abrupt border read like a terrain demonstration. There is little local storytelling, no readable destination, and no distinctive beach dressing in this camera direction. The bank's similarly thin upright grass strokes create a repeated comb-like surface. Palworld's path shot breaks its clear route with varied vegetation and rock forms; its base shot makes the open ground meaningful through inhabited activity. These frames do not need objects everywhere, but their emptiness needs a compositional purpose.

4. **Lighting.** Day versus night is immediately apparent. F01/F03 have a useful trainer cast shadow anchoring the feet and a strong cliff shade mass. The cliff's mottled texture does more work than sculpted light in describing its surface. F02/F04 preserve enough ground brightness for navigation, but the bright grass tips and isolated glow attract attention more readily than a world landmark. F02 gives the trainer useful fill; F04 leaves its body under-described. The supplementary dark Enshrouded and Once Human images show that a dark character can retain selected material planes and silhouette edges within a convincingly dark environment; F04 loses too much of that information.

5. **Horizon and depth.** The cliff consumes most of the upper-left view in every frame, leaving a small sky wedge and a glimpse of receding terrain at upper right. This produces enclosure but little inviting depth or destination. The key art and Palworld plateau use distinct near, middle and far forms and recognisable landmarks; the Enshrouded vista also demonstrates clearly separated distant layers. This beach view supplies almost no comparable visual promise. No definite terrain chunk seam or LOD transition is identifiable from these stills.

6. **Interface.** All four keep interface elements inside the screen and make the main objective readable. The clock is low contrast, health/food text is subdued, and the minimap is mostly an undifferentiated green patch with little terrain information. The empty five-slot panel and bottom command strip draw substantial attention; F03/F04 enlarge the empty panel vertically without adding visible information. These are intrinsic hierarchy/readability observations, not comparisons with Palworld UI.

7. **Artefacts.** F01–F04 show a conspicuously repetitive, pebbled sand texture and coarse tiled/mottled cliff surface. The cliff-to-sand contact has a visibly scalloped, tooth-like edge that reads unnaturally regular in places. The fine grass strokes have harsh bright edging, especially F02/F04. These are visible surface/presentation defects; a still cannot establish z-fighting, temporal shimmer, popping, or their technical cause. No definite floating trainer or geometry penetration is visible.

8. **Scale agreement.** Using the specified 1.80 m trainer as the ruler, the immediate sand texture is visually coarse and draws attention at a scale that competes with clothing detail. The cliff can plausibly be a large landform, and the bank vegetation does not show an unambiguous metre-scale contradiction because it is farther away. There are no sufficiently clear adjacent creatures, props or trees to establish comparative size errors. Companion-versus-trainer or species-role scale cannot be passed from these frames.

## Three ranked gaps from the references

1. **Character-and-creature focal appeal is weak or absent — F01–F04, most acutely F04.** Palworld's screenshots put distinctive creatures and identifiable characters at the centre of the experience, even in an open path or base. This view offers a small rear-facing idle trainer and no readable creature presence; F04 also loses most costume information. Scene/light/composition changes can restore player readability and stage existing creatures. Whether additional character or creature art is required cannot be determined from this rear view and distant glow; brighter lighting alone would not demonstrate facial expression or species appeal.

2. **Large empty surfaces lack authored material and habitat structure — F01–F04.** Palworld's paths and clearings have varied grass edges, rock structure, neighbouring activity and points of interest. Here the sand repeats across most of the foreground and the cliff is a single heavily mottled wall, with an abrupt grass bank alongside it. Scene dressing, terrain shapes, material scale/blending and clustered planting are direct scene/material work. The frames do not establish whether suitable beach props or rock assets are already installed, so any missing-asset requirement remains unproven.

3. **The view lacks the reference world's depth and invitation — F01–F04.** The key art's paths, water, settlements and landmarks make the next place visible, while Palworld's plateau builds a readable progression of spaces. This view ends visually at a wall with a narrow escape of sky. Composition, route-facing landmarks, layered landforms and lighting could address this with scene work. A bespoke landmark would need art only if no suitable existing asset can carry it; the images cannot answer that inventory question.

## Bar answers

No.** Natural sand/green daylight and blue night are compatible ingredients, but the dominant blank wall, broad repeated sand and lack of a welcoming layered vista do not carry the board's composition, landmark language or lived-in mood. This is a judgement of direction and presentation, not painted-detail fidelity. The visible failure is primarily scene composition, habitat/material treatment and lighting; these images do not prove that replacement character or creature art is necessary.

Yes.** The stylised backpacked third-person adventurer in an outdoor world, together with creature-oriented on-screen commands, plausibly signals the same broad exploration/creature-adventure intent. That answer is weaker from world imagery alone because no readable companion or encounter is shown.
