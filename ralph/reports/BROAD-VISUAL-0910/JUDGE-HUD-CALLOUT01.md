# Blind visual judgment: HUD callout comparison

Evidence: F01–F04 only, compared with `tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png` and the five `palworld-0*.jpg` references. F01/F03 are day; F02/F04 are night. Static images cannot establish motion, performance, or interaction behavior. No scores.

**Preference: F03/F04 over F01/F02, narrowly and for interface clarity only.** F01/F02 repeat the call-out action in the bottom navigation strip and the separate “Call out Biscuit” prompt. F03/F04 retain the specific prompt and omit the generic duplicate; their shorter strip gives more of the lower center back to the scene. The world and character presentation are otherwise a visual tie. The improvement does not change the overall art verdict.

The trainer is recognizable as an adventurer in every frame, but its small, visually busy rear view offers little of the expressive, immediately distinctive character presence in the Palworld references. There is no clearly readable companion in the foreground. The small luminous green form at the right cliff base cannot carry creature appeal or establish a creature silhouette. These frames therefore provide weak evidence for the central creature promise, and cannot establish creature-art or relative creature-scale quality.

## Eight criteria

1. **Silhouette and small-size readability.** In F01/F03 the blue-shirted trainer separates from the yellow sand, and the backpack remains identifiable when mentally reducing the frame. In F02/F04 the bright sleeves and backpack separate, but the dark legs merge with the ground. Across all four, the giant left rock face and empty sand dominate; the possible creature at the cliff base becomes a white-green speck rather than an identifiable animal. Thin grass becomes visual noise at small size.

2. **Colour and value structure.** F01/F03 have clear warm ground versus cool grey rock, but the yellow sand fills most of the lower view with repetitive bright detail and little broad value variation. F02/F04 replace that with brown-black ground, a saturated navy cliff and unusually bright cyan grass edges. Those night components feel less like one coherent natural surface palette. No visible oxblood usage can be evaluated here.

3. **Intentionality.** All four show a nearly featureless sand clearing beside a uniformly mottled cliff and an abrupt bank of similar thin grass blades. The scene lacks the clustered rock, plant and incidental-prop arrangements that make the reference paths feel authored and inhabited. The luminous form near the cliff is the only small focal accent, and its placement is too obscure to organize the composition.

4. **Lighting.** Day is unmistakable in F01/F03, with a legible cast shadow grounding the trainer. Night is unmistakable in F02/F04, but terrain form collapses into dark masses while the grass maintains conspicuous pale outlines. The trainer remains readable, yet the environment offers little local light guidance or the warm focal lights seen in the key art's night panel.

5. **Horizon and depth.** In every frame the close left cliff consumes roughly half the scene and the right slope closes the remaining opening. The narrow sky notch gives little sense of a destination or a larger world. This may be a valid local corridor, but these compositions do not demonstrate the layered landmarks, distant planes and inviting route progression of the references. No definite LOD or chunk seam is visible.

6. **Interface.** F03/F04 improve hierarchy by eliminating the redundant bottom-strip call-out entry; “Call out Biscuit” is still legible in both lighting conditions. All frames keep panels inside the image, but the large team panel, story panel, five-cell icon panel and bottom strip occupy substantial space around a comparatively tiny subject. The three empty team rows compete with the two active members. “FOOD” is muted against its translucent backing, especially in day, and the mostly uniform green minimap communicates little local terrain. The five-cell lower-right panel has small, visually cryptic symbols. F01/F03 also present beach guidance at the top while the story panel asks for camp, without visually explaining their relationship. These are internal usability judgments, not comparisons to Palworld UI.

7. **Artefacts.** F01/F03 expose highly repetitive fine sand texture and the cliff's repeated polygon-like mottling. In F02/F04, the thin bright grass outlines look harsh against near-black vegetation. These are visible surface-quality issues; the stills do not prove a shader bug. No definite z-fighting, geometry intersection or popping can be claimed.

8. **Scale agreement.** Using the trainer as the 1.80 m ruler, the nearby cliff plausibly reads several people tall and grass reads as low bank vegetation. The scene does not contain enough clear, co-located creatures or props to test their relative scale. The tiny figure at the far upper-right boundary is too distant and incomplete to support a proportion judgment.

## Three ranked gaps from the references

1. **Creature and character presence — F01–F04.** Palworld gives creatures readable bodies, contrasting colour masses and visible relationships to the trainer. Here a small rear-facing trainer stands alone in a broad empty foreground, with no legible companion. Scene staging can bring an existing creature into view and strengthen framing; expressive character/creature art, if absent from the available assets, requires art work. These frames cannot establish the asset inventory.
2. **Ground and cliff treatment — F01–F04.** The references combine quiet ground areas with clustered grass, flowers, stones and structured rock surfaces. These frames concentrate repetition across sand, mottled cliff and wiry grass. Scene/material adjustments can improve coverage, texture scale, value grouping and clustering; better foliage or rock assets may be needed if the current silhouettes cannot support that treatment.
3. **Depth and lighting composition — F01–F04, especially F02/F04.** The references open routes toward layered landmarks and use light to organize attention. The close cliff dominates these views, and night produces a dark wall with bright grass outlines. Camera/route composition, environmental staging and lighting are scene-adjustable gaps.

**A — Belongs to the key-art world: no.** Warm sand, green vegetation and an adventurer suggest compatible ingredients, but the repeated surfaces, enclosed composition and disconnected night lighting do not yet carry the board's natural, inviting, layered atmosphere. This is principally a scene/material/lighting gap; a shoreline subject alone is not the reason for rejection.

**B — Trying to be the same kind of game as Palworld: yes.** The third-person adventurer, outdoor environment, named creature team, call-out action and camp objective communicate a creature adventure. That establishes genre intent, not comparable visual execution.

**Commercial readiness: no.** F03/F04 are the cleaner HUD pair, but the weak creature presentation, repetitive environment surfaces, uninviting depth and unresolved interface density remain conspicuous in these supplied views.
