No source, implementation diff, manifest, or other verdict was inspected. UI is assessed for its own readability, not against Palworld's UI. These stills do not establish motion quality, performance, or unseen creature art.

The trainer is identifiable as a backpacked adventurer, but the coarse hair, clothing detail, and stiff-looking rear silhouette in F01–F08 do not demonstrate the expressive character appeal of the reference cast. No creature is clearly presented at a judgeable size; the small glow on the slope cannot establish creature quality, and the roster thumbnails in F05–F08 cannot substitute for a world view.

## Eight criteria

1. **Silhouette and small-size readability.** F01/F03/F05/F07 separate the trainer's blue-and-tan upper body from the sand, and the dark legs remain identifiable. In F02/F04/F06/F08 the legs and hair merge more with the dark ground; bright shoulders preserve the body position. At roughly thumbnail viewing size the scene resolves mainly into an enormous left rock mass, ground plane, and right grass strip. It lacks a readable companion, destination silhouette, or distinct middle-distance subject. The grass becomes scratchy detail rather than coherent planting masses.

2. **Colour and value structure.** The day frames F01/F03/F05/F07 are dominated by highly yellow sand and a mottled grey-green wall; very little of the key art's balanced meadow, sky, water, and warm settlement palette is visible. Night in F02/F04/F06/F08 is clearly differentiated, but the almost black blue wall and cyan grass outlines leave little natural middle-value variation. The vivid mint minimap attracts attention before much of the world. No visible oxblood-on-friendly-element defect can be established in these frames.

3. **Intentionality.** F01–F08 devote most of the view to uninterrupted sand and a single large rock slope. The ground carries a fine, evenly repeated surface pattern without convincing patches of accumulated stones, worn routes, plants, or human activity. The right bank has similarly thin grass blades distributed across a broad uniform patch, with an abrupt transition to bare sand. The small distant opening does not provide a convincing authored destination. The reference paths and clearings connect planted edges, rock groups, structures, and visible life.

4. **Lighting.** F01/F03/F05/F07 have a coherent daylight reading and a trainer shadow that anchors the feet. Broad rock shading provides some form, but the wall's mottled surface dominates that form. F02/F04/F06/F08 clearly read as night from the moon, blue sky, and dark wall. However, the trainer's pale shoulders and individual grass edges remain conspicuously bright against the much darker surroundings, giving them a cutout appearance. The night scene lacks the reference's warm inhabited focal point or graduated moonlit landscape.

5. **Horizon and depth.** In every frame the rock occupies most of the upper left and middle view, while only a narrow upper-right gap suggests continuation. This is a cramped corridor composition rather than the reference's layered near ground, middle-distance activity, and distant landmark. The bright far slope separates from the near wall in the day frames, but there is too little exposed distance to demonstrate an effective fog or horizon treatment. No definite LOD or chunk seam is identifiable from the stills.

6. **Interface.** The main story box and day-location instruction are readable, and panels have adequate visible screen margins in F01–F08. The roster reserves a large left-side area even when every slot is empty in F01–F04. Health and food text near the bottom left is substantially lower contrast than the main objective; FOOD is especially weak over its translucent brownish panel. The mint minimap appears nearly featureless, offering little visible terrain orientation. Small button glyphs and creature thumbnails do not survive thumbnail viewing. F03/F04 improve command hierarchy; F07/F08 improve Change Creature legibility. F05–F08 duplicate Call Out, and the separate five-icon row above the commands remains visually ambiguous. These are concrete readability issues, independent of any comparison with reference UI.

7. **Artefacts.** F01/F03/F05/F07 show a conspicuous repeated pebble-like pattern over the huge rock slope and a noisy, tiled-looking sand surface. The bank edge forms an unnaturally sharp material boundary in all frames. F02/F04/F06/F08 show very bright, narrow outlines on grass that read as rendering noise rather than soft foliage. There is no clear evidence here of z-fighting or intersecting character geometry. Popping cannot be assessed from stills.

8. **Scale agreement.** Using the trainer as the 1.80 m ruler, nearby grass appears broadly plausible at shin-to-knee height, and the wall can plausibly be a large cliff. Its huge smooth outline and uniformly scaled surface marks nevertheless make it read like an enlarged mound, with too few fractures or intermediate rock forms to communicate its size convincingly. This affects F01–F08. No visible pair of creatures supports a relative creature-scale verdict; neither their roster entries nor the distant glowing object supplies a valid measurement.

## Three largest reference gaps

1. **No compelling creature or character presentation: F01–F08.** Palworld's field and plateau shots prominently display distinctive creatures with strong bodies, colour blocks, and readable relationships to the trainer. These frames present only a small rear-view trainer and no judgeable creature. Scene staging and companion visibility can address the absence; stronger trainer appeal may require character-art work. These frames cannot establish whether suitable creature art is already installed.
2. **Bare, enclosed composition and weak environmental authorship: F01–F08.** The references use connected routes, irregular planted edges, habitation, and layered landmarks. Here a giant rock slope and broad sand plane consume the image with little exploration invitation. Camera/route composition, rock grouping, ground transitions, vegetation clustering, and meaningful visible props are scene-fixable directions.
3. **Surface and light integration: F01–F08, especially F02/F04/F06/F08.** Reference foliage forms shaded volumes and their landscapes preserve varied values. Here repetitive ground/wall patterns and luminous grass edges compete with forms. Material scale, palette, grass treatment, and lighting are scene/material adjustments; replacement foliage or terrain art may be needed if the existing assets cannot produce convincing shapes. Installed-asset availability is not established by these images.

## Bar questions

**A. Belongs to the Meadows key-art world: No.** The general natural setting and backpacked traveller are compatible, but the dominant barren sand-and-wall composition, missing readable life, and harsh night foliage do not carry the board's inviting, layered, inhabited landscape. Much of this is scene-fixable as described above. Character-art adequacy needs better evidence and potentially art work; the frames do not justify claiming that unseen creature assets need replacement.

**B. Trying to be the same kind of game as Palworld: Yes.** The third-person stylised adventurer exploring a natural world, together with the visible creature-team context, communicates a creature-adventure direction. This is a genre answer, not a shipping-quality pass. These images do not demonstrate combat spectacle or creature appeal comparable to the reference frames.

**Commercial visual readiness: No.** The cleaner command states are worthwhile local preferences, but weak scene composition, repetitive surfaces, disconnected night foliage, missing creature presentation, and remaining HUD contrast/hierarchy defects still make these views look unfinished.
