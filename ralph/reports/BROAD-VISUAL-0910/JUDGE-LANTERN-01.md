# Lantern Hollow — blind visual verdict

Judged only the supplied F01, F02, F03 and F04 images in `.artifacts/broad-visual-0910/neutral-lantern-01/`, the actual Meadows key art, and all five `docs/reference/palworld-0*.jpg` images. Also viewed the supplied Enshrouded image as a secondary, nonbinding composition reference. No source, Godot, implementation history, or other reports were inspected. Frame identifiers convey no assumed chronology. These are still-image findings, with no inference about performance or motion.

The creatures do not hold up to the Palworld references. In F01/F03 the central bird is an intensely purple feather mass with little readable face or material differentiation. F02/F04 reveal the bird profile better, but its pale purple surface still reads like one applied colour rather than designed anatomy and plumage. The tiny green figure at the trainer's feet is too occluded to assess as character art. The trainer has a readable backpack and outfit silhouette, but the back view cannot establish facial appeal or expressiveness. The humans, stone circles, small cottage, giant trunks and birds look like elements from different visual treatments. Palworld's creatures retain broad, expressive shapes and controlled surface colours even in its small reference images.

## Preference and visible differences

**No material preference between F01 and F03, or between F02 and F04.** The daylight pair presents essentially the same scene, palette, clutter and problems. The night pair has a slightly different central bird pose/position; that is insufficient evidence of an art improvement. I cannot identify a meaningful scene-quality regression within either matched pair. This is a no-change verdict at the supplied scale, not evidence that an unseen implementation is identical.

Daylight F01/F03 make the ground and trainer easier to read. Night F02/F04 separate the pale bird from the dark ground more clearly, but sacrifice terrain legibility and do not create a welcoming lantern-lit settlement. Those are differences between lighting conditions, not an assumed before/after result.

## Full rubric

**Silhouette and readability at small size.** At roughly thumbnail size, F01–F04 retain the trainer, triangular cottage roof, roofed well/shrine, large tree trunks and purple bird. Most grass becomes stippled noise, the small figure at the trainer's boots disappears, and the ground route is unreadable. The bird's distinctive colour survives better than its anatomy. Large foreground stone rings compete with the player, while the HUD covers much of the lower-right scene. Prioritize clean creature shapes, an unambiguous village route, and separation between the trainer and nearby objects.

**Colour and value structure.** F01/F03 have a mottled olive-brown floor, bright thin green grass, strongly red shrub clusters and very saturated purple birds. Several environmental colours compete with the creatures for attention. The red vegetation across the otherwise friendly cottage clearing visually spends the dark-red danger accent; no Team Tether identity can be established from these pictures. In F02/F04 the ground moves toward black while grass remains conspicuously bright, and the bird becomes lavender. The frames clearly depict one place, but lack the key art's disciplined natural greens, warm settlement accents and quiet distance. Shift visual emphasis through coordinated ground, shrub and creature values rather than adding more saturated elements.

**Intentionality.** All four frames show isolated village objects on a broad, nearly uniform clearing: a cottage left, a roofed structure right, spaced people, repeated foreground stone circles, and a loose belt of similarly sized red shrubs. There are no convincing connecting paths, doorstep transitions, fence boundaries, planted beds or activity clusters visible here. Grass is distributed as many thin vertical marks rather than distinct grassy margins and worn clearings. This reads as placed assets on a surface more than a inhabited village. The key art's settlement connects houses, well, fencing and paths; Palworld's base image ties creatures and objects to readable activity areas.

**Lighting.** F01/F03 read as daytime, but terrain form is weak and the cottage, people and grass have limited coherent cast-shadow structure. F02/F04 read as night primarily through the dark blue sky, moon and darkened ground. Cottage windows are pale, yet there is no convincing warm pool on the doorstep or a village gathering point. The stone interiors are nearly black in both conditions. The key art's night scene uses warm habitation against cool surroundings; these night frames do not establish that relationship. Object contact and localized warm light would address visible weaknesses, though their actual implementation is outside this review.

**Horizon and depth.** F01–F04 stop at a shallow, almost level tree-and-shrub boundary against sky. Huge trunks frame the scene but do not lead toward a recognizable middle-distance settlement or remote landmark. There is little visible land beyond the clearing and almost no atmospheric layering. Palworld's path, meadow and plateau references use banks, cliffs, layered trees and distant silhouettes to give the playable space a larger setting. No definite LOD or chunk seam can be identified from these stills. The supplemental Enshrouded image reinforces the value of foreground habitat, middle-distance architecture and a distant skyline; its scale, rendering style and golden lighting are not requirements here.

**Interface.** F01–F04 keep the main blocks inside the frame, and the interaction prompt is readable. However, a large empty five-slot panel and long command strip dominate the lower-right view; the story panel is also very large relative to the cottage. The scene loses substantial space to mostly empty dark rectangles. Time and shelter text have weak contrast against sky/leaves, and health/food presentation is much less prominent than the story and interaction panels. This is an internal hierarchy critique, not a comparison to Palworld's UI. The static images cannot establish controller usability.

**Artefacts.** F01–F04 contain coarse, enlarged-looking ground mottling with spindly high-contrast grass lines over it. The foreground rings show hard, geometric rims and flat-looking black recesses; their repeated construction reads more artificial than surrounding rocks. The small green figure intersects or closely overlaps the trainer's boots, but occlusion prevents confirming a clipping bug. Night F02/F04 have conspicuously dark, thin cloud streaks and an almost featureless bright moon disk that feel disconnected from the world's surface lighting. I cannot confirm z-fighting, popping or motion aliasing from these images, and no definite chunk seam is visible.

**Scale agreement.** The trainer is the provided 1.80 m ruler. All four views put enormous trunks behind a very small-looking cottage and roofed structure. The cottage's narrow door and overall mass make it read as a miniature dwelling in a giant grove, rather than a convincing house for this human cast. Perspective prevents an exact height calculation because trainer and cottage are at different depths; this is a strong apparent-scale problem, not a claimed measured dimension. The central bird is clearly substantial beside the nearby human, so these frames do not support a blanket claim that every creature is too small. The green figure at the trainer's feet is visibly tiny, but its role and occluded body do not permit judging an intended relative size hierarchy.

## The largest gaps, ranked

- **Creature and character art coherence — F01–F04.** Palworld's boss, meadow, plateau and base images give creatures expressive, designed silhouettes with deliberate colour breaks. The purple birds here read as tinted feather meshes, while the trainer and other humans do not visibly establish a shared strong character style. The bird is visible; it is not yet compelling enough to carry the scene.
- **Authored ground and settlement — F01–F04.** The key-art village and Palworld's path/base shots organize grass, worn ground and objects around readable human or creature use. Lantern Hollow presents thin scattered grass, exposed mottled ground and separated assets, with repetitive rings dominating the foreground. Connecting paths, activity clusters, believable building scale and planted edges are missing.
- **Spatial and lighting depth — F01–F04, particularly F02/F04 at night.** The key art and Palworld plateau image place the immediate scene within layered terrain and landmarks. These frames flatten into one clearing and giant trunks. At night the key art adds warm habitation and cool distance, while this village mostly becomes dark ground beneath a blue sky.

## Acceptance bars

**KeyartBarA — No.** The cottage, green grove and fantasy wildlife gesture toward the board, but the oversized trunks, miniature-looking settlement, red shrub belt, nearly flat clearing and absence of a welcoming warm night focal point do not read as the composed Meadows world in the key art. This verdict concerns palette, composition, silhouette and mood, not matching painted detail.

**PalworldBarB, same kind of game — Yes.** A third-person trainer amid conspicuous fantasy creatures and settlement/building objects reads as the same broad kind of creature-adventure game. That genre recognition is visible despite the art weaknesses.

**Palworld commercial visual quality — No.** The creature surfaces and stylistic coherence, ground coverage and authored settlement detail, and landscape/lighting depth remain substantially below the supplied shipping-game screenshots. A yes on genre does not imply a quality pass. No fight is shown, so combat spectacle is untested.

**Scene-addressable gaps:** reconnect objects with worn paths and purposeful clusters; correct apparent building scale; replace uniform grass scatter with grounded clumps and clearings; reduce competing red/green terrain accents; arrange middle-distance terrain/vegetation and a readable landmark; establish object grounding and warm village light at night; reduce empty HUD coverage.

**Art-dependent gaps:** the birds need coherent material differentiation and more convincing character appeal; the cottage and repeated stone-ring surfaces need a consistent visual treatment with the trainer and nature assets. These may require material/texture work, a better existing asset, or new art. A code-blind image review cannot know what alternatives are installed, so it cannot honestly certify that the needed art is absent from the build. Scatter or lighting changes alone will not solve those visible asset-level problems.
