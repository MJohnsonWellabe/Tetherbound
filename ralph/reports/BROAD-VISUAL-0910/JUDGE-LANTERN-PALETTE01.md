# Blind visual verdict — F01 and F02

Judged the two supplied frames against `tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png` and all five `palworld-0*.jpg` references. No implementation, earlier verdicts, or change history were inspected. This is a still-image judgment; it cannot establish animation, performance, popping, or the quality of unseen locations.

## Visual preference

**Prefer F01 overall, particularly for foliage palette and settlement coherence.** Its green shrubs and dark brown trunks establish a more consistent natural setting around the ochre cottage and well. F02 introduces repeated deep red shrubs along the middle distance; they become a conspicuous competing accent and make this friendly clearing feel less coherent. They also spend the dark red visual language that the reference reserves for danger. This is not a claim that F01 reaches the reference bar.

F02 has a genuine local advantage: the lighter gray trunks expose their folds and distinguish tree forms more clearly than F01's nearly black trunks. But their broad pale surfaces feel chalkier beside the richly textured cottage, and the red shrubs outweigh that readability gain. Neither frame resolves the material disparity between the vivid purple creatures, narrow bright grass blades, mottled earth, textured buildings, and pale stone rings.

## Eight criteria

1. **Silhouette and readability at small size.** In both F01 and F02 the trainer, cottage, well, and large trees remain identifiable. The purple birdlike creature is easy to locate but its body merges into a dense feather mass: a readable face, body markings, and differentiated wing/body forms are not apparent at this distance. The farther purple creature is primarily a colored silhouette. Against Palworld 01, 03, and 04, the creatures do not yet present comparable readable character design and material separation. The trainer's blue shirt and backpack separate him from the terrain, though lower legs become mixed with grass and stone edges. F02's red shrub silhouettes add extra points of attention without making the settlement easier to read.

2. **Colour and value structure.** F01 has the stronger palette relationship: green vegetation, brown bark, golden roofs, and blue sky. Its dark trunks nevertheless occupy large almost-black shapes while the lawn is a busy olive/yellow-green midtone. F02 improves trunk value separation but adds conspicuous dark red spots across the clearing. In both frames the saturated purple creatures appear largely one hue instead of having the controlled light/dark and accent separation visible in Palworld 03. The key art uses quieter plant masses and sunlight/shadow areas to guide attention; here many tiny grass strokes compete at once. The two frames read as the same location, with F02 materially less unified.

3. **Intentionality.** Both frames have a few useful landmarks but weak relationships between them: the cottage and well sit apart on a broad mottled clearing without a convincing connected path, tended garden edge, yard boundary, or visible activity grouping. The foreground stone rings repeat the same conspicuous form across the entire lower view. Grass stalks are scattered over much of the ground with similar visual intensity. F02's red shrub sequence makes the scatter more obvious. The reference settlement connects buildings, fences, paths, and planting into a place people use; Palworld 05 similarly groups functional objects around a recognizable work area.

4. **Lighting.** Both frames read as daylight, but the pictured world communicates the stated morning weakly: the sky is cool and subdued, the ground mottling supplies much of the surface contrast, and there is no strong readable sunlight composition across the clearing. There are visible object shadows, including near the well and creatures, so objects are not universally floating. F01's trunks lose internal form to darkness; F02 exposes that form more successfully. Neither has the reference's clear relationship between lit foliage, shaded canopy, and warmly illuminated inhabited space. Precise shadow quality cannot be established beyond these stills.

5. **Horizon and depth.** In F01 and F02 the middle ground ends at a nearly level grassy horizon behind very large tree trunks. The open sky provides breathing room, but there is little layered terrain or distant landmark to suggest a world continuing beyond this clearing. The key art and Palworld 04 use slopes, rock masses, and distant silhouettes to give the foreground a place in a larger landscape. No definite LOD or chunk seam is visible in these frames.

6. **Interface.** In both frames the main-story text and interaction prompt are readable, and the prompt has a clear border. The broad command strip and five-slot strip occupy much of the lower-right view, obscuring the foreground forms and ground. Empty dark slots have little information beyond tiny numerals. Health and food text have weaker contrast than the quest and interaction prompt; small text in the upper-left and the day/time line is less immediately legible. The UI stays within the image bounds. This assessment is internal legibility and hierarchy only, not a comparison with Palworld UI.

7. **Artefacts.** Both frames show thin, sharply alternating bright grass strokes over a coarse mottled floor, producing a noisy, stubbly surface rather than a continuous grassy meadow. Foreground stone rings surround nearly featureless black centers; whatever their intended function, they read as flat dark inserts or holes from this view. Stone shapes and their joins with the rims are visibly abrupt. These are visible presentation defects, not proof of a specific shader or geometry bug. No definite z-fighting, texture seam, or motion-dependent defect can be diagnosed here.

8. **Scale agreement.** The trainer provides a useful local ruler in both frames: the foreground rings are human-scale constructions with stones rising around shin-to-knee height. The nearby NPCs and cottage door do not reveal an unequivocal scale contradiction after perspective is considered. The very broad tree trunks visually dwarf the cottage and well, however, so the clearing reads as a small set of props placed among monumental trees rather than the balanced village/oak-grove relationship in the key art. The nearer purple creature reads broadly human-sized or larger, but depth and its bent posture prevent a defensible exact ratio. No companion-versus-practice-creature size conclusion is possible without roles or a common depth plane.

## Three ranked gaps from the references

1. **Creature material and character clarity — F01 and F02.** Palworld 01, 03, and 04 make their creatures readable through distinct body masses, markings, face/feature contrast, and material shading. The supplied purple creatures primarily read as intensely colored feather masses. Start with body/wing/face value separation and restrained hue variation; verify that a recognizable character survives at gameplay size. Palette and material edits are scene/build work. If readable identity requires different facial or silhouette forms, that needs authored art. These distant stills do not prove whether suitable art is already available.

2. **Ground and planting structure — F01 and F02, with additional palette competition in F02.** Palworld 02–05 provide convincing grass coverage, clear dirt routes or working areas, and plant masses that recede behind characters. Here the bare mottled floor and many thin bright strokes compete, while F02 adds repeated red accents. Establish coherent grassy patches, quieter ground variation, a connected dirt route between cottage and well, and planting concentrated at meaningful edges. These are primarily scene, palette, coverage, and composition changes. F01 is the better palette starting point, but it still needs this work.

3. **Inhabited settlement and landscape depth — F01 and F02.** The key-art village and Palworld 04–05 tie structures to circulation, work areas, enclosing terrain, and distant landforms. These frames show separated cottage/well props, a crowded repeated ring foreground, oversized framing trunks, and a level horizon. Compose a linked cottage yard and well approach, reduce how much repeated ring geometry dominates this view, and add layered terrain or a distant landmark visible through the trees. These are primarily scene-layout and composition issues; the stills do not establish a need for new assets.

## Bar decisions

**A — Belongs to the key-art world: No.** The cottage, well, and green grove suggest the intended subject, particularly in F01, but the harsh grass/earth texture competition, weak settlement relationships, and absent rolling landscape structure do not yet convey the board's welcoming meadow world. F02's red shrubs push farther away from its friendly palette. Most of this gap is addressable through scene composition, vegetation coverage, material/palette adjustments, and lighting. Creature identity may additionally require authored art, as distinguished above.

**B — Trying to be the same kind of game as the Palworld references: Yes.** The third-person trainer, conspicuous fantasy creatures, natural clearing, buildings, and interaction/build cues clearly communicate the same broad creature-adventure and settlement genre. This is genre recognition, not a quality pass.

**Commercial visual bar beside those references: No.** Neither frame matches the references' creature readability, continuous ground cover, coherent material treatment, and authored spatial relationships. F01 is my relative preference; neither warrants acceptance as commercially comparable on the evidence shown. No fight is depicted, so combat spectacle is unassessed.
