# Lantern Hollow — blind visual verdict

Inspected F01, F02, F03 and F04 in `.artifacts/broad-visual-0910/neutral-lantern-workshop01/`, the actual `docs/reference/tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`, all five `palworld-0*.jpg` references, and the supplied Once Human settlement and three Enshrouded images. This assessment uses images only, with no code, diffs, history or other verdicts. Its scope is this Lantern Hollow view, not the whole game. No numeric scores.

The purple creatures do not meet the reference standard for the game's central attraction. In F01/F03 their feathers, bodies and heads merge into almost solid saturated purple; F02/F04 turn that into pale lavender without establishing attractive material or anatomical separation. The trainer has a recognizable backpack and costume silhouette, but the back view does not establish facial appeal or expression. The visible supporting humans are too small to judge faces fairly; their muted colors make them secondary to the grass and purple birds.

## Pair comparison

**No meaningful preference between F01/F02 and F03/F04.** F01 and F03 show the same daytime composition; F02 and F04 show its night counterpart. Small differences in grass silhouettes, minimap direction and displayed time do not resolve a ranked gap. There is no visible change in workshop identity, architectural composition, creature material readability, or depth that warrants preferring either pair. No chronology is assumed.

## Full rubric

1. **Silhouette and readability at small size.** In all four frames the trainer, cottage roof, well roof, giant tree trunks and purple bird remain identifiable. The foreground stone rings dominate the lower frame while the smaller inhabitants lose definition in the ground texture. At roughly thumbnail scale, the workshop is not identifiable as a workshop: the view communicates a cottage, well and repeated rings in a clearing. Purple makes the birds easy to locate, but their head/body articulation is much weaker than the distinct creature forms and facial features in Palworld 01/03/04.

2. **Colour and value structure.** F01/F03 have a muddy olive-brown ground covered with bright thin green grass, gray tree trunks, red shrubs and unusually intense purple birds. The competing small color accents create noise instead of the key art's coherent green/gold landscape with reserved character accents. Red shrubs are distributed across this apparently peaceful clearing, so danger red is not visually reserved here. F02/F04 keep conspicuously bright green grass and lavender creatures against a darkened ground and blue sky; night is recognizable, but the palette does not cohere into the key art's cool nocturnal atmosphere punctuated by warm shelter.

3. **Intentionality.** All frames present one isolated cottage and one well, scattered humans, and a repeating set of stone rings close to the camera. Vegetation is broadly spread across traversable space, with no strong worn route connecting ring area, door and well. The similar red shrubs across the rear edge read like a scatter layer. Palworld 05 organizes a base into recognizable activity areas; the key art's settlement binds architecture, fences, paths and vegetation into a place. These frames lack that functional and spatial grouping. A workshop should have a visible working frontage or recognizable arrangement of tools/materials, but neither is legible here.

4. **Lighting.** Day versus night is clear from the sky in F01/F03 versus F02/F04, and there is visible shading on trunks and stone rings. Ground relief and contact lighting remain weak: foliage and soil form a mottled plane rather than a readable sequence of lit and shaded volumes. At night the cottage window is bright, yet there is no convincing warm spill or welcoming pool around the entrance or well. Once Human's supplied settlement image demonstrates directional light grounding fences and foliage; it is supplementary evidence for spatial lighting, not a request to copy its darker genre palette. Fine renderer behavior cannot be diagnosed from these stills.

5. **Horizon and depth.** All four frames have an unusually flat distant edge behind the cottage and shrubs, opening onto a large sky field between oversized trunks. There is little intermediate landform or distant landmark. Foreground, middle distance and background vegetation are insufficiently separated in color/value. The key art and Palworld 04 give routes and settlements a landscape beyond them; Enshrouded 0 supplies a particularly clear supplementary example of layered terrain and atmospheric recession. No definite LOD or chunk seam is established by these stills.

6. **Interface.** The interaction prompt is easy to read and cyan outlines reliably separate key boxes. However, in every frame the broad bottom command bar and empty-looking numbered slots cover a substantial portion of the foreground world. The quest card dominates the upper-right view, while small low-contrast status text and the clock are much less legible. Health and food occupy the safe area but are subdued against the terrain. This is a hierarchy/occlusion finding from Tetherbound itself; Palworld's UI is not used as a design requirement.

7. **Artefacts.** In all frames the straight fine grass blades and coarse foliage edges produce strong visual stippling. F02/F04 include thin dark horizontal cloud fragments that look harsh against the otherwise simple sky. The ring rims, protruding stones and trainer's footing create visually crowded intersections in the foreground; these images do not establish whether collision or actual mesh penetration is wrong. No definite z-fighting, temporal popping or motion defect can be claimed from four stills.

8. **Scale agreement.** Using the trainer's stated 1.80 m height as the visual ruler, the foreground rings are person-sized structures and the tree trunks are enormous relative to both people and the cottage. Perspective prevents an exact measurement, but the cottage/well read as miniature objects below giant repeated trunks rather than a convincingly proportioned village grove. The nearby well-side human and bird suggest a substantial creature, so there is no clear evidence here that the bird is undersized relative to a human at similar depth. Exact species-role comparisons are unavailable; do not infer them from the smaller distant bird.

## Three largest reference gaps, ranked

1. **Creature art and material definition — F01–F04.** Palworld gives each creature clear body regions, contrasting facial features, readable volume and an expressive silhouette. These birds rely on nearly monochrome purple/lavender to stand out, with feather detail becoming noise. Lighting/material changes may recover some definition, but distinctive face, anatomy and expressive silhouette may require authored art work; scatter changes cannot supply them. The images cannot establish whether stronger suitable art already exists elsewhere in the build.

2. **A settlement with visible purpose — F01–F04.** The key art and Palworld 05 use connected paths and meaningful groups of structures/props to communicate habitation. Here the cottage, well, humans and repeated rings remain separate objects in a grass-covered clearing. The workshop subject is not visually established. Scene-level fixes are available: connect destinations with worn ground, arrange a working frontage, group existing props by purpose, and create deliberate vegetation boundaries. If recognizable workshop tools/material props are unavailable, that remaining requirement needs suitable art rather than more generic clutter.

3. **Landscape depth and grounded light — F01–F04, especially F02/F04.** The key art and Palworld 02/04 layer landforms, paths, shadow and distance; the night key art gives shelter a warm focal point. These frames end at a nearly flat horizon and maintain busy grass brightness at night without persuasive warm local illumination. Landform/background composition, vegetation values and localized lighting are scene-level work. Once Human's directional settlement lighting and Enshrouded's layered landscapes reinforce this gap without changing the primary art target.

## Separate acceptance answers

**A — Belongs to the key art's world: No.** Timber architecture and green countryside establish broad vocabulary, but the giant-trunk stage, flat horizon, scattered settlement layout, aggressive red/purple accents and cold uninviting night fail the key art's natural, inhabited, welcoming composition. Scene work can address palette, grouping, paths, scale presentation, depth and shelter lighting. Creature design/material appeal and any missing workshop-specific prop art need separate art attention; the images do not prove an asset is absent from the build.

**B — Trying to be the same kind of game as Palworld: Yes.** A third-person adventurer, conspicuous fantastical creatures and a rural base/settlement read as the same broad creature-adventure/base-building genre. This answers genre recognition only. The absence of combat in these frames leaves fight presentation unassessed.

**Commercial visual quality alongside the supplied Palworld screenshots: No.** The central creatures lack comparable art definition; the settlement reads assembled rather than lived in; repetitive thin grass, compressed landscape depth and weak night lighting remain highly visible. Supplementary Once Human and Enshrouded comparisons support the staging and lighting criticisms, without requiring their realism or effects style. Neither frame pair clears that quality bar.
