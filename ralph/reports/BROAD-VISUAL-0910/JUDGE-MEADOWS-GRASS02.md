# Blind visual verdict — South Bridge Meadows

Inspected F01.png, F02.png, F03.png and F04.png in `.artifacts/broad-visual-0910/neutral-meadows-grass02/`, the actual `docs/reference/tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`, and all five `palworld-0*.jpg` references. This is one location in daylight and at night, not evidence of regional breadth. No source, prior reports or implementation history were inspected. No Godot run was made. Findings concern visible still images; motion, performance and gameplay are untested.

The trainer is identifiable as a backpacked adventurer, but these distant rear views do not demonstrate a distinctive or expressive protagonist comparable to the Palworld cast. F02/F04 lose most of the trainer's body into the ground. No clearly readable nearby creature is available to assess creature appeal, creature-to-trainer scale or combat spectacle. That leaves the game's central cast promise unproven in this set.

## Relative preference

I prefer F01 over F03 and F02 over F04. F01/F02 give the grass visible blade width and more ground volume, whereas F03/F04 frequently reduce it to thin vertical hairs over exposed terrain. F01/F02 also have a shallower empty-slot panel, which covers less of the world. These are relative preferences, not acceptance of either pair as finished.

F01's foreground grass is recognizable as grass, but its isolated, rigid triangular blades often look like planted spikes. It does not yet form the soft, interwoven ground cover in Palworld's field, meadow and base images. F03 avoids some of that chunky spike appearance by being thinner, but sacrifices convincing volume: the hillside's mottled ground remains conspicuous beneath widely separated lines. At night, F02 preserves more vegetation shape than F04, though illuminated blade edges against nearly black soil can resemble a field of pins. Neither pair resolves the shared foliage and lighting shortcomings.

## Silhouette and readability at small size

In F01/F03, the blue sleeves, light collar and backpack separate the trainer's upper body from the hill; the dark legs are less distinct against the dirt. Reduced mentally to contact-sheet size, the player remains a person, the trees remain trees and the gate remains an enclosure. Individual grass blades merge into scratchy texture rather than a coherent meadow surface. The foreground bush at lower right becomes one opaque green mass and obscures the route toward the gate.

F02/F04 retain the head-and-shoulder outline, but the legs, bush, terrain and gate surroundings merge into black masses. Pale stones remain more conspicuous than the trainer's lower body. The scene's navigable dirt surface is apparent locally in daylight, but the onward route is not cleanly connected through the dense foreground occlusion. The pale diagonal strip up the central hill reads as a route or exposed terrain edge; its abrupt, angular profile makes that reading less natural.

## Colour and value structure

F01/F03 use the right broad meadow ingredients: warm earth, yellow-green slopes, green crowns and a cool sky. However, the ground's dark mottling and saturated grass claim more attention than the subdued trainer. The close bush is a flat dark green cutout beside much more textured ground, stonework and grass. These materials do not share a convincing value treatment.

F02/F04 clearly belong to the same place under a blue night palette, but too much of the lower frame collapses to near black. The white moon and bright interface dominate. The key art's night scene preserves layered terrain and warm inhabited points of interest; these frames offer little equivalent warmth or destination emphasis. Oxblood is concentrated on the gate banners in daylight; there is no visible evidence here of indiscriminate red saturation across friendly scenery. The gate's allegiance cannot be independently established from the stills.

## Intentionality

Flowers, taller yellow stalks, low broadleaf plants and bare soil provide some variation in F01/F03. The gate, barrels, hay and banners make a recognizable placed encounter area. Nevertheless, the frequent isolated upright blades and similarly tinted distant trees feel distributed more than composed. Grass needs convincing local masses, transitions and openings, not merely more stems.

The very large foreground bush in every frame blocks an important section of the view without framing a stronger subject. The close tree trunk at left and open sky above do not compensate for that blockage. Palworld's open-field path establishes a clear route toward visible activity, and its plateau view arranges creatures and landmarks into a readable destination. Here the camera looks at a slope and a partially hidden gate, with no comparably strong creature or landmark focus.

## Lighting

Day and night are immediately distinguishable. In F01/F03, cast shade under the nearby tree and changes across the hills provide useful form, and the trainer appears in contact with the terrain. The foreground ground texture is nevertheless strongly blotched, making lighting and material pattern compete. The bush lacks enough internal shading to match the volume of other objects.

F02/F04 darken the surroundings enough to feel nocturnal, but the result sacrifices orientation. The trainer's blue rim and scattered pale stones are insufficient to explain the foreground terrain. The bright, largely featureless moon draws attention away from the playable area. The needed improvement is readable nocturnal planes and a destination light hierarchy, not daylight brightness everywhere.

## Horizon and depth

F01/F03 have useful overlapping hill shapes, a central bare tree and a grove behind the gate. The world does not disappear into heavy fog. However, the distant horizon is low and comparatively empty, while the background trees repeat similar crowns and heights. The key art's layered hills and recognizable mountain or settlement silhouettes provide a sense of a wider place that this camera does not deliver.

F02/F04 preserve skyline silhouettes but largely lose the separation between the nearer and farther slopes. The strong diagonal on the central slope looks unnaturally straight and pinched near its lower end in all frames. It is a visible shape concern, not proof of a chunk seam. No claim about LOD transitions can be made from these stills.

## Interface

The panels fit within the screen and the main objective is readable. The persistent objective card and minimap occupy the upper-right view; the long action strip and empty creature slots occupy the lower-right view. Together with the foreground bush, they remove much of the gate approach from useful visual inspection. The slot cards have very small key labels surrounded by substantial empty space.

F01/F02's shallower slot panel is plainly preferable to F03/F04's taller cards: it recovers a band of world above the slots while retaining the visible labels. Even the compact version leaves a large action strip across the lower scene. At a small viewing size, the objective and bars remain recognizable but the slot labels and button glyphs are difficult to parse. Disabled grey action text is especially low contrast. This is an assessment of this interface's hierarchy and coverage, not a comparison with Palworld UI design.

## Artefacts

F01/F02 have conspicuous hard triangular grass profiles; F03/F04 have very thin, broken-looking vertical traces. These are visibly unconvincing plant shapes, though the stills cannot establish temporal aliasing or shimmer. The lower-right bush has hard leaf silhouettes with little depth, and some forms overlap into a single flat mass. The diagonal terrain strip on the hill warrants inspection because its straight sides look cut into the landscape rather than naturally worn.

No definite z-fighting, geometry flicker, popping or texture seam is proven by this set. The chunky minimap path is visible, but its rendering style alone does not establish a malfunction.

## Scale agreement

Using the trainer as the stated human-height reference, the nearby taller grass reaches a plausible lower-leg or knee scale. The problem is stiffness and distribution, not an obvious need to make all grass taller. The foreground bush is large enough to obscure a person, which may be physically plausible but is compositionally costly. The nearby trunk reads as a substantial tree, while the gate and adjacent barrels are broadly compatible with human use.

The smaller figures near the gate are too distant and obscured for precise body-scale conclusions. No clearly readable creature allows a responsible creature-to-human or creature-to-creature scale verdict. A still with the trainer and a nearby companion is needed to establish that part of the rubric.

## Largest gaps from the references, ranked

- **First: convincing ground and foliage volume.** F01/F02 replace much of the hair-thin appearance in F03/F04 with readable blades, but neither pair achieves Palworld's layered grass carpet and softened soil transitions. Rigid separated spikes, exposed mottled hills and the flat foreground bush remain dominant. The key art also supports clustered flowers, varied groves and clear paths as composition goals, without requiring its painted leaf detail.
- **Second: a readable player and a world organized around the cast.** F01/F03 show a serviceable rear-view adventurer; F02/F04 lose the body in darkness. None shows a clear companion, expressive creature focal point or event. Palworld's supplied frames make the cast legible and central, including on ordinary paths. These frames cannot substantiate equivalent creature appeal, and the hidden gate does not supply an equally compelling substitute focal point.
- **Third: destination and depth through lighting and composition.** All frames spend substantial space on sky and foreground occlusion while the gate is pushed right and partly blocked. F02/F04 further collapse the slopes into darkness. The key art establishes layered journeys and warm nighttime destinations; Palworld's plateau and path frames guide the eye toward terrain, structures and readable activity.

## Binding bar answers

**A — Do these frames read as belonging to the Meadows key-art world? Yes.** Rolling grassy terrain, scattered broadleaf groves, warm dirt, rustic stone-and-timber construction and a blue night treatment establish the same broad rural fantasy direction. This is a palette, composition-language and mood judgment, not a claim that the full key-art ambition is met. The sparse horizon, harsh vegetation shapes, flat bush and lightless night destination remain substantial shortfalls.

**B — Beside the five Palworld references, do these look as though they are trying to be the same kind of game? Yes.** The third-person backpacked adventurer, stylized grassy exploration space, placed camp structure and creature-slot interface communicate that intention. The absence of a legible creature weakens the specific creature-adventure promise, but does not make the broad genre intention ambiguous.

**Commercial visual quality parity with the supplied Palworld screenshots? No.** A shared genre is a different claim from comparable execution. Ground cover, foliage material consistency, cast presentation, route composition and night readability do not reach that reference quality here.

Scene-level work can address scatter clustering, path margins, the foreground bush's placement, destination framing, night fill and localized warmth, background value separation, HUD coverage, and presenting the trainer with a visible nearby creature. Vegetation shape and shading need art or material treatment if the installed assets cannot produce softer, volumetric results; adding density alone will not fix the visible spikes or flat leaves. These frames do not reveal the asset inventory, so they cannot prove that new purchases are necessary. Likewise, they cannot establish whether a new character or creature model is needed: the current trainer presentation is insufficiently distinctive, and creature art is not inspectable here. That art decision needs closer cast evidence rather than an invented verdict about unseen assets.
