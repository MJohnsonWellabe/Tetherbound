# Reedhaven — blind visual verdict

Evidence: `.artifacts/broad-visual-0910/neutral-reedhaven-01/F01.png` through `F04.png`. F01/F03 show 08:00; F02/F04 show 23:00. The viewpoint and location are closely matched, with different visible creatures. No implementation files or change history were inspected. This verdict uses visible evidence, not status claims in repository prose. Still images cannot establish animation, traversal, shimmer, performance, or unseen parts of Reedhaven.

Binding image references actually inspected: `docs/reference/tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png` and all five `docs/reference/palworld-0*.jpg` images. Supplementary criteria used from the domain directive: BOTW/TOTK for a destination's built approach and clear path edges, Valheim for ground transitions, Grounded for readable vegetation layers. These are supplementary principles supplied by the directive; no additional screenshots of those games were supplied or inspected. They do not replace the two binding bars.

## Overall finding and preference

The creatures and trainer do not yet share the expressive, coherent art presentation of the Palworld references. F01 offers a readable long-tailed reptile profile, but its green, highly mottled surface competes with the ground and does not deliver a clear face at gameplay size. F03 has stronger blue/orange color separation from the grass, but the crouching rounded creature reads as a back, limbs and snout; I cannot confidently locate an eye. F02/F04 make the creatures conspicuously pale while the trainer becomes dark. Visibility is being obtained at the expense of consistent identity and lighting.

As a **location comparison, there is no meaningful preference** between F01/F02 and F03/F04: the same bare crest, sparse vegetation and distant slopes dominate. Neither pair visibly establishes Reedhaven as a built destination. There is no woven hall, dock, settlement grouping, marked approach or local activity readable in these views. This is a failure of this evidence to demonstrate the location criterion, not proof that those objects exist nowhere outside the camera.

For the visible subjects alone, I prefer F01's clearly articulated neck/body/legs/tail silhouette to F03's compressed form, and F03's daytime color blocking to F01's camouflage. Those mixed strengths are meaningful creature differences, not evidence that one location treatment is better. Neither night image is an acceptable overall presentation.

## Eight rubric dimensions

1. **Silhouette and readability at small size.** At thumbnail scale in F01/F03, the trainer remains a recognizable person and the horizon trees remain trees; shrubs become isolated dark dots. The reptile in F01 retains a species-class silhouette through its long tail and raised neck, but its dark back blends into the trees behind it. F03 remains a blue/orange lump with separated feet rather than a clear expressive face. F02/F04 preserve creature outlines because they are bright, while the trainer loses hair, backpack and lower-body definition. The location has no identifiable destination silhouette at any of these sizes.

2. **Color and value structure.** F01/F03 have a consistent blue sky and yellow-green ground, but the enormous, high-contrast mottled grass surface carries more visual activity than the subjects. Distant slopes bleach toward cream-green. F03's large blue/orange creature regions are a useful exception. F02/F04 establish blue night, yet the near-white creatures and icy shrubs look disconnected from the dark ground and trainer. The same creature's color identity is not convincingly preserved between day and night. No conspicuous oxblood element is visible; these frames do not demonstrate faction signaling either way.

3. **Intentionality.** All frames show a broad, almost uninterrupted textured surface from the trainer to the creature, with a few spaced trees and a small shrub grouping at right. There is no clear foreground/mid-layer/canopy sequence around the approach, no path-edge material transition, and no cluster of purposeful props to explain why this place is named. The existing right shrub group is a start toward clustering, but does not organize the scene. The Palworld path and base references use paths, constructed objects and vegetation edges to make clearings feel occupied.

4. **Lighting.** Morning and night are unmistakably different. Day shadows anchor the trainer and communicate directional light, although the distant land remains broadly flat and bright. At night, the trainer's legs are near-black with only small boot details surviving; the legs can be located, but their form and clothing cannot be read reliably. The bright creature surfaces in F02/F04 do not appear to receive the same illumination as the trainer. This discrepancy is stronger than any preference between the two creatures. No warm inhabited light punctuates the night destination.

5. **Horizon and depth.** The ridge, distant hills and sky provide several distance layers in every frame. However, the pale mountain faces and thin, repeated-looking tree silhouettes flatten the far scene into a washed backdrop. The right water/horizon band is readable as a bright boundary, not a composed coastal arrival. The vertical pale cut on the center hill draws attention without resolving as an inviting route or landmark. No definite LOD or chunk seam can be proved from these stills.

6. **Interface.** The location title and objective are readable and remain inside the frame. The large objective card, empty-looking five-slot panel and wide control strip take a substantial portion of the right and lower view despite limited information. The minimap provides almost no terrain or destination context in these captures. Tiny keycap marks are difficult to distinguish even at full view; at thumbnail size they disappear. Food text has weak contrast against its translucent panel. This is an intrinsic hierarchy/legibility assessment, not a comparison with Palworld UI.

7. **Artefacts.** F01/F03 ground texture is visibly smeared near the camera and breaks into noisy repeated detail over the slope; it reads as a surface texture rather than ground cover. Distant foliage appears stippled, particularly the pale trees and shrubs at night. F02/F04 have dark, torn-looking cloud fragments and a sharp band at the distant water edge. Those are visible discontinuities, not diagnoses of their cause. I cannot establish z-fighting, temporal popping, motion aliasing or a specific geometry bug from these frames. Creature feet appear to meet the terrain; no definite floor penetration is visible.

8. **Scale agreement.** The trainer is the stated 1.80 m ruler. Both creatures stand farther away yet present substantial silhouettes, so neither looks trivially small beside the trainer. The elongated creature reads as a large animal; F03's crouched posture prevents a standing-height inference. Different depths and poses prevent a defensible numerical size comparison or species scale-ladder verdict. Nearby trees and distant buildings cannot be measured against the trainer because no suitably colocated building or tree is shown. There is no obvious giant-prop mismatch in these views, but the required built destination provides no usable scale evidence.

## Three largest gaps, ranked

1. **A place to arrive at is missing from the image — all frames.** The key art's settlement and waterside panels show buildings, paths, fences and shoreline details composing a destination; Palworld's base and plateau images similarly give structures and landforms a purpose. These frames show a named empty grassy crest. A visible built approach and local dressing would change the location read much more than swapping the creature.
2. **Ground and vegetation do not form an authored habitat — F01/F03 most clearly.** Palworld's field/path images separate dirt, grass tufts, shrubs and tree edges. Here a large, blurred, mottled grass surface fills most of the picture, with insufficient mid-layer and no readable material transition. The key art's clustered natural palette is also missing in composition, independent of its painted detail.
3. **Creature identity and trainer legibility break across lighting — F01–F04.** Palworld's creatures provide readable faces and bold coherent surfaces. F01 has camouflage and mottling; F03 has a poorly resolved face; F02/F04 exchange these identities for pale surfaces while the trainer's lower body goes black. The key art's night image retains distinct trainer and companion shapes within a shared light mood.

## Binding bars and disposition

**Bar A — Do these frames belong to the world of the Meadows key art? No.** Blue sky, green terrain and a companion supply broad palette affinities, but the bare location composition, thin habitat layering, lack of an inhabited landmark and inconsistent night subject lighting outweigh them. This is not a request to reproduce painted per-leaf fidelity or turn a coastal location into a Meadows village.

**Bar B — Would someone say these are trying to be the same kind of game as Palworld? Yes.** A third-person adventurer facing a large stylized creature in an explorable landscape communicates the same broad creature-adventure genre. **Commercial visual quality comparable with the supplied Palworld screenshots: no.** The genre answer must not be treated as quality acceptance; ground resolution, habitat structure, creature expression and lighting cohesion remain conspicuously below that comparison.

**Scene/engine-addressable work:** demonstrate or compose Reedhaven's actual built approach in the gameplay view; establish local prop/vegetation groupings and a readable ground transition; reduce ground texture competition; preserve distant landform separation; bring creatures, trainer and foliage into a coherent night value structure; improve HUD density and keycap legibility. The missing destination grouping is local evidence. Ground, foliage and night symptoms recur across this set and are candidates for shared-system investigation, but a single-location blind review cannot establish code-level causes.

**Art-dependent residuals:** clear creature eye/face design, cleaner intentional surface markings and expressive silhouettes may require mesh/texture/pose art work if presentation changes do not expose existing detail. The trainer is readable by day but presents a conventional backpack-adventurer identity rather than a distinctive signature. These stills cannot prove whether the needed art already exists in the installed assets, so they do not justify claiming new asset procurement is necessary. There is no visible basis to reopen general water shading: too little water is shown.

Acceptance remains open for the Reedhaven built-destination criterion, night trainer legibility, creature face readability, and ground/vegetation quality.
