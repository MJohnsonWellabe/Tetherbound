# Blind visual judgment — F01 and F02

Viewed the two neutral frames and all six supplied reference images. No source, change history, provenance, or other judgments were inspected. This is a still-image judgment; motion, performance, traversal, and unseen content are unassessed.

**Preference: no meaningful preference.** F01 and F02 show effectively the same terrain, vegetation, character pose, water, lighting, and composition at the supplied viewing size. F01 shows the Reedhaven location label; F02 omits it, advances the displayed minute, and changes the minimap orientation. Those interface differences do not establish an improvement to the world image.

**Character and creature finding first:** In both frames, the trainer has a readable head, backpack, blue sleeves, and human proportions, but the small rear view does not demonstrate the distinctive character expression and appeal visible in the Palworld references. The feet and lower legs are partially lost in the grass. No identifiable creature is visible, so creature appeal, creature-to-trainer scale, and the visual relationship between companions and their habitat cannot be accepted from these frames. Absence here is not evidence about the entire game.

## Eight criteria

1. **Silhouette and readability at small size.** In F01 and F02, the trainer's head, pack, and arms survive a thumbnail-style read, as do the lake and the opposite hill. Individual foreground vegetation collapses into a bright striped carpet. Small rocks and sparse distant trees are weak accents rather than recognizable destination cues. The player's legs lose separation against the grass; no creature silhouette supplies a second focal subject.

2. **Colour and value structure.** Both frames have a usable broad separation between pale blue water, green land, blue-grey sky, and dark cliff faces. The grass occupies most of the lower image with conspicuous pale mint highlights over yellow-green ground. Its repeated high contrast competes with the trainer. The distant slope is a largely uniform light olive field, with little restrained colour grouping to guide the eye. No visible friendly element shows an obvious oxblood misuse; no danger element is available to assess that colour's intended role.

3. **Intentionality.** Both frames read as terrain populated by a repeated grass treatment. Grass density and blade scale remain very consistent across the foreground slope, while the opposite hill is sparsely dotted with small stones and thin trees. There are a few rock groupings and a short dark wooden-looking horizontal element at the near shore, but they do not form a strong authored waterside place. The references use paths, distinct groves, banks, structures, and clearings to explain where activity happens.

4. **Lighting.** Both frames read as daylight. Strong shading on the cut faces of the opposite hill gives those planes volume, but the foreground grass remains broadly bright and evenly insistent. It is difficult to read convincing ground contact around the trainer's feet through the blades. The frame does not strongly communicate a particular early-morning light direction despite the displayed clock. Pale water and a muted sky contribute little reflected-light or surface variation visible at this scale.

5. **Horizon and depth.** Both frames establish foreground, water, opposite bank, and more distant pale landforms, so distance is legible. The large central hill terminates in conspicuously straight, steep exposed faces and an angular notch; it reads more as a cut land block than the references' naturally layered rock and rolling terrain. Sparse tiny trees give the skyline little rhythm. Fog does not visibly erase the world. No definite LOD or chunk seam can be proven from the stills; the angular terrain boundaries are the visible defect, whatever their cause.

6. **Interface.** In both frames the main-story panel is easy to read and safely inset. The minimap has clear bounds and a visible player marker. The large empty five-slot strip and long command strip consume substantial lower-right space without conveying much state in this moment. Bottom-left health and food text has weaker contrast against the visible grass, especially the food label. Small white key prompts are difficult to identify at reduced size. F01's location title provides context without overlapping another panel; its omission in F02 is not an environmental improvement. Interface is assessed on its own merits, not against Palworld's UI design.

7. **Artefacts.** Both frames show highly repetitive straight, pale grass blades and hard-edged ground transitions that look synthetic. The water reads as an almost featureless opaque pale sheet, with an abrupt sand boundary on the far bank. These are visible presentation issues, not proof of a particular shader or geometry bug. No definite z-fighting, floating prop, or character penetration can be established. Popping and temporal shimmer are not judgeable from stills.

8. **Scale agreement.** Relative to the 1.80 m trainer, much of the nearby grass reaches into the lower-leg/knee region and makes a continuous tall field rather than varied groundcover. That height is physically plausible, but its pervasive use hides the character's contact with the ground. The large hills and nearby rock masses read larger than a person, while the distant trees are too far away to infer a precise size discrepancy. No clear contradiction in the trainer's own proportions is visible. There is no creature ruler comparison available, so companion scale remains unassessed.

## Three largest gaps from the references

1. **No central creature appeal or visible activity — F01 and F02.** Palworld 01, 03, and 04 make distinctive creatures immediately legible through large silhouettes, strong colour placement, and relationships to the player. Even the base view, Palworld 05, makes creatures part of the place. These frames offer only a small rear-facing trainer in an empty-feeling landscape. They do not demonstrate the game's main visual attraction.

2. **Repetition replaces vegetation composition — F01 and F02.** Palworld 02 and 04 use readable paths, low and tall vegetation, tree shadow, and open ground to shape space. The key art groups flowers, shrubs, and broad tree canopies around routes and water. These frames fill most of the foreground with similarly bright upright blades, then leave the far hill sparsely decorated. Density alone is not producing a richer place.

3. **Terrain and water lack an authored destination — F01 and F02.** The key art's water views have irregular banks, rocks, groves, docks, and buildings with a clear relationship to the shore. Palworld 04 gives the view a landmark and layered rock formations. Here the broad pale water plane faces a blunt angular hill with no compelling focal feature. The result is more exposed and diagrammatic than welcoming or mysterious.

## Separate bar verdicts

**A — Belongs to the key-art world: No.** Green hills, blue water, and the backpacked traveller establish compatible subject matter, but the unbroken mint-bright grass, angular bare hill faces, sparse tree silhouettes, and almost blank waterside composition do not carry the board's natural palette hierarchy, grove language, or inviting sense of place. This does not compare painted leaf detail with real-time rendering.

**B — Trying to be the same kind of game as Palworld: Yes.** The third-person stylized adventurer, open grassy terrain, exploration presentation, and creature-selection interface signal the same broad kind of game. This is a genre resemblance only. The supplied views do not demonstrate the creature presence or commercial finish of the reference screenshots.

**Actual commercial visual quality at the supplied Palworld bar: No.** Both frames are held back by repetitive foreground treatment, coarse terrain and shoreline presentation, and the lack of a strong living or authored focal subject. There is no meaningful improvement between them that changes that answer.

## Actionable split

**Scene and presentation work supported by the visible evidence:** break the continuous grass into varied masses and clearings; reduce the dominance of its pale highlights; provide lower vegetation around the trainer; compose a legible shoreline route or gathering place; soften or restructure the conspicuous straight hill cuts; group trees and rocks with purposeful scale variation; give the water visible surface variation; and stage identifiable creatures in the forward view. Improve bottom-left HUD contrast and the information density of the empty slot area.

**Art adequacy that scene changes alone cannot certify:** the images do not show enough of the trainer, or any creature, to determine whether the available character and creature art meets the bespoke appeal of the references. Likewise, the sparse thin trees shown here do not establish that a convincing broad-canopy grove can be built from the available art. A closer character/creature view and a deliberately composed grove would distinguish a staging failure from an art deficiency. No claim that replacement assets must be bought or made is justified by these two frames alone.
