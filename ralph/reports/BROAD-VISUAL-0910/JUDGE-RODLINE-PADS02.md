## Full rubric

1. **Silhouette and readability at small size.** In all four frames, the trainer, pale pointed-ear creatures, huge tree trunks, roofed well, and cottage survive reduction. The ground collapses to brown-green mottling with fine bright speckle rather than readable grass masses. F03/F04's dark entrance is a useful destination cue; F01/F02's pale rectangular frontage reads more like a closed facade. In every frame, the quest panel covers a substantial portion of the cottage and the foreground NPC is split by the action strip and greeting prompt. In F03/F04, the enlarged empty team panel intrudes into the right creature's silhouette. Keep the entrance and encounter participants clear of interface blocks, and establish a visible approach between the trainer and cottage.

2. **Colour and value structure.** F01/F03 belong to one palette, but the busy olive-brown ground, extremely green canopy, stark pale trunks, and vivid red shrub clumps compete rather than form a hierarchy. The red shrubs are ordinary landscape accents yet carry a danger-like emphasis; nothing in these frames identifies them as hostile. F02/F04 turn the sky convincingly blue but preserve bright cream creatures, pale cottage plaster, and fine luminous grass strokes. This separates the cast, but does not create the key art's coherent blue night with selective warm habitation. Establish larger quiet ground values and reserve strong accents for subjects and destinations.

3. **Intentionality.** All four frames show isolated well and cottage objects in a broad clearing, with no convincing path, porch activity, fence grouping, or occupation pattern connecting them. The grass has numerous similarly narrow upright tufts, and the repeated red shrub clumps draw attention to scatter. The enormous closely related tree forms supply a setting but overpower the small settlement. F03/F04's opening makes the house more purposeful, but the ground still runs into it without a clearly authored threshold. Palworld-05 uses a legible worked clearing and grouped facilities; the key art's settlement links doors, paths, fences, and vegetation. Concentrate vegetation into verge masses and make one continuous used surface connect the settlement elements.

4. **Lighting and grounding.** F01/F03 read as daytime and F02/F04 as night chiefly through the sky and overall darkness. Roofs and plaster remain unusually bright at night without a visible local light explaining a welcoming destination. Ground mottling is so strong that contact shadows are difficult to separate from the texture. F01/F02's well rests on a visible dark slab, but its clean outline reads as an object laid over the soil rather than an integrated apron. F03/F04 expose a small dark sliver below the well, while the cottage arch opens into ground-colored darkness with no readable step or interior floor. Give bases coherent terrain contact and the entrance a visible threshold; show local illumination where the night composition asks the player to go.

5. **Horizon and depth.** The near trees are large, but in all frames the middle distance quickly ends in a nearly horizontal ground/sky boundary. The small pale gate shape at far left does little to establish a substantial destination or distant landscape. There is little overlap of distinct landforms and no strong atmospheric recession. Palworld-04 layers the road, buildings, cliffs, tower, and mountains; the key art similarly gives paths a distant purpose. The Enshrouded image reinforces this point through structures seated on clearly readable rock shelves and successive landscape layers, not through its different sunset palette. Add an intermediate terrain/vegetation layer and a composed landmark relationship. No definite chunk seam can be established from these stills.

6. **Interface.** Main-story text and the interaction prompt are readable, and the visible panels stay within the frame. The hierarchy is nevertheless too costly: an unrelated large story card covers the cottage, empty numbered team slots occupy substantial space, and the lower action strip covers the encounter. F03/F04's increased slot-panel height is a visible regression. The minimap is largely a featureless green square and supplies little visible orientation. Small weather text, muted food text, and tiny key glyphs become difficult at reduced size. Reduce inactive panel footprint, preserve the destination and cast, and give map marks meaningful contrast. This is an independent usability judgment; Palworld's UI is not used as a design target.

7. **Artefacts.** F01/F02 show hard exposed slab edges beneath the well; F03/F04 show a separate-looking dark sliver on the ground beneath it. The latter reads as a stray piece or disconnected foundation, although a still cannot identify the cause. All frames exhibit very thin, high-contrast grass strokes and noisy ground texture that undermine the intended surface volume. In F01/F02, foliage tangles with the right creature's lower body; in F03/F04, the team panel becomes the greater obstruction. The foreground human/trainer overlap also makes their bodies hard to separate. No claim of z-fighting, popping, or animation failure is justified by these images.

8. **Scale agreement.** Using the trainer as the supplied 1.80 m ruler, the nearby fox-like creatures read as notably shorter companions, while the trunks are monumental and the cottage is visually tiny beneath them. Perspective prevents exact height measurements or a reliable world-space doorway measurement, but F03/F04's lowered roof and small arch strengthen the miniature-house impression relative to F01/F02. The images do not identify which creature is a combat partner versus a practice opponent, so that role-specific scale relationship is unjudgeable. The cropped foreground human is likewise not a usable height comparison. Check a trainer standing at the doorway and stage the cast side by side before claiming scale agreement.

## Three largest reference gaps, ranked

1. **Ground has coverage marks rather than grass volume — F01–F04.** Palworld-02/03/04 show cohesive grass beds with clear path boundaries and depth; these frames show repeated needles over dominant mottled soil. The key art supports the same broad arrangement of quiet paths and planted margins without requiring painted per-leaf fidelity. This is primarily a scene/material/density task; the existing grass asset's ability to form convincing masses must be tested rather than assumed.

2. **The settlement does not feel seated in an authored landscape — F01–F04.** The key art connects buildings to paths, thresholds, fences, and landforms; Palworld-04/05 give destinations readable approaches and inhabited clearings. Here the cottage and well sit separately on ambiguous ground, and the horizon offers little destination depth. F03/F04 improve the entrance cue but expose weaker well-base continuity. Composition, terrain contact, planting, and lighting can address much of this with scene work. Any missing threshold/foundation or settlement-detail pieces require suitable art; the screenshots cannot establish whether that art is already available.

3. **Cast presentation lacks the reference's clarity and emphasis — especially F03/F04.** Palworld-01/03 make creatures the unmistakable event, while Palworld-04 differentiates several creatures through silhouette, scale, and placement. These frames give two similar small creatures near-identical frontal presentation and allow panels to obscure the right creature and foreground human. Staging, interface footprint, scale verification, and subject lighting are scene/presentation work. Distinctive model expression and a unified character finish, if still deficient in unobstructed closer views, need character-art work; they cannot be supplied by extra grass.

## Required bar answers

**A. Do these frames read as belonging to the world in the Meadows key art? No.** The timber-and-stone cottage, giant trees, natural ground, and appealing animal companions provide compatible ingredients. Flat mottled terrain, isolated building placement, weak destination depth, and a night without a coherent inhabited light focus prevent the images from conveying that board's inviting, authored world. This is a palette, composition, silhouette, and mood judgment, not a demand for painted fidelity. Much of the gap is fixable through scene assembly and lighting; any unsuitable vegetation or building-contact art must be replaced or supplemented if scene work cannot produce the required shapes.

**B. Beside the five Palworld screenshots, would someone say these are trying to be the same kind of game? Yes.** The third-person trainer, conspicuous stylized creatures, wooded open area, cottage, and team interface clearly communicate the same broad creature-adventure genre. **That is not commercial-quality parity: these frames do not meet the supplied Palworld shipping-quality bar.** Ground volume, inhabited composition, depth, and unobstructed cast presentation remain substantial gaps. The same-genre answer must not be treated as acceptance of their finish.
