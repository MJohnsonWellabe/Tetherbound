# Rodline Post — blind visual verdict

Inspected F01, F02, F03 and F04 individually from `.artifacts/broad-visual-0910/neutral-rodline-workshop01/`, the actual `docs/reference/tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`, and all five `docs/reference/palworld-0*.jpg` images. No implementation, diffs, history or other verdicts were inspected. The supplied location is Rodline Post; the visible HUD names The Conductor Run. These stills do not establish the reason for that difference. Pair labels imply no chronology.

The two pale, large-eared creatures are immediately recognizable and have appealing facial shapes. However, their repeated appearance supplies little visual variety, and their pale bodies lose surface definition at night (F02, F04). The trainer reads as an adventurer, but the rear view and partially obscured foreground character do not establish the expressive character quality seen in the Palworld references. The largest commercial-quality shortfall is the environment surrounding them.

## Eight-criterion review

1. **Silhouette and readability at small size.** In all four frames, the trainer, two creatures, well and cottage remain identifiable when the whole image is considered at thumbnail size. Cream creatures separate well from dark ground. Thin grass becomes a speckled field rather than convincing vegetation masses. Huge repeated grey trunks compete with the small settlement as the dominant shapes; red bushes demand attention unrelated to the creatures. The foreground character is hidden by the trainer and interaction prompt, so that character's silhouette cannot be judged fairly.

2. **Colour and value structure.** F01 and F03 contain a natural yellow-green/brown base, but the ground is muddy and mottled, with intensely red bushes and bright lime grass competing against it. The key art uses green vegetation as a coherent middle ground, with sunlight, paths and flowers giving organized contrast. Here the red vegetation makes red an ordinary landscape accent; its intended danger meaning cannot be identified visually. F02 and F04 change convincingly to a blue night sky, but the near-white creatures and illuminated-looking grass retain unusually strong contrast against near-black soil. All frames clearly belong to the same place.

3. **Intentionality.** All frames read as a small cottage and well placed on a broad terrain patch. There is no strong worn route linking the well, door and inhabited space. Grass is distributed as many separated upright strokes, while shrubs repeat as isolated colored bundles. The large trunks have similar visual weight and spacing. Palworld's base reference communicates occupation through surfaces, work areas, structures and grouped objects; these frames have little equivalent settlement storytelling. An authored clearing with a legible approach and concentrated activity would matter more than indiscriminately adding scatter.

4. **Lighting.** Day and night are recognizable in their respective pairs. F01/F03 provide warm roofs and brighter terrain, but broad ground shading fails to describe much terrain form, and the bright cottage face reads somewhat detached from its surroundings. F02/F04 preserve readable subjects but lack the key art's coherent pools of warm domestic light against cooler night terrain. F02's brighter door gives a slightly stronger suggestion of habitation than F04's subdued door; neither creates a convincing light pool on the approach. Objects do not obviously float, but contact and grounding are visually weak around the creatures and sparse grass.

5. **Horizon and depth.** All four frames lead to a nearly level, shallow horizon beneath the giant trunks. The small left-side gate shape is readable, yet there is little intervening terrain structure or layered distance to make it an enticing destination. Palworld's plateau view and the key art organize nearby foliage, intermediate landforms and distant landmarks into distinct depth layers. These images feel like a local asset clearing opening onto a flat edge. No definite chunk seam or LOD transition is established by these stills.

6. **Interface.** Major text is legible, but the story panel obscures much of the cottage, and the broad action strip plus slot panel occupy substantial right-side and lower-middle space. The interaction prompt overlaps the nearby character. The food and health panels have weak contrast against vegetation, while the minimap offers little recognizable geography. F04 lacks the central location title visible in F01–F03; no cause is inferred. Keyboard glyphs in large empty-looking slot boxes do not communicate creature identity. These are internal hierarchy observations, not comparisons with Palworld's UI.

7. **Artefacts.** F01–F04 show extremely noisy grass edges and coarse, blurry ground patches at the same time, producing an inconsistent level of visual detail. Large trunks expose strong longitudinal surface streaking. Grass crosses the right creature's lower silhouette, reducing separation; these stills cannot distinguish ordinary occlusion from actual penetration. F01 has a small orange/brown object visible in the cottage doorway that is not equally visible in F03; its identity is unresolved. No definite z-fighting can be established, and movement-related defects cannot be judged from stills.

8. **Scale agreement.** Relative to the trainer, the cottage and well are broadly legible at human scale, whereas the enormous trunks make the cottage seem miniature beneath them. The combination feels less like the key art's oak village and more like tiny structures in an oversized forest. Both creatures stand farther from the camera than the trainer; their smaller screen heights therefore do not prove a world-height violation. Their shared species and similar apparent sizes provide no evidence for a role-based scale comparison. The hidden foreground character is unsuitable as a second ruler.

## Pair comparison

**No meaningful overall preference between F01/F02 and F03/F04.** Composition, settlement content, terrain coverage, creature presentation and major lighting weaknesses remain effectively the same. Small grass placement differences, minimap orientation, doorway detail and the absent location title in F04 do not change the reference gap. The brighter doorway in F02 is a minor night-mood advantage for that frame; it does not make F01/F02 a materially stronger environment. This conclusion assumes no before/after order.

## Three ranked gaps

1. **Ground and settlement integration — all frames.** Palworld's field and base images use coherent grass cover, clear paths and grouped activity areas. These frames expose a large muddy texture under separated grass strokes, with a well and cottage that do not form a convincing inhabited place. Fixable through scene composition, terrain/material treatment, vegetation massing and a purposeful courtyard/approach. Better surface and vegetation art may be needed if the current assets cannot produce coherent masses; inventory was not inspected.

2. **Landscape composition and depth — all frames.** The key art and Palworld plateau view offer rolling or stepped landforms, varied tree masses and recognizable distant destinations. These frames offer similarly dominant trunks above a shallow, nearly level horizon. Fixable principally through terrain, placement, scale relationships and distant composition; any need for replacement tree art depends on whether the existing silhouettes can be varied convincingly.

3. **Lighting and material cohesion — especially F02/F04.** The references organize sunlight, shadow and atmospheric distance, and the key-art night scene makes warm settlement light a focal point. These frames retain bright isolated vegetation and washed-out creature bodies over blackened ground, without persuasive local domestic illumination. Scene lighting and material response are actionable. Additional expressive character/creature art cannot be replaced by lighting, but these angles are insufficient to prescribe replacement of the visible cast.
