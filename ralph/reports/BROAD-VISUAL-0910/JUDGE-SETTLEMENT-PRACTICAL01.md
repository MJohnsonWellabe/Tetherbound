# Blind settlement visual review

Reviewed F01–F04 against the Meadows key-art board and all five Palworld references. This verdict concerns only the supplied stills; it makes no claims about motion, performance, or unseen assets.

**Character and creature adequacy first:** No creature is clearly identifiable in any of F01–F04. The defining subject of the Palworld references is therefore absent from this view. The trainer is legible through the blue shirt, pale collar and backpack, but the rear silhouette and earth-coloured equipment read as a conventional fantasy adventurer rather than a distinctive lead. The distant humans are too small, and the central human too shaded at night, to establish expressive character quality. These frames do not demonstrate a character-and-creature cast adequate to the reference bar. This is an evidence limit as well as a presentation defect; unseen creature art cannot be condemned or approved.

**Preferred pair: tie.** F01/F02 and F03/F04 present essentially the same settlement composition, surfaces, vegetation and day/night lighting. F03 adds the visible village title; F02 has a visible person at far right. Neither difference resolves a substantial visual gap. I cannot identify a meaningful environmental improvement in either pair.

## Eight rubric criteria

1. **Silhouette and readability at small size.** In F01/F03, roof masses, the well canopy, trainer and foreground dead tree survive a thumbnail view. Individual grass blades and flowers combine into busy green texture. The dead tree occupies a larger silhouette than the trainer and cuts across the approach to the central human, giving a gatherable prop disproportionate prominence. In F02/F04, the trainer remains conspicuously bright while the central human and well base become difficult to separate from the dark ground. No creature silhouette supplies a second focal subject.

2. **Colour and value structure.** F01/F03 have coherent warm roofs, pale masonry and green surroundings, but strong yellow-green mottling covers most of the foreground and competes with the blue shirt. The references give creature colour larger, simpler areas against quieter terrain. F02/F04 divide into a readable blue sky and roofs, luminous windows, and near-black ground. The middle values needed to describe the settlement floor are thin. No conspicuous oxblood danger colour appears on friendly elements in these frames.

3. **Intentionality.** The well, branching paths, fences, firewood and house grouping make F01/F03 recognizably arranged as a village. However, the foreground vegetation reads as a broad scatter across the entire yard; it does not form convincing worn spaces around the player route or a clearly tended domestic edge. The isolated dead tree near the visual centre feels unusually prominent for this settlement composition. The Palworld base reference uses open circulation space and clustered activity to make habitation immediately readable.

4. **Lighting.** Day and night are unmistakable. F01/F03 show directional shadows and grounded buildings, although the highly patterned ground obscures some terrain form. In F02/F04, windows are bright rectangles without enough visible warm spill onto walls, thresholds and the central gathering space. The trainer's pale shoulders, shirt and backpack remain much brighter than the nearby human and yard, weakening the sense that both inhabit the same light. The scene needs useful local light around habitation, with preserved dark areas between it.

5. **Horizon and depth.** Overlapping houses and trees establish near and middle distance in all frames. The large right-hand house and surrounding roofs largely close the view, leaving little visual connection to an explorable landscape or a distant landmark. That is acceptable for a village pocket, but it cannot demonstrate the layered hills and destination silhouettes of the key art or Palworld plateau. The night sky's very large bright moon attracts attention above a poorly readable village floor. No clear terrain chunk seam is visible; LOD transitions cannot be assessed from these stills.

6. **Interface.** The quest text and interaction prompt are legible and remain inside the frame. The quest panel, five-slot panel and lower-right control panel together occupy substantial right-side space. The bottom-left food and health text have weaker contrast against vegetation than the quest text. The small clock is less immediately readable. F03's village title is clear, though close to the roof silhouette. These observations concern the supplied interface itself, not a comparison with Palworld's UI.

7. **Artefacts.** Fine roof, grass and foliage edges appear harsh and stippled in F01/F03; the night roof edges in F02/F04 form a pronounced repeated bright-line pattern. The clouds in F02/F04 look like dark streaks or cut-out patches beneath the moon rather than softly integrated cloud masses. These are visible surface/sky presentation problems, not proof of a specific rendering fault. No definite z-fighting or mesh intersection can be established here. Popping is untestable in stills.

8. **Scale agreement.** Relative to the 1.80 m trainer, fence height and house storeys are broadly plausible. Perspective prevents a precise height comparison with the central human, but no clear human-scale contradiction is visible. The foreground grass reaches a substantial portion of the trainer's lower legs, contributing to an untended-field impression inside the village. The dead tree is nearly trainer-sized and visually dominates nearby small props. Creature-to-trainer and creature-to-creature scale cannot be assessed because no creature is identifiable.

## Three largest gaps, ranked

1. **The creature game is missing from the composition — F01–F04.** Palworld's exploration, base and fight images all make recognizable creatures prominent participants. Here the visible subjects are houses, grass, a gatherable tree and ordinary human figures. Scene-fixable: include an existing creature in an unobstructed village activity space with readable colour and silhouette, and reduce foreground competition. Art-dependent: a distinctive, expressive creature and trainer design must then hold up in that view. The stills do not establish whether adequate creature art exists elsewhere in the build; the trainer's generic rear presentation alone does not prove a replacement mesh is necessary.

2. **Night habitation lacks readable local warmth — F02/F04.** The key-art night scene preserves terrain layers and directs warm light around habitation. These frames largely offer bright window panes above a blackened yard, with a disproportionately bright player. Scene-fixable: shape window/door/lantern light around thresholds, the well and walking routes; rebalance the player against nearby people; retain middle values in the ground. No missing mesh is demonstrated by this gap.

3. **The settlement floor is noisy and insufficiently organized — F01/F03, with readability loss carried into F02/F04.** The key-art settlement and Palworld base distinguish traversable earth, planted margins and activity clusters. Here saturated mottled ground and repeated long blades dominate the foreground, while the bare tree competes with the characters. Scene-fixable: quieter ground values, worn clearings and edge planting, fewer tall blades in circulation space, and a less dominant deadwood placement. Art-dependent only if the visible material/foliage edge harshness cannot be resolved with the supplied assets' materials and presentation; no asset-inventory conclusion is possible from these pictures.

## Separate verdicts

**A. Belongs to the Meadows key-art world: yes.** The warm timber-and-stone roofs, well, low fences, wildflowers and green village setting carry the same broad settlement language. This is a palette, composition and mood match at the world-identity level, with substantial shortcomings in ground organization and nighttime warmth. It is not a claim of matching painted detail.

**B. Beside the Palworld references, reads as trying to be the same kind of game: no, on these supplied frames.** They communicate a third-person fantasy gathering village; none communicates the conspicuous creature companionship or creature activity that anchors every supplied gameplay reference. The absence in this view can be scene-fixable. The quality of creature art remains untested, and must be judged when visible.

**Commercial readiness: no.** Neither pair supplies convincing creature-game identity, and the nighttime yard readability and busy daylight ground remain materially below the reference presentation. The architecture is a useful foundation, but these views do not establish a finished commercial character-and-creature experience.
