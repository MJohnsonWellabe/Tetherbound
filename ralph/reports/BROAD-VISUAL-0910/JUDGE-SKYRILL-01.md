# Skyrill — independent image review

Evidence: F01–F04, the Meadows key art, and all five supplied Palworld references. Isolated presentation views only; no habitat, gameplay, motion, or production-world certification.

The winged, squat creature has an immediately readable friendly-dragon outline, but its face and surface finish do not meet the commercial creature-art bar. I cannot confidently identify discrete eyes. In F01/F03, a dark teal circular patch high on the viewer-left cheek is a possible eye, with another dark area on the opposite side; neither has a clear pupil, eyelid or bounded eye shape. The broad muzzle and curved mouth are locatable, but the beige mouth-adjacent pattern competes with them. In F02/F04 at thumbnail size, the face becomes a pale blob with dark speckling rather than a readable expression.

## Eight-category review

1. **Silhouette/readability:** F02/F04 retain extended wings, head fins, curled tail and planted legs at small size. Facial landmarks do not survive. F01/F03 crop the wings and body, useful for surface inspection but insufficient as silhouette evidence.
2. **Colour/value:** All frames separate a bright cyan subject from dull green ground. Pale belly versus darker back supplies useful large-scale grouping. On F01/F03, teal blotches and broken cream stripes obscure the face; very bright cyan flattens the muzzle. No visible oxblood misuse.
3. **Intentionality:** The broad proportions and wing outline read as deliberately designed. F01/F03 have patchy, block-like markings across mouth and wings that lack the clean material regions and facial focus visible in Palworld-01/05. The subject's surface reads unfinished, regardless of its origin.
4. **Lighting:** F02/F04 have a contact shadow beneath the body, grounding the pose. F01/F03 show underside shading but weak separation among brow, eye area and muzzle. Broad daylight is suggested; a specific time of day is not established.
5. **Horizon/depth:** F02/F04 have a bare, nearly straight ground/sky division and no distance layers. This limits presentation evidence; it is not evidence that a production habitat lacks depth. No visible chunk seam or assessable LOD transition.
6. **Interface:** No interface shown; safe areas and gameplay hierarchy cannot be judged. The thin grey vertical marker in all frames is unexplained presentation clutter.
7. **Artefacts:** F01/F03 reveal conspicuous blocky cream/teal surface transitions and angular cheek/mouth contours. They resemble unfinished texture/shape transitions; their technical cause is not identifiable from images. No definite z-fighting or intersection can be certified. Motion defects are unassessable.
8. **Scale agreement:** No trainer or labelled ruler is present. The grey marker provides no known unit. Absolute size, species-role scale and relative companion scale cannot be established; internal head/body/wing proportions remain readable in F02/F04.

## Set comparison

F01 versus F03 and F02 versus F04 show no meaningful visible appearance or readability difference. I see neither a defensible improvement nor a regression. The same ambiguous eye patches, mouth markings and wing/body silhouette persist. This is a visual judgement, not a claim of pixel identity.

## Ranked addressable gaps

1. **Facial landmarks — F01–F04.** Palworld-01/05 use distinct eyes and mouth forms that resolve quickly. Give this subject bounded eyes, clear gaze direction and a cleaner mouth silhouette; verify in the full-body thumbnail. The Pokemon principle is facial legibility, not style imitation. This needs creature-art work; scene lighting alone cannot supply missing eye definition.
2. **Surface hierarchy and finish — F01/F03, still visible in F02/F04.** Palworld creatures have coherent broad colour regions. Simplify broken cream markings, clarify cheek/wing transitions and preserve contrast around the eyes. This chiefly needs material/texture and possibly shape refinement; light tuning can support it.
3. **Presentation of form — all frames.** The references establish dimensional form and natural palette relationships through their lighting and surroundings. Use a less flattening light arrangement and a restrained contextual presentation with readable depth to test the cyan creature against natural terrain. This is addressable through scene, lighting and composition, but cannot substitute for the first two art gaps.
