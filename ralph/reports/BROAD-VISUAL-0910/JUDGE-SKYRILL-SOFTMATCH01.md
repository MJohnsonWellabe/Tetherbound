# Blind visual verdict — X / Y

Inspected X1, X2, Y1 and Y2, plus the Meadows key art and all five Palworld references named by the visual-judge skill. X1/Y1 are close views; X2/Y2 are distant views. This verdict uses visible images only.

**Preference: tie.** I cannot identify a repeatable, meaningful advantage for either set in facial readability, surface coherence or silhouette. This is a perceptual tie, not a claim that the files are identical.

The creature has an identifiable broad-winged, squat-bodied fantasy silhouette. Its face is substantially less resolved than that silhouette: in X1 and Y1, dark marks around the eyes compete with cyan surface patches, and the mouth reads mainly as a pale horizontal band. The eyes do not provide a strong expressive focal point. X2 and Y2 preserve the wings and upright head projections, while the expression disappears. Neither set visibly closes that gap.

## Eight rubric dimensions

1. **Silhouette and small-size readability:** X1/Y1 clearly separate the long wings, upright head projections and short legs from the plain backdrop. X2/Y2 retain the wing outline and central body, but the feet and face become minor marks. The silhouette is stronger than the expression in both sets. A blank background cannot establish readability against foliage or terrain.
2. **Colour and value structure:** Both close views use bright turquoise, pale cream wing accents and dark cyan recesses. The cream stripe organizes the wing span, but numerous small pale/dark surface patches compete with the facial features. The belly has readable light/shadow volume. The background and creature share blue hues, though sufficient value contrast preserves the outer shape. No meaningful X/Y palette advantage is visible. Friendly/danger colour allocation cannot be assessed across a cast from this fixture.
3. **Intentionality:** The paired wing markings and compact body provide a recognizable design. In X1/Y1, irregular lumpy highlights and small mottled marks across the wings and head make the surface read less deliberately organized than the large silhouette. The same issue is present in both. These frames cannot establish how the creature fits its habitat or the rest of the roster.
4. **Lighting:** Bright upper wing surfaces and darker undersides give the wings form in all four frames. In X1/Y1, strong small highlights emphasize surface unevenness rather than directing attention to the eyes. No ground contact shadow or environmental context is present, so time of day and placement on terrain are unjudgeable.
5. **Horizon and depth:** Not assessable: all four views use a uniform background without terrain, horizon, atmospheric layers or landmarks. The smaller framing demonstrates reduced subject size, not environmental depth quality.
6. **Interface:** No interface is shown. Safe areas, hierarchy and gameplay legibility cannot be judged.
7. **Artefacts:** X1/Y1 show a jagged, irregular wing edge and patchy-looking surface transitions, but the images do not establish whether these are intended forms, material details or rendering defects. I see no definite X/Y difference in these features. Stills cannot establish popping or motion defects.
8. **Scale agreement:** No trainer, ruler, prop or second species appears. Absolute or relative gameplay size cannot be determined. Internal wing-to-body proportions are consistent between the sets; neither provides evidence of a scale correction.

## Three largest reference gaps

1. **Expressive facial hierarchy — X1/Y1, more pronounced in X2/Y2.** Palworld 01 and 05 give their creatures easily separated eyes, mouth and face regions. Here, the eyes are difficult to distinguish from surrounding surface marks, so personality is weak even in the close view. Address the eye/face colour separation and suppress competing surface detail around it; these images do not prove whether additional geometry is necessary.
2. **Coherent surface treatment — X1/Y1.** Palworld 01, 03 and 05 organize creature surfaces into broad, clean shapes and colour areas. Here, small bright ridges and mottled turquoise patches draw attention across the wings and head. Larger, clearer material regions and less disruptive surface variation would address this asset-level gap. Scenery density alone would not.
3. **Integrated presentation — all four frames.** The key art and Palworld references establish creatures within grass, terrain, lighting and human-scale activity. These isolated views provide none of that evidence. This is a fixture limitation, not a finding that the game world lacks those elements; an in-world render is needed to judge it.

## Bar questions

**A — Key-art direction: yes, narrowly at the creature-design level.** The approachable fantasy proportions and bright creature accent colour are compatible with the board's inviting adventure direction. Neither set establishes the natural landscape palette, composition or mood of the actual world, so this is not an environment acceptance.

**B — Same kind of game as the Palworld references: yes.** The stylized winged companion design reads as belonging to the creature-adventure genre. **Substantial improvement toward that reference bar: no demonstrated difference between X and Y.** A genre resemblance is not evidence of improved execution.

**Commercial bar: no, for either submitted set.** Facial definition and organized surface treatment remain below the clarity of the supplied shipping-game creature references. Eye/face separation and surface simplification need creature-art/material work; environmental lighting and composition require an in-world fixture. These views alone do not justify declaring that new mesh art is required, nor can they certify the wider game's commercial presentation.
