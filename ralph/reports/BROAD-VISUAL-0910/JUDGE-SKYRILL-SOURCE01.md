# Blind visual judgment: X / Y

Reviewed X1 and Y1 at close range, X2 and Y2 at distant range, the Meadows key art, and all five supplied Palworld references. This judgment uses only the visible images. The creature is isolated against a plain blue background, so environment integration and several rubric dimensions remain unestablished.

**Preference: X.** X1 has a more readable face because the pale muzzle, small warm beak, and dark blue head form separate regions. Its pale belly and wing undersides also organize the body. Y1 turns most of these regions turquoise, including much of the face and feet; the scattered pale and yellow marks compete with the eyes. Y is more uniformly colored, but that does not make its surface more coherent. Neither version has the clean, expressive eye treatment or resolved surface shapes of the Palworld creatures.

At distance, X2 retains a pale underside beneath the blue upper body. Y2 remains recognizable as a turquoise winged creature, but the face collapses into the same color family as the torso. The overall wing and body silhouettes are effectively tied. There is no meaningful silhouette improvement between the sets.

## Eight rubric dimensions

1. **Silhouette and small-size readability:** In X2 and Y2 the broad, thin wings, upright head projections, and hanging feet remain identifiable. Individual facial features do not. X has better internal separation; Y does not introduce a stronger outline. At approximately thumbnail size, both read as a winged body rather than a distinctive character.
2. **Color and value structure:** X1 separates blue upper surfaces, a cream underside, and a small warm facial accent. Y1's cyan treatment merges the muzzle, chest, limbs, and head, while fragmented light marks add noise across the wings. X2 preserves the clearer value grouping. Neither set shows danger-color usage in a world context; that part is unestablished.
3. **Intentionality:** Both close frames have mottled markings and lumpy, irregular wing edges that are harder to read as deliberately shaped anatomy than the broad, controlled color masses in the Palworld creatures. X1's color placement explains the anatomy better. Y1's near-total turquoise coverage reads as less selectively authored. Scene arrangement and scatter are unestablished.
4. **Lighting:** Both close frames show lit upper surfaces and a dark underside, providing some volume. X1's head and wing patches can still be mistaken for uneven shading, while Y1's bright cyan upper surfaces make its pale markings harder to distinguish. No ground, contact shadow, or time-of-day context is visible; placement and environmental lighting are unestablished.
5. **Horizon and depth:** Unestablished in all four frames. The uniform background provides no terrain, fog, horizon, or distance layers. The smaller pair establishes reduced display size only.
6. **Interface:** Unestablished; no interface appears.
7. **Artifacts:** X1 and Y1 show irregular, choppy wing contours and muddled patches around the face and wing roots. These are visible polish defects, but the stills cannot establish whether they come from geometry, texture, or lighting. No definite z-fighting or intersection can be diagnosed from these views. Motion defects are unestablished.
8. **Scale agreement:** Unestablished. There is no trainer, terrain object, or second creature as a ruler. X and Y appear equally sized within each corresponding pair; that does not validate their world scale.

## Three largest reference gaps, ranked

1. **Expressive facial construction — X1 and Y1.** Palworld's close Mammorest and the base-building creatures have distinct eyes, mouths or beaks, and large controlled face regions. Here the eyes are difficult to separate from blotches on the head. X's warm beak helps locate the face, but neither face carries comparable expression. This requires deliberate creature face and surface artwork; scene dressing cannot solve it.
2. **Surface clarity — X1/Y1, persisting as color noise in X2/Y2.** The Palworld creatures use smooth, legible volumes and deliberate large markings. These wings and heads look patchy, with ragged-looking edges and small light flecks that do not clarify the anatomy. Simplifying and resolving the creature's surface and contour is art work. Lighting adjustments may reduce some noise, but the supplied views do not establish that lighting alone can fix it.
3. **Belonging to a world — all four frames.** The key art and Palworld references place readable characters within grounded terrain, vegetation, and depth. These isolated views cannot show whether either creature holds its outline against grass, canopy, or combat effects. This gap in evidence is fixable by supplying comparable in-world scenes with a trainer and ground contact; it is not evidence that the unseen world is deficient.

## Bar questions

**A — Key-art direction: Yes for X's creature palette and basic friendly fantasy silhouette, narrowly.** Its blue, cream, and restrained warm accent are compatible with the key art's natural palette and inviting companion language. Y's brighter turquoise coverage is a weaker match. Composition, landmarks, environment mood, and actual world integration remain unestablished; this is not acceptance of those dimensions.

**B — Same broad kind of game as the Palworld references: Yes.** Both visibly propose a stylized fantasy creature companion. **Substantial genre-reference improvement: No.** Y does not materially close the gap in facial readability, authored surface treatment, or silhouette, and X's preference is limited to clearer color separation. Neither is a substantial quality step beyond the other.

**Commercial visual bar: No for either set.** The unresolved faces and patchy wing/head surfaces remain conspicuous in the close views. X is the stronger choice of these two, but choosing it does not establish shipping-quality creature art or in-world acceptance.
