1. **Silhouette and readability at small size.** In both F01 and F02, the trainer, left trunk, right bush, and gate remain identifiable when considering the image at roughly thumbnail size. The trainer's legs merge into the dark ground more than the blue shoulders do. Grass becomes a fine, fairly uniform yellow-green noise band. The right bush collapses into a single dark green mass that hides a substantial part of the approach to the gate. Small figures or objects beyond the trainer do not have enough separation to identify confidently. Palworld-03 and -04 retain distinct creature shapes and large readable colour blocks; these captures offer no equivalent creature focal point.

2. **Colour and value structure.** F01 and F02 share a consistent green/brown daylight palette, but the yellow-green slopes and near-black foreground shadows create a harsher split than the welcoming greens, warm paths, and cool distance of the key art. The desaturated blue-grey sky occupies a large area without supplying the board's bright atmosphere. The dark foreground bush contributes little internal value variation. Oxblood is concentrated on the gate banners; no visible friendly element clearly misuses it, although the gate's affiliation cannot be established from colour alone.

3. **Intentionality.** The gate, barrels, and banners create a deliberate destination in both captures. Grass clusters and flowers supply some local variation. However, repeated upright blade shapes scattered over broad textured slopes still read as applied coverage rather than richly connected meadow growth. Pebbles are isolated on an otherwise broad dark foreground patch. The cropped trunk and oversized near bush dominate the framing without forming a clear reveal toward the gate. Palworld-02 and -05 use grassy margins and distinct bare circulation areas more coherently; the key art guides the eye along paths into settlements and landmarks.

4. **Lighting.** Both frames clearly read as daylight, with sunlit slopes and a shaded foreground. Shadows ground the tree and trainer, but the shaded dirt becomes very dark and heavily mottled, and the near bush looks like overlapping flat leaf silhouettes. Terrain undulation is visible on the middle slopes, so the scene is not wholly flat-lit. The strongest improvement would be to retain readable surface colour and shape in shade while keeping the sunlit/shaded separation. An exact time of day cannot be inferred independently from this lighting.

5. **Horizon and depth.** The overlapping slopes, gate, and distant tree line give both frames some depth. Their skyline is nevertheless low, largely flat, and abruptly open beneath a huge sky. There is no strong distant destination or large layered landform comparable to the key art's mountain/settlement compositions or Palworld-04's cliffs and tower. Cool atmospheric separation is weak. A conspicuously straight diagonal change crosses the central terrain toward the gate; it looks less natural than the surrounding slope, but these images do not establish whether it is a terrain join, path edge, or another surface boundary. No temporal LOD behavior can be judged.

6. **Interface.** In F01 and F02 the objective is easy to read, and the health and food panels remain within the image edges. The large right-side objective, empty numbered slot strip, and long lower control strip consume considerable scene space and hide foliage and the gate approach. Tiny white keycaps are much less legible than their adjacent labels. The very small dark clock has poor sky contrast. The minimap is visibly coarse and its surrounding border has competing outlines. 7. **Artefacts.** Both frames show highly pointed, individually obvious grass blades against the foreground dirt and serrated-looking foliage contours. The near bush presents conspicuously flat, overlapping leaf shapes and sharp internal dark lines. Ground texture appears smeared/mottled at foreground scale rather than reading as convincing soil structure. The central diagonal boundary deserves closer inspection because of its straightness, but I cannot label its cause from the still. I see no unambiguous z-fighting or floating trainer. Motion popping and temporal aliasing remain outside this evidence.

The left trunk is a very large tree trunk, but its canopy is outside the frame, preventing a full tree proportion assessment. No identifiable pair of creatures is available to test companion/target scale agreement.

## Three ranked gaps from the references

1. **Ground and foliage do not form a convincing continuous meadow — F01 and F02.** Palworld-02, -03, and -05 combine a low, coherent grassy surface with taller accents and clearly shaped bare areas. The key art likewise uses connected meadow carpets around readable paths. These captures expose a large coarse brown foreground, terminate it with conspicuous individual blades, and alternate busy tall growth with thinly covered slopes. Scene work can improve grass/soil blending, shorter basal coverage, shaded colour, and authored clump boundaries. The flat near-bush appearance and coarse surface character may also require improved plant geometry/materials and ground art; simply adding more copies would not solve those qualities.

2. **The composition lacks a compelling distant world — F01 and F02.** The key art supplies broad oak silhouettes, settlement layers, mountains, and visible routes; Palworld-04 anchors its meadow with cliffs, architecture, and a distant tower. Here a low grassy ridge and row of trees sit beneath an expansive sky, while the gate is squeezed beside the opaque foreground bush. Scene work can reposition/cull foreground occlusion, vary grove spacing and scale, shape the approach, and compose stronger terrain layers. Distinctive oak canopies and landmark architecture require suitable art if none is available; this review cannot inventory the build.

3. **The focal art lacks cohesive character-led appeal — F01 and F02.** Palworld-01 through -05 present clean, expressive creature forms and readable characters with coherent surface treatment. The key art's trainer and companion also establish a clear focal pair. These captures instead show a small dark trainer between a coarse ground texture, a flat-leaved bush, brightly stippled tree crowns, and more detailed stone/wood props. Scene lighting and framing can improve the trainer's separation, and a visible companion would allow the game's creature promise to be judged. Expressive character/creature design and consistent plant/material craftsmanship require suitable art, not scatter changes. Creature quality itself is unjudged because no clear creature is shown.
