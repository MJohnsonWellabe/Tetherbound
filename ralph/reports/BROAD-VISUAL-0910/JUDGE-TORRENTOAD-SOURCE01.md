# Blind visual judgment: X and Y

Viewed X1 and Y1 at close range, X2 and Y2 at distant range, the Meadows key art, and all five specified Palworld reference images. This judgment uses visible images only.

**Preference: X. Neither set reaches the commercial creature-art bar.** X has the more coherent surface and a lighter face and belly that preserve more readable form at distance. Y's orange and teal treatment is stronger in hue contrast but breaks into irregular chipped-looking islands across the face, shoulders, and limbs. That is not a substantial improvement toward the genre reference. Both have the same central weakness: the face offers almost no legible eyes, expression, or appealing focal point.

## Eight rubric dimensions

1. **Silhouette and small-size readability.** X2 and Y2 both retain the same squat, broad amphibian body, muscular forearms, and splayed toes. Neither gains a silhouette advantage. The head merges into the shoulder dome; eyes are essentially absent at distant size. X2's cream underside separates mouth, chest, and arms better than Y2's dark orange-brown central mass. In X1 and Y1, the thin mouth opening reads as an uneven slit rather than a clearly designed expression. Address the eye shapes and their contrast, then clarify the brow and mouth forms; recoloring the body cannot supply this missing facial structure.

2. **Colour and value structure.** X1's muted teal and cream suggest an aquatic creature with a reasonably unified natural palette, although the mottled surface is muddy. Y1 introduces warm orange against teal, but the abrupt, scattered boundaries interrupt the anatomy and resemble damaged paint. In Y2, the chest becomes a large brown shadow mass with little separation from the lower face. X2 is clearer. Oxblood danger semantics are unestablished because no faction or gameplay context is present.

3. **Intentionality.** The broad body and fingers look deliberately shaped in both close frames, but the face lacks the controlled feature hierarchy visible in Palworld's creatures. X1's surface has pervasive mottling without a strong focal hierarchy; Y1's fragments look especially arbitrary around the forehead, upper lip, and forearms. Use a few larger, anatomically motivated color regions and give the eyes and mouth their own clean treatment. World clustering and prop placement are unestablished.

4. **Lighting.** Both pairs show comparable directional illumination with a strongly shaded right side and a soft oval beneath the creature. X1 preserves more belly information. Y1's dark underside makes the lower face and chest harder to distinguish. The oval suggests ground contact, but the flat blue backdrop prevents a reliable assessment of contact integration or terrain form. Time of day is unestablished.

5. **Horizon and depth.** Unestablished in X1, X2, Y1, and Y2: these are isolated creature presentations without terrain, horizon, or environmental depth layers. The close/distant views establish projection size only. No judgment about fog, world depth, or LOD behavior follows from them.

6. **Interface.** Unestablished: no interface appears in any frame.

7. **Artefacts.** X1 and Y1 show an irregular pale/dark mouth rim, with facial patches that obscure the intended anatomy. Y1 additionally has abrupt color islands that look like surface breakup rather than a clean stylized marking. These are visible defects in presentation; their technical cause cannot be established from the images. No claim about z-fighting, animation, or popping is supported.

8. **Scale agreement.** Unestablished: no trainer, second species, or measured world object is visible. X and Y occupy equivalent apparent sizes within their respective close and distant views. Absolute creature size and role-relative scale cannot be judged.

## Three largest gaps from the references

1. **Readable, expressive creature faces — X1, Y1, X2, Y2.** Palworld's mammoth, large purple creature, and round white creatures communicate eyes, mouth, and attitude through a few clear features. These candidates present a heavy dome and mouth slit with barely visible eyes. This requires creature art work, not environment density or camera dressing.

2. **Controlled surface design — especially Y1, also X1.** The reference creatures use broad, coherent color regions that reinforce anatomy and remain readable at gameplay distance. Both candidates have confusing surface variation; Y adds harsh flakes across important forms. This needs authored material/texture work and possibly facial geometry refinement. Lighting adjustments could help values, but would not repair the pattern design.

3. **A creature convincingly inhabiting a world — all four frames.** Both references show creatures or companions within composed, vegetated landscapes with scale cues and environmental light. These isolated frames supply no evidence of that integration. This gap in the submitted evidence is addressable through in-world staging and capture; it does not establish that the actual environment lacks those qualities.

## Gates

**A — Key-art direction: No, not established by these frames.** X's natural teal/cream palette is more compatible with the welcoming Meadows direction than Y's fragmented orange/teal surface. But neither demonstrates the board's inviting creature appeal or membership in its landscape. Material and facial art need improvement; an in-world view is needed to judge palette, lighting, scale, and composition together.

**B — Same kind of game as the Palworld references: Yes, at the level of broad creature concept only.** A stylized, exaggerated amphibian could belong to that genre. This does not establish comparable execution. **Substantial improvement toward that reference between the candidates: No.** X is the stronger presentation; Y's hue change does not solve facial readability or silhouette and makes surface coherence worse.

**Commercial bar: No for both.** The missing facial focal point and unresolved surface design remain conspicuous even in the close views. X is preferable, but the preference is not acceptance.
