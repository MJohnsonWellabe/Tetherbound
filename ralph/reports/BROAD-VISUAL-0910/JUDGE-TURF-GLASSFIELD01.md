# Neutral visual judgment: F01–F04

Compared the actual supplied F01, F02, F03 and F04 images, plus `tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png` and all five `palworld-0*.jpg` reference images. F01/F02 and F03/F04 are evaluated as day/night pairs. No implementation, change identity or previous judgment was used to explain the images. No scores.

## Pair preference

**No meaningful preference between F01/F02 and F03/F04.** At the supplied full-frame viewing scale, F01 and F03 have effectively the same ground treatment and foliage integration; F02 and F04 likewise retain the same night treatment. I cannot identify a repeatable, consequential ground or foliage improvement that would justify selecting one pair. This is a perceptual verdict, not a claim of pixel identity.

All four frames show discrete thin grass blades scattered over a broad brown/olive, blurry mottled surface. The soil remains conspicuous between blades, while the much brighter blade tips read as a separate layer. The result resembles planted bristles over a textured floor more than continuous meadow turf. The underlying surface has small muddy patches almost everywhere but lacks convincing larger grassland masses, worn tracks or differentiated ground around plants. F02/F04 accentuate the disconnected bright grass because the base becomes nearly black while many blades remain strongly lit.

## Eight criteria, full frame

1. **Silhouette and readability at small size.** In F01/F03, the trainer's blue sleeves and pale collar separate the torso from the ground. The legs blend into vegetation, and a foreground broadleaf plant overlaps the lower body. Trees and bushes remain identifiable; individual grass blades collapse into fine sparkling noise. In F02/F04, the trainer stays unusually bright and therefore readable, but the dark trunks lose internal shape. The purple spider-like creature at the left tree is recognizable as a many-legged form at full size but becomes a small blue tangle at thumbnail scale. Its distant pose supplies little readable face or personality. The central reddish mounds are visually ambiguous: these images do not make their role clear. The creature presence does not achieve the distinct, expressive focal silhouettes seen in Palworld's meadow and landmark images; the trainer is serviceable at this distance, but no facial or close-detail art judgment is supported.

2. **Colour and value structure.** F01/F03 combine olive-brown ground, vivid yellow-green blades, dark trunks and an almost colourless grey tower. These belong to a generally natural palette, but the brightest distributed detail is the grass, so it competes with the character and creature. F02/F04 establish night with a blue sky and dark terrain; the bright trainer, green blade highlights and pale pinkish central mounds do not feel convincingly united by that night lighting. Red-brown mounds are visible in the day images, but their identity is unknown, so these frames cannot establish whether a danger-reserved colour has leaked onto friendly elements.

3. **Intentionality.** F01–F04 have useful larger plant clusters beside trunks and near the trainer, but short grass repeats across almost all open ground with little meaningful change in height, shape or ecological grouping. The exposed patches between blades are too pervasive to read as purposeful paths. The distant ridge is conspicuously smoother and less populated than the foreground. A continuous route, destination approach or authored clearing is not apparent in this view. The tower rises from the bare ridge without enough intermediate scene structure to connect it to this grove.

4. **Lighting.** F01/F03 read as daytime, and trunk darkness supplies contrast, but the mound and ground forms are weak compared with the dense blade highlights. Local grounding at the trainer's feet is obscured by vegetation; a convincing contact relationship is hard to read. F02/F04 read as nighttime by sky colour and ground darkness, yet illumination across trainer, grass and central mounds is inconsistent in appearance. The central mounds become pale, flat and speckled instead of receiving a coherent dark or moonlit treatment. Night should preserve form through controlled directional values rather than leave so many disconnected bright fragments.

5. **Horizon and depth.** All four frames provide a foreground and a ridge, but little layered landscape beyond them. The oversized grey tower is cut off at the top and appears as a flat vertical mass rather than a comprehensible landmark silhouette. This truncates the depth promise of the vista. F01/F03 have a light sky that separates the ridge; F02/F04 have a blue sky but black, flat-looking cloud strips that distract near the horizon. No definite terrain chunk seam can be established from these stills.

6. **Interface.** All four frames keep widgets inside the visible image, and the objective and interaction text are legible. The objective panel is large enough to dominate the upper-right scene; the empty numbered slot strip and wide control bar occupy much of the lower-right world view. They make an already weak composition harder to inspect. The minimap is mostly an undifferentiated green field, giving little visible route information. Small top-centre time text and the tiny left status are much weaker than the large task panel. This is an internal hierarchy critique, not a comparison with Palworld UI. Controller usability cannot be judged from the keyboard prompts in these captures.

7. **Artefacts.** F01–F04 contain thin light slash-like marks on the grey tower and coarse vertical surface banding. Whatever their origin, these read as stray surface marks rather than deliberate architecture at this distance. F02/F04 make the central mounds look conspicuously flat and stippled, and their black cloud strips resemble cutout fragments. Grass has conspicuous hard blade edges; still images cannot determine whether those edges shimmer in motion. The plant overlapping the trainer's calves is visually messy, but occlusion alone does not prove collision or mesh penetration. No claim of temporal popping or z-fighting is supported.

8. **Scale agreement.** Against the 1.80 m trainer, foreground blades range from low shin cover to roughly knee-height near the camera; nearby broadleaf plants reach into the trainer's lower body. These sizes can coexist, but the almost uniformly short blade family makes the surface feel modelled at one repeated scale. Large trunks plausibly belong to mature trees. The left creature is farther away than the trainer and cannot be measured reliably as though on the same plane. The tower is huge but lacks doors, joints or other readable human-scale anchors. No combat partner or matched practice creature is presented clearly enough to conclude a role-based size mismatch.

## Three largest gaps from the references

1. **Continuous meadow ground and integrated vegetation — F01–F04.** Palworld 02, 03, 04 and 05 use coherent grassy masses, clumped edges, meaningful bare routes and contact shading. These frames show thin luminous blades over omnipresent blurry brown-green patches. Improve the larger turf masses, vary clump height and density, reduce blade/base colour separation, and give bare soil a readable location and purpose. This is the leading gap relevant to the requested comparison.

2. **Readable creatures and authored landscape composition — F01–F04.** Palworld 03/04 puts clear creature silhouettes in a landscape with routes, several depth planes and legible landmarks; the key art likewise joins grove, route and distant destination. Here the only clearly creature-like distant subject is a small blue leg tangle at the left, while the principal landmark is a cropped grey slab behind a nearly empty ridge. Composition and placement can improve creature prominence; landmark design and any deficient creature expression would require adequate art, not merely more scattered grass.

3. **Unified illumination and material response — especially F02/F04, also F01/F03.** The key art's night panel unifies terrain and subjects through blue shadow with selective warm accents. The Palworld scenes keep plant, soil and character shading in the same light. Here the night ground darkens while blades and trainer remain bright and the central mounds turn pale and flat. Daytime likewise separates bright blades from muddy ground rather than revealing continuous terrain form. Lighting/material tuning and contact shading are scene-level work; the images do not identify the technical cause.

## Separate bar verdicts

**A — Belonging to the key-art world: No.** Deciduous trees, flowers, a green rolling ridge and a blue night are compatible ingredients, but the current composition lacks the key art's coherent meadow, layered destination vista and inviting, grounded light. The tower's plain cropped mass further weakens the intended landmark language. This judges palette, mood, composition and silhouettes, not painted per-leaf fidelity.

**B — Trying to be the same kind of game as the Palworld images: Yes.** The third-person backpacked adventurer, visible fantastical creature, open vegetated field, interaction prompt and creature-related HUD make the broad exploration/creature-adventure intent recognizable. This genre resemblance is not a shipping-quality pass.

**Commercial visual quality beside the Palworld references: No, for both pairs.** Neither pair supplies comparable ground cohesion, landscape staging, material/lighting consistency or readable creature presence. The ground differences visible here do not materially change that result. A quiet exploration image does not need combat effects to pass; fight spectacle itself is unassessable because no fight is shown.

**Scene-fixable work:** authored ground/grass transitions and clusters, stronger plant/base value agreement, cleaner foreground staging, clearer routes and intermediate landscape layers, better tower framing, coherent night material response, and tighter HUD hierarchy. **Potential art work:** the landmark needs readable architectural form and surface language if absent from its existing asset; distinctive creature expression and consistent character/creature art cannot be created through scatter or lighting alone. These distant images cannot establish what art exists elsewhere in the build or whether repositioning an existing creature would resolve its presentation, so no asset purchase or replacement is justified solely by this view.

## Limits

These are four stills of one composition at two times of day. They establish appearance here, not biome-wide quality, animation, facial appeal, motion aliasing, traversal feel, combat readability over time, performance or hardware behaviour. I did not infer renderer settings or technical causes from the images. Pair preference remains **no meaningful preference**; A remains **no**, B **yes**, and commercial-quality parity **no** for both pairs.
