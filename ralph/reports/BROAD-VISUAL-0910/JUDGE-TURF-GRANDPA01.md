# Blind visual judgment: F01–F04

Image-only review of F01/F02 versus F03/F04, using the visual-judge skill's eight criteria without scores. Viewed the four individual frames, `docs/reference/tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`, and all five `palworld-0*.jpg` reference images. No implementation source, change description, prior visual verdict, or history was used to make the comparison.

## Preference and evidence sufficiency

**Prefer F01/F02, narrowly, on ground and foliage integration.** The preference is carried by the daylight comparison, F01 versus F03. F01 has blurry, mottled yellow-green ground, but its broader patches give the vertical grass some separation. F03 replaces that quiet area with tightly packed yellow-green curls or flecks that read as a flat, fibrous carpet. The fine pattern is easy to see beneath the player, around the deadwood, and across the lower-right foreground. More visible texture is not a convincing increase in vegetation volume or natural turf here. It also competes with the tall, thin grass blades.

**No meaningful night preference between F02 and F04.** F04's finer pattern can still be detected at the bottom and around the player's feet, but most of the scene's ground is too dark for it to meaningfully change the overall read. The surface change does not resolve the night visibility problem.

There is **enough exposed foreground ground in F01 and F03** to judge this local surface treatment. The UI blocks sizeable areas, but the lower left, lower right, space around the player, and grass-to-path transitions remain visible. This is sufficient for a local turf comparison, not for terrain performance across a biome, long-distance tiling, slope behavior, or road traversal. F02/F04 provide insufficient lit surface detail for a strong independent night turf verdict.

## Eight criteria

1. **Silhouette and readability at small size.** In all four frames the blue-backed trainer, pitched-roof houses, fence, and dead tree remain identifiable when mentally reduced to contact-sheet scale. The trainer's back and pack read clearly, but the visible character design is a fairly generic backpacked adventurer; this rear view supplies little of the expressive, distinctive character appeal visible in Palworld 01 and 05. No unobscured creature subject is available to judge creature appeal. The purple forms cut by the side edges cannot establish a creature silhouette or relative scale. F01/F03 put a large, spiky black deadwood silhouette across the foreground and in front of the NPC approach. It competes strongly with the player and the village focal point. In F02/F04 the central NPC nearly merges with the dark well and vegetation. F03's texture collapses to a more agitated foreground field at small size without making plants more legible.

2. **Colour and value structure.** F01/F03 have a coherent rustic palette of warm roofs, pale stone, green plants, and blue clothing. However, the exposed turf is heavily yellow-olive and nearly as assertive as the grass, rather than supporting the figures with a calmer ground mass. F03 makes that yellow-green signal busier across the foreground. F01 is softer but still patchy and muddy. The key art has green meadows with clearer light, shadow, earth, and flower groupings; Palworld 02/04 also use quieter ground expanses that allow character colours to lead. F02/F04 clearly shift to blue night, but much of the ground and NPC range compresses into near-black while the trainer stays strikingly bright. No visible friendly oxblood spread is apparent in these frames; danger-colour reservation cannot be tested without a danger subject.

3. **Intentionality.** F01–F04 have an authored village structure: houses, a central roofed well, fencing, paths, and barrels convey occupation. The foreground vegetation is less authored. Similar long, sparse, upright blade shapes occur throughout the clearing, with no persuasive dense basal cover or graduated trampled edges near the player, well, and fences. F03's very even small-scale surface activity makes the space read more uniformly carpeted; it does not create a stronger patch/clearing hierarchy. The many isolated blades still appear planted through a separate ground layer. The references integrate dense grass patches, short edge growth, and exposed earth into readable land-use patterns.

4. **Lighting.** Day and night are unmistakable. F01/F03 have useful building and vegetation shadows, and the trainer appears placed on the ground. The foreground still reads predominantly as a flat coloured surface under detached tall blades; the F03 fine pattern does not add readable relief. F02/F04 have attractive pale window rectangles but little visible warm light extending into the village. The well's approach and NPC are dark while the trainer is conspicuously lit, weakening the feeling that they occupy one shared light environment. The key-art night panel preserves landscape layers and warm settlement beacons more effectively.

5. **Horizon and depth.** F01/F03 have readable near houses, midground well, and distant trees, but the huge right-hand building and cropped left house close most of the view. This is a local courtyard composition with only a narrow suggestion of the world beyond, not evidence of a broken horizon. F02/F04 lose much of the midground separation, particularly behind the well. No clear terrain chunk seam or LOD boundary is visible. These frames cannot demonstrate the distant landmark and landscape layering achieved by the key art or Palworld 04.

6. **Interface.** Across all four frames, the main-story card and gather prompt are readable, and the margins appear safe. The long action bar plus five empty slots cover a large share of the right/lower scene, including terrain relevant to this review. Tiny key indicators in the empty slots communicate little at a glance. The lower-left health and food panel has relatively weak text contrast over foliage. The location title is readable but lies across the sky/building transition. This is a direct assessment of these frames' UI; the Palworld references are not being used as a UI design bar.

7. **Artefacts.** F03 exposes a repeated-looking, fine tangled surface pattern whose scale and planar presentation read as texture rather than a bed of living grass. F01 instead exposes soft blotchy ground. Neither resolves the sharp material transition between ground and isolated blades. In F02/F04, thin bright outlines across the dark tiled roofs and sharply fragmented dark cloud shapes are conspicuous. They may be material/lighting choices, but the stills show an uneven visual finish. The deadwood's very angular branches are also visibly harsher than the organic village foliage in every frame. No definite z-fighting or intersecting geometry is established. Temporal aliasing or popping cannot be diagnosed from these stills.

8. **Scale agreement.** Using the trainer as the stated 1.80 m ruler, house doors/windows, fence rails, barrels, and the central NPC are broadly plausible in relation to the player. Foreground grass reaches from roughly shin to thigh height depending on depth; its repeated tall ribbon forms make an occupied village clearing look unusually coarse and untrimmed. The deadwood is a substantial person-height obstacle rather than minor ground litter, which explains its strong compositional dominance. No sufficiently clear creature is present to validate player/creature or creature/creature scale. Do not infer a scale pass for creatures from these images.

## Three largest gaps from the references

1. **Ground and foliage do not form a convincing continuous plant layer (F01/F03, partly visible F02/F04).** Palworld 02/03/04 use grass masses, irregular edge growth, and calmer exposed ground so plants feel rooted in a varied surface. These frames separate a flat yellow-green carpet from isolated long blades. F01's carpet is blurry; F03's is finely busy. Both need more coherent patch structure and better transitions, rather than blanket texture activity. Scene/material/scatter adjustments can address much of this; whether suitable short groundcover art exists cannot be determined from images.

2. **Night light fails to support the shared village space (F02/F04).** The key-art night scene retains readable layers and inviting warm settlement light. Here the central NPC, deadwood, and walking surface sink into black, lit windows remain disconnected bright rectangles, and the trainer looks separately illuminated. This is primarily a scene lighting/value-composition gap.

3. **The focal content does not carry the references' distinctive character appeal (all frames).** Palworld's figures and creatures remain expressive visual anchors, while these frames center a generic rear-view trainer and a dominating angular deadwood object; the NPC is small by day and very hard to read by night. The key art also directs attention through clear paths and settlement focal points instead of a foreground obstruction. Reframing or moving the obstruction and improving NPC light are scene fixes. The visible cast would need a closer, unobstructed character/creature survey before declaring that existing art can meet the expressive character bar; this sample cannot demonstrate it, and no claim about unseen assets is warranted.

## Separate bar decisions

**A — Do these frames belong to the key-art world? Yes, at the palette/mood/settlement-language level.** Warm timber-and-stone homes, pitched roofs, a well, simple fences, greenery and a blue night establish a plausible village in that Meadows world. This is a qualified art-direction match, not a claim of equal rendering or composition. The yellow-olive turf, coarse scatter, intrusive deadwood and underlit night approach weaken the welcoming quality. They are principally scene/material/lighting problems. The stills do not establish whether the cast matches the board's companion appeal.

**B — Beside Palworld, do these appear to be trying to be the same kind of game? Yes, for this settlement view.** The third-person adventurer, explorable stylized natural environment, gathering interaction and lived-in rustic settlement fit the same broad game family. The frame does not show a readable creature encounter, so it cannot independently establish the creature-combat portion of that identity. This yes is deliberately separate from commercial-quality acceptance.

**Commercial visual quality beside the supplied Palworld screenshots: No, for both pairs.** The flat turf/isolated-blade integration, noisy material detail, night visibility, and weak focal character presentation remain below that bar. F03/F04 do not close those gaps. F01/F02 are the narrower local preference because the daylight ground is calmer, not because that pair is finished.

## Static evidence limits

This verdict addresses only the visible courtyard and these four stills. It cannot assess frame rate, traversal feel, animation, combat spectacle, texture shimmer, temporal popping, dynamic weather, larger-region variation, or performance. No renderer or hardware behavior is inferred. No unseen creature, asset inventory, or implementation cause is assumed. The day images differ slightly in displayed clock time; any exact lighting identity is therefore not proven, although the conspicuous foreground surface difference is directly visible.
