# Blind visual judgment: TURF-SOUTHBRIDGE01

Judged from the actual neutral images F01.png, F02.png, F03.png and F04.png in `.artifacts/broad-visual-0910/blind-turf-southbridge01/`, against `docs/reference/tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png` and all five `palworld-0*.jpg` references. No implementation files, change descriptions, prior verdicts or capture reports were inspected. The visual-judge skill supplied the rubric. No scores are assigned.

## Preference

**Slight preference for F03/F04 over F01/F02, confined to ground/foliage integration.** F03 has a finer, more continuous grassy surface beneath the upright blades on the left foreground slope and beside the dirt clearing. This softens the impression of isolated spikes planted into a smooth dark substrate in F01. It is a small material-level improvement, not a change in scene quality or composition. In F02 versus F04, there is **no meaningful preference at ordinary viewing size**: the dark ground hides most of the distinction.

The pair comparison does not establish that one version changed grass density. Some blade/flower positions and distant small subjects differ, which could be animation or capture timing. Camera framing, trainer position, the gate, tree trunks, hill contours and HUD placement appear effectively matched. F01/F03 show 08:00 and F02/F04 show 23:00. This is one viewpoint at two times, not evidence across a route.

## Eight criteria

### 1. Silhouette and readability at small size

The trainer is recognizable in all four frames; the blue sleeves and pale collar provide a useful identity break, especially at night. However, the small rear-facing view cannot demonstrate character facial appeal or expressive design. There is no clearly presented companion creature comparable to the prominent, distinctive creatures in Palworld 01, 03, 04 and 05. Tiny distant forms on the right cannot establish creature art quality or relative roles. The defining creature partnership is therefore absent from this composition.

At reduced viewing size, F01/F03 resolve into sky, green hills, trainer, dark ground and a large dark right-hand foliage blob. That bush loses its internal branch/leaf structure and partly hides the gate approach. In F02/F04 the near ground, grass base and right bush merge into black-green areas; only thin highlighted blades interrupt them. F03's finer turf does not materially change these large silhouettes.

### 2. Colour and value structure

F01/F03 have a usable distinction between the blue sky, sunlit yellow-green hill and shaded foreground, but the brightest greens belong to terrain and foliage rather than a creature or destination. The foreground dirt is strongly ochre-brown and mottled, with a dirty almost scorched appearance in shade. The midground hill combines yellow-green patches with thin bright grass, producing competing surface marks rather than a coherent meadow mass. F03 improves fine continuity locally without correcting that larger value pattern.

F02/F04 clearly adopt a blue night palette, but most terrain values collapse toward black. The trainer looks considerably brighter than its immediate footing. The moon is a large pale flat disk while the night clouds form dark streaks; together they feel less integrated than the key art's subdued night sky and layered terrain. The gate's red banners are the only conspicuous oxblood-like accents in F01/F03; their semantic appropriateness cannot be settled from these pictures alone.

### 3. Intentionality

The gate, banners, barrels/hay and figures create an identifiable authored location in all frames. Hills and tree groupings give more structure than a completely empty field. However, F01/F03's foreground clearing is a broad amorphous expanse of mottled dirt scattered with small pale stones, without a convincing worn walking line or graduated verge. Upright grass often reads as repeated narrow spikes. The large right bush forms a single nearly uniform mass.

F03's short surface detail helps connect the grass bases but does not supply the clustered low plants, varied tuft sizes, exposed soil shapes and settled path edges seen in Palworld 02/05. F02/F04 hide much of the location dressing, so nighttime composition lacks a visible welcoming or threatening focal light.

### 4. Lighting

Day and night are unmistakably different. F01/F03 communicate slope form through broad light and shade and give the gate a readable material break. Yet the near bush is unusually flat and internally dark beside the lit hill, making it look pasted into the foreground. The sunlit grass and very dark substrate split apart most strongly on the left slope.

F02/F04 over-darken the ground rather than preserving a readable sequence of moonlit planes. The bright trainer stands out, but the footing and route ahead remain weak. The gate would benefit from a deliberate local light or a clearer moonlit value grouping. These are observations of rendered appearance, not claims about the lighting implementation.

### 5. Horizon and depth

F01/F03 contain a legible near slope, gate valley and farther tree ridge, but the horizon quickly becomes a low, thin strip with no substantial distant landform or landmark. The path climbing toward the bare central tree ends near the skyline without a strong destination beyond it. Palworld 04 and the key art use layered cliffs, mountains or architecture to continue depth and give the journey a target.

F02/F04 retain the skyline silhouette but lose separation within the land below it. No definite LOD or chunk seam can be established from these still images. The pale diagonal path-like strip on the central hill has conspicuously straight edges in every frame; it reads as an abrupt terrain transition, though these pictures alone do not prove a technical seam.

### 6. Interface

The quest text is readable and the minimap occupies a consistent safe area. However, the large quest panel covers a substantial portion of the right skyline, and the two broad lower-right control/slot panels obscure foreground foliage and part of the approach. Five empty dark creature slots add substantial visual weight without showing the game's creature identity. The small centered time text has weak contrast in F01/F03. At night, the bright HUD dominates more strongly because the terrain beneath it is so dark. This is an internal hierarchy and legibility judgment; Palworld's UI is not the comparison standard.

### 7. Artefacts

F01/F03 show coarse, blurry mottling across the large foreground dirt surface. Thin grass has sharp, repetitive edges that contrast with that blurred substrate. F03 adds fine surface texture at the grass boundary, but the foreground dirt still reads at a different visual scale from the upright foliage. All frames have large, flat-looking overlapping leaves in the right bush and a visibly angular, cropped tree trunk at the left edge.

There is no unambiguous z-fighting or floating object defect visible. Grass motion, popping, temporal shimmer and shadow stability cannot be evaluated from stills. The altered distant small shapes between matched times should not be counted as an asset improvement or regression without a controlled capture.

### 8. Scale agreement

Using the trainer as the 1.80 m ruler, the nearby grass ranges plausibly from ankle to knee height, with taller meadow growth farther along the left slope. The right foreground bush approaches or exceeds trainer height, so it functions as a substantial obstruction rather than minor ground cover. Trees and the gate do not show an obvious gross scale contradiction, allowing for perspective. The small foreground stones are plausible, but their isolated even visual prominence contributes to the scatter impression.

There is no adequately visible near creature, and distant figures occupy different depths. These frames cannot support a trainer-to-creature height judgment or an assessment that companion and practice creatures have appropriately different sizes. No species-size inference is made.

## Three largest gaps from the references

1. **Ground and foliage do not form one convincing surface — F01–F04, clearest in F01/F03.** Palworld 02/05 transition worn soil through low grass into denser tufts, with compatible texture scale and grounding. Here the broad ochre dirt, dark grass substrate, upright spikes and flat foreground bush remain visibly separate components. F03 helps the substrate locally but does not close this gap. Address the soil scale/value, path/verge shapes, tuft clustering and bush shading/material cohesion together.
2. **The creature-led visual identity is missing from the view — all four frames.** Palworld 01/03/04/05 stage distinctive creatures where their shape, colour, expression or relationship to the player can be read immediately. These frames offer a small rear-view trainer and no legible companion. Scene placement and capture composition can fix visibility; these images provide insufficient evidence to say whether the installed creature art itself can meet the reference bar. The character also needs a closer art review before its quality can be accepted.
3. **Depth and night value staging fall short — F01/F03 for the thin horizon, F02/F04 most severely for dark land planes.** The key art and Palworld 04 organize near ground, middle-distance landmarks and far landforms into a journey. These views stop at a low tree ridge, and night turns much of that space into one dark mass. Scene composition, distant silhouettes, controlled haze and readable night lighting are actionable fixes; no performance assumption is part of this judgment.

## Separate bar answers

**A. Do these frames read as belonging to the world in the Meadows key art? Yes, broadly.** Rolling meadow hills, groves, a rustic stone-and-timber gate, red danger-like banners and distinct blue night lighting share the board's setting and visual vocabulary. This is broad art-direction membership, not fidelity or polish acceptance. The harsh turf/dirt breakup, flat bush, weak distant landmark and crushed night foreground weaken the intended cozy, inviting mood. Those deficits are largely addressable through scene/material/lighting work. This verdict does not compare per-leaf fidelity to painted art.

**B. Beside the five Palworld screenshots, would someone say these are trying to be the same kind of game? Yes.** The third-person adventurer, explorable grassy terrain, rustic world props and creature-control interface communicate the same broad open-world creature-adventure category. Creature absence in the actual scene weakens that message substantially. This genre answer must not be interpreted as achieving Palworld quality.

**Commercial-quality comparison: No; neither pair reaches the supplied Palworld shipping-quality bar.** F03/F04 is only a slight local preference. The material/foliage discontinuity, nighttime readability, weak distant staging and missing readable creature presentation remain substantial. The supplied images do not prove a need to purchase or generate replacement creature art, because creatures are not shown sufficiently to assess it. They do show that the foreground bush and grass treatment need stronger visual integration; existing assets might or might not support that without replacement, which an image-only review cannot determine.

## Limits

This is a static, single-location visual comparison at matched displayed day/night times. It establishes neither motion quality, frame rate, traversal feel, creature animation quality, interaction quality nor whole-biome consistency. Slight grass/flower and distant-subject variations are visible, so this is not a perfectly frozen scene-state comparison. No source knowledge or intended change was used to assign preference.
