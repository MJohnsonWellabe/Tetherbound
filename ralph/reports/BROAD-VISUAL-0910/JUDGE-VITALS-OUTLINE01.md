# Blind visual verdict — F01–F04

Evidence: only the four assigned gameplay frames, the Meadows key art, and the five Palworld reference frames. F01/F03 are day; F02/F04 are night. No source, manifests, change descriptions, or prior verdicts were used to identify a winner. Findings concern these views, not unseen locations or motion.

**Preference: tie.** F01 versus F03 and F02 versus F04 show no meaningful improvement in scene readability or interface legibility at the supplied viewing size. F04's clock differs by one minute; that does not establish a visual advantage. Any subtle treatment difference is too small to change the assessment.

The trainer is recognizable as an adventurer, but the rear-facing, neutral pose and small presentation do not establish a distinctive character comparable to the expressive cast in the Palworld references. No creature is presented clearly enough to judge its design or appeal. The small glowing form near the rock/grass boundary cannot carry the creature fantasy in any frame. These are limitations of the supplied presentation; they do not establish asset provenance or the quality of unseen creature art.

## Eight criteria

1. **Silhouette and readability at small size.** In F01/F03 the dark trousers and brown hair remain separate from the bright ground, and the backpack identifies the trainer. In F02/F04 the torso remains readable, but hair and legs merge more with the ground. At roughly thumbnail scale the glowing distant form is a white fleck rather than an identifiable creature. The cliff dominates every frame; grass becomes a finely speckled wedge with few distinct plant silhouettes.

2. **Colour and value structure.** F01/F03 have clear light ground versus dark rock separation, but the broad mustard-yellow foreground and mottled grey wall consume most of the palette. Little of the key art's layered green meadow, warm settlement accents, or inviting blue distance is present. F02/F04 communicate night through navy rock and blue sky, while the grass becomes bright cyan wirework over almost black ground. The interface remains much brighter than most environmental subjects. No visible evidence of oxblood leaking onto friendly elements.

3. **Intentionality.** F01–F04 present a very broad, largely empty ground surface ending abruptly at a uniformly textured wall. The grass occupies a strongly bounded patch rather than building a varied transition through stones, short plants, taller clumps, and worn edges. There is no clear authored destination or small environmental story in the main view. The references organize paths around terrain, foliage groups, creatures, buildings, or landmarks.

4. **Lighting.** Day and night are immediately distinguishable. The trainer's shadow grounds them in F01/F03, but the wall's mottling supplies more apparent detail than its large-scale lighting does. In F02/F04 the bright trainer torso, luminous grass edges, and isolated white glow compete without a convincing focal hierarchy. Night loses most terrain form and lacks the key art's inviting warm light destination.

5. **Horizon and depth.** F01–F04 expose only a narrow sky opening between near slopes. The enormous left wall suppresses the midground and nearly all long-range exploration cues. The grass slope gives some recession, but there is no legible distant landmark or layered landscape comparable to either reference. No definite LOD or chunk seam can be established from these stills.

6. **Interface.** F01/F03: the lower-left FOOD lettering is muted brown over a translucent brown panel on yellow ground; the health values are subdued grey, and the small heart contributes little at a glance. F02/F04 retain the same weak hierarchy against darker terrain. All frames give the main-story panel much stronger contrast and prominence than the vitals. The five lower-right slots are large empty boxes with tiny white key labels; they do not communicate contents or state. The minimap is overwhelmingly flat green, offering little usable geography. Panels stay inside the visible frame, but the left vitals sit close to the edge. These are intrinsic interface observations, not a comparison to Palworld's UI design.

7. **Artefacts.** F01–F04 show conspicuous repeating granular ground texture and oversized polygonal mottling across the wall. The wall/ground contact contains sharp triangular notches that look unfinished. Grass is particularly brittle and noisy in F02/F04. The isolated saturated white spot at its boundary has an unclear silhouette and reads like an unexplained visual effect. No definite z-fighting, animation defect, or popping can be diagnosed from stills.

8. **Scale agreement.** Against the 1.80 m trainer, the near cliff and grass are broadly plausible in size. The uninterrupted rock texture makes the cliff feel more like a single enlarged surface than articulated geology. No sufficiently clear, comparable creature is available for a defensible creature-to-trainer measurement; no relative creature-scale pass is established.

## Three largest reference gaps

1. **Creature and character presence — F01–F04.** Palworld's creatures are legible, expressive foreground or midground subjects, with clear relationships to the trainer. Here a solitary rear-facing trainer and an indistinct distant glow leave the central promise unrepresented. Improve scene presentation with a readable companion or encounter and a view that shows its silhouette; whether creature art itself needs replacement cannot be decided from these frames.

2. **Composed, inhabited terrain — F01–F04.** The references use paths, plant clusters, rocks, buildings, and landmarks to create purposeful space. Here bare granular ground and one oversized wall occupy most of the frame. Terrain articulation, path-edge dressing, clustering, and an authored destination are scene work. The visibly repetitive ground and cliff surfaces also need material/art refinement beyond adding more scatter.

3. **Natural colour and layered light — F01/F03 and F02/F04.** The key art balances greens, earth, blue distance, and warm highlights; Palworld preserves creature separation in a rich landscape. These day frames concentrate on yellow and grey, while night turns vegetation into cyan noise and removes depth. Palette, lighting, foliage response, and composition can address much of this in the scene. Distinctive character expression remains an art/presentation question, not something colour grading alone resolves.

**A. Belongs to the Meadows key-art world: no.** A natural outdoor setting and day/night cycle are present, but the supplied views lack its rolling meadow composition, welcoming vegetation, landmark language, and inviting night destinations. Most environmental gaps are addressable through scene composition, dressing, material treatment, and lighting. The frames cannot establish which required assets are absent from the build.

**B. Beside Palworld, reads as the same kind of game: no.** These particular frames read as a sparsely staged third-person traversal scene. They do not visibly establish a creature adventure through clear creatures, encounter activity, inhabited space, or appealing cast presentation. Scene staging can establish that identity; a creature-art verdict requires a legible creature view.

**Commercial readiness: no.** Repetitive surfaces, abrupt dressing boundaries, weak vitals contrast, an uninformative minimap, and the lack of a compelling environmental or creature focal point remain visible in both pairs. This is a visual-readiness verdict for the supplied frames only.
