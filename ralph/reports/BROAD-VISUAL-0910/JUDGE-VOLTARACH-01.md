# Blind creature review: F01 / F02

Evidence: only `neutral-voltarach-01/F01.png`, `F02.png`, the Meadows key art, and all five `palworld-0*.jpg` references. No source, change history, or previous reports consulted. No numeric scores.

The creatures read immediately as stylized armored spiders, but neither frame reaches the reference creatures' shipping art bar. Bright cyan eyes provide a useful focal point; busy shell markings and repeated spikes compete with it. F01 is the stronger image because violet versus magenta helps separate the overlapping bodies. F02 makes the grouping appreciably harder to parse.

## Full eight-part rubric

1. **Silhouette and small-size readability.** In both frames, the large foreground body and its long pointed legs establish a spider identity. At a mental thumbnail reduction, the central body becomes a dark knob inside a picket of similarly sharp limbs. The smaller rear-left creature is identifiable through its eye cluster, but its legs merge with those of the foreground creature. F01's different body hues help assign limbs to individuals; F02 loses this cue. Palworld 03 gives Grintale a broad, continuous body contour, while 04 distinguishes several creatures through substantially different mass arrangements. These frames have a readable species silhouette but weaker body hierarchy and multiple-creature readability.

2. **Colour and value structure.** Both frames have real value contrast: dark undersides, saturated shells, and very bright cyan eye lenses. That contrast is spent on many small shell patches and edges, however, rather than a few stable anatomical blocks. The eyes register as luminous beads; dark sockets separate them, but there are no clearly readable pupils or directional gaze. F01's violet foreground shell is closer to the restrained purple accent on the Meadows palette than F02's pervasive hot magenta. Neither gives the face a calm, clearly bounded colour region comparable to Mammorest's tan face or Grintale's cream lower face. The Pokémon principle of clear facial colour blocks is useful here as a design principle, without copying a character. Allegiance is unknown; the images cannot establish misuse of a faction's danger colour.

3. **Intentionality and coherent identity.** The repeated armored plates, hooked legs, and cyan eyes form a coherent species vocabulary in both frames. The problem is excess repetition: almost every visible surface receives mottled contrast and almost every contour receives a point. The result leans toward an armored monster asset more than the bespoke, expressive companion identity visible in Palworld 01, 03, and 05. This is a visible style judgment, not a claim about asset provenance. F02 strengthens colour uniformity across individuals but does not add species character. Simplifying the shell treatment and choosing a few signature shapes would strengthen authorship.

4. **Lighting.** Broad shading gives the shells volume, and soft ground shadows establish a location beneath the bodies in both frames. Some leg tips appear suspended above those shadows; a still cannot determine whether this is a pose or a contact defect. Glossy eye highlights remain legible, but the shell's patch contrast competes with form lighting. These neutral views cannot establish habitat lighting, time of day, or integration with the Meadows daylight/night mood. No renderer or performance conclusion is supported.

5. **Horizon and depth.** Foreground occlusion and the smaller rear-left body indicate depth, but the flat green plane and blue backdrop contain no usable environmental depth evidence. F01's hue separation preserves more local layering than F02. Fog, landmarks, traversal composition, world camera quality, and LOD transitions are unassessable here; the neutral background is not treated as a missing habitat defect.

6. **Interface.** No interface is present. Safe areas, combat information hierarchy, and gameplay legibility cannot be judged. The eye clusters provide an intrinsic focal point, not evidence about the game's interface.

7. **Artefacts.** Both frames show conspicuously angular transitions on some shell and leg edges and irregular high-contrast surface patches. These contribute to a jagged, mottled finish compared with the cleaner broad surfaces in Palworld 03 and 05. They may be intentional; the images do not establish broken textures. Dense limb crossings obscure attachment points, but do not prove intersecting geometry. Small pale glints beside the bodies are visible without a clear static explanation. No reliable claim about z-fighting, animation, popping, or motion aliasing can be made.

8. **Scale agreement.** The foreground creature occupies substantially more frame area than the rear-left creature in both images. Different depth and overlap prevent a reliable physical size comparison. There is no trainer or other known ruler, so metre scale, role-dependent size, and habitat agreement remain unestablished. F01 improves visual separation, not proof of physical scale.

## F01 / F02 preference and regressions

**Prefer F01.** The violet dominant body and magenta rear-left body remain distinguishable despite the overlap, and the dominant violet hue is less strident beside the key-art palette. F02's magenta dominant body is vivid, but merges with the smaller creature's shell and legs. This is a regression in grouping readability and restraint. Cyan eyes retain strong contrast in both; F02 does not visibly improve gaze, expression, body contour, or surface finish. Matching colours alone is insufficient evidence of improved stylistic coherence.

## Ranked top three gaps

1. **Face hierarchy and expression — F01 and F02.** Palworld 01 and 05 establish faces through clear regions, eye direction, and recognizable mouth/beak shapes. Here the cyan lenses attract attention but read more as lights than an expressive gaze, while adjacent shell mottling interrupts the facial outline. Create a quieter facial colour block, a more legible eye/gaze structure, and a deliberate expression-bearing shape. This requires creature material/texture and potentially facial geometry work; scene lighting alone cannot supply it.

2. **Dominant mass and overlapping silhouettes — both, worse in F02.** Palworld 03 maintains an unmistakable large body mass; 04 separates multiple creatures through contours and colour blocks. Here similarly pointed legs and carapace peaks compete with the central mass, and overlap makes limb ownership unclear. Spacing and facing adjustments can improve a scene immediately. A more robust body-to-leg hierarchy and fewer competing shell points require creature art changes. Preserve F01's useful individual separation while judging those changes.

3. **Surface restraint and palette integration — both, worse in F02.** The Meadows board uses saturated accents within a natural palette, and the Palworld creatures balance bright colour with broad, readable surfaces. Here mottled highlights and dark fragments cover almost the entire shell; F02 adds a large field of hot magenta. Reduce patch frequency, organize colour around anatomy, and keep a few intentional accents. Material/texture changes are needed; exposure or background changes could moderate presentation but would not resolve the underlying pattern hierarchy.
