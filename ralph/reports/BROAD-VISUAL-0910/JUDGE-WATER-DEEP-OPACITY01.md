# Blind visual review: F01/F02 versus F03/F04

Reviewed only the visual-judge skill, the Meadows key-art board, all five supplied Palworld reference JPGs, and the four individual supplied frames. No source, manifests, history, implementation information, or other reports informed this review. Frame labels indicate the supplied images only; no provenance is assumed. No scores are assigned.

## Preference and evidence sufficiency

**Prefer F03/F04 over F01/F02, narrowly and for a visible surface defect.**

- **Day: prefer F03 over F01.** F01 contains a large, straight-edged, translucent-looking shape across the water immediately beyond the grassy rise, with an obvious corner to the right of the trainer. F03 has a more continuous turquoise field there. Removing that conspicuous angular interruption makes F03 read more plausibly as water. F03 still does not show convincing surface detail or a clearly articulated near shoreline.
- **Night: prefer F04 over F02.** The broad lighter-blue angular wedge across F02 is more conspicuous against the dark water than its daytime counterpart. F04 has a continuous blue surface through that area, making this the stronger of the two preferences. Its smooth blue gradient still reads as an under-described surface.
- **Otherwise a practical tie.** Trainer presentation, foreground planting, horizon composition, lighting hierarchy, and interface have no persuasive qualitative difference in these images. Minor vegetation and daylight differences do not establish a separate improvement.

Evidence is sufficient for those local preferences and for identifying the shared visual deficiencies below. It is insufficient to establish water quality across other viewpoints, depths, shorelines, weather, or movement; broad biome quality; or creature/combat quality. Four images of essentially one coastal composition cannot establish a whole-game visual pass.

**Character and creature assessment first:** the trainer in F01–F04 is recognizably human, with readable brown hair, a blue tunic, backpack, and boots. The small side/back presentation does not demonstrate the expressive character appeal evident in the Palworld foreground characters. The trainer's textured, segmented clothing sits against much more uniformly angular grass. No creature is visible in any frame, so creature design, appeal, style agreement, combat partnership, and relative creature scale remain unjudged. Absence is an evidence gap, not evidence that creature assets do not exist.

## Eight-criterion rubric

1. **Silhouette and readability at small size.** At a reduced overall reading, the trainer remains identifiable in F01/F03 because the brown head and blue torso interrupt the pale grass. In F02/F04 the light sleeve and backpack are especially prominent, but the dark lower legs merge into the ground. The field of similarly thin grass blades becomes a noisy pale band rather than distinct plant masses in all four frames. The small distant land sliver at upper right is mostly hidden by the objective box and cannot serve as a strong landmark. No tree/rock/bush grouping or creature silhouette is sufficiently represented for broader readability judgments. Pair verdict: tie outside the water interruption.

2. **Colour and value structure.** F01/F03 have recognizable green ground, tan/brown clothing accents, and turquoise water, but the pale sky and water occupy most of the view with little internal value structure. The dense bright grass tips compete with the trainer. F02/F04 establish a dark blue night palette, yet the near-white sleeve/backpack and strong blue water contrast with the nearly black soil as if their illumination belongs to different strengths of light. All four plausibly depict the same location. No visible oxblood element establishes either correct danger reservation or a friendly-colour leak. F03/F04 have the cleaner water colour field; the shared broad palette limitations remain.

3. **Intentionality.** F01–F04 have almost continuous coverage by narrow, similarly tall grass blades, punctuated by a few isolated taller sprigs. Coverage is dense, but massing has little hierarchy: there are few legible low patches, clumps, bare transitions, or a composed route to the water. The references use density together with clearings, paths, rocks, trees, and changes of plant height. Here a grassy dome, broad empty water, and broad sky dominate. Recompose this view with distinct vegetation masses and a readable shore transition; merely increasing grass count would not address it. Pair verdict: tie.

4. **Lighting.** Day versus night is immediately readable without relying on the clock. The trainer and grass have some light/dark separation in F01/F03, but the water's nearly uninterrupted pale fill gives it little form or response to the surrounding sky. In F02/F04, the trainer's pale clothing remains conspicuously bright against dark ground without a visible local light or moon to explain that emphasis. The grass mound remains legible, though contact around the boots is hard to distinguish from the many blade shadows. No frame proves floating objects. Pair verdict: tie; removing the angular water feature does not by itself improve lighting coherence.

5. **Horizon and depth.** F01–F04 divide into foreground grass, an almost empty water band, and a flat horizon. The distant shore at right is the only substantial intermediate depth cue and is largely covered by the objective box. Distance elsewhere dissolves into a uniform horizontal band rather than the layered ridges, groves, and landmarks of the key art and Palworld plateau. F01/F02 add an angular boundary within the water that distracts from natural depth; F03/F04 avoid it. F03/F04 therefore win locally, but the horizon still has little spatial information. The images cannot establish whether any line is a LOD or chunk boundary.

6. **Interface.** Judged on its own, not against Palworld's UI: the large objective text is readable and contained within the screen, but its panel obscures almost the only distant land feature in F01–F04. Two broad lower-right panels consume substantial foreground area. The empty-looking slot boxes with tiny white number tiles carry little visible content for their footprint. The lower-left health/food panels allow busy grass to show through and have weaker contrast than the objective and action bar. The top-centre clock is comparatively small and muted. Safe margins appear intact at this capture size; other aspect ratios are not demonstrated. Pair verdict: tie.

7. **Artefacts.** F01/F02 show the large angular water shape described above; its long straight boundary and abrupt corner read as a rendering/geometry discontinuity rather than an organic shore or water variation. Its cause is unknown. F03/F04 do not display that conspicuous shape, which is the basis of the preference. All four show strongly planar grass blades and wispy, flattened cloud patterns; F02/F04 particularly turn the clouds into dark cutout-like streaks. These are visible presentation concerns, not proof of specific shader faults. The small far-right shore has a rough, jagged edge, but its distance and UI obstruction prevent reliable diagnosis. No still establishes z-fighting, popping, or motion aliasing.

8. **Scale agreement.** Using the stated 1.80 m trainer as the ruler, grass close to the trainer generally reaches the shin/knee region, with a few taller sprigs. That is plausible for a tall-grass patch, though such widespread similar-height coverage contributes to repetition. Backpack, boots, and limbs are broadly coherent at this view size. Perspective makes the nearest foreground blades unsuitable for a direct height comparison with the trainer. There is no visible creature, usable tree, building, or nearby large rock for a meaningful cross-object metre check. Companion-versus-practice-target scale cannot be assessed. Pair verdict: tie; insufficient evidence for broader scale acceptance.

## Three largest gaps from the references, ranked

1. **Missing visible creature-led appeal and action evidence — F01–F04.** Palworld 01 and 03 make expressive creatures and a clearly readable encounter the focus; Palworld 04 and 05 also supply distinctive creatures alongside people. These four frames show only a small trainer viewed from the side/back. That leaves the project's defining character/creature proposition unproven. A different scene and camera can supply the missing evidence; these images cannot determine what creature art exists or whether new art is needed.

2. **World composition and depth — F01–F04.** The key art connects foreground flowers/rocks, a shaped shore, oak groves, settlement elements, and distant ridges. Palworld 02 and 04 use paths, rock faces, vegetation masses, and landmarks to make open areas feel located and explorable. Here most of the image is empty sky/water, with a nearly uniform grass foreground and a tiny obscured land sliver. This is principally addressable through scene composition, shore dressing, foliage clustering, landmark placement, and framing.

3. **Surface and light coherence — F01–F04, with the additional angular water defect in F01/F02.** Palworld's ground cover varies in height, value, and density while supporting readable subjects; the key art's water has clear palette/depth relationships with its banks. These frames have pale repeated grass spikes and broad, minimally articulated water. F02/F04 add a bright trainer against very dark soil without a visible motivating light. F03/F04 remove one conspicuous discontinuity, but need stronger surface cues and light integration to approach those references. Material/foliage artwork may need refinement as well as lighting and placement changes; stills cannot establish which suitable assets are already available.

## Separate bar answers

**A. Do these frames read as belonging to the Meadows key-art world? No, for the submitted views in both pairs.** Green grass, blue water, and a tunic/backpack trainer provide partial palette and subject agreement. The images do not carry the board's defining composed shorelines, varied meadow growth, oak/rock/landmark language, layered landscape, or inviting inhabited atmosphere. F03/F04 improve continuity without closing that gap. Density distribution, shoreline composition, lighting, and camera framing are scene-level opportunities. More persuasive foliage and water surface artwork may be necessary, but no claim about unavailable build assets is possible from these images.

**B. Beside the Palworld references, would someone say these are trying to be the same kind of game? Yes, at the broad genre/intention level, for both pairs.** A stylized human adventurer with a backpack in a traversable natural 3D landscape is consistent with that outdoor third-person exploration direction. This is a narrow yes: there is no creature or combat evidence to verify the more specific creature-partner promise.

**Commercial visual quality is a separate question: neither pair demonstrates the supplied Palworld shipping-quality bar.** The cleaner water in F03/F04 earns a local preference, not commercial-quality acceptance. The composition, repetitive foliage, surface/light integration, and absence of evaluable creature presentation leave substantial gaps.

## Still-image limits

It does not diagnose the cause of the angular water feature. It cannot infer asset provenance, the availability of unseen art, or conditions outside this one view. Broader acceptance needs additional framed evidence and runtime inspection; the local F03/F04 preference does not depend on those unobserved claims.
