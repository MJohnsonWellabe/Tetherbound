# Neutral visual comparison: F01–F04

Reviewed the four supplied frames against `tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png` and all five `palworld-0*.jpg` references using the full eight-part visual-judge rubric. F01/F03 show day; F02/F04 show night. No code, capture history, provenance mapping, or other judge reports were inspected. No Godot run was performed. Findings apply to these views, not unseen areas or motion.

**Preference: no meaningful preference.** F01 and F03 look equivalent in the visible scene; F02 and F04 also look equivalent. Neither pair shows a perceptible advantage in composition, material readability, character visibility, or lighting. There is no visible water surface here, so these frames cannot support a water-appearance verdict.

## Full rubric

1. **Silhouette and small-size readability.** In all four frames the trainer reads as a backpacked human, but no companion or wild creature has a readable silhouette. The tiny glowing green/white object at the cliff foot does not communicate a creature identity. That absence is the first major gap against the Palworld references, where distinctive creatures are focal subjects. The trainer's blue sleeves and backpack give useful separation, but the dark legs lose distinction at reduced size, especially in F02/F04. The cliff, sand and grass remain identifiable as broad zones; finer scene content vanishes.

2. **Colour and value structure.** F01/F03 have a clear warm sand/cool stone/green grass separation, broadly compatible with a natural adventure palette. However, the huge yellow foreground and grey cliff dominate nearly the entire image; there is little secondary colour organization. F02/F04 establish blue night, but the cliff becomes one large dark mass and the pale grass reads as a field of bright needles. No conspicuous oxblood misuse is visible.

3. **Intentionality.** Across F01–F04, the path occupies an unusually large empty area with a repetitive fine-grained surface. The cliff is one continuous rounded slope covered by similarly sized rock marks, while the grass forms a dense, sharply bounded band. Add localized stone outcrops, varied vegetation clusters and a more graduated path edge, and frame a recognizable destination. Those changes would make this stretch feel like a place rather than a terrain passage.

4. **Lighting.** Day and night are immediately distinguishable. F01/F03 have a useful trainer shadow and broad directional shading on the slope. F02/F04 retain trainer visibility, but its bright sleeves stand apart from the very dark environment, and the cyan-white grass receives more visual emphasis than the route. Stronger selective illumination of the route and destination, with less uniform grass brightness, would improve night hierarchy. Still images cannot establish lighting behaviour during movement.

5. **Horizon and depth.** In all four frames the cliff blocks most of the horizon. The narrow upper-right opening gives some recession but no substantial sequence of near, middle and distant landmarks. The reference board and Palworld plateau view make travel direction and distant destinations visible. These frames do not. No definite LOD or chunk seam can be established from this view.

6. **Interface.** The story text is readable and remains within the frame. The large right-hand objective card, slot strip and bottom control strip compete with the narrow visible travel opening. F01/F03 have weak contrast for the lower-left food label; small white keycaps and empty numbered slots are hard to interpret at reduced size in every frame. The minimap is almost entirely green and supplies little visible route context. This is a local usability finding, not a UI comparison against Palworld.

7. **Artefacts.** F01–F04 show a conspicuously scalloped sand/rock boundary and a strong change from bare path to dense grass. These read as coarse terrain/material transitions rather than a natural bank. The cliff texture has uniform polygon-like mottling that does little to explain geological form. No definite z-fighting or intersecting geometry is visible. Popping and motion aliasing are not assessable.

8. **Scale agreement.** Using the trainer as the 1.80 m ruler, the cliff and hillside vegetation do not show an obvious gross relative-scale contradiction. The cliff lacks smaller intermediate objects that would help communicate its size. There is no readable creature beside the trainer, so companion-versus-target scale and creature-role scale cannot be judged. The tiny distant glowing object is insufficient evidence for a size defect.

## Three largest reference gaps, ranked

1. **Creature identity and presence — F01–F04.** Palworld's creatures have immediately identifiable shapes, colours and meaningful prominence in the scene. These frames offer no readable creature subject. Bringing existing creatures into a useful view is a scene/composition task; whether their art meets the bar requires new visible evidence. These frames do not justify prescribing a new creature mesh.

2. **Authored terrain and surface transitions — F01–F04.** Palworld's open path combines rock ledges, vegetation clusters, shadows and smaller ground accents around a clear traversal space. Here a huge repetitive sand surface meets a uniformly mottled cliff and a dense grass strip. Terrain shaping, material scale/variation, edge blending and placement are actionable scene/material changes.

3. **Destination, depth and atmosphere — F01–F04.** The key art leads the eye through foreground detail toward settlements, distinctive mountains or mysterious structures; Palworld's plateau supplies a similarly legible distant goal. Here the close cliff dominates and the remaining opening supplies little destination information. Scene framing, landmark placement and night light hierarchy can address this. If a suitable landmark asset is absent, that part requires art, but asset availability cannot be determined from these images.
