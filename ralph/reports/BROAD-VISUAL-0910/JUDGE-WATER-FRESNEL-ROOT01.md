No supplemental references were used. These are still-image judgments; animation, performance, and traversal are outside the evidence.

**Preference: F03/F04, narrowly.** F03 gives the water a more recognizable turquoise body than the nearly white slab in F01. F04 has a darker, more integrated blue surface than the conspicuously pale band in F02. This is a visible material improvement, especially at night, but it does not change the overall quality verdict. Consequently these images provide no positive evidence for the bespoke creature appeal that carries the references, and the scene feels uninhabited.

## The three largest reference gaps, ranked

1. **An inhabited creature adventure is missing from the visible composition (F01–F04).** Palworld's field, plateau, and base shots put distinct creatures and human activity into the environment. Here a lone rear-view trainer faces an empty bank and distant empty hill. Place visible creature activity and a recognizable shoreline destination in the playable view. Whether the available character and creature art is sufficient cannot be established from these frames.
2. **Terrain and vegetation read as a constructed surface with scatter, rather than an authored landscape (F01–F04, clearest in F01/F03).** The references use broken rock forms, vegetation clusters, shaped clearings, and layered silhouettes. These frames show long straight cuts through a smooth hill and a nearly uniform grass carpet. Reshaping those terrain transitions, grouping plants, and adding varied shoreline vegetation are scene-addressable work. The existing grass and rock presentation also needs a more coherent surface treatment; the images alone cannot establish whether that requires additional assets.
3. **Water and lighting do not create the reference's depth or mood (F01–F04).** The key art's streams have readable shore relationships and varied surface values; its night scene balances cool distance with warm habitation. The lake here is a broad colour band, especially pale in F01/F02, while grass highlights carry excessive attention. F03/F04 reduce the water mismatch but leave the underlying flatness. Shore treatment, material response, light balance, and a motivated local light near an inhabited destination are addressable scene/material work.

## Direct bar answers

**A — Belongs to the Meadows key-art world: No, for either pair.** The greens, water, hills, and cool night indicate the intended setting, but the planar hillside, uniform grass, empty composition, and weak shoreline treatment do not deliver its inviting, lived-in landscape or its night mystery. Scene composition, terrain, vegetation distribution, materials, and lighting can address the observed gaps. Bespoke creature/character art adequacy remains unproven because no creature and no useful character close-up are shown; these frames cannot justify a particular replacement asset.

**B — Trying to be the same kind of game as Palworld: Yes, for either pair.** A stylized third-person traveller, open natural terrain, visible survival/building commands, and a five-slot creature interface communicate the same broad exploration and creature-adventure category. This is a genre read, not a judgment that the execution reaches the reference.

**Actual commercial visual quality beside the supplied Palworld screenshots: No, for either pair.** F03/F04 are the better presentation of this location, but the small water improvement does not close the conspicuous terrain, vegetation, scene-life, and lighting gaps. These frames do not support commercial-quality visual acceptance.
