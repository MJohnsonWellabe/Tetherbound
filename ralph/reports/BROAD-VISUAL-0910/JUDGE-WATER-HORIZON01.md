# Blind visual judgment — water and horizon

Inspected F01–F04, `tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`, and all five `palworld-0*.jpg` reference images. Judgment is based on visible images only. No source, implementation history, or earlier verdicts were consulted. F01/F02 are the day/night pair compared with F03/F04. Camera framing differs slightly, so foreground placement is not a controlled comparison.

**Preference: F03/F04.** They have a meaningfully cleaner distant water surface. F01 has a conspicuous yellow-green band of thin horizontal strips across almost the whole horizon; F02 retains its blue-grey equivalent. F03/F04 remove that distracting band and create a more continuous expanse of water. This is a local improvement, not acceptance of the overall scene. F04 makes the remaining angular boundary in the water especially obvious.

The trainer is identifiable and reasonably proportioned, but the small, dark, side-on figure does not carry the expressive character appeal visible in the Palworld references. Hair and clothing break into coarse, busy edges at this presentation size. There are no visible creatures in any supplied frame, so creature art, relative creature scale, companion appeal, and combat presentation cannot be judged. These pictures do not demonstrate the central creature promise.

## All eight criteria

1. **Silhouette and small-size readability.** In F01–F04 the trainer remains identifiable as a person, with a distinct backpack and hair silhouette. Legs and boots partly merge with the dark grass, most noticeably in F02/F04. The distant right-hand island reads as a thin strip rather than a memorable place. At thumbnail size, the composition becomes three broad bands—sky, water, grass—with one small person; no strong environmental landmark survives. Grass has strong individual blade contrast at full size but becomes repetitive texture at small size.

2. **Colour and value structure.** F01/F03 have plausible green, teal, and blue ingredients, but the highly contrasted yellow-green foreground sits against pale, low-variation cyan water. F01's yellow-green horizon strip adds an unrelated-looking stripe to that relationship. F03 is more coherent. F02/F04 read as night through the blue sky and dark ground, but bright blue water and a conspicuously bright trainer upper body feel weakly connected to the surrounding illumination. None of these frames contains enough visible danger or friendly colour coding to assess oxblood usage.

3. **Intentionality.** F01–F04 have an almost uninterrupted carpet of similarly upright, similarly scaled grass. A few taller yellow plants and shrubs offer variation, but there are few clear clusters, openings, shoreline transitions, or authored destinations. Water can appropriately occupy a large fraction of a coastal view; here the absence of a strong island, headland, vessel, or other focal shape makes that space read as unfinished rather than composed. The distant island is largely hidden behind the objective panel.

4. **Lighting.** Day versus night is unambiguous across both pairs. Foreground blades have directional tonal variation, and the trainer contacts the ground plausibly enough that obvious floating is not demonstrated. However, F01/F03 water is so smooth and pale that its form is difficult to read. F02/F04 retain a very bright water field under a dark sky without a clear visible light or reflection pattern explaining it. Night clouds have hard dark undersides and flattened streaks near the horizon, reducing the sense of a deep sky. These are image appearance findings, not renderer or light-source diagnoses.

5. **Horizon and depth.** This is the clearest difference: prefer F03/F04. F01/F02 have a broad distant strip with many fine horizontal lines and a hard lower edge, visually dividing the sea into disconnected layers. F03/F04 give the distant sea a simpler transition into haze. Nevertheless, all four contain a large angular tonal boundary running from the left near shore toward the centre-right and then toward the distant island. F04 shows it especially clearly as a dark blue polygon against lighter blue water. All four have an extremely level, almost featureless horizon with faint vertical smudges under it. Distance is communicated mainly by colour wash rather than overlapping landforms, recognisable islands, or a destination silhouette. The haze erases useful depth structure rather than revealing a layered world.

6. **Interface.** The main objective is legible, but the large upper-right panel obscures the only substantial distant land shape in every frame. The large lower-right bars occupy considerable shoreline/foreground space while the five slots contain only tiny numerals. Health and food text at bottom left have weak contrast against the grass, and the top-centre clock is small and subdued. The minimap is readable as a green square with markers but conveys little visible coastal structure. These are internal hierarchy and legibility findings, not comparisons to Palworld UI.

7. **Artefacts.** F01/F02's horizontal horizon strip reads as an artefact rather than a deliberate sea feature. F01/F02 also show pronounced fine vertical streaking across the left water area. The angular water boundary persists in F03/F04 and looks unnatural for an uninterrupted sea surface. F01–F04 show faint vertical dark marks at the far horizon and visibly jagged edges on the distant island. These are visible symptoms only; the images cannot establish their technical causes. No claim of temporal popping, z-fighting, or motion aliasing is supported by these stills.

8. **Scale agreement.** Using the trainer as the 1.80 m ruler, surrounding grass reaches roughly shin to thigh height, with isolated taller plants; that is a plausible tall-grass setting. The trainer's backpack, boots, and limbs broadly agree in scale. The distant island's vegetation is too small and far away to measure reliably. No creature-to-trainer or large-to-small-creature comparison is available. There is no demonstrated catastrophic scale mismatch, but the supplied pictures cannot validate the broader scale requirement.

## Three largest gaps from the references

1. **A convincing water surface and layered horizon — F01–F04.** The key art uses shaped banks, rocky edges, reflected light, and successive landforms to make water part of a coherent landscape. Palworld's plateau and base views use distant terrain and overlapping silhouettes to establish a world beyond the foreground. These frames have a blank horizontal distance and a polygonal colour boundary; F01/F02 additionally have the striped horizon band. Water appearance, haze, shoreline composition, and distant land placement are scene/material work. A stronger distant landmark may need suitable art if none is available; inventory cannot be determined from pictures.

2. **An authored, inhabited composition — F01–F04.** The references have routes, vegetation clusters, clearings, landmarks, buildings, and creatures creating a visual reason to look or travel somewhere. These views devote most of the image to undifferentiated sky, water, and a uniform grass carpet. Clustered planting, a more deliberate shoreline, a visible destination, and framing that keeps the island clear of the interface are scene/composition changes. These images do not prove that new assets are necessary to address them.

3. **Creature and character appeal as the focal point — F01–F04.** Palworld's characters and creatures have clear colour blocks, expressive silhouettes, and visible relationships to each other. Here a small side-on trainer is the only actor, and its dark lower body is lost in busy grass. A better pose/view, lighting separation, and visible companion can improve the presentation. Whether the underlying trainer or absent creature art requires replacement cannot be decided from these frames; close character and creature views are needed. The current images do not establish that art bar.
