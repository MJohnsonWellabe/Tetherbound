# Cloudreach Cliffs — blind visual judge, ROUND 2 (interim)

The verdict that redirected the last round of work. Kept because it is the evidence for
*why* the cloud sea and the distant relief were re-coloured: this judge could see the
frames and still reported "no cloud sea", which is what proved the layer was present but
had no value separation from the sky rather than absent.

Frames: `shots/atmos_after2/`, stands 01, 02-broken-causeways, 04, 11.

Reproduced verbatim.

---

Read at sheet size first. At sheet size the four frames read as **one green hill, photographed four times**. Every frame is a bright upper half of blue-and-white sky sitting on a lower half of one flat mid-green, with a hard line between them and nothing past it. Nothing in the sheet says "high." Nothing says "cliff." Nothing says "sky region." A viewer given the sheet cold would name this a starter meadow, not the vertical place after it.

## Specific defects, in order of severity

**1. Frame 11 — the world ends in an exposed terrain backface.** Right of the black rock slab, just past the green gem, the ground stops at a hard straight edge and you can see the **underside of the terrain mesh**: a flat dull grey-brown plane, then sky. No cliff face, no skirt, no rock, no rubble, no fog to hide it. This is the exact spot the region's premise wants a drop, and it renders as an unfinished polygon boundary. It is the single worst thing in the set.

**2. Frame 11 — a white spiral scratch pattern is projected across the face of the central monolith.** Dozens of thin concentric white curves radiating from an off-centre point across the entire rock face, plus two short teal dashes floating in front of it. This does not read as strata, weathering, or carving; it reads as a misprojected decal or a UV/normal-map failure. It is the most read-as-a-bug object in the survey.

**3. Frame 01 — a flat vertical green triangle down the right face of the central butte.** A perfectly planar, razor-edged, uniformly-lit green wall running from the butte's crown to the ground, with the grass texture smeared vertically and no blades, no shading, no variation. It reads as a tarp hung on the rock.

**4. Frame 01 — a saturated cyan bar sits on the gatehouse parapet.** Centre of the battlement, behind the wall: a flat, fully-saturated teal rectangle with a pale slab beside it, unlit and unshaded. No cyan exists anywhere else in the frame.

**5. Frame 01 — the frame has no shadows at all, and the other three do.** The foreground tree casts nothing. The trainer casts nothing. The log cart casts nothing. Frames 02, 04 and 11 all have long, clear, correctly-directed cast shadows.

**6. Frame 11 — the green gem reads as a debug marker.** A bright saturated green faceted cube on the lawn with a soft blue-white glow that renders as a visibly rectangular smudge quad, no cast shadow, no grass displacement, no socket.

**7. Frame 02 — the bridge's ropes and anchors are not connected to anything.** Both guide ropes run down from the anchor post tops and terminate in mid-air.

**8. Frame 02 — the causeway spans nothing.** The deck's near end is a raw straight-cut polygon edge lying flat on the lawn. It then rises over unbroken green hill to a bell gate, behind which is more green hill.

**9. Frame 04 — roughly 70% sky over a bare green dome.** There is no mid-ground content anywhere in the lower two-thirds of the frame.

**10. Frame 04 — grass renders as scattered debris with alpha-dither stipple.**

**11. Frame 04 — the floating island is a flat grey cutout.** No directional lighting, no colour variation, no vegetation, no shadow and no atmospheric separation from the mesa behind it — same value, same hue.

**12. Frame 04 — the far landmark barely separates from the sky.**

**13. Frame 04 — the sun is a flat white disc, and the sky disagrees with the other frames.** Three cloud languages across four frames.

**14. Frame 11 — the wooden poles connect to nothing.**

**15. Frames 01 and 11 — scatter reads procedural, not authored.**

**16. All four frames — the grass has a visible density ring around the camera.**

**17. Rock materials disagree with each other across the set.**

**18. Frame 11 — the two creatures are in two different art styles.**

**19. Frame 11 — the two creatures are the same size class, and both are shorter than the trainer.**

**20. The trainer's hands and idle pose read as unfinished.**

**21. Frame 01 — the gate gates nothing.**

**22. Terrain triangles are visible as shading creases.**

**23. Value and hue structure is two-note across the whole sheet.** On the plus side: **the oxblood is clean.**

**24. Depth does not read.** Only frame 01 shows any distance desaturation, and only about one value step. Frames 02 and 04 have none. There is no haze band, no atmospheric layering, no cloud sea, and no terrain anywhere lower than the player's feet in any of the four frames.

---

## The three things that most separate these frames from the references

**1. There is no down.** The player never stands on an edge and never looks down. For a region built on altitude this is the defining failure.

**2. The ground is bare where the references are packed.**

**3. The world has no dark values and no warmth.**

---

## The two bar questions

### A. Do these read as belonging to the world of `tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`? — **No.**

Carried: the green hue family; the cartoon-oak stylisation; the mossy sandstone masonry;
the purple flowers; the oxblood correctly absent from everything friendly.
Sank it: two hues, one value band, no distance; both landmarks within one value step of the
sky; almost no signs of habitation; five things that read as broken.

### B. Shown beside `palworld-0*.jpg`, same kind of game? — **No.**

Carried: the over-the-shoulder framing; the trainer's costume language; the white creature.
Sank it: density, depth, creatures-as-events, and the artifact list.
