# Blind visual judgement — `shots/dress_before/`

Six frames, read first at contact-sheet scale and then full size, against
`docs/reference/tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png` (art direction board) and
`docs/reference/palworld-0*.jpg` (the bar). No score. Defects only, each
named to a frame.

---

## 0. Creatures and characters — said first, said plainly

**C1. `11-aerie-ground-connection` — the snow leopard is a photoreal wildlife
model and it does not belong in this frame.** It has accurate feline anatomy,
rosette fur rendered as a real coat, and glassy blue eyes. It stands two metres
from a trainer built on stylised proportions (big head, chunky boots, painted
cloth) and under trees that are toon canopies made of flat leaf clumps. Three
different art languages share one frame. Every Pal in `palworld-01`, `-03`
and `-04` is built from the same shape vocabulary as the human beside it —
rounded volumes, thick limbs, oversized features, a matching level of
abstraction. This leopard shares nothing with the trainer except the render
pass. It reads as a nature-documentary asset dropped into a cartoon, and it is
the single most style-breaking object in the survey.

**C2. `11-aerie-ground-connection` — the creature scale is inverted against the
1.80 m ruler.** The trainer's hip is at roughly 1.0 m; the leopard's shoulder
sits at about that line and its ear tips reach his waist. That puts it at
~0.85 m at the shoulder and ~1.5 m nose-to-rump: a life-size snow leopard, i.e.
a large dog beside a person. The second creature — the small white winged thing
at the trainer's left shoulder — is smaller still, roughly 0.45 m. **The entire
creature cast visible in this survey is shorter than the human.** In
`palworld-01` the boss is three to four times the human's height; in
`-03` the field boss is longer than the human is tall and has three times his
visual mass; even the ordinary companion Pals in `-04` stand at human shoulder
height with far more bulk. A creature-training game whose creatures are the
smallest characters on screen has the relationship backwards.

**C3. `11-aerie-ground-connection` — the small white creature has no silhouette.**
It is built from thin white planes, it sits directly in front of a pale dirt
road, and it overlaps the trainer's shoulder. At sheet scale it is invisible.
At full size it does not resolve into an animal — it is a white smear. Nothing
about its shape, value or colour separates it from what is behind it.

**C4. All six frames — the trainer is in a rigid symmetrical A-pose with the
fingers splayed, every time,** including in `09-final-arena-space`. No weight
shift, no contrapposto, no idle, no hand at rest. Every Palworld reference has
the player actively posed — bow drawn (`-02`), spear planted (`-03`), riding
(`-04`), mid-stride (`-05`). Here the protagonist reads as a mannequin dropped
into six locations, which flattens the mood the board asks for ("cozy and
inviting") into a screenshot of a level.

**C5. `02-lower-cliffs-galefoot`, `09-final-arena-space` — the trainer has no
value or hue separation from the ground.** His costume is teal jacket / tan
pack / dark brown trousers, all mid-value, all in the same band as the grass
and the dirt. In `09` the only thing that separates him from the arena floor is
his cast shadow. Every Palworld player character carries one saturated accent —
green hair, orange hair, red hair, a blue sash — that pops from any background.

**C6. `09-final-arena-space` — the boss does not read as an event.** The
opponent NPC renders about 45 px tall in a 1280-wide frame, dead centre,
unlit, with no framing, no scale cue and no VFX. He is smaller and less
prominent than the fence posts on the right wall. This frame is called the
final arena. `palworld-01` and `-03` give their field boss a third of the
frame, a shoved-back camera, impact sparks and a name plate. Nothing in `09`
says a fight is about to happen here.

## 1. Silhouette and readability at sheet scale

**S1. Five of six thumbnails resolve to the same image: green wedge, blue sky,
small grey blocks.** `02`, `08`, `09`, `11` and `12` are not distinguishable
from each other at 30% without reading the captions. Only `06` has a nameable
shape. Each Palworld thumbnail resolves instantly to one landmark with its own
value (the ruin plateau in `-04`, the tower in `-05`).

**S2. `02-lower-cliffs-galefoot`, `12-cliffhold-ground-connection` — the cliff
backdrop is one boulder module repeated edge to edge at one tint and one
value,** with a nearly flat top line across the whole frame. No peak, no
ridgeline, no height variation, nothing to navigate by. Both wide panels of the
key art build the composition around a single distinct peak or mountain; the
board's own bullet list says "silhouettes and landmarks visible from distance."
Neither frame has a landmark.

**S3. `06-summit-final-approach` — the summit monument has no silhouette mass.**
The thing the player climbs a whole region to reach is an open lattice of thin
striped poles. Against sky it is a wire mesh; at sheet scale it reads as
scaffolding around an unfinished building rather than as a monument. The one
element with mass — the white finial — is a small bright spike at the top.

**S4. `08-upper-cliffhold-east-arrival` — the dark rocks on the right slope are
zero-thickness dark quads lying flush on the terrain, with no cast shadow.**
They read as decals painted on grass, not rocks. At sheet scale they read as
holes in the ground.

**S5. `08`, `12` — distant trees on the clifftops render as three or four dark
green pixels each,** with no LOD silhouette. They read as dirt specks on the
rock, not as trees, so the far hills lose the tree language entirely.

## 2. Colour and value structure

**V1. Nothing in these frames is bright — measured, not impressionistic.**
95th-percentile luminance: `02` = 135, `09` = 143, `08` = 162, `11` = 152,
`12` = 155, `06` = 167 (0–255). The Palworld shots land at 216–234 and the key
art at 187. There is not one true highlight anywhere in this survey: no
sun-struck grass, no white in a cloud, no specular on stone, no water. The
top end of the range is simply missing.

**V2. Half of three frames is one mid-tone.** Fraction of pixels inside ±13
luminance of the frame median: `09` = 0.59, `02` = 0.55, `08` = 0.53. The
references sit at 0.09–0.19. `02`, `08` and `09` are literally more than half
a single value. That is why they read flat regardless of what is in them.

**V3. `08-upper-cliffhold-east-arrival`, `12-cliffhold-ground-connection` — the
grass cards fight the terrain they grow from.** The cards are a saturated
yellow-straw green; the terrain texture beneath them is a desaturated
blue-green. They do not read as growing out of the ground, they read as a
second material laid on top. In `08` they form a hard-edged rectangular band
along the ridge with a straight cut edge running the width of the frame.

**V4. All six frames — the terrain base texture is a smeared blur with no
close-range detail.** In `02`, `09` and `12` the ground within three metres of
camera is a soft green gradient: no blade, no soil, no rock breakup, no
variation. `palworld-02` and `-05` both carry visible ground detail and
dirt/rock breakup right at the camera, which is what stops their empty ground
from reading as empty.

**V5. `09-final-arena-space` — the danger red is spread across architecture
rather than reserved.** Beyond the banners (correct use), the same crimson
appears as flat untextured beams across three separate stone walls and as a
solid red bar over the gate; and the most saturated object in the frame is a
magenta/violet machine at right that is not the threat. On the friendly side
the reservation holds — `02`, `08` and `12` carry no red at all — so this is a
`09`-only leak, but it is a leak onto set dressing.

**V6. `02`, `06` — the shadow side has no colour.** Shadowed grass, shadowed
stone and the arch interior in `06` all fall to the same neutral near-black
with no bounce and no sky-blue fill. The key art's oak-grove panel and the
`palworld-01` forest both keep colour alive in shadow.

## 3. Intentionality — this reads as placement, not authorship

**I1. `02-lower-cliffs-galefoot` — the props are one-of-each on a grid.** Two
crates, one barrel, one hay bale, three fence segments, set in a neat row on a
hard-edged dirt pad in front of the houses. Nothing clusters, nothing overlaps,
nothing is stacked, nothing is knocked over, nothing repeats at a different
scale. `palworld-05` piles and overlaps its base props so the eye reads
"someone lives here." This reads as an inventory laid out.

**I2. `02`, `12` — the bushes are one bush model at one scale with the same
purple flower cluster, repeated at roughly even intervals** across the
foreground (five instances in `12`, four in `02`). The rocks are the same two
or three models, also at one scale. Even spacing plus uniform scale is the
signature of a scatter pass, and it is legible here.

**I3. `08-upper-cliffhold-east-arrival` — the four cottages are two models at
one scale,** all roofs at the same pitch, same blue-slate tint, distributed
evenly along the ridge. No hero building, no height variation, no orientation
variation. The key art's settlement panel varies roof height, roof pitch and
building footprint and anchors the composition with a well and a banner.

**I4. `09-final-arena-space` — the arena floor is a perfect oval of dirt with a
razor-sharp edge against grass and absolutely nothing on it.** No debris, no
scuff marks, no scatter, no props, no border transition. Roughly a third of the
frame is featureless ground. This is the climax space of the region.

**I5. `02`, `08`, `12` — the same bare leaning pole prop appears repeatedly with
nothing on it.** Two in `02` (one against each near cottage), three in `08`,
one vertical in `12`. Whatever it is meant to hold — a banner, a rope, a
washing line — it is empty, and it reads as stray geometry rather than as a
prop.

**I6. `12-cliffhold-ground-connection` — the fences are disconnected segments
with gaps.** Three separate rail sections stop in mid-air rather than meeting a
post, a gate or a building corner. Same in `02`.

## 4. Lighting

**L1. All six frames are the identical high-noon clear sky.** Same sun
elevation, same short hard shadows, same pale-blue gradient, essentially the
same cloud coverage. The art board carries an explicit **DAY** panel and an
explicit **NIGHT** panel, a sunset panel built on orange rim light across a
menhir, and an oak-grove panel built entirely on dappled light through canopy.
Its own note says "day and night create different moods and encounters." Not
one frame in this survey has a time of day other than midday, so the survey
cannot show the mood the board is asking for.

**L2. `11-aerie-ground-connection` — the trees have no form.** Each canopy is
the same green on its sunward and shadow side; the only shading is the flat
blob it casts on the grass. No dappling, no leaf breakup in the shadow, no
translucency on the lit edge. In `palworld-01` the canopy above the boss reads
as a lit volume with light passing through it.

**L3. `02`, `09`, `11` — the trainer's shadow is a detached soft blob offset
from his feet with no contact darkening.** In `02` it sits noticeably to his
right of where he stands. He reads as hovering a few centimetres above the
ground in all three frames.

**L4. `06-summit-final-approach` — the frame has no mid-tone.** The sky is the
brightest thing by a wide margin, the stone base wall drops into an unreadable
dark band, and the gate arch interior is pure black. There is nothing bridging
the two, so the monument sits as a cut-out against sky.

## 5. Horizon and depth

**H1. `08-upper-cliffhold-east-arrival` — the world ends.** Left of the tower
the terrain silhouette cuts against a flat grey-blue sky with nothing beyond
it: no distant range, no haze band, no second land mass, no sea. Every key-art
panel stacks three or four depth layers (near meadow → mid hills → far peaks →
sky), and `palworld-04` and `-05` both put a hazed distant landmark behind the
playable ground. This frame has exactly one layer.

**H2. `02`, `12` — no atmospheric perspective.** The cliff 200 m back carries
the same value and the same saturation as the rock 20 m back. Distance does not
read; the backdrop feels like a painted wall standing ten metres behind the
houses rather than a landscape.

**H3. `06-summit-final-approach` — a hard horizontal terrain seam runs the full
width of the frame at mid-height,** where the near ground plane ends and the
plateau begins, and on the right side **sky is visible through the gap under
the plateau lip**. Below that seam the near ground is a set of large flat
triangular facets, several of them near-black, with no ground cover on any of
them. Roughly the bottom 40% of this frame is broken geometry.

**H4. `11-aerie-ground-connection` — a chunk of rock and a spray of loose
boulders float unsupported in the sky at upper left,** detached from any
terrain.

## 6. Artefacts

**A1. `11-aerie-ground-connection` — the central mesa is covered in violently
stretched texture.** Smeared vertical bands and swirled white streaks that
follow no geometry, plus a hard dark blob painted across its face. It looks
like broken UVs or a projection at the wrong scale. This is the frame's focal
landmark and it is the worst-looking surface in the survey.

**A2. `12-cliffhold-ground-connection` — a bare pale pole runs ground-to-sky
straight through the centre of the hero building,** bisecting the gable and the
door. It carries nothing.

**A3. `12-cliffhold-ground-connection` — the hero building's chimney is a pale
untextured block that intersects the roof ridge at an offset and connects to no
wall below.** It floats through the roof. The chimney on the left cottage in
`02` sits at a similar odd offset to its ridge.

**A4. `02-lower-cliffs-galefoot` — path decal borders are showing.** Thin dark
curved outlines are visible on the grass tracing the edges of the dirt paths,
including one closed loop in the lower-centre grass with no path inside it.
Same thin outline is visible around the prop dirt pad at left and around the
paths in `12`.

**A5. `08-upper-cliffhold-east-arrival` — a plain flat grey quad lies on the
grass to the trainer's right** with a hard rectangular edge and no shading; and
a cluster of white speckles sits on the centre cottage's plaster wall beside
his head. Both read as texture/decal errors rather than as anything in the
world.

**A6. `02`, `09` — hard aliased stair-stepping on every roof edge, fence rail,
banner edge and window frame.** No edge smoothing anywhere in the survey. The
Palworld shots are clean on the same kind of edge.

**A7. `06-summit-final-approach` — the white finial on the dome is flat
untextured white and shades differently from every other object in frame.** It
reads as a different asset pipeline pasted onto the top of the monument. The
floating blue eye disc behind the lattice is likewise unattached to any
structure and at a scale that relates to nothing.

**A8. `09-final-arena-space` — the arena wall is one crenellated stone module
(~4 m) repeated left to right,** with visible texture tiling at close range on
the near-left wall.

---

## 7. Scale agreement

**Sc1.** Covered above and worth restating as a scale finding: in
`11-aerie-ground-connection` both creatures are shorter than the 1.80 m
trainer (leopard ~0.85 m at the shoulder, white creature ~0.45 m), which
inverts the human-to-creature relationship every Palworld reference shows.

**Sc2. `02-lower-cliffs-galefoot` — the trees on the clifftops are roughly a
twelfth the height of the cliff they stand on,** while the tree beside the
cottage in the same frame reads at about 7 m. The clifftop trees read as
miniature scenery rather than as the same forest, which makes the cliff mass
feel like a diorama backdrop instead of terrain the player could reach.

**Sc3.** Where the survey is fine: the buildings in `02`, `08` and `12` agree
with the trainer — door lintels sit around 2 m, ridge heights around 5 m,
benches and crates read at plausible human sizes. The tower in `08` and the
dome in `06` both read at a believable 20–25 m and 12 m against him. Building
and prop scale is not the problem; creature scale is.

Nothing to judge — no HUD is present in any frame. Worth flagging only that the

# VERDICT

## 1. The three things that most separate these frames from the references

**1st — the creature art is from a different game.**
`11-aerie-ground-connection`. The Palworld shots' entire proposition is a
bespoke creature standing beside a human in the same drawing style, at a size
that makes it matter — `-01`'s boss towers over the player, `-03`'s field boss
outmasses her, `-04`'s companions stand at her shoulder with real bulk. This
frame answers that with a photoreal snow leopard at 0.85 m and a 0.45 m white
plane-creature that will not resolve. The style mismatch and the inverted size
relationship are both visible in the still, instantly, and they are the first
thing anyone would say about this survey.

**2nd — there is no bright end to the picture.**
`02-lower-cliffs-galefoot` and `09-final-arena-space` most of all. The
references reach 216–234 at the 95th percentile and spend 9–19% of their pixels
near the median; these frames stop at 135–143 and spend 55–59% near the median.
Concretely, `palworld-03` has sun blowing out the grass and the sky, `-04` has a
white haze-lit plateau, and the key art has sunlight on the meadow — a
brightness the eye reads as *daylight*. `02` and `09` have no highlight at all,
so a clear noon sky reads as overcast.

**3rd — the world has no distance and no landmark.**
`08-upper-cliffhold-east-arrival` for distance, `02-lower-cliffs-galefoot` for
landmark. In `08` the terrain silhouette cuts against empty sky with nothing
behind it; every key-art panel and `palworld-04`/`-05` stack a hazed far layer
that says the world continues. In `02` the entire backdrop is one repeated
boulder module at one value with a flat top line, where the board's own note
demands "silhouettes and landmarks visible from distance." A player in these
frames has nothing to walk toward.

## 2. The two bar questions

### A. Do these frames belong to the world in `tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`?

**No.**

What carries: the **village architecture family genuinely matches**. The
timber-framed cottages with slate roofs and stone bases in `02`, `08` and `12`
are recognisably the board's starting-settlement panel built in 3D, and the
Team Tether banner language in `09` matches the stronghold panel. If the board
were only about buildings, this would be a yes.

What sinks it: the board is a world of **oak groves, clear streams and ponds,
and wildflower meadows**, with layered distant peaks, a sunset, and a night
state. This survey has **no water in any frame**, one grove (`11`), meadow
density nowhere near the board's flower-carpeted foregrounds, no distant peak
in any frame, one time of day across all six, and a grey-green blocky cliff
vocabulary that appears nowhere on the board. The board's mood note is "cozy
and inviting, but with hints of mystery"; `09`'s empty dirt oval and `06`'s
black-shadowed lattice read as neither cozy nor mysterious — they read as
unfinished.

### B. Beside `palworld-0*.jpg`, would someone say these are trying to be the same kind of game?

**No.**

Someone shown these side by side would say Palworld is a creature game and this
is a village walkthrough. The reasons are the three ranked above plus: in every
Palworld frame something is *happening* — a boss roars, sparks fly, a bow is
drawn, Pals are working — while in all six of these frames a mannequin stands
still in an A-pose. `09-final-arena-space` is the clearest failure of the
comparison: the shot named for the climax has a 45-pixel opponent standing in
an empty dirt circle.

### What is fixable by changing the scene, and what needs art that is not in the build

**Fixable in-scene (this is the work list):**

- V1/V2 — exposure, sun intensity, sky brightness and tonemap so the frames
  reach a real highlight; then a bright value accent placed deliberately in each
  composition.
- V3/V4 — retint grass cards to the terrain, add a close-range ground detail
  texture, kill the straight-cut grass band edge in `08`.
- V5 — pull the crimson off the untextured wall beams and desaturate the
  magenta machine in `09`.
- I1/I2/I3/I4/I6 — cluster and overlap props, introduce scale variety in bushes
  and rocks, stack and knock things over, break the even cottage spacing in
  `08`, dress the `09` arena floor and its grass/dirt border.
- I5 — remove or load the empty leaning poles in `02`, `08`, `12`.
- L1 — vary time of day across the survey; the board's day/night/sunset panels
  are unrepresented.
- L2/L3/L4 — canopy shading and dappling, contact shadows under the trainer,
  ambient fill in `06`'s arch and base wall.
- H1/H2 — distance haze and a far silhouette layer behind `08`; atmospheric
  perspective on the cliffs in `02`/`12`.
- H3 — the broken foreground geometry, the seam, and the see-through gap in
  `06`; this is a bug, not a look.
- H4 — the floating rock and boulders in `11`.
- A1 — the stretched mesa texture in `11`.
- A2/A3/A4/A5/A7/A8 — the pole through the building and floating chimney in
  `12`, path decal outlines in `02`/`12`, stray grey quad and wall speckles in
  `08`, the unshaded finial and unattached eye disc in `06`, wall module tiling
  in `09`.
- A6 — enable edge smoothing.
- S4/S5 — give the flat rock decals in `08` real geometry; give distant trees a
  billboard LOD with a readable silhouette.
- S2 — the cliff backdrop can be re-massed from existing modules into varied
  heights with one dominant peak; that is composition, not new art.

**Needs art that is not in the build:**

- **A creature cast drawn in the same stylised language as the trainer, at a
  size that reads beside a 1.80 m human** (C1, C2, C3, Sc1). No amount of
  lighting, density or scatter work makes a photoreal snow leopard belong next
  to a toon tree, and scaling it up would only make the style mismatch larger.
  This is the survey's central hole and it is an asset problem.
- **A trainer idle/pose set** (C4). Not scene dressing.
- **An accent colour on the trainer's costume** that separates him from grass
  and dirt (C5) — a re-texture, small, but still art.
- **A summit monument with silhouette mass** (S3). The `06` lattice cannot be
  lit or dressed into a landmark; it is see-through by construction.
- **Boss presentation for `09`** (C6) — a creature or NPC with enough scale and
  VFX to be an event, plus whatever framing the encounter uses.
