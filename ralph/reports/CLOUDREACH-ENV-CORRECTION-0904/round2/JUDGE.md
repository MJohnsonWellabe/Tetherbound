# Cloudreach round 2 — blind visual judgment

Reviewed `contact-sheet.png` at contact-sheet scale and all ten `shots/*.png` individually at their native 1280×720 resolution. Compared only with `tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png` and the five supplied `palworld-0*.jpg` images. No source, prior judgments, implementation notes, or other reports were inspected. Creature absence is excluded by the task. Still images cannot establish motion quality or interaction quality.

**A. Do these frames resemble the world of the Tetherbound key art? No.** Green land, timber cottages, stone arches, and a backpacked adventurer supply some of its vocabulary. The prevailing image is nevertheless an exposed, sparsely dressed plateau against smooth brown walls, rather than the board's layered, inviting natural landscape and integrated settlements. This is a composition, material-language, and mood judgment, not a demand for painted per-leaf fidelity.

**B. Are these frames commercially comparable to the supplied Palworld references? No.** They clearly suggest a third-person fantasy adventure, but the environment presentation does not reach the supplied game's bar. This conclusion does not depend on missing creatures: Palworld's ground coverage, cliff construction, landscape layering, and transitions between structures and their surroundings already establish a substantial gap.

## Prioritized, addressable defects

1. **The cliffs look like enlarged smooth brown shapes rather than a convincing landscape.** In `03-windscar-ravine.png`, nearly the entire background is a wall of broad tan facets with small shelf-like protrusions and very little material or vegetation variation. `05-upper-cloudreach-cliffhold.png` gives most of its central composition to a similarly featureless mound. `02-lower-cliffs-galefoot.png` places the cottages directly against a jagged brown screen. At sheet scale these dominate everything else. The cliff masses need readable large strata or fractures, a coherent rock material, broken bases and ledges, and selected vegetation transitions. Compare the differentiated rock faces and planted ledges in `palworld-04-plateau-landmark.jpg`; the issue is not that these cliffs must become ordinary rolling Meadows hills.

2. **Too much traversable foreground is a blurry surface with no physical ground cover or authored edge.** `02-lower-cliffs-galefoot.png`, `06-summit-final-approach.png`, `07-fly-only-destination.png`, and `08-upper-cliffhold-east-arrival.png` have large yellow-green carpet-like foregrounds. Thin isolated strips of grass leave the surface's repetitive mottling as the dominant detail. `01-arrival-first-reveal.png` is greener but has the same broad bare foreground. Palworld's open field and base screenshots retain grass clumps, dirt variation, and object-ground transitions even where the player has room to move. Create irregular planted margins, patches of exposed earth, rocks embedded in those margins, and deliberate clear routes. Do not fill the traversable center uniformly; give its emptiness a visible reason.

3. **The destinations read as separate objects placed on open ground, rather than places with a history and purpose.** In `08-upper-cliffhold-east-arrival.png`, the few cottages and extremely slender tower are stranded in an almost empty plateau; the broad approach reveals little settlement structure. `02-lower-cliffs-galefoot.png` has scattered barrels and fences but an abrupt dirt island around the houses. In `06-summit-final-approach.png`, a symmetrical clean fortress front has only one rock group and one cart as its principal grounding details. `07-fly-only-destination.png` is a clean gate, steps, and paired rocks with almost no surrounding context. The key art's settlement and stronghold use enclosing vegetation, connected paths, work areas, worn foundations, and layered entrances. Build those relationships with the existing scene kit where possible: connect doors to paths, cluster supplies where used, develop a planted or rocky perimeter, and give major entrances a coherent approach.

4. **The bridge and path surfaces expose construction shortcuts at full size.** In `02-broken-causeways.png`, the bridge deck has substantially the same golden granular appearance as the dirt paths, its supports are plain oversized dark cylinders, and the ropes end at an enormous rectangular frame. `03-windscar-ravine.png` repeats that raw assembly. It reads as a platforming test structure more readily than crafted crossing infrastructure. Give the deck a recognizably wooden surface and board construction, visible joinery/attachment at the posts, and consistent material scale. In `04-high-roost-before-fly.png` and `08-upper-cliffhold-east-arrival.png`, the path border breaks into obvious rectangular and triangular flakes against the grass. Replace the visibly geometric edge with a coherent worn transition; verify it from these exact low-angle approach views.

5. **The stone architecture and hero machinery use incompatible levels of surface detail.** `09-final-arena-space.png` puts a densely textured dark apparatus beside extremely regular clean brickwork and a simple pale post with a bright cap. The apparatus collapses into a dark tangle at contact-sheet scale; the fortress reads as a large kit-built shell. `06-summit-final-approach.png` also shows flat unmarked banners and almost perfectly uniform masonry; `07-fly-only-destination.png` exposes clean repetitive blocks and a smooth cyan ornament. Unify roughness, color range, weathering, and detail scale. Give the apparatus a legible major silhouette/value accent and a designed mounting area. Add limited architectural wear and variation where water, vegetation, or use would produce it. More loose barrels alone will not resolve this mismatch.

6. **Depth exists, but the horizon often looks like scenery panels and the upper routes lose their visual invitation.** Pale distant cliffs separate from near cliffs, which is useful. However, `08-upper-cliffhold-east-arrival.png` exposes a huge nearly uniform horizontally rippled wall on the left and an empty blue-gray drop on the right; `01-arrival-first-reveal.png` shows similar pale banded masses at both edges. In `04-high-roost-before-fly.png`, the sky occupies most of the frame while the destination ahead is a small dark rectangular structure and the airborne landform is only a faint gray silhouette. Vary the silhouette and spacing of distant masses, provide overlapping middle-distance forms, and preserve a distinct destination value/color against the sky. In `05-upper-cloudreach-cliffhold.png`, the tower is identifiable but the enormous bare rock underneath receives most of the attention; the landmark needs a more intentional relationship to its rock and approach.

## Other rubric observations

- At small size, the trainer, trees, cottages, and fortress are identifiable. The trainer's light sleeves and backpack help against both grass and stone. The rear-only views do not establish face quality or expressive character appeal, so these images do not justify a character-art rejection or an acceptance of its full quality.
- The daylight direction and cast shadows are readable. The arena's shade is plausibly darker, but its apparatus and the trainer's lower body lose separation. Lighting is not the first defect to fix: the large bare surfaces remain bare in both sun and shade.
- Oxblood is visibly confined to fortress banners in this sample. It has not become a general friendly color.
- Person-to-door and person-to-tree scale is broadly plausible. The narrow towers in `05-upper-cloudreach-cliffhold.png` and `08-upper-cliffhold-east-arrival.png` read more like chimney-like pillars than occupied watchtowers; their silhouette needs a clearer architectural purpose.
- No gameplay interface is shown, so interface quality cannot be assessed. No claim about popping, performance, or inaccessible traversal follows from these stills.

## What can be fixed in the scene, and what needs art work

**Scene/composition work:** irregular ground-cover clusters and clearings; route margins; embedded rock and vegetation transitions; coherent settlement yards; fortress approach dressing; asymmetrical supporting detail; apparatus framing; foreground/middle-distance/background overlap; better destination contrast. These are directly visible gaps and do not inherently require new creature or character assets.

**Art/material/geometry work on the displayed elements:** the large cliffs need better rock surface language and form; bridge surfaces and construction need a credible treatment; fortress masonry, ornament, banners, and apparatus need compatible detail and material treatment; geometric path-edge artifacts need correction. Scatter or lighting alone cannot solve these. From images alone I cannot determine whether suitable replacement materials or meshes are already installed. The evidence supports improving or replacing the displayed treatments, not a claim that a new asset purchase or generation is necessarily required.

## The three largest separations from the references, ranked

1. **Terrain credibility and depth:** `03-windscar-ravine.png` and `05-upper-cloudreach-cliffhold.png` are dominated by smooth brown cliff masses. `palworld-04-plateau-landmark.jpg` builds a layered landscape from differentiated rock faces, ledges, vegetation, and distant silhouettes.
2. **Ground coverage and surface transitions:** `02-lower-cliffs-galefoot.png`, `07-fly-only-destination.png`, and `08-upper-cliffhold-east-arrival.png` expose broad mottled carpets. `palworld-02-open-field-path.jpg` and `palworld-05-base-building.jpg` make open ground read through grass-to-dirt transitions and physical detail around usable spaces.
3. **Integrated, authored destinations:** `06-summit-final-approach.png`, `08-upper-cliffhold-east-arrival.png`, and `09-final-arena-space.png` read as clean structures with sparse peripheral props. The key art's village/stronghold panels and Palworld's base/plateau frames connect architecture to terrain, approach routes, vegetation, and signs of use.

**Final answers: A — No. B — No.** Creature absence was not used in either verdict.
