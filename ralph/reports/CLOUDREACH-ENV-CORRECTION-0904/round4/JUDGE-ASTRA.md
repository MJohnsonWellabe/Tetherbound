# Cloudreach environment — blind visual verdict

Reviewed the F1–F12 contact sheet, enlarged F2/F5/F9/F10/F11, the Tetherbound Meadows key-art board, and all five Palworld reference screenshots. No game source, configuration, history, implementation reports, prior judgments, or performance data informed this review. Creatures are excluded from blocking criteria by the owner's direction. This is an environment verdict; it does not certify the creature art.

**A. Do these frames belong to the world of the Tetherbound art-direction board? Yes.** The green/gold ground, blue sky, warm timber, stone ruins, flowers, and reserved red stronghold banners support that relationship. F1, F4, F7 and F12 are its strongest evidence. The large cliffs and exposed crossings can belong to a higher, harsher part of that world without becoming another Meadows. This is a palette, mood and landmark-language judgment, not a claim that the painted board's detail has been matched.

**B. Beside the five Palworld references, do these frames meet the same kind of real-time game presentation bar? No.** The intended genre is apparent, but the environment still presents too many flat, disconnected surfaces and unfinished-looking constructions to hold the owner's shipping-game comparison. This failure stands with creatures excluded.

## Three largest gaps, ranked

1. **The terrain reads as separate dressed platforms instead of a continuous highland landscape.** In F2, the grassy top ends at an abrupt, almost extruded cliff edge; in F5, a narrow green ramp is outlined by two unusually uniform bright grass rails against mostly sky; in F6 a huge rounded rock mass has little grounding detail or visible relationship to the route; F9 and F12 expose broad bare plateau margins. Palworld 04 establishes connected foreground, broken rock shelves, inhabited middle distance and hazy mountains. Its exposed geology remains spatially connected. Break the grass-cap edges with inset shelves, rock outcrops and sparse vegetation clusters; establish visible intermediate landforms and route connections. Preserve the height and openness rather than filling Cloudreach with forest. **Primarily scene/terrain composition work.**

2. **Ground cover and surface transitions look laid out in bands, with large areas of undifferentiated floor.** F3 has a broad, uninterrupted green apron; F5 and F9 show conspicuous straight boundaries between path, short green ground and dense tall grass; F10 is dominated by repeating cobblestones; F12 has houses on a largely uniform green/yellow patch. Palworld 02 and 05 retain usable open paths and work areas while breaking their edges with irregular short vegetation, worn soil, rocks and shaded pockets. Vary the width and density of route-edge vegetation, soften the visible strip boundaries, and concentrate smaller details at foundations and rock feet. F10 particularly needs surface variation and meaningful perimeter groupings without filling its usable combat space. **Scene scatter, terrain painting and material blending can address this; more grass everywhere would not.**

3. **Several destination objects read as assembled primitives rather than finished places.** F11's large flat cyan rectangle, conspicuous horizontal division and tall striped posts do not communicate a convincing environmental feature or apparatus. F2's similarly stark overhead frame and suspended oval forms need stronger material and construction logic. In F9 the path visually terminates at a huge stone pillar; even if traversal works around it, the arrival composition advertises an obstruction rather than an entrance. F10's repeated scaffold towers and large plain side blocks leave the arena feeling like a layout awaiting its final dressing. Palworld 04's ruin and distant monument have distinct silhouettes embedded in their surroundings; Palworld 05's objects describe specific activities and occupy believable working areas. **Approach alignment, grouping, footing and edge treatment are scene-fixable. F11 may require a finished material/VFX treatment or more articulated object art if its current flat panel and posts are the complete available asset. The screenshots cannot establish that the necessary art is absent from the build.**

## Evidence limits and art split

Lighting establishes daylight, tree shadows ground objects, and the trainer remains identifiable in the sheet. The evidence does not support declaring a global lighting failure. Distance often becomes a pale void with little intermediate structure; that is visible, but these stills cannot distinguish camera framing from absent terrain or prove a fog-system defect. No obvious UI defect can be assessed because HUD is absent. The trainer provides a useful scale reference; cliffs and masonry are monumental, but still images do not establish their intended dimensions or usability.

The largest confirmed deficiencies are scene-fixable: landform continuity, ground transitions, route framing, prop clustering and foundations. **No purchase or new mesh generation is justified solely by these frames.** F11's presentation needs finished object/material work if better treatments are unavailable; that is conditional, not an inventory finding. Motion, popping, collision, flying/traversal feel, gameplay reliability and hardware performance remain untested by this review. The screenshot's software-rendering conditions were not independently established and no performance conclusions are drawn.
