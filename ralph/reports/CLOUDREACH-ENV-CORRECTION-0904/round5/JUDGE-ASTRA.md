# Cloudreach visual judgment

Image-only review. Creature art is excluded from acceptance at the owner's direction; environment and human characters are included. No source, history, implementation reports, or previous judgments were inspected. No performance or motion conclusions are drawn.

The sheet contains 12 views. I also inspected all 13 individual frames, including `10-real-fly-silhouette.png`, which does not appear on the sheet. Frame numbers below refer to the sheet labels, not the filename prefixes.

## Human character and small-size readability

The trainer reads coherently as a young adventurer: brown hair, pale cuffs, a tan backpack, dark trousers, and substantial boots remain identifiable across the sheet. The visible proportions agree with the buildings, bridges, and trees. These rear views do not establish facial appeal or expression, and they do not provide evidence that a replacement human mesh is necessary.

There is a human presentation problem in **F10 / `09-final-arena-space.png`**: the opposing human is a tiny dark figure on the same brown-grey value field as the far wall. At sheet size the character loses identity and barely establishes a confrontation. The trainer remains readable, but the encounter's other participant does not. Strengthen the destination's light/value separation and the approach composition so the opponent has a readable silhouette before relying on fine costume detail.

The village roofs, bridge, arch, and fortress survive thumbnail viewing. Rocks and bushes are distinguishable, but grass and flowers frequently collapse into either a uniformly green floor or a sharp fringe around its edge.

## Addressable defects

1. **The terrain dressing has a lawn-and-border structure.** In **F3 / `02-lower-cliffs-galefoot.png`**, the broad foreground is essentially uninterrupted green texture, while grass and flowers are pushed to the far perimeter. **F9 / `08-upper-cliffhold-east-arrival.png`** and **F12 / `12-cliffhold-ground-connection.png`** repeat the pattern: houses and isolated props sit on a large, evenly textured open floor. Palworld 02 and 05 give even useful clearings irregular grass intrusion, worn margins, localized activity, and transitions between materials. Keep usable walking space, but form smaller connected clearings with uneven verge clusters, doorstep wear, and activity around the existing props.

2. **Road surfaces expose their construction boundary.** In **F3**, the road begins as a conspicuous rectangle directly under the trainer. In **F5 / `04-high-roost-before-fly.png`** and **F9**, long straight outer borders remain visible outside the ochre center of the path. The effect is a strip laid on top of the lawn. The reference paths change width and merge into grass and soil. Remove the visible rectangular edge and interrupt the margins with irregular wear, short vegetation, and occasional exposed stone.

3. **The cliffs are large silhouettes with insufficient internal structure.** **F4 / `03-windscar-ravine.png`** and **F6 / `05-upper-cloudreach-cliffhold.png`** devote much of the composition to broad, nearly uniform rock faces. Their shallow ledges and grainy grey surface do little to describe separate strata or changes in weathering. F6 also exposes a very straight diagonal grass-to-rock boundary at the top. Palworld 04 makes its cliff mass legible through substantial ledges, crevices, varied lit planes, and vegetation occupying particular shelves. The existing cliffs need stronger intermediate-scale forms and grounded transitions; scattering tiny bushes across the same huge face will not supply those forms.

4. **Distance becomes a grey cutout instead of a layered destination.** In **F5**, the main distant mass and floating silhouette have little readable surface information. In **F11 / `11-aerie-ground-connection.png`**, the enormous central pillar is a muted block behind the trees. In the extra **`10-real-fly-silhouette.png`** frame, the pillar and lower rock columns are flattened so heavily that the scene largely resolves to grey silhouettes around the player. Palworld 04 separates near, middle, and far terrain while retaining useful forms on the destination. Adjust the relationship among haze, terrain values, and landmark lighting so the flight target remains an inviting readable place. The pictures alone do not establish the technical cause.

5. **The final arena reads as an empty paved enclosure.** **F10** spends most of its ground area on repeated paving with very little variation; small crates, banners, and apparatus sit far out on the boundary. There is no strong visual center linking the opponent, approach, and dangerous machinery. This is not evidence about combat effects, because no fight is shown. It is evidence that the arena's resting composition does not build anticipation. Use existing perimeter structures, lighting, banners, floor wear, and a clearer backdrop to focus the confrontation while preserving its playable center.

6. **Several asset surfaces disagree about stylization.** In **F1 / `01-arrival-first-reveal.png`** and **F11**, trees have thick sculpted trunks and chunky, solid-looking leaf clusters. In **F7 / `06-summit-final-approach.png`**, the pale blue, sharply faceted boulder looks materially unrelated to both the granular background cliffs and the densely patterned fortress masonry. **F8 / `07-fly-only-destination.png`** contrasts very large smooth-outlined stones on the steps with much smaller busy masonry above. Palworld 04 has stylization and variety without making neighboring stone forms look like different material systems. Align rock colour, roughness, shape language, and texture scale; give the trees more convincing canopy depth and fewer uniformly solid leaf clumps. These are visible asset/material problems, not merely placement problems.

7. **Settlement details look placed beside buildings rather than generated by their use.** In **F12**, the foreground shrubs and stones form separated decorative islands, while the small workbench, basket, and lumber to the right have little worn working area connecting them to the house. In **F3**, barrels and crates punctuate an otherwise vacant square. The key-art settlement and Palworld 05 convey activity through denser, related arrangements. Group the available objects around doorways and work areas, connect them with appropriate ground wear, and vary their spacing and orientation.

8. **The aerial landmark effects lack a clear silhouette in stills.** In **F11**, the wind marks read as a loose bundle of thin white crossing lines on the grey pillar. In **F2 / `02-broken-causeways.png`**, similar fine marks run into the sky at the right edge. The lines do not clearly describe a volume or direct the eye toward a destination at sheet size. Give the effect a more intentional overall contour and value grouping. Animation quality cannot be inferred from these stills.

The sunlight and cast shadows ground the trainer and trees convincingly. The survey keeps a consistent blue-sky, green-ground, warm-wood palette. Oxblood is visibly confined to the fortress/arena danger presentation. These are strengths to preserve. There is no conspicuous relative-scale error among the trainer and environment objects in these views. No UI is visible in the sheet; the extra flight frame's panels fit within its edges and its main objective is readable. The minimap's nearly uniform field conveys little terrain in that particular view, but a still cannot establish its behavior elsewhere.

## Three largest reference gaps, ranked

1. **Ground integration and inhabited density — F3, F9, F10, F12.** Palworld 02/05 and the key-art settlement connect terrain, paths, structures, vegetation, and activity into a place. These frames retain large exposed floor textures, disconnected props, and hard road-strip boundaries. Scene dressing, path blending, ground variation, and composition can address much of this gap with the visible asset set.

2. **Cliff form and atmospheric depth — F4, F5, F6, F11, extra flight frame.** Palworld 04 keeps substantial, irregular rock shelves and layered destinations readable. These cliffs are either broad granular slabs or grey silhouettes, so their enormous size magnifies their lack of internal form. Scene lighting, haze, terrain arrangement, and vegetation placement can help; adequate cliff forms and material treatment also require art work beyond scatter changes.

3. **Consistency of asset finish — F1, F7, F8, F11.** The references maintain a coherent relationship among canopy mass, stone form, surface detail, and lighting. Here solid leaf clumps, sculpted trunks, faceted blue rocks, grainy cliff walls, and differently scaled masonry coexist without that agreement. Materials and texture scale can be aligned in the scene, but canopy and cliff form deficiencies require changes to the shown assets or use of better matching existing art. A blind image review cannot establish whether suitable unused art is installed; this is not a finding that new meshes must be purchased or generated.

## Bar questions

**A. Do these frames read as belonging to the world in `tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`? Yes.** The natural green/blue/ochre palette, warm timber villages, stone stronghold with danger banners, recognizable adventurer, and magical cyan landmark establish the board's broad world language. This is a palette, silhouette, and mood judgment rather than a claim of equal detail or completed presentation. The empty staging weakens the welcoming, inhabited quality substantially.

**B. Shown these frames beside `docs/reference/palworld-0*.jpg`, would someone say these are trying to be the same kind of game? No.** Against the skill's explicit shipping-game visual bar, the inhabited scene finish and consistent stylized realism visible in those references are not present across this survey. The widespread lawn-like clearings, exposed path boundaries, weak cliff treatment, sparse arena staging, and inconsistent material styles sink it. The first group is addressable through scene work; the canopy/cliff shape and surface-cohesion issues also need art/material work on the shown assets. Creature art is not a reason for this verdict.
