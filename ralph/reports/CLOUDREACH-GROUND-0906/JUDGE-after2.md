# Cloudreach Cliffs — blind visual judge, after the fix round (round 2 of 2)

Frames: `_sheet_after2.png` (same twelve stands and capture tool as round 1). Judge given the sheet, the frames and `docs/reference/` only.

Blind review. Material: `_sheet.png` plus the twelve full frames, judged against
`docs/reference/tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png` (palette, mood, landmark
language) and `docs/reference/palworld-01..05.jpg` (the real-time bar). Ruler in
frame: the trainer is 1.80 m. Nothing is scored.

Read at sheet size first: twelve frames of a saturated lime-green table-top with
brown striped lumps on it under a clean cyan sky. The trainer reads in every
frame (good — dark trousers and a tan pack on green carry at 30%). Cottages,
the bridge, the iron dome and the stone arch are identifiable at thumbnail size.
What does not survive the thumbnail is any sense that this is a high place:
no frame has a drop, a valley floor, a cloud layer or haze under the player. At
sheet size these could be twelve stands on a golf course with sandstone garden
rocks.

## 1. Defects, ordered by how much they hurt the "finished, lush, vertical
cliff-and-sky world" reading

1. **There is no vertical in this cliff region.** Every horizon is a flat
   table edge: `02-broken-causeways` (the bridge crosses to a mesa but the gap
   below it is a featureless grey-blue band, no valley floor, no cloud, no
   depth), `04-high-roost-before-fly` (a razor-straight terrain edge with sky
   under it, nothing beyond), `08-upper-cliffhold-east-arrival` (the world ends
   at a hard grey haze line behind the cottages, reading as a sea or a void).
   Nothing looks down. Palworld-04 sells a plateau with a distant spire and
   layered haze; the key art sells height with stacked hills, a mountain and
   massed cumulus. None of that language is present.

2. **The ground is a flat lime plane beyond the camera's feet.** Grass blades
   exist only in a foreground ring (`01`, `02-broken-causeways`, `07`) and then
   stop dead; from three metres out the terrain is a single evenly lit green
   texture with no cover, no flowers, no wear, no variation. Worst in
   `02-lower-cliffs-galefoot` (the entire village square is bare green felt),
   `04` (patches of grass sit on the felt like dropped hay), `05`, `08`, `11`,
   `12`. Palworld-02/03 carry grass and flower cover to the horizon; this is
   the single biggest density gap.

3. **Faceted low-poly terrain with hard-edged brown wedges and green planes
   stamped on slopes.** `06-summit-final-approach`: the whole lower half of the
   frame is dark brown paper-fold facets alternating with green triangles and a
   thin drawn path line — it reads as an unfinished heightmap, not a summit
   approach. `03-windscar-ravine` left third: brown polygon slabs with a
   green stripe along each ridge (texture-projection seam). `08` left third:
   the same brown wedges. `01` and `05`: a perfectly flat, edge-lit green
   triangle glued to the side of a rock dome like a piece of astroturf; in `05`
   a tuft of grass floats in the air above the ridge line of that wedge.

4. **The cliff material is striped desert badlands, not lush cliffs.** Every
   cliff in `01`, `02-broken-causeways`, `02-lower`, `03`, `05`, `12` is a
   bulbous tan/brown hoodoo with horizontal candy-stripe bands. No ledge holds
   grass, no crack holds a bush, no face has moss, no top has a tree line. The
   shapes read as melted candle or layered cake. In `05` the main mass is a
   single dark-chocolate lump with almost no surface detail, and the summit
   watchtower sits on it like a chess piece. The key art's rock is grey-green,
   vegetated and integrated into the hills; Palworld-02/04 cliffs carry
   grass on every shelf.

5. **A detached chunk of cliff hangs in the sky with pebbles falling from it**
   (`04`, top-centre-left; `11-aerie-ground-connection`, top-left). At full
   size it is a striped stump with loose rocks below it. If it is meant to be a
   floating aerie it has no underside, no roots, no mist, no anchor — it reads
   as a mesh that lost its parent and is a bug to any player who sees it.

6. **Rocks are in three unrelated materials, none matching the cliffs.**
   Pale translucent green cubes that read as jade or ice (`03` right,
   `05` bottom-right pile, `06` left, `08` meadow, `12` foreground); mid-grey
   round boulders (`01`, `02-lower`); near-black matte blobs with no highlight
   that read as bin bags or tents (`11`, two of them either side of the
   clearing). Beside tan striped sandstone none of these belongs.

7. **The summit dome does not read as a finished hero object**
   (`06-summit-final-approach`). It is a rusted-iron ribbed cage, open lattice,
   spanning a mossy stone-block curtain wall, with an eye emblem at the crown
   and a white crystal spire on top. What it looks like: an unbuilt
   planetarium frame or a giant birdcage on a ruined fort. There is nothing
   inside it, no machinery, no cables, no glow, no ground treatment — the
   trainer stands under a lattice on the same felt ground. It has silhouette
   (good at sheet size) but no interior and no story. To its right a bare
   lumber stack and a plank shed sit half over the edge of the plateau.

8. **The final arena is an empty stone car park** (`09-final-arena-space`).
   Sixty percent of the frame is one flat, tiling cobble texture; the walls are
   a stone-block kit with crenellations; the props are two wooden scaffold
   towers that read as oil derricks and a blue boiler with pipes that reads as
   a steam locomotive body; one NPC stands alone in the middle. The banners
   are a pinkish crimson, not the board's oxblood — they read as festival
   bunting rather than a threat. Nothing says a fight happens here.
   Palworld-01 and -03 make a fight look like an event; this is a stage before
   the set is loaded.

9. **The cottage in `12-cliffhold-ground-connection` looks unfinished.** Its
   whole front is an open timber lattice over a doorway (a shopfront without a
   shop), a bare plaster panel next to it, and a single thin diagonal pole
   running from the grass to the roof ridge like a guy-wire. The right edge of
   the frame is a plain stone-block tower wall cut off by the frame. In
   `08` the same tower type reads as a square stone chimney with a wooden lid
   and one flat yellow-green rectangle for a banner.

10. **Creatures are smaller than the trainer and mismatched with each other**
    (`11-aerie-ground-connection`). The white fox-bat (nearer the camera than
    the trainer) stands about 1.0–1.1 m; the white spotted lynx beside the
    totem stands maybe 1 m at the shoulder. Both are white and both sit at
    the same value on green, so they cancel each other out. The fox-bat is
    stylised and expressive; the lynx is a realistically proportioned cat with
    a realistic spot texture — they look sourced from two different games.
    This is the only frame with creatures in a creature game.

11. **The giant striped trunk-totem in `11` reads as a tree stump wrapped in
    tape.** Two bare wooden posts, white string criss-crossed over a striped
    cylinder, no purpose visible. Whether it is a nest, a tether pylon or a
    tree, it is not saying which.

12. **Sky and light say "noon on a clear day", not "Cloudreach".** Every frame
    has the same flat cyan gradient with thin wisps; there is no cumulus mass,
    no cloud below eye level, no warmth in the light. `04` is 60% of that sky
    with a moon and nothing else in it. The key art's sky is half cloud; the
    palette board's greens are deeper and cooler than the lime here.

13. **No aerial perspective.** Far cliffs in `02-broken-causeways` (right) and
    `03` are as saturated and contrasty as near ones; distant trees are pin
    dots (`02`, `03`, `08`). Where haze exists it is only a horizontal grey
    band at the horizon (`02`, `08`), which reads as water or nothing rather
    than as distance.

14. **Grass blade scale is inconsistent between stands.** `07-fly-only-
    destination`: individual blades are two-metre wire spikes over the
    trainer's head. `02-broken-causeways`: yellow-green spikes. `01`: dense
    knee-high blades. `04`/`11`: sparse tufts. Same plant, four sizes.

15. **Small artefacts.** `01`: a pale blue-white slab between the gatehouse
    crenels reads as sky showing through a missing roof piece. `02-broken-
    causeways`: a scatter of dark dashes across the sky above the far pillar
    (flying creatures or debris — at full size they are flat unlit sprites).
    `05`: a lumber pile sitting on the plateau edge at frame right, and the
    floating grass tuft above the wedge. `08`: pale green rocks at the plateau
    edge appear to sit on the terrain crease.

16. **Lighting placement is mostly fine but flat on terrain.** Cast shadows do
    ground the trainer, trees and cottages (`04`, `11` long shadows; `02-lower`
    cottage shadows). Terrain itself has no form: the green plane is one value
    everywhere. Cliff faces get some directional shading but no AO in the
    creases.

## 3. Verdict

### The three things that most separate these frames from the references

1. **Ground cover and terrain form** (`02-lower-cliffs-galefoot`, `04`, `05`,
   `06`, `08`, `11`). Palworld-02 and -03 carry grass, flowers and scrub to the
   horizon with dirt worn where feet go, and their cliffs have grass on every
   shelf; the key art's ground is a mix of meadow, path, rock and stream. Here
   the ground is a flat lime plane past the first three metres, terrain is
   faceted brown wedges where it is not flat, and the cliffs are bare striped
   lumps. This is the structural gap: a world that looks generated rather than
   grown.

2. **No vertical, no atmosphere** (`02-broken-causeways`, `04`, `08`, `05`).
   A region named for cliffs and clouds shows no drop, no valley below, no
   cloud layer beneath the player, no aerial haze, and a clear noon sky with
   thin wisps. Palworld-04 and the key art both read height and distance through
   layered silhouettes and haze; these frames read as one flat plateau seen
   from ground level.

3. **Hero structures and creatures not carrying the game** (`06`, `09`, `11`,
   `12`). The summit dome is an empty iron cage, the arena is a bare cobble
   lot with pink banners, the front-line cottage is an open lattice with a
   pole on it, and the one frame with creatures shows two small white sourced-
   looking animals in two different styles, both shorter than the trainer.
   Palworld-01 puts a huge, characterful creature in the centre of a fight
   and the frame is about it. Nothing here is about a creature.

### Bar question A — do these frames belong to the world in `tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`?

**No, narrowly.** What carries it: the village stands (`02-lower-cliffs-
galefoot`, `12`, `08`) share the board's settlement language — stone-and-
timber cottages with steep roofs, a dirt path, purple wildflowers, a cartoon
oak (`01`, `03`, `11`) that is a decent stylised match for the board's trees;
the trainer's proportions and outfit fit the board's DAY panel; oxblood is
mostly kept off friendly elements. What sinks it: the ground is one lime
value instead of the board's deep, varied greens; the rock is desert-canyon
stripe instead of the board's grey-green vegetated stone; the sky is empty
cyan where the board is half cumulus; the light is hard noon where the board
is warm; and the terrain's exposed facets and wedges (`03`, `06`, `08`) make
it read as a builder's prototype rather than a painted, lived-in meadow.

### Bar question B — beside `palworld-0*.jpg`, would someone say these are trying to be the same kind of game?

**No.** What carries anything: the trainer is a readable stylised character
with a backpack and a third-person camera; cottages and a bridge say "open
world with settlements"; the arch-and-egg in `07` and the dome in `06` are
landmark-shaped. What sinks it: Palworld's frames are dense to the horizon,
hazed with distance, and centred on large expressive creatures in motion;
these frames are empty past the foreground, have no depth cue, and show
creatures once, small, white-on-white, and stylistically split. The arena
(`09`) does not look like a place a fight happens. Someone shown the two sets
would say one is a shipped creature game and the other is a level blockout
with a village kit dropped in.

### What is fixable by scene work vs what needs art not in the build

**Scene work (density, palette, lighting, composition, scatter):**
- Extend grass/flower/scrub cover from the camera ring to the horizon; add
  worn dirt where paths and doorways are (`02-lower`, `04`, `05`, `08`, `11`, `12`).
- Smooth or sculpt the faceted terrain in `03`, `06`, `08`; remove the flat
  green wedges in `01`/`05` or dress them with cover and rock so they read as
  slopes.
- Give the region a vertical: put a drop, a valley floor, a cloud/fog layer
  and distant lower terrain behind every plateau edge (`02-broken-causeways`,
  `04`, `08`); add aerial perspective to the far cliffs (`02`, `03`).
- Sky: massed cumulus and a warmer sun angle; the moon-and-wisps sky in `04`
  makes the region's name a lie.
- Pick one rock material that agrees with the cliff material and use it for
  every boulder; retire the jade cubes and the black blobs (`03`, `05`, `06`,
  `08`, `11`, `12`).
- Resolve the floating cliff chunk in `04`/`11`: either anchor it (root mass,
  underside, mist, tether) or remove it. Same for the floating grass tuft and
  lumber pile in `05`.
- Unify grass blade scale across stands (`07` spikes vs `01` blades).
- Arena (`09`): fill it — banners in true oxblood, cages, fire, machinery,
  creatures, an arena floor treatment; break the cobble plane.
- Dome (`06`): interior, ground treatment, glow, cables — dress the object
  that is there; fix the foreground terrain under it.
- Scale creatures in `11` up so they stand above the trainer; separate their
  values from each other.
- Close or dress the open lattice cottage in `12`; remove or purpose the pole.
- Fix the sky-bleed slab on the gatehouse in `01`.

**Needs art not in the build:**
- A cliff material and ledge geometry that reads as lush vertical rock with
  vegetated shelves; the striped hoodoo material cannot be re-lit into that
  (`01`, `02`, `03`, `05`, `12`).
- A finished summit hero object with an interior and a purpose; the current
  dome is a lattice shell (`06`).
- Creature art that agrees in style: the lynx in `11` is a realistically
  textured cat beside a stylised fox-bat; one of them has to change to match
  the other and the trainer.
- A banner/heraldry texture set for towers and the arena; the flat yellow
  rectangle in `08` and the pink flags in `09` are placeholders.
- Cumulus sky art if the procedural sky cannot produce massed cloud.
