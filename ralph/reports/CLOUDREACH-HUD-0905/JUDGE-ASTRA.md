# Cloudreach HUD — blind visual review

Reviewed the supplied contact sheet, all four original 1280×800 frames, the Meadows key art, and all five Palworld reference images. No source, configuration, history, previous review, implementation, or performance evidence was inspected. This is a still-image assessment. Creature art is excluded as a blocking criterion by the owner. Palworld is used for world presentation, not UI design.

## HUD verdict

**No blocking visible defect in the UI itself is demonstrated by these four frames.** Text stays inside its panels; panels stay inside the image; the five-member roster, reward receipt, and relay instructions can all be read at original resolution. The controlled foreground creature remains visible between the panels. This does not establish behavior, timing, controller operation, or readability in motion. Final owner acceptance remains external.

### Frame-specific findings

- **F1 — `captain-crosswind-live.png`:** The top enemy label, bottom-left creature status, and bottom-right actions form a clear hierarchy with an open center. Both human figures remain visible. The enemy panel starts about 21 pixels below the top; bottom panels have approximately 38 pixels of clearance. Nothing is cropped. The bottom-left Energy track is nearly black against a dark translucent panel, making its extent difficult to read at a glance. Make the track boundary more distinct without making an empty value look filled. The small level and Energy labels are readable at native size but disappear before the larger combat labels when viewing the sheet small. The foreground creature occupies much of the center/lower view; that is camera/world composition, not HUD obstruction.
- **F2 — `relay-crown-live.png`:** All five names, bond values, levels, and bars fit. The selected Galecrest row is identifiable from both its outline and left accent. The receipt communicates victory, 150 Coin, 1 Rare Candy, and each creature's progression without clipping. Its smaller stat lines need deliberate reading; twelve tightly stacked body lines make it a receipt to consult rather than information that can all be absorbed at a glance. The crown prompt is high contrast and has roughly 100 pixels of bottom clearance. The upper portion of the bright relay head falls behind the receipt, weakening the visible outline of the prompted object. Enough of the head, shaft, and joint remains exposed to locate it in this still.
- **F3 — `relay-east-live.png`:** The five-row roster overlays most of the bright relay head and portions of the apparatus on the left. The bright geometry also shows through behind the roster's middle rows, so the backdrop varies more than it does in F2. Names remain readable, and the creature and bottom-center east prompt are unobstructed. This is the clearest composition weakness: the panel occupies the same screen area as the prompted world object. - **F4 — `relay-west-live.png`:** The creature and west prompt remain unobstructed. Together they substantially constrain the world view, although neither overlaps the foreground creature or the instruction. The stat receipt could give more space back to the scene by grouping the repeated stat gains or moving detail to a secondary view; no disappearance timing can be inferred from these images.

On the contact sheet, the teal victory title, five-row team structure, selected row, creature silhouette, and relay prompt remain identifiable. Receipt details stop being readable first. No screenshot-edge clipping, panel-to-panel collision, or prompt truncation is visible. This establishes the supplied image margins, not a hardware overscan or handheld motion test.

## Environment findings — separate from HUD acceptance

- **All four frames:** A large, uninterrupted cobblestone surface and broad ochre bands dominate the view. The repeated ground treatment, sparse perimeter props, and near absence of planted edges make the space read as a bare arena. The key art's stronghold gains its identity from a substantial gate structure, overgrown margins, and a surrounding landscape; that richness is not present in these compositions. This is not a request to fill the fighting floor with obstacles.
- **F1/F2:** Large pale cliff masses terminate much of the view, with tiny isolated trees above them. There is little layered landscape behind the wall. Shadows give the cliffs some form, but the overall beige/olive palette and muted sky provide less depth and color separation than the references.
- **F2/F3/F4:** The relay's flat pale head and simple cream pipes/red collar sit beside a much more weathered, detailed machine base. The abrupt difference in surface treatment is conspicuous, especially the large white head in F2. It reads as inconsistent prop finish. The frames alone cannot establish whether suitable replacement materials or assets already exist.
- **F1:** The luminous circular boundary establishes a combat space, but this captured instant contains little visible action emphasis beyond that boundary. Palworld's fight stills show strong directional effects and contact focus. This is a finding about the selected instant, not evidence that effects or attacks are absent from the game.

## Three largest reference gaps, ranked

1. **Ground and perimeter composition — F1 through F4.** Palworld's open-field, meadow, and base shots break ground into paths, planted areas, shadow masses, and object clusters. The key art similarly frames its stronghold with vegetation and architecture. These frames repeat broad paved bands across most of the image with only a thin prop line around the edge. Scene dressing, ground variation, and perimeter composition can address this while retaining a clear arena floor.
2. **Depth and landmark hierarchy — especially F1/F2.** The key art's stronghold and Palworld's plateau provide substantial focal architecture against layered terrain. These views present short walls, repeated scaffold silhouettes, and a close pale cliff backdrop, with little landscape progression beyond them. Reframing, lighting, backdrop layering, and better grouping of existing structures are scene-level opportunities; a still cannot determine whether the build contains enough architecture to close the entire gap.
3. **Consistent prop finish — F2/F3/F4.** Palworld's base objects and the key art's structures have coherent surface and silhouette language within their respective images. Here a flat white polygonal head and smooth pipes contrast sharply with a heavily detailed machine base. Materials, lighting, and proportion/composition may reduce the discrepancy; if the required authored prop treatment is unavailable, this remainder needs asset work. This is not a creature-art rejection.

## Reference questions

**A. Do these frames read as belonging to the world in `tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`? No.** The natural stone, timber scaffolds, oxblood banners, and blue creature provide some shared language, but the bare tan arena, pale cliff backdrop, and inconsistent relay finish dominate. The board's verdant setting, strong landmark composition, and inviting landscape depth are missing from these particular views. Ground/perimeter treatment, palette, lighting, and framing are scene-fixable gaps. A more coherent relay finish and stronger architecture may require art work if suitable resources are not already installed; their availability is outside this blind review.

**B. Shown beside `palworld-0*.jpg`, would someone say these are trying to be the same kind of game? Yes.** A third-person human/creature scene, readable foreground companion, outdoor combat enclosure, and creature-focused action framing communicate the same broad game family without using UI styling as the comparison. This answer does not claim matching presentation quality: the environment density, depth, prop consistency, and captured combat emphasis remain visibly behind the references. No creature-art concern is used to block this assessment.

**Final scope conclusion:** The supplied HUD is readable and has no demonstrated blocking visible defect. Panel overlap with relay geometry and the dense reward receipt are concrete composition improvements. The environment/reference shortcomings are separate findings and do not turn this HUD review into an approval of the whole location. Final owner acceptance remains external.
