# Cloudreach Cliffs — visual judge, round 6

Reviewed blind against `docs/reference/tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png` and the five
`palworld-0*.jpg` bar shots. Trainer taken as the 1.80 m ruler.

## Specific defects, ordered by how much they hurt the region reading as a finished, lush, vertical cliff-and-sky world

1. **The region never shows a cliff.** Every one of the 12 frames stands on flat,
   contained, gently-rolling lawn. There is no frame with a drop-off, an exposed edge,
   a view down into a valley, or a gap the player is crossing. `03-windscar-ravine` has
   no ravine — it's rock chunks in front of a flat green field. `06-summit-final-approach`
   is a gate on a flat plaza with no sense of having climbed anything. `09-final-arena-space`
   is a flat cobblestone yard that could be anywhere. Compare Palworld's
   `palworld-04-plateau-landmark.jpg`, which is shot from a promontory with the ground
   falling away toward a valley and a distant tower visible below the ridge — that single
   frame does more "vertical cliff" work than this entire sheet. This is the biggest gap
   between the brief this region is named for ("Cloudreach Cliffs") and what the frames
   show.

2. **The floating/high landmarks read as broken or missing geometry, not as built,
   tethered islands.** `04-high-roost-before-fly`'s destination silhouette is a flat,
   uniformly grey shape with no shading, no texture, and no legible structure — it looks
   like an unrendered LOD stand-in, not a floating island. `11-aerie-ground-connection`'s
   background landmark is the same flat grey slab, carrying a stray scribble of white
   texture across it, plus a scatter of bare wooden poles that read as ordinary fence
   posts rather than a rope/anchor bridge system. Neither shows rails, ropes, chain,
   planking, or an anchor point of any kind. The brief's explicit ask — that floating
   islands and bridges read as "tethered/built/safe" — is simply not addressed anywhere
   in this set; the two frames that should carry it instead read as unfinished art.

3. **Cliff rock reads as grey blob mass, not designed geology, and the cliffside
   settlements are the Meadows village with no change.** `01`, `03`, and `05` show the
   same lumpy, uniformly grey-brown rock sculpt — no strata banding, no ledges, no
   colour variation between faces, nothing that reads as climbable or weathered
   differently from any other rock. In `01` the mass behind the arch accidentally reads
   as a giant slumped stone face (brow ridge, nose, mouth) — an unintended resemblance
   that undercuts the landmark. In `05` a building is perched on top of the cliff via a
   flat green pyramid "roof cap" that has no material or structural relationship to the
   rock underneath — it reads as a toy glued onto a rock, not a cliffhold built into
   one. Meanwhile `02-lower-cliffs-galefoot`, `08-upper-cliffhold-east-arrival`, and
   `12-cliffhold-ground-connection` use the identical thatched/timber-frame cottages
   from the Meadows key art with no windswept, rope-lashed, or stone-buttressed
   treatment — a player dropped into any of these three frames would have no visual cue
   they had changed biome at all.

4. **Ground cover collapses to a flat mown lawn everywhere except two frames.** Only
   `01-arrival-first-reveal` and `02-broken-causeways` carry the tall grass-blade
   foreground scatter that reads as "wild." Every other frame — `03, 04, 05, 06, 07, 08,
   09, 11, 12` — is a uniform, short-mown green plane with nothing scattered on it
   beyond the player and one central landmark: no scree, no wind-bent grass, no rock
   rubble, no alpine ground cover distinguishing "cliffs" from "meadow." Palworld's own
   plateau shot keeps grass tufts and rock scatter under the player's feet all the way to
   the landmark's base; this set drops that density the moment the frame isn't a grass
   field with a tree in it.

5. **Empty frame area.** `06-summit-final-approach` and `09-final-arena-space` in
   particular are enormous unbroken sky and ground with a single structure dead-center
   and nothing else in frame — no secondary props, foliage, or terrain incident to hold
   the eye or suggest a lived-in place. `07-fly-only-destination`'s gem-arch is a
   well-composed single prop, but it sits on the same flat empty grass as every other
   frame, so a destination that's supposed to require flight reads instead like a
   decorative gate anyone could walk to.

6. **Horizon and depth are thin.** `01`, `02-broken-causeways`, `05`, and
   `08-upper-cliffhold-east-arrival` all end abruptly in flat blue sky or soft haze at
   the edge of the grass plateau, with no midground incident — no distant hills, peaks,
   or cloud layering — to build the "vertical cliff-and-sky" read the region name
   promises. Only `04`'s road-to-horizon composition and `09`'s background rock massing
   suggest any real distance, and `04`'s payoff (the floating island) is the broken-looking
   grey silhouette from defect #2.

7. **Scale disagreement between natural and built elements.** The cliff faces in `01`,
   `03`, and `05` stand only about 3–4× the trainer's 1.80 m height — too low to read as
   the defining landmark of a region named for its cliffs. The stone gate in
   `06-summit-final-approach`, by contrast, towers correctly at roughly 6–7× trainer
   height. The inconsistency itself is the tell: built architecture is scaled to feel
   monumental, but the natural geology the region is named for is not.

## What a player would notice first, by stand

- **01-arrival-first-reveal** — the rock behind the arch accidentally reading as a
  giant sleeping face before it reads as a landmark.
- **02-broken-causeways** — the bell-gate causeway just sits on the hilltop; nothing in
  frame shows what it crosses or why it's "broken."
- **02-lower-cliffs-galefoot** — this is visually identical to a Meadows village; no
  cliff or windswept cue anywhere.
- **03-windscar-ravine** — there is no ravine, just rock chunks in front of flat lawn.
- **04-high-roost-before-fly** — the floating shape in the sky looks like a rendering
  glitch, not a floating island.
- **05-upper-cloudreach-cliffhold** — the little pyramid-roofed structure balanced on
  the rock reads as glued-on, not built-in.
- **06-summit-final-approach** — the gate stands on flat grass; nothing signals
  "summit."
- **07-fly-only-destination** — a nice prop (the gem arch) stranded on ordinary lawn,
  not read as a place that requires flight to reach.
- **08-upper-cliffhold-east-arrival** — another unmodified Meadows-style village on a
  flat green field.
- **09-final-arena-space** — a very large empty cobblestone yard; the Team Tether tech
  props (train-shaped object, pylon) look small and undetailed against the walls
  around them.
- **11-aerie-ground-connection** — the flat grey slab with the scribble texture behind
  the creatures looks broken/unfinished and pulls focus from the two creatures with the
  player, which are otherwise the best-composed shot in the set.
- **12-cliffhold-ground-connection** — same read as 02/08: an ordinary Meadows cottage,
  no visible ground-to-cliff transition.

## Verdict

### The three things that most separate these frames from the references, ranked

1. **No verticality or exposure anywhere in the set.** `palworld-04-plateau-landmark.jpg`
   is shot from a promontory with the ground falling away and a distant tower visible
   below the ridge in a single frame. None of the 12 Cloudreach frames show a cliff
   edge, a drop, or a view down — a region named for its cliffs never shows one
   (worst in `03-windscar-ravine`, `06-summit-final-approach`, `09-final-arena-space`).
2. **The floating/high landmarks read as unrendered grey geometry, not as tethered,
   built islands.** The key art and the brief both call for rope/rail/anchor language
   on anything floating; `04-high-roost-before-fly` and `11-aerie-ground-connection`
   instead show a flat, textureless grey slab with no supporting structure at all.
3. **Rock geology is undifferentiated blob mass and the cliffside settlements are the
   unmodified Meadows village.** `01`, `03`, `05` show one grey-brown rock sculpt with no
   strata or ledge variety; `02-lower-cliffs-galefoot`, `08-upper-cliffhold-east-arrival`,
   `12-cliffhold-ground-connection` reuse Meadows cottages verbatim, so neither terrain
   nor architecture visually distinguishes this region from the biome below it.

### The two bar questions

**A. Do these frames read as belonging to the world in `tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`?**
**No.** The palette (greens, sky blue, warm wood) and the magic-object framing of
`07-fly-only-destination` are consistent with the board, and the flower/grass frames
(`01`, `02-broken-causeways`) evoke its wildflower-meadow language. But the key art's
vocabulary for elevated or mysterious places — a standing stone at sunset overlooking a
mountain silhouette, real drama of scale and distance — is entirely absent. Nothing in
this set carries the elevation or geological character the board promises for anything
above the Meadows floor.

**B. Shown beside `palworld-0*.jpg`, would someone say these are trying to be the same
kind of game?** **No, for this batch specifically.** `palworld-04-plateau-landmark.jpg`
demonstrates real elevation and exposure in one frame that this cliffs region never
matches anywhere in twelve. Ground-cover density in most of these frames (flat mown
lawn) is well below every Palworld reference, which never shows a bare green plane at
this scale. The creature pair in `11` and the gate/village compositions are close
enough in silhouette language to pass on their own, but they can't carry the set.

### What's fixable by changing the scene vs. what needs art that isn't in the build

**Fixable by scene work** (terrain placement, camera framing, scatter, materials —
no new asset production):
- Stage at least some of these beats where an actual drop or valley view is visible
  below camera — pick vantage points on the existing terrain that expose elevation
  instead of standing on the flattest part of each plateau.
- Scatter ground cover (rock rubble, scree, wind-bent grass, moss patches) across the
  mown-lawn frames instead of leaving bare green plane; the tall-grass treatment already
  used in `01`/`02-broken-causeways` shows the asset exists and just isn't placed
  elsewhere.
- Recolour/re-material the cliffside cottages (roof, timber, stone tint) so `galefoot`,
  `upper-cliffhold`, and the ground-connection frames read as a different settlement
  from the Meadows starting village, per the project's own material-differentiation
  rule.
- Dress the causeway and floating-landmark approach in `02-broken-causeways` and
  `07-fly-only-destination` with rope, plank, and visible anchor-point detail so they
  read as built and safe rather than a prop standing alone on grass.

**Not fixable without new art or terrain authoring:**
- The grey, textureless floating-island silhouettes in `04` and `11` are not a lighting
  or scatter problem — from the frame, they read as missing or placeholder geometry.
  Making them read as real islands needs actual shaded, textured landmark geometry, not
  a camera or dressing change.
- A true cliff edge or ravine drop requires the terrain mesh itself to have vertical
  relief where the player stands; every current vantage point sits on a flat plateau, so
  this needs terrain authoring, not just restaging the camera on what already exists.
