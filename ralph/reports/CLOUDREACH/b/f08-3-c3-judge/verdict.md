# F08#3 High Perches — code-blind visual verdict (c3 + c2 southeast)

Judged only the 8 named frames against the Meadows key art, Cloudreach Sky Aviary stronghold board, Cloudreach creature-roster board and the Palworld references. No source, JSON or reports were read.

## 1. Bar A (identity vs key art and aviary board): FAIL
- The aerie is five or six plain cylindrical stone columns with flat plank caps, iron-band rings and a few timber braces (c3 `glide-approach-day`, `court-high-oblique-day`, c2 `southeast-glide-approach-day`). They read as factory smokestacks or bare ruined columns. None of the board's identity carriers are present: no glass/gold dome, no pale cut-stone towers with slate cone roofs, no blue/white banners, no arches or bridges, no waterfalls pouring off the rock, no trees on ledges. One small cream pennant and one tiny blue panel are the only heraldry, and neither is readable. The c2 stone gatehouse arch is the only piece that uses board vocabulary.
- The perch rock is a smooth bulbous stack of blobs with a uniform noise texture (c3 `glide-approach-day`, c2 `southeast-glide-approach-day`). It looks like clay, not the board's fractured, stratified cliffs with vegetation.
- The deck is a flat grass disc with a hard circular lip (c3 `court-high-oblique-day`). The board's aviary is built into the rock.
- The palette is desaturated green-grey stone under a hazy grey-green sky. The board uses warm cream stone, cobalt, gold and saturated sky blue. Nothing in view says "sanctuary above the clouds".
- Credit: the hawk carrier (c3 `rig-fly-departure-day`) is a detailed, well-textured creature. It fits the roster board's painterly, soft-feathered tone better than anything else in these frames.

## 2. Bar B (genre/finish vs Palworld at gameplay distance): FAIL
- The clouds are faceted low-poly slabs with hard polygon silhouettes (c3 `court-high-oblique-day` right half, `rig-fly-departure-day` lower right). Stacked-disc "puff" clouds with visible banding float mid-air (c3 `glide-approach-day` left).
- The cloud floor and far fill show as a flat, untextured pale plane with a hard straight rectangular edge (c3 `court-high-oblique-day/night` left third, `rig-fly-departure-day/night` behind the left mesas). At night a hard, infinite flat "sea" horizon line cuts across the frame (c3 `court-high-oblique-night`, `glide-approach-night`).
- The distant mesas are flat-shaded vertical-fluted extrusions with pastel green caps and no foliage (all frames). There is no grass density, trees, atmospheric layering or lighting richness at Palworld's level. Palworld's finish comes from dense foliage, textured terrain and soft volumetric haze; these frames have none of that.
- Night is a single global blue tint on the day scene. The pale clouds and floor plane glow almost as bright as day (c3 `court-high-oblique-night`), and only a few tiny warm deck lights add night character.
- Characters are fine: the trainer model and the hawk are at an acceptable genre level (`rig-fly-departure-day`).

## 3. Criterion read, F08#3 "correct high-perch camera": PASS (camera/framing only)
- (a) Readable height: PASS. In c3 `glide-approach-day/night` the deck sits clearly above the neighbouring mesas, and the spire drops out of the bottom of frame. c3 `court-high-oblique-day/night` shows the perch towering over a low cloud floor, with the left mesa well below. `rig-fly-departure-day` shows the mesas and grass shelf far below at the left. The weaker frame is c2 `southeast-glide-approach-day/night`. The cliff wall behind at left-centre (x≈550–700) and the right mesas top out only slightly below deck level, which partly flattens the height read. c3 fixes this.
- (b) No rim crowding: PASS. In every c3 frame the pillar tops keep clear sky margin (tallest cap about 60–170 px from the top edge) and the aerie sits centred. Hawk wingtips stay well inside frame in `rig-fly-departure-*`. One caveat, c2 only: a large dark foreground rock is cut by the left edge in `southeast-glide-approach-*`. It crowds the left third but does not touch the aerie.
- (c) Departure legible: PASS, with a caveat. Trainer and carrier are both large and readable (`rig-fly-departure-day/night`). The trainer hangs from the hawk's talons with both arms raised, the relationship is unambiguous, and the hawk is clearly several times the trainer's size. Caveat: the head-on hawk body completely hides the deck and court, and its body sits right on the deck lip. As a still, it reads about equally as "lifting off" or "perched on the rim". Some ground gap under the talons, or a slight side angle, would make "departure" unambiguous.
- (d) Day and night: PASS. All four pairs keep their silhouettes at night: pillars against sky, perch against the cloud floor, trainer and hawk readable. Night legibility is carried by the bright blue-tinted clouds, not by authored night lighting (see Bar B).

The camera criterion is met. The failures are environment-art failures, not framing failures.

## Worst 3 defects
1. **The aerie has no aviary identity.** It is bare cylindrical smokestack-like columns with plank caps, with no dome, towers, banners, waterfalls or cream/cobalt/gold palette (c3 `court-high-oblique-day`, c3 `glide-approach-day`, c2 `southeast-glide-approach-day`).
2. **The cloud floor and fill are untextured.** A flat pale plane with hard rectangular edges, faceted low-poly cloud slabs and a hard flat night horizon break the illusion of height (c3 `court-high-oblique-night` left, c3 `rig-fly-departure-day` left/lower right, c3 `glide-approach-night` horizon).
3. **The perch rock and mesas are primitive.** The spire is a smooth blob-stacked mass with uniform noise texture, the deck is a hard-edged grass disc, and the mesas are flat-shaded fluted extrusions with no foliage. This is well below Palworld finish at gameplay distance (c3 `glide-approach-day`, c3 `court-high-oblique-day`, c2 `southeast-glide-approach-day`).

Framing note, lower priority: the departure pose is a head-on hawk that occludes the deck, which makes lift-off vs perched ambiguous (`rig-fly-departure-day/night`). In c2 `southeast-glide-approach-*`, the back mesas sit near deck height and a left-edge foreground occluder crowds the frame.
