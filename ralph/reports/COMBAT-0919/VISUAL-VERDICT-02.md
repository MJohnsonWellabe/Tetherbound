# Blind visual verdict — COMBAT-0919, capture B

Reviewed `combat-captures-0919-b/motion-contact-sheet.png` and native frames 0000, 0005, 0012, 0020, 0024, 0029 against the Meadows key art and five Palworld reference images. This verdict concerns this capture on its own; it makes no before/after claim. No source inspection, game execution, or new capture. No scores.

## Art and readability

The most conspicuous art defect is the mismatch between the creature and the rest of the cast. Ripplet's near-white graphic patches, hard blue value boundaries, and enormous glossy eye read differently from the textured human and beige wildlife (0000, 0024, 0029). Its fin-and-tail silhouette is distinctive and instantly recognizable at contact-sheet size, but surface markings overpower its rounded form. Palworld's stylized creatures and trainers share a more coherent material language and clearly shaded volumes. This needs deliberate character/material art work, not more environmental detail. Asset provenance cannot be inferred from the images.

At small size Ripplet dominates while the trainer, the active opponent, and background animals are difficult to separate. In 0000 a piece of a beige creature is visible immediately beside Ripplet, with another rabbit farther up the central lane. In 0005 and 0012 gray effects hide the near-contact area. The large Bramblebun heading alone does not establish which visible body is its owner. Frame 0029 clearly exposes the fallen rabbit. Camera offset and target identification should make that participant's entire silhouette readable during the encounter.

The world has real settlement structure: timber houses, fences, a broken arch, flower groups, and wooded hills (0000, 0029). It is recognizably inhabited. Ground treatment is less authored: frequent thin grass blades sit on a strongly mottled yellow-green texture, spreading competing small edges through most of the foreground. The key art and Palworld 02/05 use calmer path/clearing surfaces beside concentrated vegetation. A quiet fighting patch and deliberate plant clusters would help the small actors and effects read.

## Rubric checks

- **Colour/value:** Strong light/dark range is visible in 0000, but the dark central foreground hides the smaller animals while brilliant white markings pull attention to Ripplet. Meadow greens and purple flowers fit the biome; the gray-blue sky and busy yellow-green ground are less inviting than the key art. No conspicuous oxblood leakage onto friendly subjects is visible.
- **Lighting:** Daylight direction and shadows place actors and props on the ground. Ripplet's graphic markings fragment its volume, especially the back in 0000 and face in 0024. This supports an integration concern, not a claim about the implementation causing it.
- **Depth:** The hillside, village, and left mountain establish distance in 0000/0029. The background has less atmospheric separation and less landmark hierarchy than the key art or Palworld 04. No clear seam can be demonstrated in this largely fixed view.
- **Interface:** The combat panels are inside safe margins and have a clear three-part layout: enemy, companion, actions (0000–0024). Native-size names and action labels can be read. Disabled icons/labels are very dark, and the several unlabeled colored bars require interpretation. In 0005, the amber incoming message and cyan weakness message compete vertically above the same target lane. In 0012, the two cyan messages repeat the opportunity cue and cover space needed to see the opponent. Consolidate that feedback. In 0029, the result text is legible and the exploration HUD is visible; the underlying scene remains readable. These are independent interface judgments; Palworld UI is not used as a design benchmark.
- **Combat event:** Frame 0005 has a gray puff over Ripplet's face; 0012 has a larger opaque radial puff; 0020 spreads translucent puffs across the creature and field. These communicate activity, but hide the actor and do not show a clear source-to-target impact relationship in the still. Palworld 01/03 keep combat direction, opponent body, and impact location more explicit. Favor directional/contact effects that leave faces and silhouettes open.
- **Artefacts:** No definite HUD collision, z-fighting, or geometry penetration is established by these sampled images. Hard white creature areas and granular vegetation are visible concerns, but their origin cannot be diagnosed from stills. Do not classify them as motion aliasing without motion evidence.
- **Scale:** Ripplet is visibly larger than the trainer and nearby fallen rabbit in 0029. That hierarchy reads. Perspective prevents a precise metre estimate; the trainer and settlement broadly agree in scale. The pictures do not justify prescribing a species size. Its screen occupation does, however, conceal the much smaller opponent in 0000–0012.

1. **Coherent creature and trainer presentation — 0000, 0024, 0029.** Palworld combines expressive bespoke silhouettes with a shared surface/volume language. Ripplet's bright graphic areas and eye style diverge from the textured trainer and beige creatures. Lighting/material tuning is potentially scene-fixable, but a consistent character-art pass may require reworked textures/models. The images cannot show whether alternative suitable art is already available.
2. **Fight participants and contact remain obscured — 0005, 0012, 0020.** Palworld 01/03 make the opponent, direction, and point of impact the event. Here the foreground creature and puffs conceal the close target, while stacked text explains the event. Camera composition, target marking, effect placement, and feedback hierarchy are presentation work; expressive attack/contact poses and distinct effects may need authored art. Still images cannot establish the inventory or quality of available animation.
3. **Ground hierarchy and atmospheric composition — 0000, 0029.** The key art and Palworld 02/04/05 balance clearings, denser vegetation groups, and layered distance. Here granular grass/terrain fills the fight area and a muted sky weakens the pastoral invitation. This is primarily scene work: quieter ground values/textures, intentional plant grouping, combat clearing placement, and atmospheric/palette balance.

## Bar questions

**A. Do these frames read as belonging to the Meadows key-art world? Yes.** Rolling meadow, purple flowers, timber settlement, rustic structures, and wooded slopes establish that setting. The answer concerns belonging, not equivalent finish. Ground/depth issues are largely scene-fixable; character coherence needs a deliberate art/material pass.

**B. Shown beside Palworld, would someone say these are trying to be the same kind of game? Yes.** The trainer, prominent fantasy companion, varied wildlife, pastoral settlement, and creature encounter make the intended genre apparent. This does not meet Palworld's visual finish: the character ensemble and combat event readability remain substantial gaps. Camera, HUD, effects placement, and terrain hierarchy are scene/presentation work; coherent character surfaces and expressive combat art may need to be made or reworked. A blind image review cannot determine whether that art already exists elsewhere in the build.
