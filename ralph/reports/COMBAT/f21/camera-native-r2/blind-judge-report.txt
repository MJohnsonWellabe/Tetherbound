F21 #4 adjudication: PASS within the supplied still matrix
All nine intended ally/target pairs have separate, readable main creature silhouettes. There is visible background between the two actors rather than one masking the other's face, torso, or limbs. Main bodies, feet, tails where visible, and giant antlers remain inside the image edges. The camera accommodates every supplied size combination without sacrificing one main creature to the edge or to the other creature.
The HUD does not materially hide either main creature in this matrix. On a second original-detail inspection, 04 remains the closest case: the target's right rear foot meets the Orbs strip vicinity with almost no spare margin. I do not see a materially missing foot silhouette that warrants failing the literal actor-framing criterion, but this is weak clearance rather than polished spacing. A cleaner layout should leave an obvious gap there. Background creatures clipped by the edges are visually distracting, but they are not the two matrix actors being assessed. The human trainer is often partly or fully hidden by the left creature; if that human is intended to count as an additional required fight actor, these images do not pass that expanded requirement.
This is a narrow visual PASS on these nine supplied static compositions, not proof that the camera maintains this result during movement, attacks, orbit, or dynamic approaches. The claimed catalog-class identity is supplied mapping metadata; I did not verify it through code or catalog files.

Full requested Palworld/Animo creature-and-world plus Valheim-lighting craft bar: UNMET
The creatures establish useful expressive features: large readable eyes, differentiated species shapes, rounded appealing boar/bear forms, stone armor, and a striking oversized antler design. Their silhouettes read clearly enough for this framing exercise. However, the surfaces often resemble blunt color and texture patches rather than a consistently finished material system. The dark stag masses lose some internal form against the shaded field, and the bright cream/green pieces make an abrupt contrast with those near-black areas. These are recognizably authored creature designs, but the overall creature finish is not at the polished reference bar.
The world provides grass, flowers, trees, terrain, rocks, and fencing, but composition and material detail are less coherent than the supplied references. Dense repeated thin grass and flower marks create widespread high-frequency visual noise. The ground reads as mottled yellow-green texture, and the steep overhead framing makes the meadow a surface rather than a layered landscape with inviting depth. The reference board offers broad scenic hierarchy, hills, distant silhouettes, water, paths, settlements, and atmosphere; the supplied combat views do not demonstrate that breadth or comparable world composition. Their absence in this one arena is a limit of the evidence, while the visible foreground noise and simplified background finish are direct craft shortcomings.
The sunlight establishes illumination direction and grounded shadows, but the broad dark patches and sharp form transitions do not show the reference board's softer atmospheric separation, nuanced foliage light, or cohesive ambient fill. Strong contrast alone does not satisfy the requested Valheim-like lighting mood. The oversized HUD, simple square portraits, background edge fragments, and frequently hidden trainer also leave the presentation looking unfinished despite the successful main-pair separation.
The full visual-craft verdict considers creature finish, world composition/materials, lighting, readability, and UI together. It is not based only on the small 04 HUD-clearance issue, and the craft shortfall does not revoke the independently assessed literal #4 still-matrix PASS. No separate Animo or Valheim original reference was supplied in this judging packet, so exact equivalence to those titles cannot be established from this packet.
