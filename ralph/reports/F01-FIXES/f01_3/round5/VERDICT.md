**The rubric’s ranked three largest reference gaps:**

1. **Character and creature art lacks the reference’s coherent, bespoke appeal.** In **003, 025, 034 and 047**, faces and outfits read as variations of generic townsfolk; **070–072** combines highly mottled boar surfaces with an angular, almost solid-yellow fire. Palworld’s **01, 03 and 04** gives creatures clear material grouping, expressive shapes and a consistent relationship with the trainer. Lighting can improve separation, but this gap needs character, creature and prop art work.

2. **Night lighting loses local form and hierarchy.** In **039, 042, 060 and 091**, dark grass, clothing and shadowed ground merge; **065, 080 and 097** turn major gate beams almost black. Interiors **003 and 025** swing to exceptionally bright walls. The key art’s night panel preserves blue landscape layers and makes warm inhabited areas deliberate focal points. This is principally fixable through scene lighting, values and composition.

3. **The landscape feels repetitively scattered rather than authored.** **057–058 and 087–090** repeat isolated grass blades, flower stems, rocks and similarly sized trees across broad fields. **005–008** presents repeated houses and stone aprons around a largely bare dirt street. The key art clusters vegetation around clearings and landmarks; Palworld **02, 04 and 05** uses stronger terrain masses and purposeful inhabited spaces. Much of this is fixable through clustering, ground coverage, placement and terrain composition; greater foliage and prop variety may require additional art.

**A — Yes.** These frames belong recognizably to the key art’s meadow world: rustic settlement, rolling terrain, flowers, fences, blue night and warm inhabited lights carry it. The lighting and scatter gaps weaken that connection.

**B — No.** Beside the Palworld references, the inconsistent creature/character/prop treatment and repetitive environmental presentation do not sustain the same visual ambition. Scene changes can address lighting and composition; they cannot supply the missing coherence and expressive quality of the art.
