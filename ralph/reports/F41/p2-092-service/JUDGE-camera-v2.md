Verdict: WEAK. Some views improve separation, but the trio is not reliably readable together; the opening pair is better in A, while later frames still hide or shrink one of the figures. HUD panels occupy much of the lower and side frame, and the boss/scene also lose space to them (01–06).

Ranked reference gaps:
1. Readable character grouping and scale: trainer and companion are tiny beside the boss or disappear into its silhouette (01-B, 03-A, 06-A). Palworld 01/03 keep the player, companion and large creature legible as distinct actors. Camera placement can help; stronger character/companion silhouettes and detail need art work.
2. Ground and palette: the broad near-black brown ground and muted green props flatten most of the view (01–06), unlike the sunlit green/gold value range and visible terrain forms in keyart and Palworld 02/03. Lighting, palette and scene dressing can improve this.
3. Depth and event composition: sparse distant props and thin tree trunks leave a large empty field with little readable landmark or layered foliage (01, 06), unlike the groves, paths, cliffs and strong boss framing in the references. Density, scatter and camera composition can improve this.

A. Keyart world: no. The brown, sparse combat ground and low-key palette do not read as its bright meadow, groves and welcoming landscape. Lighting, palette, density and composition are scene-fixable; the trainer/companion models need authored art to approach the reference.
B. Palworld game-kind bar: no. The boss has a strong shape, but the tiny trainer/companion and sparse, dark arena lack the distinct creature-led encounter and rich, readable world shown in Palworld 01–03. Camera, lighting and environment dressing are fixable; character/companion art is not a scene adjustment.
