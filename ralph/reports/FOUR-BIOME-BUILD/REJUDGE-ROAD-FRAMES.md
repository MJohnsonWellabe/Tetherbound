# Independent blind visual verdict: frame A and frame B

Reviewed only the full visual-judge rubric, the two supplied frames, and the six reference images listed below. Both frames were inspected at their original 1280 × 720 size and at 384 × 216 (30%), using in-memory reductions. No source, change description, prior report, or asset inventory informed this judgment.

## Cast first

**Neither frame establishes a cast at the Palworld reference standard.** In frame A, the foreground trainer is identifiable and the face is visible, but the small facial features, muted cream/blue/brown clothing, and ordinary adventurer outline do little to establish a memorable protagonist. The background humanoids are readable as people, with less individual identity. In frame B, the trainer's backpack and light sleeves separate the back silhouette, but the brown hair, trousers, and pack merge with the road's colours at small size. These are specific limitations of the visible presentation; their sourcing cannot be determined from pictures.

There is no clearly identifiable foreground companion in either frame. Frame B contains several small pale upright or branching forms near the far road and trees. They are too distant to identify confidently as creatures, people, or props, so they cannot establish creature appeal, expression, or the relative size of companion and opponent. Palworld 01, 03, 04, and 05 make distinctive creatures immediate subjects through silhouette, colour, scale, and framing. These frames do not.

## Full eight-part rubric

1. **Silhouette and small-size readability.** Frame A's houses, road, trees, signboard, fence, and trainer survive reduction. The trainer's legs lose separation against the dark ground and cast shadow. The pale repeated structures left of the road remain conspicuous but their function is unclear; I cannot identify them reliably. Frame B's gate, fence, trees, rock, and trainer remain distinguishable, but the distant pale forms do not. Its road offers a clear visual route toward the centre until the gate cuts across it. That is a compositional observation, not evidence of a traversal defect. In both frames, individual flowers become granular ground noise rather than readable plant groups.

2. **Colour and value structure.** Both frames plausibly occupy the same place: olive/yellow-green grass, ochre road, warm timber, and a subdued blue-grey sky. They do have dark tree/shadow masses and bright flowers/plaster, rather than a single value. However, the mottled yellow-green terrain dominates both frames and the orange-brown road competes strongly with the trainer. Frame A's almost black-green crown at upper right and frame B's near-black crown at the right edge look markedly harsher than the more softly grouped foliage in the key art. The sky provides less blue relief than that board. No identifiable Team Tether marking appears, and I cannot establish oxblood misuse from these images.

3. **Intentionality.** Frame A's buildings, well-like structure, fencing, sign, and bench give the settlement a purposeful arrangement. Frame B's fence, gate, roadside bench, and tournament board similarly suggest use. Both frames weaken this with frequently repeated little white-flower clumps and thin grass distributed across broad areas at similar density. Frame B especially needs larger quiet ground patches and stronger plant clusters around trunks, rocks, and fence bases. Its tall trunks vary somewhat in size and spacing, but the undergrowth does not form an equally clear hierarchy.

4. **Lighting.** Both frames communicate bright directional daylight. Frame A's long trainer shadow anchors the figure, while the building and tree shadows give the ground some form. Frame B also has useful alternating lit and shaded areas around trunks and road edges. Exact clock time is not independently provable from lighting. Both frames' strongly mottled ground competes with the broader lighting shapes, and dark foliage loses interior form. Frame A's strong trainer shadow is more visually prominent than the trainer at reduced size. Neither still supports a claim that objects are generally floating.

5. **Horizon and depth.** Frame A has foreground road, midground houses, and a rear mountain wall; the mountain's busy surface competes with the settlement rather than receding softly. Frame B gives a longer view down the road, but the far rolling terrain remains close in green hue/value to the middle distance. Both would benefit from clearer atmospheric separation. Neither offers the distinctive distant destination of the key art's hall/mountains or Palworld 04's tower and stepped cliffs. No unequivocal terrain chunk seam or LOD boundary is visible. Fog is not visibly swallowing the world.

6. **Interface.** In both frames, the story panel is legible at full size and has a clear title/body hierarchy. The five large empty slots and long command strip occupy substantial lower-right space without explaining the empty slots' purpose. Their tiny key glyphs become unreadable at 30%. Frame A's strip and slots cover fence/roadside details; frame B's cover the right side of the path and verge. The top-centre time and bottom-left food text have weak contrast. Panels remain inside the frame. Frame B's cropped tournament board is world signage, not an interface safe-area violation. These findings are assessed directly, not against Palworld's UI.

7. **Artefacts.** In both frames, fine grass and leaf edges appear stippled and jagged at full resolution, particularly in frame A's dark crown beside the story panel and frame B's upper-right crown. Thin grass lines and repetitive ground mottling create a visibly noisy surface. The images establish this appearance, not its rendering cause. I see no unequivocal z-fighting, broken geometry intersection, or terrain seam. Static pictures cannot establish popping, motion aliasing, or performance.

8. **Scale agreement.** Using the rubric's 1.80 m trainer as the intended ruler, neither frame shows a definite gross scale contradiction. Frame A's houses plausibly accommodate people; frame B's gate and fence plausibly stand below adult head height, and its nearby bench reads as person-scale. These are perspective-qualified judgments, not metre measurements: the props and people occupy different depths. Frame A's background humanoids cannot be compared directly by screen height alone. Frame B's distant pale forms cannot support a species, role, or creature-size comparison. Companion-versus-practice-target scale therefore remains unproven in both frames.

## Three largest reference gaps, ranked

1. **Cast presence and identity — frames A and B.** Palworld 04 makes several radically different creature silhouettes readable together, and Palworld 05 makes even small friendly creatures immediate personalities. These frames offer a modestly differentiated trainer and no assessable foreground companion. Reframing a suitable existing companion could address absence; the visible trainer's limited distinctive shape and material language needs character-art attention. The screenshots cannot establish what suitable art exists elsewhere in the build.

2. **Ground and foliage hierarchy — frames A and B, especially B's broad verge.** Palworld 02 and 04 separate substantial grass masses, quieter paths, and shaded plant groups. The key art arranges meadow flowers into complementary patches. These frames mix repeated white sprigs, isolated thin blades, and strongly mottled ground across most exposed surfaces. Cluster planting, quieter ground colour, stronger road-edge transitions, and calmer foliage values are scene/material changes. Any need for replacement foliage assets requires inventory inspection beyond this blind review.

3. **Depth and destination — frame A's rear mountain wall and frame B's far road.** The key art's settlement and stronghold panels give the eye a destination within layered terrain; Palworld 04 uses a tower and stepped cliffs. Frame A has appealing nearby architecture but little separation from its rear hillside; frame B's route ends visually in undifferentiated distant greens and tiny ambiguous forms. Camera composition, atmospheric colour/value separation, and landmark placement can address much of this. Whether a suitable landmark asset is already available is unknown.

## Acceptance answers

**A. Belonging to the key art's world: YES for frame A; YES for frame B.** Timber/plaster settlement architecture, fenced ochre paths, wooded rolling meadow, and flowers carry the connection. Frame A communicates the settlement side more convincingly; frame B communicates its surrounding countryside. These are world-direction answers, not claims of equivalent finish. The harsh dark foliage, muddy yellow-green ground, and weak distant landmark composition remain substantial departures.

**B. Beside Palworld, trying to be the same kind of game: NO for frame A; NO for frame B.** On the evidence shown, both present a human adventurer in a rustic landscape, without the prominent, distinctive companion cast that makes the references read as a creature-centred game. The visible environment also lacks the references' disciplined vegetation masses and depth hierarchy. Companion staging and scene composition are actionable scene work. Improving the trainer's distinctive design is art work; whether missing companion quality requires new art cannot be decided when no companion is clearly shown.

**Shipping readiness: NO for either frame against the supplied visual bar.** The interface coverage, noisy vegetation/ground treatment, and unresolved cast presentation need work. Neither frame establishes combat spectacle, animation quality, traversal quality, or performance; those remain unjudged.
