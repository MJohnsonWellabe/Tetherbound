The trainer is readable in daylight, but the character and creatures do not yet carry these frames as a creature adventure. In F01/F03 the trainer has a recognizable human silhouette, blue sleeves and a backpack, but the rear view presents a fairly generic adventurer. The distant pale blue figures lack the clear bodies, faces and distinctive silhouettes visible in the Palworld references. F02/F04 lose most of the trainer’s separation below the shoulders. These views cannot establish facial quality or animation appeal, but they do establish that the creatures currently fail to become compelling visual subjects.

Reviewed F01–F04 against the Meadows key art and all five supplied Palworld screenshots. Small-size judgments below concern the shapes and value masses that survive a thumbnail reading.

1. **Silhouette and readability at small size.**
   F01/F03 retain the trainer, central grove, right-hand tree wall and sky as separate masses. Thin grass becomes stippled noise; small shrubs, flowers and distant figures stop reading as distinct things. The blue-white group right of the central grove is conspicuous, but its identity is unclear without inspection. F02/F04 reduce the trainer to blue shoulder patches above a largely lost lower body. Bushes merge into the ground, and individual trees merge into dark walls. Improve the clear ground immediately behind the trainer and provide restrained night separation for the complete body. Put at least one creature at a distance and orientation where its silhouette is unmistakable.

2. **Colour and value structure.**
   F01/F03 have strong colour, but much of the ground is ochre-yellow interrupted by harsh green foliage. The combination feels drier and more brittle than the key art’s welcoming green meadows with warm paths and distinct flower accents. The trees contain useful dark values, but the clearing has too much competing fine contrast. F02/F04 have a coherent blue night sky, yet the world below it collapses into near-black greens and browns. The bright interface becomes the principal value structure. Both pairs clearly depict the same place. No visible oxblood contamination of friendly elements is evident.

3. **Intentionality.**
   F01/F03 show a deliberate large-scale clearing between tree groups, which is a useful start. Within it, similarly sized upright plants appear repeatedly across nearly every available patch of ground. The sparse individual grass stems do not create the convincing grass carpet and shaped clearings of the Palworld meadow/path references. The central grove contains many close, similarly vertical trunks and relatively narrow crowns; it reads more as a planted stand than the spreading oak groves of the key art. Cluster flowers and shrubs, vary the understory by location, and establish a legible route or destination through the clearing. F02/F04 inherit the same scatter pattern, with darkness making its spatial organization harder to read.

4. **Lighting.**
   Day and night are immediately distinguishable. F01/F03 show directional shading and some grounding shadows, so the terrain is not uniformly flat-lit. However, the warm mottled ground competes with the actual lighting gradients, making the hill’s form less clean than it could be. F02/F04 have a bright moon and readable blue sky but insufficient separation across the trainer, ground and tree bases. The luminous spot on the left provides a useful local accent, but does not establish a readable nighttime play space. Raise or redirect the environmental fill enough to retain broad forms while keeping the night mood.

5. **Horizon and depth.**
   All four frames establish foreground, grove and a narrow distant opening. Depth then stops quickly: the tree wall and shallow horizon provide little of the key art’s inviting valley, layered hills and recognizable distant destination. F01/F03 have a pale, indistinct vertical shape left of the central grove, but it does not function as a strong landmark. F02/F04 retain sky/tree separation while losing much of the ground-depth structure. Compose a more substantial distant landmark through the opening and separate the intervening landforms by value. No unequivocal terrain chunk seam or LOD boundary is visible in these stills.

6. **Interface.**
   The principal panels stay within the frame and the story instruction is readable. In every frame, the large five-slot block above the bottom action strip occupies substantial play space while communicating little beyond small key labels. The story card, minimap, slots and action strip compete with a relatively small central character. F01/F03 also give the health/food panels less contrast than the large right-hand panels. F02/F04 make this imbalance stronger because the bright interface overwhelms the dark world. Reduce the visual weight of empty slots and reserve the strongest interface contrast for immediately useful information. The tiny clock text is subordinate, although its placement over the moon in F02/F04 is awkward.

7. **Artefacts.**
   F01/F03 contain conspicuous fine pixel breakup along grass stems and foliage edges, particularly across the midground. F02/F04 retain many isolated bright stems against near-black ground. These are visible image-quality defects, but still frames cannot establish shimmering in motion or identify their technical cause. The foreground fern intersects or obscures the trainer’s boot area in every frame, weakening the sense of clean foot contact. No definite z-fighting or terrain seam is established. The white-blue distant figures have broken-looking outlines at this viewing distance, but the images do not distinguish surface treatment, effects and aliasing well enough to diagnose a geometry bug.

8. **Scale agreement.**
   Taking the trainer as 1.80 m, the foreground fern reaches roughly lower-leg height and spans a substantial part of the trainer’s height horizontally. Many nearby upright plants reach knee-to-waist territory, making this read as scrub mixed with tall weeds rather than predominantly meadow grass. That is a visible vegetation-scale choice worth revisiting. The large trees are plausibly much taller than a person, though their heavy trunks and narrow upright crowns feel unlike the broad oak forms in the key art. The creatures are too distant and insufficiently identified to compare companion versus practice-target size reliably. These frames do not establish that role-scale relationship; I would not infer it from screen height across different depths.

**Visible differences between the pairs**

F03/F04 preserve essentially the same camera, terrain composition, vegetation distribution, trainer placement and interface as F01/F02. The pale blue figures right of the central grove change arrangement and apparent pose; smaller pale figures become more separately visible in F03/F04. The left-hand pale figure changes orientation or position in the night comparison. Clouds and displayed times also differ.

I do not see a convincing broad terrain, palette, sharpness or lighting improvement in F03/F04, nor a clear new regression in those areas. The changed creature arrangement alters a local cluster’s readability, but it does not resolve the larger character/creature presentation gap. The later pair is visually equivalent at the level of the acceptance verdict. This is not evidence about temporal stability or performance.

**The three largest gaps from the references, ranked**

1. **Character and creature presentation — F01–F04.** The Palworld screenshots put expressive, distinct creatures in readable relationships with the trainer, including large bodies and clearly differentiated roles. Here the trainer is small and rear-facing, and the creatures read as distant pale fragments. Night further erases the trainer. Scene framing and lighting can address presentation immediately; expressive silhouettes, coherent surface treatment and memorable character design require suitable art, which these distant views do not demonstrate.

2. **Ground and vegetation structure — F01/F03, also visible in F02/F04.** Palworld’s meadow/path scenes use cohesive grass coverage, exposed routes and foliage masses that support the action. The key art organizes flowers, understory and broad oak groves into inviting places. These frames substitute repeated upright plants and scattered stems over an ochre ground surface. Distribution, density, palette and clearing design are scene changes. More coherent grass/understory forms and convincing broad oak silhouettes may require new or revised assets; scatter alone cannot supply those shapes.

3. **A readable destination and nighttime world — F01–F04.** The references offer layered landforms, recognizable structures or landmarks, and readable subjects within the environment. These frames terminate largely in tree walls, while night loses the trainer and much of the terrain. Landmark placement, composition, atmospheric separation and night fill are scene-fixable. A distinctive settlement or landmark requires appropriate art if none is available; its availability cannot be determined from the supplied frames.

**A. Do these frames read as belonging to the Meadows key-art world? No.**
The rolling clearing, green groves and blue night sky point toward it, but the ochre scrub field, upright tree stands, weak distant destination and nearly black nighttime ground miss its welcoming meadow and mysterious-but-readable night. Palette, scatter, composition and lighting are scene work. Broad oak forms and stronger environmental landmark assets are art work where suitable assets are unavailable.

**B. Shown beside the Palworld screenshots, would someone say these are trying to be the same kind of game? No.**
The third-person trainer suggests an adventure, but these particular images do not communicate a compelling creature-centered world at the supplied reference bar. Creature silhouettes are too remote and indistinct, vegetation reads as scattered components, and there is little readable activity or lived-in structure. Bringing creatures into a deliberate composition and improving night readability are scene work. Closing any remaining character/creature design and vegetation-form gap requires art that holds up in those improved views; these frames do not demonstrate it.
