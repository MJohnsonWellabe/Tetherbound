Original final review, preserved verbatim:

The trainer is identifiable as a backpacked adventurer in F01–F04, but the small, subdued figure lacks the distinctive proportions, costume silhouette and expressive presentation of the Palworld characters. The teal sleeves and pale collar carry most of its recognition; its brown hair, backpack and trousers merge with the ground. No creature is clearly identifiable in these frames, so creature appeal, companion/enemy scale relationships and combat presentation cannot be judged. The images do not demonstrate the creature partnership central to both references.

I reviewed F01–F04 and all six specified reference images, using the visual-judge rubric. These conclusions concern visible still-image appearance only.

**1. Silhouette and readability at small size**

In all four frames, the major tree trunks, right-hand boulder and trainer remain identifiable when considering the composition at roughly 30% size. The trainer’s pale collar and teal shoulders survive; the lower body loses separation against the dark, mottled terrain, especially in F02/F04.

The large trunks create a readable grove, but the many similarly green leaf shapes consolidate into a continuous overhead mass. The cream plants and red bushes become bright dots rather than distinct plant types. There is no small-size destination silhouette comparable to the key art’s settlement, mountain or standing stone, or Palworld-04’s tower and ruins.

**2. Colour and value structure**

F01/F03 have a consistent green, brown and grey palette, but the ground occupies a large part of the image with closely competing olive and dark-brown patches. It lacks the references’ broader, quieter areas of grass, path and shadow that organise detail into readable regions.

The repeated crimson bushes in every frame are among the strongest colour accents. Their appearance as ordinary grove vegetation weakens the visual reservation of oxblood for danger; these images contain no additional contextual signal establishing them as threatening.

F02/F04 convincingly shift the sky and distant landscape toward blue. However, the cream plants, bright green ground leaves and pale boulder retain enough relative prominence to feel only loosely integrated into the night palette.

**3. Intentionality**

F01–F04 look assembled from recurring tree and undergrowth pieces. Similar pale trunks, upright foliage clusters and repeated red-bush/cream-plant combinations recur through the middle distance. Some grouping exists around tree bases, but there is little transition between a foreground grove and the broad open ground beyond.

The central opening provides space in front of the trainer without becoming an authored route or destination. Compared with the key art’s paths, fences, streams and clearings, or Palworld-02’s path junction and enclosing banks, the scene lacks an explanation for where the player should go and what makes this particular place memorable.

**4. Lighting**

Day and night are immediately distinguishable in both pairs. F01/F03 have warmer terrain and a lighter sky; F02/F04 establish a blue night background.

In every frame, trunk surfaces have visible shading, but the surrounding terrain reads predominantly as a textured plane. Ground shadow and material mottling are difficult to separate, reducing the sense that light describes terrain shape. The trainer’s feet lack a strong, readily readable contact cue.

F02/F04 would benefit from a clearer night lighting hierarchy: the pale rock and cream vegetation remain conspicuous without an evident local light source. The result conveys a time change more strongly than a distinctive night atmosphere.

**5. Horizon and depth**

F01/F02 contain a conspicuous fine, pale granular band across distant terrain and the low left hill. It competes with the tree silhouettes and makes the transition into distance visually rough.

F03/F04 substantially reduce that granular appearance. The distant ground and hill read as smoother, more coherent forms, with a calmer transition into haze. This is the clearest improvement between the pairs.

All four still have limited depth staging beyond the grove. The broad, sparsely articulated horizon lacks intermediate terrain features and a distant focal landmark. The blue haze in F02/F04 separates distance, but cannot supply the missing landscape composition. I see no definite chunk boundary that can be established from these images.

**6. Interface**

The principal labels are readable at full size, and panels remain inside the frame. The quest panel has a clear heading and objective hierarchy.

In all four frames, five large empty slots and the wide command strip occupy a substantial part of the lower-right view while communicating relatively little. The tiny white key badges become visual specks at small size. The top-left “Calm” label and top-centre time have much less visual authority than the quest panel; the health and food panels are also subdued against the ground.

At reduced size, the interface remains prominent as a collection of dark rectangles while much of its text becomes unreadable. This judgement concerns the supplied interface itself, without comparing it to Palworld’s UI.

**7. Artefacts**

F01/F02 show the distant fine-grained terrain pattern noted above; F03/F04 visibly improve it. The foreground ground texture remains very soft and smeared by comparison with the sharply outlined nearby leaves and bushes in all four frames.

The canopy consists of conspicuous repeated leaf shapes, and the large trunks carry strong, stretched-looking vertical surface patterns. These read more as material and asset limitations than proven rendering bugs.

F02/F04 also show thin, very dark cloud streaks against the blue sky that look graphically harsh. There is no clear still-image evidence of z-fighting or broken geometry. Popping, temporal shimmer and performance cannot be established from these captures.

**8. Scale agreement**

Using the 1.80 m trainer as the reference, the substantial tree trunks and nearby boulder read as plausible large forest features. Perspective prevents exact measurement of their physical dimensions.

The oversized-looking cream shelf plants and bright broad leaves push the grove toward a fantasy scale, while the trainer’s comparatively fine-grained clothing and the sharply patterned trunks suggest a different level of visual stylisation. This is more a consistency-of-art-language problem than a demonstrable metre-scale error.

There are no sufficiently identifiable creatures to assess companion, practice-target or boss size relationships.

**Visible comparison between the pairs**

F03/F04 are visibly better than F01/F02 in the treatment of distant terrain. The granular, pale horizon texture becomes smoother in both day and night, improving depth coherence and reducing distraction.

The corresponding trainer, trees, boulder, plant placement and interface show no substantial compositional difference. Cloud arrangements differ slightly. The later pair does not visibly repair the foreground ground softness, repetitive grove composition or character presentation.

I do not see a material visual regression introduced by F03/F04. Their distant terrain has less fine contrast, but the resulting smoother surface reads as an improvement here. That conclusion does not establish the cause of the difference or prove stability during movement.

**Three largest gaps from the references, ranked**

1. **Character and creature presence — F01–F04.** Palworld gives its trainers and creatures distinctive shapes, expressive poses and immediate prominence. The key art also presents the trainer together with a companion. These frames offer a small, generic-looking rear-view adventurer with no clearly readable creature. Costume silhouette, proportions and stronger character art require art work; camera framing and subject/background separation are scene changes.

2. **Authored landscape and destination — F01–F04.** Both references organise natural density around routes, terrain changes, settlements or landmarks. These frames show a repeated grove opening onto largely unarticulated ground. Terrain shaping, clustering, clearings, routes and composition are scene-fixable. Distinctive settlement or landmark designs require additional art if suitable assets are unavailable.

3. **Ground and vegetation cohesion — F01–F04, with partial improvement in F03/F04.** The references combine readable broad ground regions with varied foliage edges and controlled detail. Here, soft mottled ground, strongly patterned trunks, flat leaf shapes and saturated repeated shrubs compete at different visual scales. Palette, density, lighting and placement are scene-fixable; stronger ground materials and more cohesive tree/undergrowth assets require art work. The later pair improves the distant texture problem, not this broader mismatch.

**A. Do these frames read as belonging to the world in the Tetherbound key art? No.**

Green woodland and a blue night palette provide a partial connection, but the welcoming landscape structure, readable landmarks, coherent natural materials and trainer–companion relationship are missing from these views. Scene composition and lighting can close part of the gap; character, vegetation and material quality also need art work.

**B. Beside the Palworld screenshots, would someone say these are trying to be the same kind of game? No.**

These particular images communicate a generic third-person forest exploration scene. They do not visibly establish the expressive creature relationships, populated world or strong character presentation that make the comparison persuasive. This is a verdict on the evidence in these frames, not a claim that the game contains no creatures elsewhere. Better subject selection and framing can demonstrate existing content; character and creature art must independently meet the visual bar when shown.
