## Creatures and characters first

**Day and Night:** The crab is a recognizable, substantial creature with clear claws, stalk eyes, and a shell silhouette. Its presentation does not meet the supplied Palworld character-art standard. Repeated crabs crowd the left side and overlap so heavily that shell edges and legs become one mass, especially on the small contact sheet. A huge quantity of shell bumps and grooves competes with the face; the eyes and mouth have too little contrasting structure to establish an expressive focal point. Separate the visible bodies, preserve open space around the front crab's face, and give face, claw, shell, and underside distinct material/value roles. The references achieve memorable faces and readable body masses before surface detail.

**Day:** Pale peach crabs are bleached toward the nearly white wall and waterfall. The shell has intricate sculptural detail but little convincing separation between the hard shell, softer underside, and claws. The trainer's pale clothing and gray-green hair also blend into the ground and claw. At sheet size the trainer survives mainly as a thin vertical interruption.

**Night:** The crabs become predominantly mauve silhouettes with weak internal form. The front trainer's face, torso, and legs fall into dark blue values close to the floor; the large crab remains identifiable more easily than the player. The visible trainer design is a conventional stylized adventurer whose outfit accents and face do not communicate much personality at this camera scale. These views do not establish character appeal comparable with the expressive trainer and creatures in the Palworld references. A closer character view would be needed to distinguish framing/lighting losses from limitations of the underlying character artwork.

## Full rubric findings

1. **Small-size silhouette and readability.** **Day:** Giant claw and shell shapes remain readable, but the row of crabs does not separate into clear individuals. The almost white cascade merges with the wall, and its identity depends heavily on the interaction text. **Night:** The corridor and blue waterfall bands read, but the trainer becomes a small dark mark and rear creatures merge with each other and the quest panel. Create silhouette clearance between actors and preserve a contrasting background behind the trainer. No trees or bushes are visible to judge their category separation.

2. **Colour and value structure.** **Day:** Most of the wall, water, and creatures occupy pale values, while the green ground looks mottled and comparatively saturated. This reverses the references' stronger separation of colorful creatures from restrained natural terrain. Reduce the broad white veil and restore darker crevices and grounded material values. **Night:** The large blue wall, blue floor, and blue trainer compress into a narrow dark family; muted pink crabs provide contrast, but little inviting or mysterious local colour remains. Give the player and important approach surfaces selective readable light without brightening the entire scene. The two views share geometry and crab colours and read as the same location. Neither frame visibly demonstrates oxblood danger coding; pale pink crabs are not evidence of oxblood leakage.

3. **Intentionality and density.** **Day and Night:** The scene is mostly a bare textured floor and an uninterrupted wall covered with similarly sized polygonal facets. Crab repetition supplies most of the visual population, with several bodies packed at the left edge and another group at the far end. This reads as staged placement rather than a creature habitat. Introduce deliberate bank/ledge shapes, scale variation in rock formations, localized vegetation or wet-edge details, and creature groups with breathing room and a clear route. An enclosed waterfall location need not imitate a meadow, but it still needs authored environmental structure.

4. **Lighting.** **Day:** Time of day is bright and clear, and shadows beneath the nearest crab and trainer do anchor them. However, the broad pale overlay suppresses wall relief and shell modelling. **Night:** Time of day is unmistakable and the wall has more visible facets, but dark contact regions swallow the player's lower body and several crab legs. The luminous blue stripes cross actors and floor without clearly communicating a convincing waterfall volume. Preserve contact shading while giving focal subjects enough directional shape and separation.

5. **Horizon and depth.** **Day and Night:** The corridor narrows toward the upper right, so there is some perspective depth. The blank wall dominates the upper half, the far end is largely hidden by the quest panel, and there is no composed distant focal point. The waterfall is not presented as a complete landmark with readable source, fall, impact, and destination. Day haze erases much of the wall's form; Night keeps wall form but collapses the approach into darkness. A framed glimpse toward the destination and a clearer cascade silhouette would make this location read as a place. No open horizon is visible, so long-range landscape quality cannot be judged. No LOD transition can be established from these stills.

6. **Interface, judged independently of Palworld UI.** **Day and Night:** Panels stay inside the image and the interaction prompt is immediately legible. The large quest box masks the distant creatures and the corridor's destination. The large five-slot panel consumes substantial view area while showing essentially tiny button marks instead of readily identifiable contents. The command bar is legible at full resolution but competes with the central exploration view. Move or reduce obstructing panels and make slot contents meaningful at a glance. **Day:** Health/food text has relatively low contrast against translucent pale surroundings. **Night:** Health is clearer, but the time readout remains small and dim. Controller/keyboard glyphs are too tiny to identify reliably on the contact sheet.

7. **Visible artefacts.** **Day and Night:** A straight diagonal boundary cuts from the bottom foreground toward the right side, abruptly dividing the waterfall-tinted area from ordinary ground. It reads as the edge of a translucent geometric surface rather than a natural water boundary. The oversized wall facets and stretched vertical texture pattern further expose constructed geometry. Crab bodies overlap heavily on the left; the image does not prove physical interpenetration, but the resulting tangles are a visible presentation defect. Resolve the hard water-surface edge and give water/ground intersections irregular, spatially credible treatment. Motion popping and z-fighting cannot be established here.

8. **Scale agreement, using the 1.80 m trainer.** **Day and Night:** The nearest crab's shell/eye silhouette stands substantially above the trainer, roughly around twice the trainer's projected standing height. Perspective prevents an exact world-space measurement. Its claw is comparable in length to much of a human body. This convincingly reads as a giant creature; it is not visibly undersized beside the trainer. The surrounding wall can plausibly be enormous, but its uniform giant facets and lack of smaller ledges or ground objects provide poor secondary scale cues. No clearly identified practice-versus-companion pair is supplied, so that role-based relative-scale comparison is unproven. The clustered creatures appear broadly similar in scale; there is no basis to assign them different intended roles.

No fight is shown. Whether combat looks like an event remains unjudged, rather than passed or failed.

## Three ranked reference gaps

1. **Creature and trainer presentation — Day and Night.** Palworld's boss and field images use distinct material/value blocks, expressive faces, and separated silhouettes to make creatures and humans the visual reason to play. Here repeated pink shell masses obscure each other and the trainer; Day bleaching and Night darkness remove character information. Placement, camera clearance, and lighting are scene fixes. Expressive facial design and more deliberate character material artwork must also be demonstrated; scene scatter alone cannot supply them.

2. **Authored environment and natural surface transitions — Day and Night.** The key art and Palworld path/plateau images organize rock, ground cover, traversable space, and landmarks into layered places. Here a nearly empty textured corridor, repetitive wall facets, and a straight-edged translucent water surface dominate. Bank geometry, selected habitat detail, clustering, and a composed cascade can be addressed in the scene. Convincing water/rock surface treatment is not demonstrated in these frames and requires material/geometry artwork as well as placement.

3. **Controlled value and depth — Day and Night.** The key art retains grounded natural values in daylight and readable subjects against a layered blue night landscape; Palworld preserves foreground/midground separation. Day instead washes the major forms toward white; Night merges the player and ground into dark blue. Retuning lighting, water opacity, material values, and focal composition is the primary scene-level remedy.

## Acceptance answers

**A. Do these frames belong to the key-art world? No.** The broad idea of a natural location with oversized companions fits, but the exposed corridor construction, weak cascade landmark, pale Day wash, and undifferentiated Night darkness do not carry the reference's grounded, inviting, layered world. This is a palette/composition/material-language judgment, not a demand for painted per-leaf fidelity.

**B. Would someone say these are trying to be the same kind of game as the Palworld references? Yes.** A third-person stylized trainer standing beside enormous fantastical creatures in an explorable natural setting clearly communicates that ambition. This answers the stated kind-of-game question; it does not establish equivalent artwork quality. Creature expression, environment authorship, and lighting remain far below the supplied shipping-game examples.

**Commercial shipping-art readiness: No.** The straight water edge, bare repetitive enclosure, actor tangles, lost player readability, and unresolved creature material/face hierarchy are prominent defects in ordinary gameplay framing.

**Scene fixes versus art needs:** Actor spacing, camera framing, HUD obstruction, controlled water opacity, light/value separation, environmental clustering, and the composition of the approach are addressable scene work. The frames do not show expressive character surface design, a convincing cascade material/intersection treatment, or sufficiently authored cliff/habitat forms; those require artwork to be supplied or improved, not merely more scatter. No asset inventory was inspected, so these images cannot establish whether appropriate replacement art already exists elsewhere in the build or needs to be made. Performance, animation quality, traversal, and off-camera content are outside this evidence.
