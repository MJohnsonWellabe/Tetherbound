## Structure, contact and readability

- **Ordinary day — complete outline, weak construction.** The arch opening is easy to recognize against the sky even at reduced size. Its flat outer top, broad upper panel and very thin legs give it a board-with-a-hole silhouette. There is little visible structural depth, jointing, footing or material transition to explain its construction. Introduce readable thickness, connections and a deliberate top treatment if this is meant to carry landmark importance. This is a criticism of its visible form, not a claim about how the mesh was made.
- **All three — conspicuous vertical material streaks.** The orange-brown structure is covered in high-contrast vertical marks, especially the legs and upper panel in ordinary day. These repeat without clearly differentiating structural pieces and read as an oversized surface pattern. Reduce their contrast and establish a consistent material scale with recognizable construction units. The arch's warm hue itself can work against the cool cliffs.
- **Canonical day and canonical night — crown outside frame.** Only the two upright lengths appear; the central tree obscures part of the opening. In these views the object could be mistaken for two unusually tall bare trunks. Recompose or provide another normal gameplay view that shows the crown and its relationship to the landscape. These images alone do not demonstrate whether players usually see it that way.
- **All three — contact remains partly obscured.** Both legs extend down into the grass and appear to meet the terrain; there is no obvious detached floating base. However, vegetation hides the precise junctions, and the trainer further occludes the lower center in ordinary day. Ground penetration, footings and structural support cannot be cleared from these pictures. A clear base view would resolve that evidence gap.
- **Ordinary day — no strong beacon cue.** The opening and the warm arch are visible, but no conspicuous light source or distinct focal device makes the structure read specifically as a beacon. A tiny pale projection at the top does not carry that function at this scale. If beacon identification matters, its visual signal needs to survive the ordinary view. This does not assert any missing gameplay function.

## Other actionable visible defects

- **All three, foreground:** large areas of smooth green ground sit between isolated long angular grass blades. The grass reads as thin wires or crossed strokes rather than a layered meadow surface. The denser left midground in the canonical frames does not resolve the sparse foreground. Use coherent clumps, varied blade widths/heights and ground-cover transitions while preserving the path.
- **Canonical day and ordinary day, cliffs and sky:** huge pale vertical rock columns have broad, repetitive vertical faces, while bright cloud shapes form large graphic streaks. The combination reads like a rough environment blockout behind a more detailed trainer. Add stronger rock mass variation, smaller surface landmarks and less uniformly dominant cloud contrast. These are visible art-direction gaps; the stills cannot identify an LOD or shader bug.
- **Canonical night:** the foreground is very dark while cliffs, distant haze and cloud backdrop remain exceptionally pale. Bright thin grass lines cut across the dark ground, and the trainer's lower body becomes difficult to separate. The cool palette signals night, but the luminous backdrop and crushed foreground do not form a convincing night value hierarchy. Rebalance sky/haze/terrain values and maintain local character separation.
- **All three, interface:** the large five-card panel contains little visible information yet covers a substantial area of the lower-right scene. The instruction bars also consume a broad horizontal strip. In ordinary day the bottom bar overlaps the trainer's lower legs. Reduce empty panel area or supply a more compact resting state; judge this by scene obstruction and readability, not by copying Palworld's UI.
