## Character art comes first

The long forest-green coat, cream fur collar, gold piping, beard and swept hair create a readable stern fantasy official. The costume has a coherent identity and plausible adult proportions. At small size the pale collar and head survive; the dark trousers, coat interior and torso details merge. The result is more severe and densely textured than the clean, expressive character shapes in the Palworld references. Its many small gold marks do not substitute for readable large material and facial forms.

B adds a brown staff with a gold loop and green ornaments. The loop survives reduction and gives the silhouette a useful asymmetrical identifier. Its smooth shaft and rounded ornaments, however, look considerably simpler than the weathered, heavily textured costume. The prop reads as an added primitive assembly. This is a visible style mismatch, not a claim about where either asset came from.

In B `front-idle` and `three-quarter-idle`, the diagonal shaft crosses the cuff/forearm area and runs past the loosely curled hand without a persuasive palm-and-fingers grip. The stage does not communicate whether it is held, attached or floating. In B `front-walk`, the upper and lower shaft sections are separated by the coat silhouette; that occlusion alone does not prove mesh penetration, but the already unclear hand contact makes the carry look unconvincing. This is the strongest reason the added prop cannot receive component acceptance.

## Three largest gaps to the references, ranked

1. **Material and feature hierarchy are less coherent.** In both sets' `front-idle` and `three-quarter-idle`, the flat disk, black eye region and densely mottled trim do not resolve into the clean expressive focal forms visible on Palworld's human and creature subjects. B adds another surface style through its plain shaft and rounded green ornaments. Improve the medallion's resolved design, face readability and separation of large cloth/leather/metal regions; the staff needs matching art treatment. These are character/prop material and texture work, not foliage or landscape work. The frames do not establish that a replacement human mesh is necessary.
2. **B's prop does not convincingly belong to the hand.** B `front-idle` and `three-quarter-idle` show an unclear cuff/shaft/hand relationship. In Palworld 01–03, hand-held equipment and rider/character poses read as part of the action even in small images. B's added identifier cannot compensate for its unresolved carry. Placement, grip pose and attachment work may address this without new art; a new mesh is not proven necessary.
3. **The walking costume loses its convincing shape.** In both `front-walk` images, the raised arm pulls a long dark flap upward beside the torso, and the body reads as a stiff posed cutout rather than the articulated action seen in Palworld 01 and 03. Inspect and improve the visible sleeve/coat deformation, then provide consecutive motion frames or a clip. This is a character deformation/pose concern; environmental dressing cannot repair it. The required mechanism cannot be determined from stills.

## Required bar answers and precise acceptance scope

**Bar A — Do these frames read as belonging to the Meadows key art's world? Yes, at the character-art compatibility level, for both A and B.** The natural dark greens, leather browns, pale collar and restrained gold plausibly belong to that stylised fantasy setting. B's simpler prop weakens coherence but does not make the overall palette foreign. This answer establishes only a plausible costume fit. The empty stage provides no evidence that the playable world meets the key art's composition, life, density or mood.

**Bar B — Beside the Palworld frames, would someone say these are trying to be the same kind of game? Yes, narrowly for the stylised adventure-character category, for both A and B.** The stylised human proportions, layered travel clothing and readable silhouette support that category. This is not a shipping-quality character pass: the finish, expression, grip and walking-garment defects above remain. No creature is shown, so these frames cannot demonstrate the defining creature-adventure relationship or accept the overall world against the Palworld bar.

**Final narrow disposition:** A is the cleaner current character presentation. B supplies a useful staff silhouette but introduces an obvious integration and art-style problem; do not treat it as an accepted visual improvement. Shared medallion, facial readability and walking-coat issues remain in both. Rejudge the corrected character and complete prop on the same stage, then use actual world and motion evidence for the acceptance questions this set cannot answer.
