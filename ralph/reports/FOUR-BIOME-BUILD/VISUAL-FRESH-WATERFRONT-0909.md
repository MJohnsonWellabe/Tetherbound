## Characters, creatures and scene defects first

**No creature is visible in any supplied frame.** The images do not demonstrate the creature companionship or populated habitat that makes the Palworld references immediately legible as a creature adventure. This is an evidence failure for these views, not a claim that creatures are absent elsewhere.

**The trainer is readable by day but not yet an expressive focal subject.** In F03 and Day, the blue vest, pale shirt and brown hair distinguish the person from the sand. At the supplied camera distance the face and hands have little readable expression; black contours and narrow limb details are more conspicuous than a strong character gesture. Night reduces the legs, gloves and hair to near-black shapes and the face to a small cold patch. These frames cannot establish that a different mesh is necessary: close character evidence is missing, and the night loss is visibly a lighting/value problem.

**The waterfront reads as a small arrangement on a bare surface.** All frames have the same broad sand apron, a few disconnected rectangular paving pieces, two rocks flanking a stone doorway and a nearly continuous pale far bank. The left cliff provides enclosure, but there is little vegetation, working waterfront clutter, landform overlap or habitat between foreground and skyline. The large near cliff displays conspicuous angular facets and coarse texture. The doorway has a readable outline but little visual reason to be a gate or return destination beyond the filename; it resembles a freestanding stone rectangle.

## Full rubric

1. **Small-size silhouette/readability.** When read at thumbnail scale, all three views retain the cliff, arch, trainer and water band. The trainer is a clear small blue/brown figure in Day; Night loses internal separation and the dark gate merges toward its own shadow. The Day railing reads as a fence strip, not clearly a dock extending over water. No creature silhouette survives because none is present.

2. **Colour/value structure.** F03 and Day have a strong dark cliff/light sand division, but the sand, paving, far bank and stone arch are clustered in pale yellow-grey values. The water is a very pale cyan band rather than a richly differentiated surface. The foreground grass is a blurred, highly green strip. Night is recognizably blue, with a brighter water band behind dark silhouettes, but offers little warm/cool focal contrast. No obvious oxblood leakage is visible in the scene; the small red interface marks are not enough to judge faction colour use.

3. **Intentionality.** The left cliff and centered gateway establish an intelligible composition. However, the paired rocks, separate rectangular path slabs and right-hand rail strip feel placed as individual objects rather than grown into a shore. The regular repeated rail bays in Day/Night expose the construction rhythm. A continuous worn approach, bank transitions and locally clustered shoreline dressing would give the objects a shared purpose.

4. **Lighting.** Day and night read distinctly. Day shadows anchor the trainer, gate and cliff, but bright sandy surfaces wash out subtle form. Night preserves water and cliff form yet suppresses the trainer's dark clothing and gate masonry. The doorway casts a very hard, large geometric shadow across the approach. There is no visible local light or warm destination cue around this entrance. Exact light settings and hardware lighting fidelity cannot be diagnosed from these files.

5. **Horizon/depth.** Foreground cliff, gateway, water, opposite bank and distant peak establish basic layers. The far bank remains a long, nearly level wall with little overlapping vegetation or terrain silhouette variation. The distant central peak is a pale simple mass. Night separates the bank from sky but does not add a stronger landmark hierarchy. No definite LOD or terrain chunk seam is demonstrable in a still.

6. **Interface.** Main objective and control prompts are legible and within the image. The objective panel occupies substantial upper-right scene area, while the empty five-slot panel and wide bottom control bar obscure much of the lower-right shore. Health/food contrast is weaker than the objective's. The red/white marks in empty slots are visually cryptic in this still; their intended state cannot be established. At small size the central scene is easier to read than the small time label and detailed control glyphs. No Palworld UI comparison is used.

7. **Artefacts.** F03's large flat tan rectangle at right reads as unfinished geometry. Day/Night show an open timber structure in that same region instead. The pale paving pieces remain sharply rectangular against the sand, and Night's clouds have conspicuous dark broken edges against the blue sky. These are visible discontinuities, not proven z-fighting or runtime faults. Static files cannot show popping, flicker or motion aliasing.

8. **Scale agreement.** Using the 1.80 m trainer as a reference, the gate opening and rail height are broadly plausible for people; the gate stands farther away, so direct pixel-height ratios would mislead. ## Three largest gaps to the references

1. **No creature-adventure subject — all frames.** Palworld's field and base images visibly pair expressive creatures with the player and habitat. These views show one small trainer and no creature. Supply visible creature life and judge it in context; close character/creature evidence is also needed before accepting their art quality.
2. **Bare shore and disconnected objects — all frames.** The key art's waterfront uses irregular banks, plants, rocks, a readable dock and overlapping landforms; Palworld's open areas combine clear routes with ground layers and nearby occupied space. Here the sand apron, isolated slabs, arch and fence remain separate visual statements. Scene layout, shoreline transitions and clustered dressing can address this.
3. **Weak material/form hierarchy and destination cue — Day and Night.** The references separate characters and landmarks from textured, layered surroundings. Day compresses much of the waterfront into pale sand/stone values; Night drops trainer and entrance detail into dark blue-black. The generic stone rectangle and flat far bank supply little destination character. Lighting, palette and composition are scene work; a more distinctive gateway, refined rock forms or stronger character expression may require art work if the installed assets cannot support them. Asset availability was not inspected.
