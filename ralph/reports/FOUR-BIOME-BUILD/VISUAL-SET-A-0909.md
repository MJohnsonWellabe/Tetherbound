# Blind visual verdict — set A

Reviewed only the visual-judge skill, the Meadows key art, all five specified Palworld references, and F01–F04. F01/F02 and F03/F04 are treated as day/night pairs. No source, change description, history, or other report informed this judgment. Frame references below refer to the supplied neutral images.

The trainer and creatures do not carry these frames at the reference standard. The trainer is recognizable as a backpack-wearing adventurer in F01 and F03, but the rear silhouette is conventional, with little distinctive shape beyond the pack and hair. The blue sleeves separate the arms reasonably well in daylight. In F02 and F04 the legs and torso largely disappear into the foreground. The pale and blue forms beyond the trainer are more readily read as jagged rocks or crystalline props than expressive creatures; their anatomy, faces, and relationships to the player cannot be established at this distance. That is a failure of what these frames communicate, not proof of what their unseen models contain. Palworld's references make the creature bodies, heads, and major gestures immediately legible, even in the distant encounter in reference 02.

## All eight rubric categories

1. **Silhouette and small-size readability.** Mentally reducing these frames to roughly 30% leaves a recognizable central person in F01/F03, two dark tree masses, a pale object on the left, and a bright irregular group near the central grove. The latter group does not resolve into identifiable creatures. In F02/F04 the person becomes a weak dark shape, while the moon and objective panel dominate. F03 separates the center-right pale forms more than F01, but makes the smaller ones easier to miss. The broad foreground fern obscures the trainer's lower-leg/ground relationship in all four. Keep a clear patch immediately around the trainer and stage creature silhouettes against a quiet, contrasting surface; a closer presentation is also needed to establish character appeal.

2. **Colour and value structure.** F01/F03 share a coherent green landscape and blue sky, but mustard-yellow ground, bright lime leaves, and reddish trunks form a harsher combination than the key art's olive greens, warm paths, and varied shaded foliage. The foreground has many narrow high-contrast marks without a broad, calm meadow colour mass. F02/F04 clearly belong to the same location, but compress most land and character values into near-black while leaving a very bright, nearly featureless moon. Blue accents help locate distant forms without explaining them. No visible friendly object can confidently be identified as using the reserved oxblood danger colour; brown/red bark alone does not establish such a violation.

3. **Intentionality.** F01/F03 contain actual grove clusters and an open center, so the whole layout is not uniformly scattered. Within that opening, however, many similarly sized leafy sprigs recur at conspicuously regular-looking intervals. Fine upright grass strands cover exposed mottled ground without forming the thick, uneven patches and worn routes of Palworld reference 02. Dark rounded shrubs appear as isolated spots rather than edges to a path or clearing. The large central grove has a fairly uniform crown line and reads like a planted block. Compose continuous vegetation patches with different heights, a deliberate walkable route, and larger quiet ground areas instead of increasing isolated sprig counts.

4. **Lighting.** The day/night distinction is unambiguous. Daylight in F01/F03 gives tree crowns and trunks some form, with useful shade on the grove interiors. The ground remains visually busy enough that broad terrain form is weaker than individual grass marks. F02/F04 lose most foreground form and the trainer's separation despite the dominant moon. The green light on the left is a useful local accent, but no similarly useful illumination catches the player. The key art's night scene retains blue terrain layers and readable character contours; these frames need that value separation. Fine contact-shadow quality cannot be confidently assessed where foliage hides the feet.

5. **Horizon and depth.** F01/F03 show a near slope, a middle grove, and a narrow distant horizon. The central-right opening gives some recession, but the flat pale horizon and large sky occupy space without providing a strong destination. The key art's mountain silhouettes, settlements, river bends, and monuments turn depth into exploration; Palworld reference 04 similarly provides cliffs, a building, and a distant tower. The present left-hand pale form is too small and ambiguous to do that work. F02/F04 retain sky-versus-tree separation but lose much of the distance between terrain layers. No unambiguous chunk seam or LOD boundary is visible. A single view cannot establish whether other viewpoints contain stronger landmarks.

6. **Interface.** The objective is legible and the panels sit within the image edges. The large objective panel, minimap, five empty-looking slot cards, and wide bottom command strip consume a substantial share of the right side and lower field. At small size the objective remains prominent while slot numerals become tiny bright marks; the empty cards do not communicate enough to justify their visual weight. In F02/F04 interface contrast overwhelms the nearly black player and landscape. The food label has weak contrast in daylight. The time label overlaps the bright moon in the night pair, weakening that placement. These are internal hierarchy observations, not a comparison with Palworld UI.

7. **Artefacts.** F01/F03 show conspicuous thin grass strokes and speckled foliage edges, producing a scratchy texture in the ground and crowns. The horizontal cloud fragments look streaked in daylight and resemble dark cutouts at night. These are visible presentation defects, but the images cannot establish their technical cause. The foreground fern overlaps the legs in all four; it prevents reliable inspection of foot placement and does not by itself prove clipping. No definite z-fighting or terrain seam can be established. Popping, temporal aliasing, animation, and performance are unjudgeable from these stills.

8. **Scale agreement.** Using the stated 1.80 m trainer as a ruler, the fern in front of the player is a large, leg-high foreground plant. That can be plausible, but its placement makes it compete with the character. The nearest trees are substantially taller than a person; their heavy trunks and compact rounded foliage make them read more like chunky orchard trees than the key art's spreading oaks. Distant pale forms are at different depths from the trainer, so their world-space heights cannot be responsibly derived from screen height alone. No clearly identified companion/practice-target pair is visible; the required relative role-size distinction is therefore unverified. F03/F04 show a visibly smaller overall center-right silhouette group than F01/F02, but these images do not prove a specific scale ratio or cause.

## Visible comparison of the two pairs

F03 versus F01: the conspicuous center-right blue-white group occupies less of the view, and several smaller pale forms are separated below/beside the remaining taller forms. This opens more ground at the grove edge and reduces the single crowded bright mass. That is a local compositional improvement. It is also a readability tradeoff: the small white shapes have less recognizable anatomy and disappear sooner at reduced viewing size. Neither version establishes creature appeal.

F04 versus F02: the corresponding group has a smaller blue presence and more visible dark gaps. The pale object on the left also presents a different contour. These are observed differences only. The night foreground remains comparably dark, the trainer remains poorly separated, and the same foliage pattern and interface dominance remain. There is no visible broad improvement in the landscape, trainer presentation, or night value structure in the later pair. The different displayed minutes do not establish the cause of any difference.

## Ranked three gaps from the references

1. **Characters and creatures are not the visual attraction — F01–F04.** Palworld references 01, 03, and 04 give creatures clear body masses, faces, strong scale contrasts, and distinctive silhouettes. Here the trainer is a generic rear-view adventurer and the distant forms cannot reliably be read as creatures. Scene-fixable work: clear the feet, improve night separation, and frame creatures at a readable distance against quiet ground. Art work: establish distinctive trainer identity and expressive, recognizable creature shapes wherever a closer inspection confirms that these are absent. Lighting cannot supply missing anatomy or character design; the distant views do not establish whether the needed art is already available.

2. **The meadow reads as exposed ground with repeated sprigs — F01/F03, also visible in F02/F04.** The key art and Palworld references 02/03/04 use connected grass masses, varied foliage heights, worn paths, and purposeful clearings. These frames emphasize yellow mottling, fine vertical lines, isolated shrubs, and recurrent leafy stalks. Scene-fixable work: build coherent patches, vary spacing and scale, tune ground/plant colours together, and create a readable route. Art work: broader grass clumps and a less repetitive vegetation vocabulary may be needed if existing assets cannot produce those masses; the current frames cannot establish asset inventory.

3. **The view has weak destination structure by day and loses its playable foreground at night — F01–F04.** The key art layers landforms and settlements and retains useful blue night contours; Palworld reference 04 leads toward a distinct landmark. These frames end in a narrow, weakly articulated horizon, while F02/F04 prioritize the moon and UI over the trainer. Scene-fixable work: stronger foreground-to-distance value steps, readable night fill, a quieter sky, and a destination composed through the grove opening. Art work: a distinctive landmark or settlement silhouette is needed if none exists to stage here. A palette adjustment alone cannot create one.

## Direct bar answers

**A. Do these frames read as belonging to the Meadows key-art world? No.** The green grove, open grassland, blue sky, and night blue shift share broad ingredients, but the yellow bare-ground pattern, blocklike tree crowns, weak landmark/depth structure, and unreadable night foreground do not yet communicate its inviting, inhabited meadow world. Scene composition, planting, palette, and lighting account for much of this gap. Distinctive tree/landmark art is an additional requirement wherever the current asset set cannot furnish the needed forms.

**B. Beside the five Palworld references, would someone say these are trying to be the same kind of game? No, on the evidence of these frames.** A third-person adventurer in a stylized outdoor landscape is evident, but expressive creatures, a readable relationship between trainer and creatures, and a world visibly organized around their presence are not. The pictures currently communicate a sparse exploration scene more strongly than a creature-centered adventure. Better staging and lighting can expose the cast; they cannot substitute for distinctive trainer and creature art if that art is missing. A closer view is required to distinguish poor presentation from inadequate underlying creature models. No fight is shown, so encounter spectacle is untested rather than condemned.

These verdicts apply to the supplied view and its two day/night pairs. They are not claims about unseen locations, animation, traversal, or hardware performance.
