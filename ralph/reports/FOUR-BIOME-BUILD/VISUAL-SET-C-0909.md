# Blind visual verdict — set C

Reviewed only the visual-judge skill, the meadows key art, all five specified Palworld reference images, and F01–F04. F01/F02 and F03/F04 are the supplied day/night pairs. No source, change history, other reports, or rendering processes informed this judgment. No numeric scores.

## Trainer and creatures first

The trainer in F01 and F03 is recognizable as a stylized young adventurer: brown hair, light shirt, blue outer garment, dark trousers, boots and belt accessories. The proportions and outfit are internally coherent. The light chest and blue shoulders provide useful daylight separation. However, the trainer occupies a small part of a large field, and the face, hands and equipment collapse into a few marks at approximately 30% viewing size. The figure lacks the immediate character emphasis and readable equipment silhouettes visible in Palworld-01, -02 and -03. This is partly a framing problem; these frames do not justify declaring the underlying trainer model unusable.

F02 and F04 lose much more: the head and lower body sink into the near-black foreground, leaving a blue chest patch as the principal player marker. Their broad background value range does not help identify the playable subject.

No creature is shown clearly enough to assess expressive design, species identity or partner appeal. Small yellow forms at the right and a small green glowing form near the center distance cannot reliably be classified as creatures rather than environmental objects at this size. Their lack of readable anatomy is itself a presentation gap if they are intended as creatures. It would be dishonest to infer their models, roles or intended sizes. These views provide no equivalent to the distinct mammoth, purple cat, mounted creatures or working animals in the Palworld references. A creature-centered art pass remains unproven.

## All eight rubric categories

### 1. Silhouette and readability at small size

F01/F03 retain the trainer's light torso, the central leafy tree, the monumental stone pillar and the long vertical trunks. The grass becomes a busy field of streaks. The trees are identifiable, but the yellow and green small forms do not retain an identifiable subject silhouette. F02/F04 retain the cliff and sky far more strongly than the player; legs and boots merge with the terrain. At a small viewing size the large interface panels compete with, and often dominate, the scene's subjects. Improve the player's local value separation and give important creatures enough screen area and uncluttered backing to show their bodies.

### 2. Colour and value structure

F01/F03 share a consistent lime/olive foreground, blue-grey cliffs and pale blue sky. There is a useful broad palette relationship to the key art, but the foreground has relatively little warm soil, flower colour or local tonal grouping; repeated green grass marks cover much of the same green ground. F02/F04 produce a pronounced dark-ground/white-distance split. The pale cliffs and clouds feel much brighter than the night atmosphere established by the dark blue upper sky. The key art's night scene distributes cool dark values through both landscape and sky while retaining selective warm habitation light. Here the bright background wins over the trainer and route. No visible oxblood leakage onto a recognizable friendly subject can be established from these frames.

### 3. Intentionality

In F01/F03, tall narrow grass marks repeat across a broad, lightly textured clearing, while a dense bright band follows the left crest. The central leafy tree and the two extremely tall, straight trunks do not form a convincing grove at this crop; the trunks read more like parallel vertical boundaries. The rightward dirt path supplies some direction, but its destination is concealed behind the objective panel and is not visually anchored by a camp or settlement. F02/F04 preserve the same arrangement with less readable ground. The key art and Palworld-02/-05 use groundcover transitions, paths, rocks and occupied spaces to tell the viewer where activity belongs. These frames need stronger clusters, quieter travel ground and an identifiable destination, rather than simply more evenly distributed vegetation.

### 4. Lighting

F01/F03 clearly read as day. The trainer casts a visible shadow to the right and the terrain has light/shade variation, so the figure does not obviously float. F02/F04 clearly signal a different time through a moon, blue sky patches and a dark foreground, but the near-white distant cliff faces and clouds undermine a coherent night exposure. Grass tips retain bright, thin highlights while the trainer's face and trousers lose definition. The practical fix is to balance background brightness, foreground ambient visibility and subject separation together. Merely increasing all night brightness would leave the same hierarchy problem.

### 5. Horizon and depth

All four frames have a strong monumental ravine silhouette: a large left pillar, distant right cliffs, a descending valley and faint connecting horizontal structures. This is the most successful large-scale world feature. F01/F03 show several distance layers, although pale sky and pale far terrain merge in places. F02/F04 compress distant forms toward similar icy-white values; atmospheric separation becomes less informative even as the foreground falls darker. The key art and Palworld-04 provide a clearer progression from occupied foreground to navigable middle distance to landmark. No moving LOD transition or chunk seam can be confirmed from these stills.

### 6. Interface

All four frames keep the principal panels inside the image. The location title and objective text are legible at full size, but the top instruction banner covers the central tree/landscape, the large upper-right quest panel hides part of the path destination, and the lower-right empty five-slot panel consumes substantial play space. Tiny white keycap marks become unreadable at reduced size. Health and food remain visually subordinate; the disabled-looking creature command has weak contrast. The minimap's colored blur does not disclose a useful route in these images. Reduce the obstruction of the scene, clarify occupied versus empty action slots and maintain readable control labels at the intended display size. These are interface observations, not comparisons against Palworld's UI.

### 7. Artefacts

F01–F04 show many grass elements as thin angular sticks, including conspicuous kinked, branching-looking marks in the foreground. At full size their sparse outlines compete with the solid trainer and terrain surfaces; at reduced size they become noise. Fine bright edging on distant cliff boundaries, particularly in F02/F04, is visually distracting, although the stills do not establish whether this is an edge treatment, aliasing or another cause. The extremely pale, broad cloud forms also intersect the composition in abrupt flat patches. No definite z-fighting, temporal popping, moving aliasing or geometry penetration is proven. The grass appearance is a visible defect; its implementation cause is not established.

### 8. Scale agreement

Using the trainer as the supplied 1.80 m ruler, nearby grass reaches roughly knee to waist height in several foreground patches. That can describe tall grass, but its thin, isolated stems look oversized relative to their visual mass. The leafy tree behind the trainer reads as a modest tree, whereas the two straight trunks rise far beyond the crop and have a dramatically larger apparent scale. Perspective and off-screen canopies prevent a precise height judgment; the juxtaposition nonetheless lacks a clear grove hierarchy. The giant cliff pillar can plausibly serve as a monumental landmark rather than a metre-scale error. No reliable companion-versus-practice-target scale comparison is possible: the small distant forms have neither clear identities nor established relative depths/roles. F03/F04 provide no clearer scale evidence than F01/F02.

## Comparison of the two pairs

F03 retains the composition, character size, daylight exposure, palette, interface, path and landmark relationships of F01. Some grass stalk positions and local ground marks differ, but there is no clear improvement in the principal subjects or world composition at normal or small viewing size. Those variations do not establish a vegetation-quality gain or regression.

F04 likewise retains F02's dark trainer, very pale cliffs, bright grass filaments and large overlays. Its displayed clock reads one minute later; this is an observable difference, not an explanation for any rendering behavior. Neither the night readability problem nor the scene's depth hierarchy is materially resolved in the later view. No clear major visual regression is visible either. The later pair leaves the verdict unchanged.

## Three largest gaps, ranked

1. **Character and creature presentation does not carry the game — F01–F04.** Palworld's frames make the trainer, companions and opponents distinct, expressive foreground subjects. These views show a small trainer and no reliably identifiable creature. Night loses the trainer's lower silhouette almost completely. Scene-fixable: subject framing, clear ground behind silhouettes, local lighting and deliberate companion placement. Art-dependent: if readable close views reveal generic or incompatible creature designs, expressive creature art is needed; these images do not establish what creature assets exist or whether they can meet that requirement. The trainer's underlying art cannot be rejected solely from these views.

2. **The foreground reads as a grass distribution rather than a inhabited place — F01/F03, persisting in F02/F04.** The key art and Palworld-02/-05 combine varied groundcover, terrain edges, paths and signs of occupation into purposeful spaces. Here long thin grass marks and open green ground dominate, with little readable destination or environmental activity. Scene-fixable: clusters, clearings, path-edge transitions, scale variation and a visible destination using suitable existing assets. Art-dependent: more convincing groundcover silhouettes and distinctive camp/landmark/occupation props are required wherever those suitable assets are absent. The frames cannot determine the inventory.

3. **Night value structure breaks the intended mood and subject hierarchy — F02/F04.** The key art's night landscape remains a cool, coherent world with selective warm accents; these frames put near-white cliffs and clouds behind a nearly black playable foreground. Scene-fixable: rebalance sky, distance, ground and character illumination, preserving readable depth and localized light. No new character or creature art is inherently required to address this exposure relationship.

## Direct acceptance answers

**A. Do these frames read as belonging to the world in the meadows key art? No.** The natural green/blue family and monumental stone forms are useful connections, and a ravine need not duplicate a meadow. However, the sparse, wiry foreground, weakly inhabited composition and stark white-distance/black-ground night image do not yet sustain that board's welcoming, layered and mysterious world. Scene changes can address composition, density distribution, route emphasis and night values. Groundcover and distinctive occupation/landmark art are art gaps if equivalent assets are not already available; no asset inventory was inspected.

**B. Beside the five Palworld images, would someone say these are trying to be the same kind of game? No, on these submitted views.** They communicate a stylized third-person outdoor adventure, but not the creature-centered world, expressive relationships or occupied activity that make the supplied comparison recognizable. This is a verdict on the visible frames, not a claim about unseen gameplay. Better framing and deliberate readable creature presence are scene-fixable and must be demonstrated first. The necessary expressive creature art cannot be verified here, and lighting/scatter work alone cannot demonstrate it. Still images also cannot establish whether a fight feels like an event when none is shown, or prove motion quality, traversal or performance.
