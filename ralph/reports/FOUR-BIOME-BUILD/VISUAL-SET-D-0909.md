# Blind visual verdict — set D

Reviewed only the visual-judge skill, the Meadows key art, all five specified Palworld references, and F01–F04. F01/F02 and F03/F04 are treated as separate day/night pairs. No source, change history, other reports, or runtime evidence informed this review. No numeric scores are assigned.

## Trainer and creatures first

The trainer is recognizably a stylized adventurer in all four frames. Brown hair, blue sleeves, a tan backpack, and dark trousers make a coherent figure. In F01/F03 the silhouette separates reasonably well from the yellow-green terrain. In F02/F04 the dark hair and legs lose separation against the dark hill; the pale collar and sleeves carry most of the recognition. The rear view does not establish facial appeal or expression. Nothing here supports calling the trainer's proportions broken, but these views do not demonstrate the expressive character presence of the Palworld references.

The lone yellow-green creature left of the structure is much less successful as the game's subject. In F01/F03 its thin limbs, mottled surface, and small screen footprint make its body design hard to parse. At thumbnail reading it becomes a pale upright hook-like shape, while the empty wooden enclosure remains the dominant subject. In F02/F04 the creature becomes almost cream-white and separates more strongly, but this brightness does not reveal a compelling face or a strong body silhouette. The Palworld creatures retain distinct, broad shapes, readable color blocks, and clear body parts even when they are secondary subjects. These frames do not establish comparable creature appeal. They also cannot establish whether this particular creature's unseen close view would succeed.

## Eight-category review

1. **Silhouette and readability at small size.** Across F01–F04, the trainer, central enclosure, hill, water, and sky remain identifiable when mentally reduced to thumbnail scale. The creature's specific identity does not. There are no vegetation silhouettes to distinguish: the apparent grass exists chiefly as surface texture. The enclosure wins visual priority through its central placement, height, and contrast, while the trainer and creature do not form an engaging shared composition. In F02/F04, improve the trainer's separation from the ground without simply brightening the entire character.

2. **Color and value structure.** F01/F03 have a clear light-sky/dark-shadow range, but most land is one mottled yellow-green field, with little purposeful separation of path, shore, planted cover, and bare soil. Pale water and the washed-out far shore compress the distant values. F02/F04 introduce a credible blue night sky and darker land, yet the pale creature and bright foundation feel disconnected from that night palette. The four frames still read as the same location. No visible friendly element shows a clear misuse of oxblood; this view does not provide a meaningful danger-color comparison.

3. **Intentionality.** F01–F04 read as a terrain surface with an isolated enclosure and creature placed on its crest. The broad foreground and slopes have no authored clusters, ground cover, shore detail, trail, or secondary activity. This is not an objection to an open clearing: the references frame their openings with vegetation, rock, architecture, and purposeful routes. Here the opening occupies almost everything. The enclosure's blank pale base and lack of an approach or surrounding use make it feel insufficiently integrated with the site. Give it a visible reason to occupy this hill, expressed through its immediate surroundings.

4. **Lighting.** Day and night are immediately distinguishable in each pair. F01/F03 have useful long cast shadows that attach the trainer and enclosure to the hill and expose the slope. The ground texture competes with that form. F02/F04 retain unusually strong illumination on the enclosure's front/base and creature while the hill darkens substantially. The visible scene offers no obvious nearby light source that explains their prominence. The night image needs a more coherent relationship among subject illumination, ambient ground values, and sky; a legible local light source could provide an intentional focal point.

5. **Horizon and depth.** The foreground hill, lateral water, and distant islands establish basic layering in all four frames. The background is nevertheless a thin horizontal strip of pale land and flat-looking water, without a memorable distant destination. The key art and Palworld plateau reference use different-height terrain masses and strong landmarks to carry the eye beyond the foreground. F01/F02 also have conspicuous fine stippling along water and distant shore regions. F03/F04 look cleaner there, improving separation of the island contours. This does not create new depth or landscape identity. No definite terrain chunk seam can be established from these stills.

6. **Interface.** The instruction banner and story card are readable and remain inside the image. They compete for attention while the large, apparently empty five-slot panel and long bottom action bar occupy substantial space over an already sparse scene. At small size, slot key labels collapse into white marks. Bottom-left food labeling has weak contrast, particularly in the daytime frames. The minimap has a large uniform fill and little readable topography; its change from mint in F01/F02 to olive in F03/F04 does not by itself improve navigation. F04 displays the location heading that is absent in F02. No inference is made about why. These are independent interface observations, not comparisons against Palworld's UI.

7. **Artefacts.** F01/F02 show more grainy/stippled distant water and shoreline transitions than F03/F04. Fine sparkling-looking detail also makes the daytime distant creature hard to parse. The ground surface in every frame looks stretched and blurred across parts of the slope; horizontal directional streaking is especially visible on the left slope in F03/F04. In F02/F04 the sky has conspicuous dark, jagged cloud-like patches whose hard silhouettes look visually rough. These are visible appearance defects; their technical causes are not established. No motion popping, animated clipping, or temporal aliasing can be diagnosed here.

8. **Scale agreement.** Using the stated 1.80 m trainer as the reference, there is no obvious impossible person-to-enclosure relationship: the enclosure is farther away, uphill, and plausibly taller than a person. Perspective prevents a reliable exact size estimate. The distant creature is smaller on screen than the trainer but is also farther away; that is not sufficient evidence of an incorrect world scale. Only one creature is visible, with no established role or nearby counterpart, so companion-versus-practice-target scale cannot be judged. The lack of grass, stones, and other familiar objects leaves the hill and enclosure with few additional scale cues.

## What differs between the pairs

- **F03 versus F01:** the distant water and shore edges look smoother and less stippled; island contours are easier to follow. The minimap fill is olive rather than mint. The left hillside has more conspicuous smooth, directional texture streaks. Trainer, creature, enclosure, major lighting, and overall composition appear substantially unchanged.
- **F04 versus F02:** the distant water and island edges again look cleaner. The location heading is visible in F04, and the minimap fill differs. The pale creature, bright enclosure base, dark trainer silhouette, sparse hill, and rough dark sky shapes remain.
- **Overall:** the later pair improves a visible surface-cleanliness issue. It does not materially close the character/creature presentation, scene density, or depth gaps. The smoother left-slope surface makes stretched-looking ground texture easier to notice, so the improvement is not uniform across the image. No cause or intended change is inferred.

## Three ranked gaps against the references

1. **Creature identity and character-led composition — F01–F04.** Palworld places expressive, unmistakable creature shapes and readable trainers at the center of the experience; even its wider plateau scene offers distinct creature silhouettes. Here the distant thin creature is difficult to parse and the empty enclosure commands the composition. Scene-fixable portion: improve subject distance, framing, backdrop, and night separation. Art portion: the visible creature needs stronger body-shape and material/color-block readability if this is representative of the intended presentation. A closer view is required to determine whether existing art already supplies detail hidden here; these stills do not certify that it does. The trainer's face and expressiveness are unproven rather than visibly defective.

2. **An almost entirely bare world surface — F01–F04.** The key art and Palworld references use clustered foliage, stones, flowers, paths, and signs of habitation to make open land feel like a place. These frames offer textured ground almost to the exclusion of everything else. Scene-fixable portion: authored shore and hill clusters, varied ground cover, a legible approach, and contextual dressing around the enclosure. Art portion: coherent foliage/shore-detail assets and a ground surface that survives this camera distance are required; their availability elsewhere in the build is unknown.

3. **Weak atmospheric and material integration — F01–F04, strongest at night in F02/F04.** The references separate foreground, midground, and distant terrain while maintaining a coherent lighting mood. These frames have a washed-out, thin distant strip by day and a pale creature/bright base against dark land by night. Rough sky shapes and smeared ground compete with the scene. Scene-fixable portion: subject-to-environment exposure, a motivated night focal light, better background landform composition, and controlled depth values. Art portion: improve the visibly stretched ground and rough sky appearance if material/texture assets cannot support the required presentation. F03/F04's cleaner shores are welcome but do not resolve this gap.

## Direct bar answers

**A. Do these frames read as belonging to the Meadows key-art world? No.** The green land, blue sky, stylized trainer, and day/night shift provide broad palette connections. The near-total absence of meadow cover, authored natural clusters, settlement context, and layered landmarks sinks the comparison. Most immediate work is scene composition, density, palette, and lighting. Creature readability and suitable ground/foliage/sky art remain art requirements where the existing assets cannot deliver them; a blind image review cannot inventory absent assets.

**B. Beside the five Palworld references, would someone say these are trying to be the same kind of game? No.** A third-person trainer and a fantastical creature suggest related genre ingredients, but these particular frames read as an empty terrain demonstration around a prop, not a populated creature-adventure world. The missing creature presence and authored environment are too dominant for that visual bar to pass. Scene dressing, framing, and lighting can close substantial portions of the gap. Distinctive, readable creature art and coherent environment materials must also be demonstrated; scene density alone cannot establish creature appeal.

Neither answer is a claim about the rest of the game. There is no fight in these frames, so whether combat looks like an event is untested. Still images cannot prove motion quality, traversal feel, temporal stability, performance, or hardware behavior.
