## Three largest gaps from the references, ranked

1. **Creature faces do not consistently lead the design — F01 and F02.** Palworld's forest boss has readable eyes, mouth and contrasting muzzle within a large body; its field boss and base creatures preserve similarly simple facial anchors. Here the cyan creature's bright surface, the brown animal's patchwork face and the crab's claws compete with or obscure expression. The spider demonstrates the stronger local approach: a compact, contrasting eye group. Improve facial value separation and quiet adjacent detail. Whether deeper facial proportion or expression changes require new art cannot be determined from this view alone.
2. **Surface hierarchy is noisy and inconsistent across the roster — F01 and F02.** The Palworld examples retain broad body-colour regions and controlled transitions between secondary materials, with detail subordinate to silhouette. The cyan wings, magenta spider plates and coral crab are dominated by bright broken markings and hard contrasts. The brown creature adds a different patchwork treatment. Material, colour and lighting adjustments can address the visible competition; texture or character-art revision may be necessary if the markings and poorly distinguished features remain under neutral presentation. The images cannot prove what alternate art is installed.
3. **The presentation supplies no living-world evidence — F01 and F02.** The key-art board uses paths, vegetation clusters, water and landmarks to establish a welcoming world. Palworld places its creature silhouettes against layered ground, foliage and distant forms, and gives fights spatial context. These frames supply a bare platform and empty background. A world-space presentation with a trainer, ground vegetation and a readable backdrop is a scene/composition task. This is an evidence limitation of the isolated stage, not a finding that the unseen game world lacks those things.
