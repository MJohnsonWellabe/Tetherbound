## Eight-category rubric

1. **Silhouette and small-size readability.** F01/F02 retain their broad wing span particularly well. F03/F04 retain their ears and face; F09/F10 retain canine identity. F05/F06 keep the pale-chested bird/dragon outline but lose facial specificity. F07/F08 lose the most information: the narrow torso and very thin appendages convey less personality than the broad, distinct facial and body shapes in Palworld 01, 03 and 05. No B image changes that ordering. Contrast against a plain dark background is favorable and does not prove terrain readability.

2. **Colour and value structure.** Cyan F01/F02, yellow F03/F04 and magenta F07/F08 are dominated by very strong chroma rather than a controlled body/accent relationship. F07/F08 are especially disconnected from the natural palette and restrained fantasy accents of the key art. F09/F10 have strong background separation but excessive uninterrupted white, obscuring chest volume. F05/F06 have more useful pale/dark mass separation, although the blue-black-white surface breakup remains busy. No danger-colour reservation can be assessed without faction context; no such claim is made.

3. **Intentionality.** Each subject has a distinctive major motif: wings, long ears, dark feathered wings, magenta fins, or blue canine accents. Those are positive authored-looking decisions. The finished treatment is inconsistent across subjects: the smooth cyan belly, scratchy yellow perimeter, speckled pale chest, needlelike purple appendages, and glaring white fur do not yet share a coherent degree of simplification. F02 and F06 modestly improve local surface calm. Terrain clustering, lived-in world design and scatter are absent from this evidence.

4. **Lighting.** All ten subjects cast a clear shadow toward screen right, providing some grounding. F01/F02 show torso volume, and F06 improves chest continuity. ## Three ranked gaps against the references

1. **Creature surface hierarchy and expressive finish.** F07/F08 reduce to a saturated thin shape; F05/F06 have noisy wings/chest; F09/F10 lose chest form to white. Palworld 01, 03 and 05 sustain broad readable body masses, distinct faces and deliberately separated accents. F03/F04 are the strongest facial read here, but their harsh yellow/black finish still lacks that surface coherence. Scene lighting/material adjustment can address harsh contrast and clipped-looking values; inadequate facial/body shape separation, especially F07/F08, needs creature-art work if it persists across useful views. A still cannot establish whether suitable alternative art already exists in the build.

2. **Palette integration.** F01/F02 and especially F07/F08 are almost entirely electric cyan or magenta. The key art combines natural greens, earths and blues with limited fantasy accents; Palworld allows bold creatures while retaining distinct body and accent regions. Palette/material authoring and balanced lighting are addressable without proving a need for replacement geometry. The B set does not visibly solve this gap.

3. **World relationship and contextual readability are unproven.** Every F image is predominantly empty neutral background and a platform. The key art and Palworld 02/04 establish creature scale, grounding, distance and landscape contrast through actual terrain and surrounding objects. This gap needs contextual scene captures and composition, not an automatic demand for new creature art. The current images cannot show that those references' world-level qualities have been reached.
