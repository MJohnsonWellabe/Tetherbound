## Full eight-category rubric

1. **Silhouette and readability.** F01/F02's ears, F05/F06's wide face, and F07/F08's pointed leg spread give useful large shapes. F13/F14 are weakest: even full size the eyes and beak receive little separation, and at 30% the feathered body becomes a dark lump. F11/F12's dark chest obscures limb separation. The clean empty background makes these generous tests; none establishes readability against foliage or terrain. There is no trainer to judge.

2. **Colour and value structure.** F03/F04's lime-and-purple contrast, F07/F08's magenta-and-cyan contrast, F09/F10's cyan body, and F11/F12's electric blue dominate through saturation. These read less naturally integrated than the reference cast, whose large local colour areas remain legible through distinct light, mid-tone, and shadow shapes. F13/F14 have insufficient internal value separation. F01/F02's warm gold, dark chest, and pale muzzle are more successful. No friendly/enemy identities are supplied, and no clear oxblood usage is visible; danger-colour discipline cannot be assessed.

3. **Intentionality.** The faces and outlines contain deliberate focal choices: F05/F06 centre the eyes and mouth; F07/F08 centre three cyan eye shapes. Across F03/F04, F11/F12, and F13/F14, dense repeated coat/feather breakup competes with the larger shapes. F05/F06 and F09/F10 share closely similar frontal body plans, making colour more informative than silhouette for distinguishing them. There is no environment scatter to evaluate, and these pictures cannot establish how the assets were made.

4. **Lighting.** All frames show a consistent directional light and cast shadows to the right that generally establish contact. F05/F06 communicate rounded face and belly form more clearly than F13/F14 communicate head and torso. F07/F08 and F09/F10 contain severe bright-versus-dark surface divisions that obscure some internal connections. B's local improvements do not change that broad lighting impression. A time of day does not read from the blank stage.

5. **Horizon and depth.** F01–F14 show a finite pale platform and dark backdrop. There is no landscape, atmospheric depth, landmark, or navigable distance to compare with the references. The visible stage boundary is not evidence of a world seam. Fog and LOD cannot be assessed.

6. **Interface.** No interface is present in F01–F14. Safe area, hierarchy, legibility, and combat information are unassessable. The centred subjects are uncropped, but that does not constitute interface validation.

7. **Artefacts.** No clear z-fighting, exposed hole, or definite geometry penetration is identifiable in these stills. Sharp tonal islands on F01/F02 and dense ragged contour/surface breakup on F03/F04, F11/F12, and F13/F14 are visually distracting, without sufficient evidence to label their cause a bug. F07/F08 have heavily overlapping limbs, but the image alone does not establish an intersection error. Motion artefacts cannot be judged.

8. **Scale agreement.** The 1.80 m trainer ruler is absent. The platform views vary and the subjects occupy similar image heights, so screen size cannot establish world size or a scale regression. Companion/practice-target roles are not identified. Anatomical proportions can be described—the broad-headed squat bodies in F05/F06 and F09/F10 are readable—but intended interspecies size agreement remains untested.

## Three largest reference gaps, ranked

1. **A coherent, expressive cast with readable large forms.** Palworld 01 and 03 make eye, mouth, head mass, and body mass legible with broad shape and value organisation. F13/F14 hide the face; F11/F12 lose central limb separation; F03/F04 scatter attention over coat detail. Improving exposure and light placement can help, but stable facial/value design and a consistent treatment of coat detail require creature-art refinement. Scene density cannot repair them.

2. **Colour that feels part of a natural fantasy world.** The key art balances muted vegetation and stone with warm creatures and selective accents. Palworld 03/04 allow colourful creatures while retaining dimensional bodies. F07/F08, F09/F10, and F11/F12 lean so strongly into magenta, cyan, and blue that much of the body is described by hue and black recesses. Scene lighting and contextual palette can be tuned, but enduring local-colour/value separation needs art/material revision. These images do not reveal what alternate art is available in the build.

3. **Evidence of an inhabited adventure rather than isolated specimens.** The key art and Palworld 02/04/05 establish paths, vegetation, terrain layers, structures, and relationships between people and creatures. Every reviewed frame is a small subject on a bare stage. A scene/capture change can supply composition, terrain, planting, landmarks, and encounters; these frames cannot establish whether the necessary environmental art exists. This is a limit of the submitted evidence, not a claim that the game itself contains no world.
