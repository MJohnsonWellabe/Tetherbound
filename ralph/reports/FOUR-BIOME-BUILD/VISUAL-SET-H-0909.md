# Visual review: isolated set H, 2026-09-09

Image-only review of F01.png through F16.png in `.artifacts/review-20260909/set-H/`. Consecutive odd/even frames are matched subjects, with odd=A and even=B. All sixteen images were inspected at their supplied 1280 by 800 size and together at 30% (384 by 240 per frame). All six references were inspected: the Meadows key art and Palworld references 01 through 05. No source, history, treatment description, or other visual cohort informed this assessment. There are no numeric scores.

The creature art does not yet hold up to the reference creatures' clean form hierarchy and expression. The turtle is the strongest friendly character; several others let saturated surface marks and tiny protrusions compete with their faces. B offers modest surface cleanup in several subjects, not a broad improvement in design or readability. No subject shows a convincing silhouette improvement, and none shows a clear overall regression.

## Eight-category rubric

1. **Silhouette and small-size readability.** Broad families are distinguishable in all pairs: turtle, frilled amphibian, clawed round animal and upright reptiles. F07/F08 best preserve a face at 30%. F01/F02, F09/F10 and F15/F16 retain their body shapes but lose facial identity; F05/F06 retain a mouth rather than a complete expression. Palworld 01 and 03 give the large creatures clear facial focal points with broad surrounding forms. The neutral dark background here is a favourable test: separation from grass, rocks or combat effects remains unproven. No trainer is shown.

2. **Colour and value structure.** F01/F02 use glaring yellow against near-black detail; F05/F06 push almost pure magenta against black; F09/F10 use vivid cyan; F11/F12 are nearly all hot red with pale claws. Each is easy to colour-code, but the set lacks the natural greens, warm neutrals and moderated accents of the key art. F07/F08 bring turquoise into a more grounded olive/gold shell relationship. F15/F16 are too dark internally for their plate structure to carry at small size. Palworld supports bright creatures, but its broad colour regions describe anatomy more clearly. F11-F16 include red creatures, but their allegiance is unknown, and those bright reds cannot be equated with Team Tether oxblood or called a semantic violation.

3. **Intentionality.** The eight subjects have recognisable individual outlines. Yet their surface language varies widely: speckled high-contrast reptile in F01/F02, broad orange and blue patches in F03/F04, richly segmented turtle in F07/F08, and shredded-looking fine side fins in F13/F14. This reads less like a cohesive cast than the consistent large-form treatment of the Palworld creatures. That is an appearance judgement, not an assertion about asset provenance. Authored placement, clustering and world composition cannot be judged on these blank stages.

4. **Lighting.** Directional shadows visibly anchor most feet, notably F03/F04 and F07/F08. Strongly lit chests and dark undersides provide form, but F01/F02's yellow and F11/F12's red surfaces are so intense that subtle anatomy is hard to separate. F15/F16's dark head needs clearer plane separation. F11/F12 look suspended over the central shadow; a still cannot distinguish an intended lifted pose from poor contact. No natural time of day can be established from the grey background.

5. **Horizon and depth.** F01-F16 show only a shallow platform against a uniform dark field. There is no environmental depth, atmospheric progression, landmark, or traversable horizon to judge. The slab boundary is visible staging, not evidence of a terrain seam. Palworld 04 and the key art use layered terrain and landmarks to place their creatures in a world; this cohort cannot demonstrate that dimension.

6. **Interface.** F01-F16 contain no HUD, labels, prompts or trainer. Safe area and UI hierarchy are unassessable. The subjects are centred with extensive blank margins and are not cropped, but that is presentation framing rather than a gameplay interface pass.

7. **Artefacts.** F01/F02, F05/F06 and F15/F16 have brittle bright/dark surface breaks that can look like broken detail at full size. F13/F14 have very thin jagged-looking side strands. These are observable visual defects or ambiguities; the images do not establish z-fighting, texture stretching, geometry intersections or their causes. B reduces some harshness without removing it. Static images cannot establish popping, flicker or motion aliasing.

8. **Scale agreement.** No 1.80 m trainer or shared environmental ruler is present in any frame. Similar screen heights across separate framed subjects do not prove similar world sizes. Relative creature role sizes, trainer proportions and metre agreement therefore remain unassessable. Within each pair, apparent size and proportions remain stable. No size correction should be inferred from this cohort.

## Three largest gaps, ranked

1. **Readable creature faces and coherent major forms.** Palworld 01 makes Mammorest's face, mouth, tusks and limbs legible; Palworld 03 gives Grintale a simple expressive facial mass. In F01/F02 and F15/F16, detail and dark patches swallow the face; F11/F12 crowd the centre with competing red forms. F07/F08 show a more successful direction already visible in this cohort. Better framing and gentler lighting can help presentation, but enduring face/shape hierarchy requires creature-art refinement beyond scene scatter or density.

2. **A coherent natural palette with controlled saturated accents.** The key art combines greens, stone and warm neutrals with selective accents, while Palworld separates bright creatures into intelligible colour regions. F01/F02, F05/F06 and F11/F12 instead put extreme colour or black breakup across large areas. Lighting and presentation can moderate the perceived harshness; the surface colour grouping itself needs art/material refinement. The images cannot establish whether an adequate alternative already exists elsewhere in the build.

3. **Creatures participating in an inviting, inhabited world.** The key art supplies paths, settlements and ecological context; Palworld 02, 04 and 05 place readable creatures among paths, vegetation, structures and activity. F01-F16 give none of that context. This is a missing demonstration caused by the scope of the images, not a finding that the game world is empty. Scene composition and an in-world capture can address the evidence gap. Whether additional environment art is required cannot be determined here.

## Overall reference-bar answers

**A. Do these frames read as belonging to the Meadows key-art world? No.** This answer applies to both the odd-file A set and even-file B set. The dominant neon colour relationships and inconsistent surface treatment do not carry the board's grounded, inviting natural palette. The turtle in F07/F08 comes closest in appeal and organic colour balance. Environment, settlement and landscape mood are absent from the evidence, so this is an isolated cast/presentation finding, not a rejection of unseen biome scenes. Scene lighting and framing can improve presentation; face, surface grouping and style consistency need art refinement.

**B. Beside the Palworld screenshots, would someone say these are trying to be the same kind of game? Yes, narrowly for the creature cast.** This answer applies to both A and B. The expressive turtle, exaggerated claws, frilled amphibian and elemental-looking reptiles clearly aim at a fantastical creature-companion game. That genre resemblance does not mean the art meets Palworld's quality bar: the first two ranked gaps remain substantial, and these stages cannot establish the open-world, inhabited-place or combat-event half of that comparison.

The cohort does not provide a full-game visual acceptance result. There is no trainer, environment, interface, shared scale reference or motion; performance and implementation causes are outside this review. B's limited surface improvements are worth retaining visually, but they do not change either reference-bar answer.
