# Independent image-only review — 2026-09-09

## Review conditions

This is a **reused reviewer, not a fresh task**. Before this review I worked on networking and received operational names concerning Crown/Water renders, creature texture imports and an Arlo texture loader issue. I had not viewed the visual implementation, its reports, intended appearance or prior verdicts. During this review I read only the visual-judge skill, its Meadows keyart and all five Palworld reference images, then the supplied contact sheet and full-size frames 01–08, followed by the supplied canonical day/night frames 09–10. No implementation/source map was read. Frame 08's supplied crop removes header whitespace, not body or shadow. Frame ordering is not treated as before/after or an acceptance hint.

The contact sheet was judged at small size before individual frames. These are static visual findings, not functionality, traversal, performance, animation, whole-biome or shipping acceptance. The lineup lacks a trainer ruler, so creature-to-human scale cannot be established. No numerical scores are assigned.

## Frames 01–03: first landmark

At small size the crenellated arch is identifiable, but it reads as a toy castle cutout on a rectangular display plinth. The trainer and figure inside it are recognizable. Frame 01 exposes broad, pale, visibly stippled distant hills and very sparse ground cover. Frame 02 has tree framing and foliage, but the arch remains a flat dark slab; its foreground trainer loses leg/ground separation in the deep shadow. Frame 03 is a different close night view: the arch fills the foreground and hides most of the figure beneath it. The camera difference prevents attributing that occlusion to an asset change. Night reads from the blue sky, but the saturated foliage and light-blue creatures remain conspicuous against a nearly black carpet of ground.

Ranked gaps:

1. **Landmark construction and ground contact (01–03).** Flat teal faces, square crenellations, four cyan rectangles and an exposed straight-edged base have none of the material hierarchy, irregular mass or settled foundations visible in the keyart stronghold and Palworld plateau building. Scene/material work can improve the base, edging, lighting and local dressing. The structure also needs a more resolved architectural form; the supplied frames do not demonstrate that art exists.
2. **Natural palette and ground layers (01–03).** Red rounded bushes, bright lime broad leaves and cobalt creatures compete with the focal point, while the ground is mostly a blurry mottled texture. Palworld 02/04 use low grass, rock edges and clustered vegetation to connect the path and larger forms. Scene-fixable: organize foliage into clusters and clearings, temper the strongest secondary colors, and give the landmark an approach instead of evenly scattered accents. Red vegetation also weakens the keyart's reserved dark-red danger language; its gameplay meaning cannot be inferred here.
3. **Depth and night readability (01,03).** Frame 01's distant surfaces show coarse stippling; frame 03 loses layered landform depth behind large repeated trunks and busy black cloud streaks. Scene/render-fixable: stabilize distance presentation and establish a gentler foreground/midground/background value hierarchy. Still images cannot identify the stippling's technical cause.

**A: No.** The natural setting gestures toward the keyart, but the flat architectural icon and disconnected bright scatter do not belong to its grounded, inviting landscape yet. **B: No.** The simple landmark and ground treatment fall well short of the authored environment language of the five Palworld references. These frames need both scene dressing and better-resolved landmark art; adding more scatter alone would not close the gap.

## Frames 04–05: second landmark, day/night

Both frames show an upright masonry doorway almost edge-on. Stone blocks are visible, with a strong grounded shadow. Frame 04 is warm daylight; frame 05 is clearly cooler night. The night frame gives the far doorway edge a bright face, while its front and the trainer's dark clothing become harder to separate. There is no demonstrated geometry change between the pair. The tall background cliff and its repeating faceted surface dominate both views; the foreground is almost uninterrupted patterned sand/ground.

Ranked gaps:

1. **Place-making (04–05).** The doorway is isolated: no meaningful threshold, approach, adjacent construction or environmental story connects it to the cliff. Palworld 04's ruin and landmark are part of a layered destination. Scene-fixable: frame a front-facing approach and integrate foundations, stones and surrounding terrain. More bespoke doorway shape/detail may be needed, but this near-edge view is insufficient to judge its front design.
2. **Material scale and surface hierarchy (04–05).** Large dark doorway blocks, the giant cliff's polygon-like pattern and fine repetitive ground texture disagree in visual frequency. Scene/material-fixable: reduce repetitive contrast, establish coherent masonry scale and break the cliff into readable masses rather than one nearly continuous wall.
3. **Night subject separation (05).** The trainer and doorway front converge into dark blue/black, while the lintel side becomes disproportionately bright. Scene-fixable: adjust light direction/value relationships so the opening and person remain readable without making the scene daylight-bright.

**A: No.** Natural colors and stone are compatible ingredients, but this barren, disconnected object does not yet read as a place in the keyart world. **B: No.** It lacks the ground layers, environmental context and composed destination visible in Palworld 02/04. Most demonstrated gaps are scene/material work; a better front view is needed before requiring replacement architecture art.

## Frames 06–07: creature lineup

Creature appeal remains a larger issue than the small palette differences. The yellow long-eared animal and cyan broad-winged creature have approachable, distinct silhouettes. The central spider is legible and imposing. The squat orange creature's face is hard to parse, and its body partly overlaps the spider. Strong noisy mottling and very bright surface highlights across several bodies compete with their faces. Palworld's creatures have clearer large color areas and expressive focal features; these do not yet present a consistently polished roster.

Visible pair differences: frame 07 changes the spider's central circular eye cluster from magenta to cyan, giving it substantially better separation from its magenta/black shell. The yellow creature gains purple on its forehead/ears and back instead of the darker brown-gold treatment visible in 06; that makes the accent easier to spot but adds another competing bright hue. I see no convincing silhouette, proportion or staging change, and no material change to the other three creatures sufficient to call a regression. The cyan wing still crosses above the yellow animal and into the spider's visual space in both frames.

Ranked gaps:

1. **Surface noise and palette restraint (06–07).** Large areas of cyan, fluorescent yellow, magenta and coral all demand attention. Clean up material contrast and reserve the brightest accents for faces or defining features. Primarily material/texture work; 07's eye change is a useful local improvement, not a roster-level solution.
2. **Face design/readability (06–07).** The orange creature's features and the crab's small dark eyes are less immediately expressive than the clear face masses in Palworld 01/03/05. Lighting can help; unresolved facial forms or markings require art changes. The images cannot establish whether replacement art already exists elsewhere.
3. **Silhouette isolation (06–07).** Wing/ear/spider overlap and a crowded orange body make individual appeal harder to judge. Scene-fixable: separate the lineup slightly and provide individual three-quarter views. Relative size among the shown creatures is clear, but these frames cannot establish their scale beside a person.

**A: No.** The current synthetic saturation and mixed surface treatment are not integrated with the keyart's natural palette. **B: Yes.** Distinct, stylized companion creatures plainly target the same kind of creature-adventure game. This categorical yes is not art-quality parity: the material noise and inconsistent facial appeal still separate the lineup from the references.

## Frame 08: two trainers

Both bodies and shadows are complete. The figures share a youthful readable face, clear hair silhouette, cream shirt, dark trousers and boots. The right trainer's jacket reads as a stronger blue with more visible surface pattern; the left jacket is more muted/dark teal. That improves sleeve/torso separation on the right. At contact-sheet size, it remains a modest accent change rather than a new character identity. No visible silhouette, limb proportion, foot placement or garment-coverage regression is established by this pair.

Ranked gaps:

1. **Character identity (08).** Both figures remain visually almost the same generic adventure youth. Palworld's player reference has strong hair/accessory and garment masses. A genuinely more distinctive identity would require costume/accessory or character art design, not only a jacket recolor; this observation does not assume the pair is meant to represent different characters.
2. **Texture coherence (08).** The right jacket and dark trousers show more fine speckle than the broad clean face/shirt forms. Material/texture-fixable: retain the stronger blue separation while avoiding noisy cloth that disappears at gameplay distance.
3. **Production-context proof (08).** A flat ochre stage demonstrates body coverage and shadow contact, but cannot show whether either treatment separates from foliage, sand or night backgrounds. Scene-fixable evidence gap: match gameplay views before claiming general readability improvement.

**A: Yes.** Teal/blue, cream and leather accents, readable proportions and an approachable face are compatible with the keyart's trainer language. **B: Yes.** The stylized adventure trainer reads as part of this game category. Neither yes proves distinctive hero quality or a gameplay-distance benefit from the accent edit.

## Frames 09–10: canonical meadow, day/night

The shared camera makes this pair directly comparable. Daylight has a recognizable rolling meadow, grouped trees, several creatures and a clear trainer silhouette. Night keeps their positions but loses the trainer's legs, most creature features and much of the terrain form to nearly black values. A pale circular moon and a small left-hand glow become stronger focal points than the playable space. This is a visible readability regression from day to night, not evidence of a geometry change or proof that night is meant to be daylight-bright.

Ranked gaps:

1. **Night value separation (10).** The keyart's night panel retains readable hills, silhouettes and paths with selective warm/cool accents. Here the trainer's blue upper back is visible but the legs and immediate terrain largely merge. Scene-fixable: retain low night values while giving the character and traversable land distinct value bands; avoid using the bright, hard-edged moon as the main source of perceived contrast.
2. **Ground layering and repetition (09–10).** Thin upright grass strokes sit over yellow-green mottled ground, with repeated similar-height leafy stems spread across it. Palworld 02/03 builds broad grassy masses, softer transitions and intentional clearings. Scene/material-fixable: cluster low cover into fuller patches, vary the repeated stem spacing/scale and maintain readable open ground. More object count is not itself the answer.
3. **Creature and horizon readability (09–10).** The dark creature on the left and pale cluster right of center do provide inhabitants, but their body surfaces read as speckled patches at this distance; at night their forms largely vanish. The central tree cluster becomes a solid dark wall, with little receding terrain separation. Lighting, clustering and material simplification can improve this. Expressive creature-face quality still depends on art and cannot be established at this camera distance.

**A: No for this day/night pair.** The day frame clearly moves toward the keyart's green meadow and grouped groves; the night frame does not preserve its readable, inviting/mysterious layered world. **B: No.** This pair still lacks the reference game's cohesive ground masses, controlled surface noise and clear inhabitant silhouettes. The demonstrated environmental gaps are mainly scene/material fixes. No new environment art requirement is established by these two images alone.

## Three largest reference gaps across the supplied frames

1. **Creature surface and face coherence (06–07):** Palworld uses clearer feature hierarchy and large color masses; these bright mottled surfaces often compete with their own faces.
2. **Landmark art and setting (01–05):** the references establish destinations through architecture, foundations and surrounding terrain; the supplied landmarks read as isolated simple objects.
3. **Ground, depth and night value composition (01–05,09–10):** Palworld layers grass, clearings, rocks and distant masses; these views often substitute a repeated ground texture and bright scatter, while frame 10 loses the player and terrain into the same dark band.

The local eye-contrast and jacket-separation improvements are visible. They do not close the larger environment and roster-art gaps above.
