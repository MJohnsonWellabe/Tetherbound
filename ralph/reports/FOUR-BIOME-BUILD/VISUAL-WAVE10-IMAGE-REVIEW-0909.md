# Wave 10: Torrentoad A/B sequence image review

Reused reviewer, not a fresh task. Operational context and earlier creature appearances are known. No author change intent, capture source, manifest or author report was read for this sequence. Inputs were only the eight `A/B-t000`, `t050`, `t100`, `t120` PNGs in `shots/creatures-attack-anticipation/torrentoad/`, plus a fresh inspection of the Meadows key art and all five Palworld reference screenshots.

## Visible sequence comparison

**B shows the clearer sequence of distinct poses.** Both start with effectively the same broad crouched orange/teal creature. A-t050 and A-t100 remain very similar in height and silhouette, followed by a low head/raised-back pose at A-t120. B-t050 already has a pronounced lowered head and raised back; B-t100 rises visibly with the forefeet lifted clear of the plane, and B-t120 settles toward the starting stance. At small size B communicates compression then release more clearly than A's long similar-pose interval.

That is a narrow pose-readability preference, not proof of a better attack in motion. These four samples do not show interpolation, acceleration, input latency, hit timing, a target, or recovery duration. The filenames' time labels are not independent timing evidence. A targetless rise can read as a hop or body bob rather than an attack; none of these stills establishes a convincing impact.

## Concrete defects and regressions

1. **Forefeet visibly disappear into the plane in the low pose:** B-t050 loses the front digits and reads as truncated forearms at ground level; A-t120 exhibits the same defect. The rest of the sequence plainly shows long spread digits, making the disappearance conspicuous. B exposes that compromised pose earlier in its sequence. This is a pose/contact or staging defect to resolve with existing assets, not a reason to require a new mesh by itself.
2. **Surface markings compete with the face in every frame.** Teal patches over the back are readable, but scattered grey and brown chips across the brow, muzzle, limbs and toes resemble worn paint. The eye and mouth shapes are difficult to separate from that noise. Palworld's creature examples present much clearer face features and broad, intentional colour regions. Material/texture revision can address much of this; source inspection would be needed to distinguish surface detail from geometry limitations.
3. **The creature reads as a heavy generic frog-like brute more than a distinctive companion.** The large forelimbs and broad back survive reduced size, but the small obscured eyes and tight mouth communicate little expression. B changes the mass silhouette without adding a clearer expressive face or attack direction. Better pose staging and facial contrast are available within the scene/material lane; a substantially more distinctive facial design may need authored creature art or rig work.

The raised B-t100 forefeet have visible separation from their shadows; this is legible lifting rather than automatically a grounding error. The low-pose intersections are the stronger concrete contact defect. No colour/style improvement between A and B is apparent: they share the same surface problem.

## Direct bars

**A — Commercial visual bar: No.** B's stronger sampled anticipation/release does not overcome the disappearing digits, noisy material and weak facial readability. The reference creatures have clearer expressions and more controlled surface grouping.

**B — Category coherence: Yes.** The exaggerated proportions and brightly coloured fantasy animal plausibly belong to a stylized third-person creature adventure of the same broad category. This is not parity with the Palworld examples.

For the skill's separate key-art question: **No as currently presented**; the mottled, paint-chipped surface lacks the board's clean natural palette grouping, and a bare stage cannot establish environmental belonging. No per-pixel comparison against painted art was made.

Prefer B only for the visible progression of poses. Neither sequence earns full creature or animation acceptance from these stills. Relative trainer scale, world lighting, movement continuity, combat function, performance and whole-biome quality are outside this evidence.

## Narrow-change clarification

B's more distinct sampled compression/rise/settle progression is a supported narrow visual improvement. **No new B-only visible regression was identified in these eight images.** The lost digits at B-t050 are also visible in A-t120; they are a shared low-pose defect, not evidence that B introduced a new contact failure. B-t100's lifted feet are visibly separated from their shadows and do not independently establish a defect. This comparison does not infer when contact should occur or how either sequence interpolates.

The image evidence therefore does not supply a new B-only regression that would block the narrow pose change. Commercial acceptance remains No because the shared contact, material and facial-readability defects remain. Neither the narrow preference nor the absence of a newly identified regression automatically accepts the change for shipping; that decision requires its other scoped evidence and an explicit treatment of the shared defects.
