## Verdict

**A narrow improvement is supported in B: its sampled poses have a clearer compressed-to-raised contrast.** This is acceptance of visible pose distinction only. It is not acceptance of a complete attack animation or commercial creature quality.

**The commercial art bar is not met.** The creature has a readable broad amphibian silhouette, but its face lacks clear eye expression and its patchy orange/brown/cyan surface makes the head and torso look mottled rather than deliberately designed. These weaknesses remain in both alternatives.

## Defects and regression assessment

- **Apparent floor clipping or loss of front-foot silhouette: `B-t050` and `A-t120`.** The spread front digits disappear at the floor and the forelimbs terminate as blunt, nearly flat stumps. Compare the intact fingers in either t000 frame. This reads as penetration/contact failure in the offered views, although an alternate angle would be needed to distinguish occlusion from penetration conclusively. B exposes the same visible problem at a different sample; it is not a defect unique to B.
- **Raised hands need motion evidence: `B-t100`, also `B-t120`.** The front digits sit above their separated shadows. This is a visible change in support, not proof of floating or sliding. Check their trajectory and intended body support in motion before accepting contact quality.
- **Unclear face: all eight frames, especially `A-t050` and `B-t100`.** Cyan patches cross the brow, cheek and mouth line without producing easily located eyes. At small size the face becomes an orange mass with turquoise scrapes. The references give creatures distinct eyes, brows and simple facial value groups that preserve expression.
- **Noisy surface hierarchy: all eight frames.** The orange, brown and cyan boundaries form irregular speckles and broad blotches across the same anatomy. Chest folds and arms merge into a muddy mass on the shadowed side. The cyan reads more like scattered exposed underpaint than controlled species markings.

**No newly introduced defect unique to B is established by these eight images.** The floor-contact failure exists in A as well. That does not make it acceptable in B, and the stills do not clear B of possible motion defects.

## Three largest gaps from the references, ranked

1. **Creature appeal and facial authorship — all frames, clearest in `A-t050` and `B-t100`.** Palworld's boss and meadow creatures use legible faces and controlled body shapes; this face has little visible eye expression and indistinct facial landmarks. Requires creature surface/face art work, and potentially anatomy or rig work if facial landmarks cannot be exposed with the installed asset. Scene dressing alone cannot solve it. These images do not establish that buying a new mesh is necessary.
2. **Designed color and material grouping — all frames.** The references reserve saturated accents and separate body regions into coherent readable groups. This creature's strong orange/cyan contrast is fragmented over a brown base. Better markings and surface treatment are required; lighting can assist value separation but cannot author the markings.
3. **Convincing pose contact and presentation — `B-t050`, `A-t120`, and the blank setting in all frames.** Reference creatures have recognizable limbs and grounded relationships to their surroundings; the crouches here lose the digits at the floor. Contact/pose adjustments are actionable without new art. A real world/combat view is also needed to assess the references' environmental and encounter presentation; an empty test floor supplies no evidence for those bars.
