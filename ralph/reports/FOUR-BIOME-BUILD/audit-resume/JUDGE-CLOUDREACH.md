# Blind visual verdict: Cloudreach

The creatures do not yet meet the reference's appeal and material coherence. The two yellow animals at Galefoot have recognisable faces and ears, but their nearly solid bright yellow surfaces with dark patches flatten their forms and look disconnected from the detailed timber houses. The white-blue wolf has a clear face and a promising silhouette, but is crowded against the frame edge and another saturated creature. The front-facing trainer is readable and stylistically approachable; neither the trainer nor these creatures is staged in an expressive relationship. This reads as a prototype environment containing some developed assets.

Inspected the supplied Cloudreach sheet at thumbnail scale and all 24 canonical `cloudreach__*.png` frames at full resolution in `shots/catalogue/cloudreach/round-20260909T004031Z/`. Used the full unmodified visual-judge rubric, Meadows key art, and five Palworld references. No source, logs, implementation notes, or other reports were read. Different biome content is not itself a failure against the Meadows board: the comparison concerns shared palette discipline, silhouette, landmark language, composition, and mood.

## All eight rubric categories

1. **Small-size silhouette and readability.** Trainer silhouette is reliably distinguishable in unobstructed day shots, helped by a pale shirt against green ground. The bridge is identifiable at thumbnail scale in `cloudreach__broken_causeways__03__three_bells_bridge__day.png`, and the wolf and yellow animals read as creatures. Several other locations reduce to a brown column or stone close-up. `cloudreach__windscar_ravine__05__windscar_beacon__day.png` is a direct failure of scene readability, not a subtle art preference.

2. **Colour and values.** Green grass, grey rock and pale sky make a consistent highland base. The yellow animals in `cloudreach__gate_lower_cliffs__02__galefoot_waycamp__day.png` and clipped purple animal in the aerie are much more saturated and flatter than their surroundings. The nighttime distance is unusually pale compared with the player, particularly `cloudreach__broken_causeways__04__broken_skyroad_arch__night.png`. The camp's dark red tower banner warrants a faction-colour check; the image does not prove that a friendly object uses danger colour.

3. **Intentionality.** The bridge and rising village paths show purposeful connectivity. Many named landmarks are communicated by repetitive giant primitives over empty floors: `cloudreach__high_roost_sky_shrine__08__the_high_perches__day.png` is the clearest example. Patchy grass edges have geometric contours; repeated animals at the camp resemble a placed pair more than a lived-in creature space. The Palworld base reference has functional activity and varied occupied ground; this camp's bed, ring and duplicated animals do not reach that level of life.

4. **Lighting.** Directional shadows ground the trainer and provide substantial shape in daylight. Day/night tint changes are visible, but `cloudreach__upper_cloudreach__09__cliffhold__night.png` could read as dark foreground under a bright overcast sky without the time label and moon. Bright creatures, pale distant cliffs and dark ground do not feel exposed as one scene. Some night ground is readable, so this is not a claim that every night frame is black.

5. **Horizon and depth.** The cliffs are Cloudreach's strongest environmental feature. The bridge, road, and repeated height levels convey distance in `cloudreach__broken_causeways__03__three_bells_bridge__day.png`. However, long uninterrupted cliff bands, visibly simple cloud shapes and weak intermediate terrain detail expose the construction of the vista. At night, haze washes away distant form in `cloudreach__upper_cloudreach__10__old_wind_observatory__night.png`. No certain LOD/chunk transition can be diagnosed from stills.

6. **Interface.** HUD remains within safe margins, and interaction/route instructions are legible. The large empty slot cards and command strip consume a large share of the lower-right area in every image. At Galefoot and the aerie they conceal the very creatures that should sell the game. Time and status values have weaker contrast than the persistent quest box. Judged on its own, not against Palworld UI.

7. **Artefacts.** Shrine and beacon camera obstruction are unequivocal in the supplied frames. The overlook omits the trainer and view. The close shrine cylinder exposes stretched masonry; Cliffhold/Galefoot expose hard polygon material borders; overlook night exposes strong repetitive floor striping. These are visible defects regardless of whether their causes are geometry, material setup, or camera placement. No temporal popping or motion performance conclusion follows.

8. **Scale agreement.** With a 1.80 m trainer, houses, bridge rails, and the bed broadly agree about human scale. The yellow pair are large compared with the bed and person, but intended species role is not known; size alone is not a failure. The wolf is taller than the trainer and plausibly a large companion. The huge plain pillars lack intermediate scale cues and make spaces feel miniature around them. No close, same-depth companion/practice pair with known roles appears, so role-based scale hierarchy remains unverified.

## Three largest reference gaps, ranked

1. **Creature material coherence and staging.** Palworld's creatures have controlled colour groups, readable anatomy and expressions integrated into a scene or event. `cloudreach__gate_lower_cliffs__02__galefoot_waycamp__day.png` has flat bright duplicate animals behind a bed; `cloudreach__windscar_ravine__06__windscar_flight_aerie__day.png` crops and overlays its most promising creature. Placement, framing and lighting are in-scene work. Better creature colour/material art and expressive variation require art work.

2. **Landmarks remain primitive or hidden.** The key-art stronghold and Palworld plateau use articulated destinations with clear silhouettes and approaches. `cloudreach__high_roost_sky_shrine__08__the_high_perches__day.png` presents anonymous columns; `cloudreach__high_roost_sky_shrine__07__sky_shrine__day.png` blocks the landmark with a cylinder. Camera clearance, composition, and asset placement are scene work. Developed shrine/perch/ruin architecture needs new or substantially reworked art, unless appropriate unused art already exists; screenshots cannot establish the inventory.

3. **Ground and atmosphere expose the assembly.** Palworld's terrain transitions and foliage form a coherent surface; key-art distance is layered rather than a collection of equally pale shapes. `cloudreach__upper_cloudreach__09__cliffhold__day.png` shows angular dirt/grass patches, and `cloudreach__upper_cloudreach__10__old_wind_observatory__night.png` shows a bright flattened cloud mass. Ground blending, foliage clustering, value balance and haze are scene/material work. Grass form, cloud visual treatment and close masonry texture may need new art.

## Direct bar answers

**A. Belongs to the Meadows key-art world? No.** It shares a fantasy traveller, green land, stone, timber buildings and distant cliffs. The giant primitive columns, visibly tiled ground, flat cloud shapes and pale night background do not sustain the board's natural stylised world or warm/mysterious mood. This is not a demand that a mountain region look like a meadow.

**In-scene A work:** camera clearance; meaningful landmark and creature framing; richer clustered ground cover; soft material transitions; balanced night sky/terrain/creature exposure; layered cliff composition. **New/reworked A art:** finished shrine/ruin/perch forms, coherent cloud and grass treatment, consistent close-up surface art. Inventory beyond the visible frame is unknown.

**B. Would a viewer say it is trying to be the same kind of game as Palworld? Yes.** The front-facing stylised trainer, large cute animals, wolf, camps, wilderness paths, bridges and stronghold communicate creature-adventure intent. This literal yes does not establish comparable shipping quality. No combat event is shown, so fight spectacle is unproven.

**Scene/art split for the remaining B quality gap:** stop hiding the creatures behind HUD/camera edges, place them in readable activities, and establish environmental contrast in-scene. Refine the creature materials and expressive art; obtain or make finished landmark/environment pieces where these images only show primitive forms.

## Product questions

- **Commercial finish or prototype?** Prototype with recognisable regional ambition and some developed assets.
- **Three visible blockers?** Obstructed landmark cameras; primitive/visibly tiled architecture and ground; mismatched nighttime exposure between characters, land and sky.
- **Reason to explore?** Yes in the bridge and ascending cliff paths. Too many other locations fail to show their destination.
- **Distinct regional identity?** Yes at the broad level: high cliffs, suspended bridges and a cloud sea distinguish it from ordinary meadow terrain. Shrine, aerie and summit identities remain underdeveloped within that region.
- **Creature as visual/emotional focus?** Intermittently a visual focus at camp and aerie, but not an emotional focus. Duplication, cropping and material imbalance weaken appeal.
- **Comparable quality tier?** Early independent 3D adventure/prototype, below the supplied finished Palworld scenes in integrated art quality.

**Readiness: not visually ready for acceptance.** Still frames cannot establish animation, traversal, flight feel, combat, performance, or popping. Occluded landmarks must be reframed before their hidden contents can be judged.
