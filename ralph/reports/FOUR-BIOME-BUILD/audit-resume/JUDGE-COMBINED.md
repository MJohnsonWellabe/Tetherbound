# Blind combined visual verdict: four biomes

The creature cast and trainer do not yet form a consistent, expressive centre for a finished game. The Meadows owl, Cloudreach wolf and Water Aquaryn show promising silhouettes. They coexist with flat bright yellow duplicates, extreme magenta/cyan/blue creature close-ups, dark unreadable animals, and characters apparently suspended or obscured. These are not minor differences between regions: they prevent the cast from reading as one deliberately designed product at the reference quality.

Reviewed the complete 116-frame combined sheet at small size and all four legible split pages, after inspecting every canonical individual frame: Cloudreach 24, Meadows 20, Stormwood 24, Water 48. The images' actual combined order is Cloudreach → Meadows → Stormwood → Water. The tall all-frame sheet was useful only for broad value/palette patterns at its displayed size; the split pages and individual images supplied readable detail. Used the full unmodified visual-judge rubric, Meadows key art and five Palworld references. No source, implementation context, logs or other reports were read, and no existing verdict was revised to fit a combined result.

## Cohesion across the regions

The broad region concepts are distinguishable: a village/meadow, cliffs above clouds, a grey-trunk forest with red undergrowth, and bare islands surrounded by water. The trainer and HUD supply continuity. Timber houses and some familiar creature forms also connect regions.

The level of environmental development is inconsistent. Meadows village contains roofs, fences, people and layered props (`meadows__band1_lower_meadows__01__grandpas_village__day.png`). Water Reedhaven offers a bare plateau with a creature and scattered tiny people (`water__reedhaven__03__reedhaven_woven_hall__day.png`). Cloudreach contains detailed cottages beside giant simple pillars (`cloudreach__high_roost_sky_shrine__08__the_high_perches__day.png`). Stormwood places plain white/cyan monuments among textured trees (`stormwood__cinder_verge__02__verge_rod_station__day.png`). Different cultural architecture would be welcome; different apparent stages of completion are not coherent art direction.

Night is especially inconsistent. Meadows regularly crushes players and paths into black; Cloudreach keeps a pale sky and almost white distance above dark foreground; Stormwood retains relatively flat visibility on nearby trees/props; Water combines dark trainers with bright sea or washed animals. This is visible simultaneously on the combined pages, without needing a claim about renderer internals.

## All eight rubric categories

1. **Silhouette and small-size readability.** Roofs, cliff bands, white primitives, red shrubs and bare islands establish coarse categories. Strong individual creature profiles survive in `meadows__band4_upper_meadows_ironwood__07__the_ironwood_grove__day.png` and `water__tidal_cradle__09__aquaryn_tidal_basin__night.png`. The survey too often replaces identifiable player/creature relationships with an obstructing surface: `meadows__band2_stone_and_root__04__the_burrow_warrens__day.png`, `cloudreach__windscar_ravine__05__windscar_beacon__day.png`, `stormwood__conductor_run__06__the_capacitor_grove__day.png`, and `water__veilfall__16__veilfall_mountain_crown__day.png`. These failures span every biome.

2. **Colour and value structure.** Each biome has a coarse colour identity, but the cast and environment do not share consistent saturation/exposure rules. Compare the purple owl's controlled broad separation in Meadows with the hot magenta spider in `stormwood__dynamo__11__the_glass_field__day.png`, the pale yellow pair in `cloudreach__gate_lower_cliffs__02__galefoot_waycamp__night.png`, and the beige animal against a dark trainer in `water__reedhaven__03__reedhaven_woven_hall__night.png`. Oxblood is clearly useful on Meadows Hall banners; red undergrowth across Stormwood competes with its exclusivity as a danger signal. Faction ownership of every coloured object/animal is not visually established.

3. **Intentionality.** Meadows village, Cloudreach bridge and some ascending routes communicate design. Open fields and Water locations repeatedly look like bare terrain with evenly scattered assets or pickups. `water__first_shore__01__first_shore_welcome_beacon__day.png` does not stage a welcome; `stormwood__deepwood__09__lantern_hollow__day.png` stages plain repeated bases; `cloudreach__high_roost_sky_shrine__08__the_high_perches__day.png` stages giant columns. Named places do not consistently have a visible focal object, approach, clearing and supporting local detail.

4. **Lighting.** Day/night differences are visible, and many directional shadows ground objects by day. Their night treatments do not feel like one world's exposure policy. `meadows__band3_the_river_lock__06__old_mill_crossing__night.png` loses the path and trainer; `cloudreach__broken_causeways__04__broken_skyroad_arch__night.png` has a pale distant vista over dark foreground; `stormwood__cinder_verge__02__verge_rod_station__night.png` keeps an almost white monument; `water__veilfall__15__the_veilfall_cascade__night.png` almost erases the world in white. Local motivating light is rare outside windows, pickups and the Hall. No performance or hardware conclusion follows from these pictures.

5. **Horizon and depth.** Cloudreach cliff masses and Water island spacing provide clear regional depth. Meadows often lacks a strong distant vista and Stormwood's tree layer exposes empty hills. Compare `cloudreach__broken_causeways__03__three_bells_bridge__day.png` with `stormwood__glowmoss_hollows__04__crown_overlook__day.png`. The key art and Palworld plateau establish layered destinations; the supplied views often hide landmarks behind nearby trunks, pillars or ridges. Water's broad hard-edged tonal divisions and Cloudreach's flat cloud ovals look artificial. Still images cannot identify their implementation cause or test LOD transitions.

6. **Interface.** The consistent HUD helps these images belong to one game, and prompts/quest copy are legible within safe margins. Large blank slots and the long command strip repeatedly hide important lower-right world space. In `cloudreach__windscar_ravine__06__windscar_flight_aerie__day.png`, panels overlap a promising creature; in `stormwood__dynamo__11__the_glass_field__day.png`, they add to an already obstructed frame. Status/time contrast is weak. This is an independent interface judgement; the rubric forbids comparing UI to Palworld.

7. **Artefacts.** Camera/actor/prop overlap is systemic in the delivered survey. Concrete examples: blocked Burrow frames; close shrine cylinder in `cloudreach__high_roost_sky_shrine__07__sky_shrine__day.png`; apparent floating people/supplies in `meadows__band5_stronghold_approach__10__meadows_hall__day.png`; clipped trainer legs and giant spider in `stormwood__conductor_run__06__the_capacitor_grove__day.png`; waterfall whiteout with sharp boundary in `water__veilfall__15__the_veilfall_cascade__night.png`. These are visible presentation defects. Motion could explain some individual airborne poses, but not make the delivered obstructed views acceptable. No specific collision, shader, or capture mechanism is inferred.

8. **Scale agreement.** Taking the trainer as 1.80 m, Meadows buildings/fences and Cloudreach bridge/bed generally support plausible human scale. Owl and Aquaryn have legible large-creature relationships in their good frames. Stormwood giant animals fill so much of the camera that faces and ground contact are excluded, while nearby adult-looking people and oversized candy introduce awkward person/prop relationships (`stormwood__conductor_run__05__rodline_post__day.png`). Large species are not inherently wrong. Intended roles, exact camera distances and practice/companion pairing are not established, so role-based hierarchy remains unverified. The camera must accommodate the relative sizes actually shown.

## Three largest reference gaps, ranked

1. **A coherent, expressive creature-and-trainer presentation.** Palworld's boss/field shots communicate faces, anatomy, creature personality and a clear event involving a person. Here the best silhouettes are isolated exceptions; `stormwood__hollow_crown__08__the_crown_heartstone__day.png` shows a giant lower body, and `water__veilfall__16__veilfall_mountain_crown__day.png` shows a faceless close surface. Scene work: camera clearance, placement/contact, encounter staging, relative-scale accommodation and light contrast. Art work: consistent character/creature material language, controlled colour groups and expressive variation. No fight is actually shown; fight spectacle is unproven rather than inferred from noncombat images.

2. **A consistently authored and finished environment.** The references use continuous ground/foliage, believable occupied places, and developed landmarks. `water__reedhaven__03__reedhaven_woven_hall__day.png` lacks settlement presentation; `stormwood__hollow_crown__07__the_crown_arch__day.png` presents a plain primitive; `cloudreach__high_roost_sky_shrine__08__the_high_perches__day.png` presents anonymous columns. Scene work: habitat clusters, approach paths, focal compositions, terrain/material transitions and local prop use. Art work: finished regional architecture, landmarks and vegetation where the current visible asset forms are inadequate. The images do not reveal whether suitable unused assets exist.

3. **Unified lighting, atmosphere and surface treatment.** The key art uses natural colour/value groups, inviting local warmth, and layered distance; Palworld's ground and creatures share a scene's light. `meadows__band1_lower_meadows__01__grandpas_village__night.png`, `cloudreach__upper_cloudreach__10__old_wind_observatory__night.png`, and `water__veilfall__15__the_veilfall_cascade__night.png` expose three incompatible failures: crushed foreground, over-pale distance, and whiteout. Scene/material work can address exposure, local lights, haze, water/effect strength and ground blending. New/reworked grass, cloud, cliff and close texture art may be needed to remove visibly synthetic forms.

## Direct bar answers

**A. Do the combined frames belong to the Meadows key-art world? No.** Shared traveller, fantasy species, timber/stone and natural terrain ingredients are visible. The complete survey does not sustain the board's grounded habitat, natural palette discipline, landmark composition or inviting/mysterious mood. Biome variety is not the problem; differences in apparent asset completion and lighting coherence are.

**In-scene A work:** eliminate blocking camera/actor arrangements; give every location a visible approach and focal point; compose vegetation and occupied spaces; align day/night value relationships; integrate water/cloud effects and surface transitions. **New/reworked A art:** a coherent expressive cast, developed regional landmark/architecture kits, and consistent foliage/terrain/effect art. Existing visible primitives and noisy surfaces cannot all be solved by scatter density alone.

**B. Would viewers say this is trying to be the same kind of game as Palworld? Yes.** Across the survey, a third-person trainer explores natural regions with fantastical creatures, camps, settlements, challenge prompts and a hostile stronghold. That communicates the same broad creature-adventure ambition. The exact question asks about intent/type; this yes is not acceptance of shipping quality. The supplied Palworld references are substantially more integrated, inhabited and expressive.

**Scene/art split for the remaining B quality gap:** clear and stage the creature/player experience, restore readable contact/scale/composition, and build coherent habitats in-scene. Refine or make expressive creature/character art and finished regional environment assets where current visible forms cannot support the intended identity.

## Product questions

- **Finished commercial game or prototype?** Prototype with some developed assets and recognisable world ambitions; not a finished commercial visual presentation.
- **Three visible blockers?** Repeated camera/actor/prop occlusion and apparent placement errors; large areas of bare or primitive environment presentation; inconsistent creature material and night-lighting treatment.
- **Reason to explore?** Some: village, suspended bridge, island peak, owl, wolf and Aquaryn. Too many named locations fail to show a distinct destination or inhabited place, weakening the promise between those moments.
- **Distinct regional identities?** Yes in broad terrain categories. Meadows is grassy/settled, Cloudreach cliff/cloud, Stormwood grey woodland/red undergrowth, Water open islands. Subregional identities and a common finish standard remain weak.
- **Creature as visual/emotional focus?** No consistently. A few creature silhouettes are promising, but many animals are tiny, hidden, visually intrusive or detached from habitat. No shown emotional interaction establishes the relationship at the heart of the game.
- **Comparable quality tier?** An early independent 3D adventure/asset-integration prototype, below the supplied shipping Palworld scenes in integrated quality. This is a visual tier judgement, not a budget or performance judgement.

**Combined readiness: not visually ready for acceptance.** All 116 supplied canonical frames were inspected; the judgement does not infer hidden assets or unseen gameplay. Static evidence cannot establish animation appeal, combat feel, traversal, swimming/flight, interaction reliability, performance or popping. Occluded scenes need readable replacement frames before their hidden content can be accepted.
