# Blind visual verdict: Stormwood

The creatures and trainer are the first problem. Giant magenta spiders, a cyan quadruped and a blue bear dominate multiple views through extreme proximity and saturated surface treatment. Their clipped bodies and obscured faces read as camera intrusions, not expressive creature encounters. Repeated elevated trainer placement makes several scenes look physically broken. Small yellow fox-like creatures have the strongest approachable silhouettes, but the overall cast lacks the controlled materials and integrated staging of the Palworld references.

Inspected the Stormwood sheet at thumbnail scale and all 24 full-resolution canonical `stormwood__*.png` images in `shots/catalogue/stormwood/round-20260909T004513Z/`. Applied the full unmodified visual-judge rubric with the Meadows key art and five Palworld references. Read no source, implementation context, logs, or other reports. No numeric scores or performance inference are used. This darker forest need not duplicate the Meadows biome; it still needs to belong to its visual world.

## All eight rubric categories

1. **Small-size readability.** Grey trunks, red shrubs, pale primitives, magenta spiders and blue/cyan large animals remain distinguishable on the sheet. But recognisable colour blobs are not sufficient creature readability: `stormwood__conductor_run__06__the_capacitor_grove__day.png` loses body and face behind limbs and frame edges. Player readability fails through occlusion in the Struck Sentinel, rod station, spider, and Stormheart frames. In `stormwood__glowmoss_hollows__04__crown_overlook__day.png`, the unobstructed trainer reads clearly enough.

2. **Colour and values.** The region consistently uses grey trunks, green canopy, dark mossy-brown ground, red shrubs and cream fungi. This is an identifiable base. The magenta/cyan/blue creature surfaces and white monuments do not fit its value and saturation discipline. `stormwood__cinder_verge__02__verge_rod_station__night.png` retains an almost white central object with no convincing emitted-light relationship to nearby ground. Red shrubs spread oxblood-adjacent colour throughout ordinary vegetation (`stormwood__glowmoss_hollows__03__the_lantern_pools__day.png`), reducing red's exclusivity as a danger cue. Exact faction ownership or shader colour cannot be inferred, but the visual competition is clear.

3. **Intentionality.** Many trees have similar trunk colour and scale, and undergrowth repeats as separated red/green/cream clusters. `stormwood__glowmoss_hollows__04__crown_overlook__day.png` looks like a sparsely populated field of trees more than layered deep forest. The repeated pale bases at Lantern Hollow and duplicate orange creatures at the rod station resemble staged assets. Landmarks need authored approach spaces, occupied clearings and stronger foreground/middle/background relationships.

4. **Lighting.** Day/night are easy to tell from sky and moon. The ground and objects often remain nearly uniformly legible at both times, with limited motivated lighting. `stormwood__deepwood__09__lantern_hollow__night.png` does not show a compelling lantern-lit hollow; a house window is bright, but the scene lacks a warm focal pool. Day shadows provide some contact and tree form. Neon creature brightness is insufficiently integrated, and floating compositions undermine contact regardless of shadow quality.

5. **Horizon and depth.** Tree overlap gives some near depth, but the forest often stops after a thin layer, exposing nearly empty low hills. `stormwood__glowmoss_hollows__04__crown_overlook__day.png` and `stormwood__hollow_crown__08__the_crown_heartstone__day.png` lack a composed distant forest or landmark. Fog does not appear to hide a rich middle distance; it exposes the sparse horizon. No definite LOD transition is provable from still images. The horizontal beam in the Stormheart shots is a compositional obstruction, not enough evidence to name a chunk seam.

6. **Interface.** Panels stay inside safe margins and interaction text is readable. The large blank five-slot panel and command strip consume substantial space, often over already congested creature bodies. `stormwood__dynamo__11__the_glass_field__day.png` illustrates the combined obstruction. Tiny key labels and low-contrast time/status text have poor hierarchy relative to the quest panel. Minimap interiors are mostly featureless in these views, providing little visible landmark support. Interface is judged independently of Palworld's UI.

7. **Artefacts.** Central tree/creature occlusion, clipped trainer legs, and repeated elevated actor placement are directly visible. Near-white rod, plain arch and plain circular bases look unfinished against the textured world. Oversized glowing candy intersects people in `stormwood__conductor_run__05__rodline_post__day.png`. Without motion, a single airborne actor could be jumping; repeated images of humans, creatures and pickups in awkward overlaps establish a presentation defect, but not its technical cause. No claim about collision code, frame rate, popping or animation timing is made.

8. **Scale agreement.** Taking the trainer as 1.80 m, the nearby adult-looking person at Rodline Post is much shorter despite being closer to camera; the trainer's apparent elevation complicates floor comparison. The pickup candy is a large fraction of that person's torso, and a potion plant at Lantern Hollow reaches prominently between the trainer's boots. These prop/person relationships are visibly awkward. The fox-like creatures at Rodline Post are smaller than the trainer; the cyan quadruped, spider and blue bear are many times larger in the supplied framing. Large species are allowed; screenshots do not establish their intended roles. Same-colour distant bears cannot be measured against the close giant without distance information. A companion-versus-practice scale hierarchy is therefore unverified, while the camera's inability to accommodate these relative masses is demonstrated.

## Three largest reference gaps, ranked

1. **Creature presentation is intrusive and stylistically incoherent.** Palworld's boss images keep expressive faces, a coherent action silhouette, and a readable trainer relationship even with huge animals. `stormwood__conductor_run__06__the_capacitor_grove__day.png` and `stormwood__hollow_crown__08__the_crown_heartstone__day.png` offer clipped limbs/lower body instead. Camera distance, actor placement and lighting are scene work. Controlled creature colour/material art and expressive forms need art work; framing alone cannot make the current hot-pink/black treatment coherent with the forest.

2. **Visible development props and physical presentation failures.** The key art's shrine/stronghold language and Palworld's inhabited base are integrated structures and objects with legible purposes. `stormwood__hollow_crown__07__the_crown_arch__day.png` and `stormwood__deepwood__09__lantern_hollow__day.png` show plain primitives, while `stormwood__deepwood__10__the_fallen_giant__night.png` shows apparently suspended actors. Grounding, placement, camera clearance and material assignment are scene work. Finished arch, monument and hollow props require art if no suitable existing assets are available; the images do not expose that inventory.

3. **A thin scattered forest replaces environmental depth and discovery.** The key art's grove creates canopy enclosure, layered vegetation and a path; Palworld's forest fight has dense context around a clear event. `stormwood__glowmoss_hollows__04__crown_overlook__day.png` ends in empty hills behind a few trunks. `stormwood__glowmoss_hollows__03__the_lantern_pools__night.png` does not offer pools or a glow-led destination. Clustering, terrain, sightlines, lighting and ground treatment are scene work. More varied canopy/understory and developed landmark art may be needed.

## Direct bar answers

**A. Belongs to the Meadows key-art world? No.** Grey-trunk woodland, fantasy animals and timber structures share broad ingredients, but the neon creatures, flat white/cyan props, weak environment layers and physically incoherent staging break the board's natural palette, grounded silhouettes and inviting mystery. A darker forest can belong to that world; these images do not yet achieve it.

**In-scene A work:** clear camera intrusions; ground and separate actors/pickups; verify relative scales against people; make destinations and forest layers legible; reserve danger colours more deliberately; motivate night lighting. **New/reworked A art:** coherent creature surfaces, finished monument/arch/hollow assets, and richer vegetation/landmark forms where the current collection cannot support enclosure and regional character.

**B. Would someone say this is trying to be the same kind of game as Palworld? Yes.** The traveller, small friendly-looking animals, huge fantastical creatures, settlements, collection prompts and wilderness communicate the intended creature-adventure genre. The exact “trying to be” wording is satisfied; shipping visual quality is not. These are not fight frames, so combat spectacle remains unproven.

**Scene/art split for the remaining B quality gap:** meaningful creature framing, contact, encounter composition and environmental density are in-scene work. A harmonised creature cast and finished environmental props need art beyond that work. Large on-screen area alone does not make an animal expressive or appealing.

## Product questions

- **Commercial finish or prototype?** Prototype, with visible development-stage forms and presentation failures.
- **Three visible blockers?** Repeated camera obstruction by trunks/creatures; apparently elevated/intersecting people, animals and pickups; plain bright primitives mixed with textured art.
- **Reason to explore?** Weak. Creature variety suggests discoveries, but the landmarks in their named locations are often hidden or do not read as finished destinations.
- **Distinct regional identity?** A grey-trunk forest with red undergrowth is recognisable. Storm, conductor, glow-pool and deepwood identities are not clearly expressed in the stills beyond names and isolated glowing objects.
- **Creature as visual/emotional focus?** Frequently a visual obstruction; rarely an emotional focus. The small fox-like creatures are the strongest approachable exception.
- **Comparable quality tier?** An early independent 3D adventure/asset-integration prototype, substantially below the supplied shipping Palworld presentation.

**Readiness: not visually ready for acceptance.** Stills cannot determine whether airborne subjects are animation moments, whether movement/interaction works, or how the game performs. They do establish that these delivered views cannot serve as finished creature/location presentation.
