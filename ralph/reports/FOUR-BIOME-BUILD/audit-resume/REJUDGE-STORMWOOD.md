# Stormwood: independent blind visual judgment

Evidence: 24 canonical individual PNGs in `shots/catalogue/stormwood/round-pylon-20260909T020124Z`, the stormwood contact sheet, combined-116.png and combined-page-01 through combined-page-04.png in `shots/catalogue/audit-pylon-sheets/`. All canonical individuals were opened, including blocked views. The full, unmodified `.claude/skills/visual-judge/SKILL.md` was used. Primary references inspected: `docs/reference/tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png` and all five `docs/reference/palworld-0*.jpg`. No code, implementation reports, or history informed this judgment. Sheet thumbnails were judged at small size; full frames supplied detail.

## Creatures and characters first

The cast is the first problem. `stormwood__deepwood__10__the_fallen_giant__night.png` presents an enormous turquoise, black-mottled frog whose spikes and internal markings make the face difficult to find. `stormwood__hollow_crown__08__the_crown_heartstone__day.png` is dominated by a blue bear's underside, with the trainer partly under its silhouette. `stormwood__conductor_run__06__the_capacitor_grove__day.png` sets acid-green/purple and magenta-black creatures against red bushes and bright ferns. There are identifiable fantasy-animal ideas, and the small foxes in `stormwood__conductor_run__05__rodline_post__day.png` have immediate appeal, but the whole cast reads as differently treated assets assembled together rather than a deliberately unified creature roster. This is a judgment of the shown art, not a claim about where it was sourced.

## Full rubric

1. **Silhouette and readability at small size.** The foxes in `stormwood__conductor_run__05__rodline_post__day.png` retain ears, face and body. The frog in `stormwood__deepwood__10__the_fallen_giant__night.png` is recognizably a huge crouched animal but loses facial hierarchy amid spikes and blotches. The trainer becomes a tiny dark figure against dark ground in `stormwood__glowmoss_hollows__04__crown_overlook__day.png`. `stormwood__cinder_verge__01__the_struck_sentinel__day.png`, `stormwood__conductor_run__06__the_capacitor_grove__night.png`, `stormwood__dynamo__11__the_glass_field__day.png` and `stormwood__dynamo__12__the_stormheart_tree__day.png` are dominated by near surfaces/characters; their named locations cannot be read.
2. **Colour and value structure.** The consistent grey tree trunks, dark moss ground and red bush clusters distinguish this region. However, `stormwood__conductor_run__06__the_capacitor_grove__day.png` combines saturated magenta, lime, purple, scarlet and green at similar competing importance. Red/oxblood-like vegetation is everywhere in `stormwood__glowmoss_hollows__03__the_lantern_pools__day.png`, so red is no longer a reliably reserved danger accent. This does not prove the bushes are friendly, but their broad decorative use visibly weakens the faction colour signal.
3. **Intentionality.** `stormwood__deepwood__09__lantern_hollow__day.png` gives people, a cottage and well a settlement association, but they are isolated on a broad vacant patch. `stormwood__glowmoss_hollows__03__the_lantern_pools__day.png` and `stormwood__glowmoss_hollows__04__crown_overlook__day.png` repeat the same red bush/fern/mushroom group vocabulary without a persuasive path or ecological transition. `stormwood__hollow_crown__07__the_crown_arch__day.png` distributes many blue bears toward the horizon in a way that reads like a creature field rather than a staged encounter area. The result is visually homogeneous despite many different place names.
4. **Lighting.** `stormwood__conductor_run__05__rodline_post__day.png` has clear daylight and grounding shadows. Night in `stormwood__conductor_run__05__rodline_post__night.png` retains substantially brighter local object visibility than Meadows night; this is useful for readability but inconsistent on the combined sheet. The mushroom/fern floor in `stormwood__glowmoss_hollows__03__the_lantern_pools__night.png` remains broadly lit without a strong local light story. `stormwood__conductor_run__06__the_capacitor_grove__night.png` is nearly black except for a magenta surface, not a readable mysterious grove.
5. **Horizon and depth.** In `stormwood__glowmoss_hollows__04__crown_overlook__day.png` the sparse trunks open directly onto pale, almost empty rolling distance. `stormwood__hollow_crown__07__the_crown_arch__day.png` has a flat exposed horizon and many small repeated animals. These do not convey a deep enclosing forest or strong landmark hierarchy. The names imply variety that the visible landscape does not deliver; names are not evidence that unseen landmark art exists.
6. **Interface.** Quest and interaction prompts are readable within safe margins. In `stormwood__hollow_crown__08__the_crown_heartstone__day.png` the large lower-right slots overlap the bear and nearby person; in `stormwood__deepwood__09__lantern_hollow__day.png` the prompt covers the foreground prop grouping. The tiny slot keycaps within huge empty panels remain inefficient. “Calm” and “Sheltered” are legible on full frames but weak at thumbnail size. No judgment of functional state or progression follows from those labels.
7. **Artefacts.** The sentinel pair `stormwood__cinder_verge__01__the_struck_sentinel__day.png` / `stormwood__cinder_verge__01__the_struck_sentinel__night.png` is blocked by a trunk. `stormwood__dynamo__11__the_glass_field__day.png` / `stormwood__dynamo__11__the_glass_field__night.png` show close player hair against magenta/black geometry. `stormwood__dynamo__12__the_stormheart_tree__day.png` / `stormwood__dynamo__12__the_stormheart_tree__night.png` show an extreme creature close-up instead of an environment. The pale, plain arch in `stormwood__hollow_crown__07__the_crown_arch__day.png` and repeated circular post structures in `stormwood__deepwood__09__lantern_hollow__day.png` visibly lack the material finish of the surrounding assets. `stormwood__deepwood__10__the_fallen_giant__night.png` catches the frog above the trainer with no readable ground contact; a jump pose is possible, so a persistent floating bug is not proven.
8. **Scale agreement.** The 1.80 m trainer and small foxes in `stormwood__conductor_run__05__rodline_post__day.png` form a clear small-creature relationship, while the frog in `stormwood__deepwood__10__the_fallen_giant__night.png` and bear in `stormwood__hollow_crown__08__the_crown_heartstone__day.png` are overwhelmingly larger. Relative magnitude is readable, though proximity prevents exact metre estimates. The low cottage and well in `stormwood__conductor_run__05__rodline_post__day.png` feel diminutive beside the trainer and giant forest trunks; human-use openings need a more persuasive scale. No still identifies which creatures are practice targets or intended companions, so role correctness cannot be certified.

## Product reading and evidence limits

Cohesion: a repeated forest palette exists, but saturated creature treatments and plain props clash with the detailed trainer/trees. Authoredness: weak across the broader set; it reads like repeated scatter punctuated by assets. Biome distinction: recognizably darker grey-trunk woodland compared with Meadows, but its own locations are poorly differentiated. Cast appeal/readability: small foxes work; big cast presentation and materials do not. Navigable landmarks: the repeated tree/prop fields lack obvious route priorities, and several putative destinations are obstructed. Visual readiness: **not ready at the shipping-art bar**.

Scene-fixable: camera clearance, creature separation, composition, ground clustering, paths and deeper layered tree groups, and controlled saturation/value. Art-needed: a unified cast finish and finished versions of the plain arch/circular structures; a distinctive forest landmark language is also absent from what can be seen. Art may exist outside these frames, but the images cannot establish that. No still-image conclusion about combat quality, locomotion, animation, progression or performance is justified.

## Three ranked reference gaps

1. **Expressive, coherent creature art.** Palworld 01/03 give giant creatures clear faces, simple dominant masses and controlled colour. `stormwood__deepwood__10__the_fallen_giant__night.png` and `stormwood__conductor_run__06__the_capacitor_grove__day.png` make high-contrast mottling and incompatible saturated treatments dominate. Recolouring and staging can help; a consistent creature finish requires art work.
2. **Authored forest depth and destinations.** The key art's grove layers canopy, path, undergrowth and distance; Palworld 02 supports routes with terrain and vegetation. `stormwood__glowmoss_hollows__04__crown_overlook__day.png` exposes an almost empty far field beyond scattered trunks, while `stormwood__glowmoss_hollows__03__the_lantern_pools__day.png` repeats decorative clumps. Scene layout can improve this substantially.
3. **A complete, unobstructed world view.** The references show usable spatial relationships. `stormwood__cinder_verge__01__the_struck_sentinel__day.png` is a trunk close-up, `stormwood__dynamo__11__the_glass_field__day.png` is hair and magenta geometry, and `stormwood__hollow_crown__07__the_crown_arch__day.png` is a plain pale arch. Camera/staging fixes are needed, followed by finished landmark art where primitive-looking props remain.

**A.** Do these frames read as belonging to the world in `docs/reference/tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png` — the project's own art direction?

**No.** The repeated red ornamental ground cover, neon creature combinations, empty far woodland and plain pale structures do not yet read as the key art's natural, inviting world with mystery. Density, colour discipline, lighting and composition are scene work; coherent cast finish and finished landmark/prop art are also needed.

**B.** Shown these frames beside `docs/reference/palworld-0*.jpg`, would someone say these are trying to be the same kind of game?

**Yes.** A human adventurer among small appealing animals and conspicuously large elemental monsters in an explorable outdoor setting clearly aims at the same broad creature-adventure category. The answer concerns aspiration and kind of game; it is not acceptance of this visual execution.


## Frame-specific clarification: Verge Rod Station

Evidence limited to `stormwood__cinder_verge__02__verge_rod_station__day.png` and `stormwood__cinder_verge__02__verge_rod_station__night.png`, re-opened for this observation.

**Readable material/texture finish: yes.** The foreground structure has clearly differentiated dark teal/charcoal supports, worn-looking gold bands, bright cyan inset panels and a jointed stone circular base. Edges, recesses and surface colour variation remain readable in both day and night. It reads as a textured, decorated fantasy mechanism, not as one of the plain pale arch/circle props criticized elsewhere in this report. The cyan panels give it a useful focal accent.

**Visible defects and limits:** The extremely close framing crops its upper termination and the bottom of its base, so its complete silhouette cannot be judged. Surface detail on narrow vertical insets looks stretched/soft at this viewing distance, and the dark central channels compress together, especially at night; this is a visible clarity limitation, not proof of a UV defect. The lower command strip and interaction prompt cover part of the base/body. I do not see a clear z-fighting seam, missing-texture checkerboard, or unequivocal broken mesh on the visible portion of the structure. A still cannot establish flickering, motion or correctness of hidden faces.

**What is obscured:** The structure occupies the central foreground and completely prevents a useful view of the trainer and the person named by “Challenge Officer Maren.” It conceals the immediate encounter space and any route or station elements directly behind it. Two orange creatures, trees, red bushes, ferns and mushrooms remain visible to the right and around the margins, but there is no visible 1.80 m trainer ruler with which to calibrate the structure or those creatures reliably. The visible plinth gives some grounding; overall height, trainer-to-pylon proportion, approach clearance and the wider station composition are not assessable from this pair. The primary demonstrated problem here is framing and lost spatial context; these pictures do not establish that the pylon lacks finished material treatment. No before/after or source inference is made.
