# Water gate: independent blind visual judgment

Evidence: `C:/Projects/Tetherbound/.artifacts/water-return-runtime-run-02/gate-day-view.png`, `gate-night-view.png`, and `gate-normal-view.png`, each opened and inspected. The normal image is a substantially matching daytime view, not an independent environment or functional state. The full, unmodified `.claude/skills/visual-judge/SKILL.md` and the previously inspected Meadows key art and five Palworld reference screenshots govern this review. No source, runtime reports, history, or change explanations were read for this judgment. This verdict concerns these three images alone.

## Characters and creatures first

The trainer has a recognizable adventurer silhouette, blue jacket, pale collar and backpack in `gate-day-view.png`. The gate's dark near upright sits directly behind the trainer's dark head and torso, reducing separation; that overlap is worse in `gate-night-view.png`. No creature is visible, so these images cannot demonstrate cast appeal, companion readability or creature-to-trainer scale. They also cannot establish that creatures are absent from the game.

## Full rubric

1. **Silhouette and readability at small size.** In `gate-day-view.png`, a tall freestanding rectangular frame is recognizable, but the oblique angle makes it read first as a thick upright/slab with a top bar. The opening is narrow in projection and does not visually invite passage. At small size, the night gate remains a strong dark shape against the cliff, while the trainer merges into its lower part. The prompt supplies more purpose than the object itself.

2. **Colour and value structure.** Sand, green slope and grey cliff form a coherent coastal palette in `gate-day-view.png`. The gate is a largely uniform blue-grey mass with little material differentiation. In `gate-night-view.png` its broad front face becomes very dark saturated blue; lighter side planes help identify volume but do not supply surface identity. The player has insufficient contrast against that face. No oxblood appears on a clearly friendly object in these views.

3. **Intentionality.** The gate is deliberately placed as a conspicuous object, but `gate-normal-view.png` shows it alone on bare sand without a worn approach, stone apron, framing props, marker or surrounding construction that explains its place in the world. The cliff and empty beach do not create a purposeful threshold composition. It reads as an object set down for interaction rather than an authored arrival/departure site.

4. **Lighting.** Day versus night is clear. Long cast shadows in `gate-day-view.png` and `gate-night-view.png` establish that the frame is grounded and has thickness. Night side-plane lighting preserves some form. However, there is no visible local light or controlled accent identifying the opening, and the gate/player overlap becomes a dark vertical mass. The result is legible as geometry but weak as a destination.

5. **Horizon and depth.** The near beach, large cliff and small sea opening at right establish spatial layers in `gate-day-view.png`. The cliff occupies nearly the entire backdrop and has repetitive polygon-pattern surfacing, with no mid-distance foliage or structures to reinforce scale. Its crest is a broad smooth grass cap. This is a limited, shallow composition compared with the references' layered approaches. No still can establish LOD changes, chunk loading or popping.

6. **Interface.** Safe margins are adequate. The cyan-bordered “Enter Stormwood” interaction prompt is clear, and its controller button symbol makes the intended action readable in all three images. The large five-slot panel and command strip consume a substantial right/lower portion of the view despite little visible slot information. The lower-left food label has weak contrast. The objective “Meet Pell at First Shore” and gate action compete as different directions; the images do not establish whether that state is correct or incorrect. The readable prompt is not evidence that activating it works.

7. **Artefacts.** The gate's visible faces are plain, sharply edged and essentially untextured-looking in `gate-day-view.png`; there is no visible masonry, metalwork, ornament or wear that would give this large structure a finished material identity. That is an unfinished visual result, not proof of a missing-texture bug. The cliff in `gate-night-view.png` has a conspicuous change between softer large polygon pattern at left and more strongly faceted-looking detail at right; its cause cannot be identified. I see no unequivocal gate z-fighting, broken mesh, or ground gap. The dark trainer/gate overlap is a staging/readability issue. Motion defects remain unassessable.

8. **Scale agreement.** With the trainer defined as 1.80 m, the visible gate is several trainer heights tall: roughly a four-to-five-metre impression in this perspective, not a reliable measured world dimension. Its depth relative to the trainer and oblique angle make exact ratios unsafe. Such a large fantasy threshold is plausible, but the massive uprights lack intermediate details that explain the scale. The opening's usable width cannot be judged from this edge-biased view. No creature scale comparison or companion/practice-role comparison is available.

## Purpose, finish, product reading and limits

**Purpose:** Visually identifiable as a freestanding gateway/frame, but a travel destination is communicated chiefly by the interaction text. The structure alone offers little distinction between an ordinary doorway, construction frame and supernatural passage.

**Finish:** The trainer and textured terrain have more surface information than the gate. Plain flat faces and sharp block construction do not meet the detailed landmark language of the key art or Palworld plateau reference. Day/night lighting gives the gate volume but does not make its material read as finished.

**Cohesion and authoredness:** The natural beach ingredients are compatible with a fantasy coast; the isolated plain blue frame is poorly integrated. **Navigable landmark reading:** it is conspicuous enough to notice, but the angle and absence of approach cues weaken its threshold. **Visual readiness:** not ready as a finished showcase of a travel landmark at the reference bar.

This is not a whole-biome rejudge and does not replace the broader four-biome verdict. No creature appeal conclusion is possible from an empty cast view. These pictures establish neither collision clearance, successful activation, destination correctness, reliable return travel, combat, progression, performance, nor any other functional/gameplay acceptance.

## Scene-fixable versus art-needed

**Scene-fixable:** turn/frame the approach so the opening and trainer separate clearly; establish a deliberate path or apron; place secondary props and grounding detail; compose a readable background; use a restrained local light/value accent to identify the threshold. These are visual requirements, not source prescriptions.

**Art-needed:** the displayed gateway needs finished surface treatment and an identifiable architectural or magical design. Changing camera, density or sunlight will not add the absent material/ornamental hierarchy to its broad plain faces. The images cannot prove whether suitable finished art already exists elsewhere, so they do not justify a specific purchase or a claim that replacement assets are unavailable.

## Three ranked reference gaps

1. **A finished, meaningful landmark.** The key art's rune stone and stronghold, and Palworld 04's architectural landmarks, have readable construction, material differentiation and identity. `gate-day-view.png` offers a plain blue block frame. Scene framing helps reveal the doorway, but a finished landmark design/material treatment is art work.
2. **An authored approach and inhabited context.** The references connect paths, vegetation, built features and destinations. `gate-normal-view.png` places the frame on largely empty sand in front of a uniformly treated cliff. Approach layout, prop grouping and shoreline detail are scene work; purpose-specific props may need art.
3. **Clear player/threshold presentation.** Palworld 02/04 and the key art preserve subjects against their surroundings. `gate-night-view.png` puts the trainer against a nearly black upright and provides no focal separation for the opening. Camera composition, spacing and local value/light can address this.

**A.** Do these frames read as belonging to the world in `docs/reference/tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png` — the project's own art direction?

**No.** The sand and green/grey coast are compatible ingredients, but the isolated plain blue gateway lacks the natural integration, material identity and authored landmark language of that world. Scene staging, approach dressing and lighting are fixable; finished gateway art/material treatment must also appear.

**B.** Shown these frames beside `docs/reference/palworld-0*.jpg`, would someone say these are trying to be the same kind of game?

**No.** From these three gate images alone, the evidence is a generic third-person adventurer, a bare coast and an interactable doorway. There is no creature partnership, creature encounter or inhabited activity context to make the particular Palworld-like creature-adventure aspiration visually legible. This is an evidence-limited answer about what this narrow view communicates, not a claim that the wider game has a different genre and not a substitute for a shipping-quality verdict. A view that includes the actual cast and a meaningful destination context could communicate that aspiration; improving the plain gate's finish alone would not establish it.
