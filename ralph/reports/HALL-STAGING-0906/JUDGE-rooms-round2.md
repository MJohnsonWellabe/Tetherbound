# Judge: Meadows Hall interior rooms, round 2

Frames: T-01-approach-room.png, T-02-warden-arena.png, T-03-legendary-side-wall.png
(+ sheet). Compatibility renderer, software GL, night interiors.

## Per-frame read: torch-lit or dark/cold/institutional?

### T-01 — approach room
Reads as **warm-tinted but not torch-lit**, and cold-dominant where it matters. The
ceiling and pillar-tops carry a broad, pleasant orange-pink wash and are the single
best-lit surface in the whole set — but I cannot find an actual flame, sconce, or
brazier mesh anywhere in the frame (checked both side walls at full crop). The warmth
is an ambient/top light with no visible source, which fails "flames legible as the
source" outright. Meanwhile the floor — where the player actually stands and where
pooled torchlight would matter most — is almost total black from the midpoint of the
room to the camera; there is no pool of warm light anywhere on the ground. The two
teal conduit beams are the brightest, most saturated, highest-contrast shapes in the
image, running the full width of both walls, and the left wall carries a large,
blown-out white-blue terminal/pipe cluster that is colder and brighter than anything
warm in the frame. Net effect: attractive orange ceiling, but the light logic reads as
sci-fi ambient wash + neon conduits with warm set-dressing behind it, not "wall
torches with teal accent." The trainer-scale grunt figure in the doorway is legible
and reads at a believable ~1.8 m against the door frame — that part is fine.

### T-02 — warden arena
The darkest and coldest of the three, and the one place where the intended technique
actually appears correctly, in one small area. There are two real torch/sconce meshes
flanking the archway (a wall-bracket flame at left, a small wall lantern at right),
each with a visible flame and a real, if narrow, pool of warm falloff on the stone
immediately around it — this is the one spot in the whole set that matches the brief.
But it is a single lit doorway in an otherwise cavernous dark room. In front of it: two
very bright, fully-saturated teal beams crossing the whole foreground floor, competing
directly with the torches for brightest object in frame. To the right, a barred
cell/machine with a blown-out white light behind purple-blue casing is the single
brightest object in the entire image — cold, institutional, and pulls focus away from
the torch-lit archway it sits beside. Far right, a small lantern cluster on a table is
correctly warm and correctly a legible point source, but it sits in near-total black
with no visible falloff on any nearby surface — it reads as a glowing dot floating in
void, not as light touching a room. The oxblood Team Tether banner on the left is
flat, unlit, and reads as signage rather than as a light source competing with
anything, which is the one place oxblood is used correctly (secondary, static,
identity-only).

### T-03 — legendary side wall
The worst of the three, and the one that reads as unambiguously cold/institutional.
The left third of the frame is filled by a large teal-lit crystalline/mechanical
structure (the containment machine) at high saturation and high brightness — it is
the dominant light source and dominant color in the image by a wide margin, not a
secondary accent to anything. A very thick, very bright cyan beam crosses the entire
foreground floor toward camera, reinforcing teal as the frame's key light rather than
its accent. Against that, there is exactly one warm element: a small wall sconce with
a legible flame roughly center-frame, which does produce a nice, correct little pool
of warm light on the stone immediately around it (this is well done in isolation) —
but it is tiny, distant, and isolated. Everything to the right of it — roughly the
right half of the frame, full height — is at or near total black, with stone blocks
only readable through faint cool rim/ambient light. That expanse is unreadably dark:
there is no way to tell what, if anything, is on that wall. This room does not read as
"lit by wall torches with a teal accent"; it reads as "a teal machine room with one
candle in the corner."

## Is anything unreadably dark?
Yes, in two of three frames. T-01's floor from mid-room to camera is solid black with
no detail. T-03's right two-thirds (side and far walls, most of the floor) is at or
below the threshold where stone geometry is only barely inferable — effectively dead
space in the frame.

## Is the teal accent secondary or dominant?
**Dominant in all three frames**, not secondary. In T-01 and T-02 it ties or beats the
warm elements for brightest/most saturated object in frame; in T-03 it is not close —
the teal machine and beam are the frame's key light, and the one warm torch is a minor
detail by comparison. None of the three rooms would read as "torch-lit hall with a
faction accent" if teal were desaturated in post — they would read as "sci-fi corridor
with warm set dressing," which is backwards from the brief.

## Ranking (best to worst torch-lit read)
1. **T-01 (approach room)** — best overall value/warmth on the ceiling, most legible
   composition, best-scaled character reference, but no legible flame source anywhere
   and a dead-black floor.
2. **T-02 (warden arena)** — the only frame with a correctly-executed torch (flame +
   falloff) at the archway, but it's a single bright spot in the darkest, coldest room
   of the three, undercut by a blown-out cold cell-light stealing focus right next to it.
3. **T-03 (legendary side wall)** — worst: a large, high-saturation teal machine and a
   thick cyan floor-beam are the dominant light and color of the frame, one small torch
   is nearly lost against them, and roughly half the frame is unreadably dark.

## The three things that most separate these frames from the references, ranked

1. **No legible flame anywhere near the amount of warm color on screen.** The keyart's
   night panel (bottom-right of the keyart board) shows one small campfire doing all
   the emotional work of the scene — a single warm point the eye locks onto, with dark
   blue night around it for contrast. These frames invert that: large warm-tinted
   surfaces (T-01's ceiling) or large cold-teal surfaces (T-02, T-03's machines) with
   no equivalent single, legible fire the player's eye can find. T-01 in particular has
   plenty of orange but no fire.
2. **Teal is the key light, not an accent, in all three rooms.** Nothing in the keyart
   or the owner's castle board uses a saturated cyan glow as the dominant color of an
   interior; the closest analog (the tether pylons/banners) is oxblood and used sparingly
   as faction identity, not as the brightest, largest light source in a room, which is
   what the conduit beams and the T-03 machine are here.
3. **Falloff doesn't reach the floor.** In the keyart and in the castle board's torch-lit
   passages, warm light pools on the ground the same way it pools on walls — it's where
   a person would actually be standing. Here the floor is the darkest plane in every
   frame; light sits on ceilings (T-01), on a narrow archway (T-02), or on a distant wall
   (T-03), never under the player's feet.

## Bar questions

**A. Do these frames read as belonging to the world in
`tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`?** **No.** The keyart's stronghold and night panels are
warm-key, blue-cool-ambient, with fire doing legible work and faction color used as
small graphic accents (banners, pylon glow) against stone that still reads brown/warm.
These three rooms are cool-key with warm used as texture color rather than as light,
and the faction teal has taken over as the primary light source in two of three rooms.

**B. Shown beside the Palworld screenshots, would someone say these are trying to be
the same kind of game?** **No**, but this is the weaker of the two questions since none
of the Palworld references are interiors — the honest answer is these frames don't
give enough evidence either way for exteriors/scale/creature comparison, but on the
specific dimension that transfers (readable, confident light sources with real falloff,
not murk with neon slashed across it) these rooms read closer to a dim tech-corridor
than to Palworld's consistently legible, evenly-exposed environments.

## What's fixable by scene changes vs. what needs new art

**Fixable by lighting/scene work (no new art required):**
- Add actual torch/sconce meshes with visible flames at regular intervals along both
  side walls in T-01, and let their falloff reach the floor (raise intensity/range or
  lower the light height so the pool lands on the ground, not just the ceiling).
- Reduce the conduit beam emission/bloom in all three rooms, or shift its hue cooler/
  desaturate it, so it reads as a thin accent line rather than the brightest object in
  frame — this alone would fix "dominant vs. secondary" in all three.
- Dim or reduce the size of the T-03 machine's teal emission, or add a warm rim/key
  light in front of it so it doesn't function as the room's sole light source.
- Add fill/bounce light or extra torches to light the dead-black floor and far walls in
  T-01 and T-03 — the geometry already exists, it's simply unlit.
- The T-02 blown-out white cell window should be pulled down in exposure so it doesn't
  outshine the correctly-done torch archway next to it.

**Not fixable without new art/assets:**
- None of the core defects here are missing-asset problems — the torch meshes exist
  (T-02, T-03 prove it), the stone and machine assets exist. This is entirely a
  light-placement and light-balance pass, not something that needs new geometry or
  textures bought or made.
