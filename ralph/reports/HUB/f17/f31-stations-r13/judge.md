## 2. Can a player tell the six apart at this camera distance?

Five of six, yes.

- **Forge** — unmistakable day and night (chimney, glowing mouth, anvil on a stump).
- **Cooking station** — unmistakable day and night (hanging cauldron over fire).
- **Farm plot** — reads clearly in the close frames; in the row view it is small, low and partly behind the signpost by day, but still identifiable.
- **Creature shelter** — reads clearly close up; in the row view it depends entirely on the small paw emblem, which is legible but is the only signal. Without the paw it would read as a storage shed or a village outbuilding.
- **Shrine/altar** — reads as *something ceremonial* at both distances, but its identity is carried by the two candle stands, not by the object itself; the bare white plinth on its own reads as a monument or grave marker. Stronger at night than by day.
- **Workbench** — **does not read.** It is a bare wooden trestle table with nothing on or under it: no vice, tools, scraps, saw, pegboard, half-finished item or worn surface. At 10 m it is a table, and nothing says "station". It is also the station most confusable with the kitchen's prep table, which is the same bare-table idea; the only thing separating them is that one happens to stand beside a cauldron.

**Confusable pair:** workbench vs cooking-station prep table (two plain tables).
**Weakest single read:** workbench (unreadable). **Second weakest:** altar (readable only via its candles).

- Tall foreground grass covers the bottom third of every frame; the stations sit above it, so this costs nothing except depth cues.
- Several close views crop a station at the frame edge (shelter in forge_kitchen_*, forge in altar_*, cauldron and shrine in farm_*). Acceptable for "closer views of parts of the row", but it means no single close frame shows all six.
- Everything is at mid-distance with the player character between camera and row; the character does not occlude any station.
- **Red/oxblood:** none of the six stations uses a strong red or oxblood colour. The only saturated warm notes are the forge's yellow-orange mouth and the cauldron's orange flame, both fire glow rather than surface colour. The shelter is tan/brown, the shrine white/grey, the forge grey stone, the tables bare wood, the farm plot dark soil and green. No reserved-colour conflict seen.
- Lighting is readable in both time-of-day passes; night is dark but not so dark that any station disappears.

## 4. Verdict

### Row view (row_day.png, row_night.png): **PARTIAL**

- Forge — PASS: chimney, lit mouth and anvil silhouette are unmistakable at 10 m, day and night.
- Cooking station — PASS: hanging cauldron over visible fire reads instantly, strongest at night.
- Farm plot — PASS (marginal day): clear green planting rows in a soil box; small and partly behind the signpost by day, better at night.
- Creature shelter — PASS (marginal): identity rests on one small paw emblem; the building form alone would read as a shed.
- Shrine/altar — PASS (marginal): white plinth plus two candle stands reads ceremonial, but the plinth alone reads as a monument/headstone.
- Workbench — **FAIL**: a bare trestle table with no tools or work in progress; it reads as furniture, not as a crafting station, and is confusable with the kitchen's prep table.

### Close views (forge_kitchen, altar, den, farm, day and night): **PARTIAL**

- Forge — PASS: anvil, stump, chimney, bucket and quench pot make the trade obvious.
- Cooking station — PASS: cauldron, frame, fire ring and prep table together read as cooking.
- Creature shelter — PASS: paw gable plus bed pad plus feeding bowl plus creature-sized entry; the clearest station in the set.
- Farm plot — PASS: tilled box with regular seedling rows and a harvest crate.
- Shrine/altar — PASS (marginal): the candles carry it; the plinth has no offering, bowl, icon, cloth or carving of its own.
- Workbench — **FAIL**: still an empty table even at close range; nothing identifies it as a bench.

**Overall: PARTIAL.** Five stations read as distinct, identifiable objects at the normal camera in both lighting conditions. The workbench fails both views, and the altar passes only on the strength of its flanking candles.
