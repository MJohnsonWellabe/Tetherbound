## 0. Read this before the verdicts: an evidence-integrity finding

The owner, seeing the first frames in this queue, said plainly:

> "some of those renders are just a bad shot not actual game. the game doesn't
> have the haze and has real grass"

That is correct and it is load-bearing for this whole pass. It is also the
single most valuable finding in it, so it goes first rather than buried.

**A large fraction of the committed evidence in this backlog does not show the
build.** Concretely, in every `T1-CAST/shots/band24/*.png` frame and in the
`T1-NIGHT/.../day_night_after_dawn_keyframe/*.png` set:

- **There is no grass geometry anywhere in frame.** The ground is a bare splat
  texture. `band2-2025-burrowback-17deg.png` shows two creatures standing on a
  hillside with not one blade of grass on it; `band4-4027-pipwing-15deg.png` is
  the same. The build has real grass. These captures ran without it.
- **The near ground is smeared to an out-of-focus mush.** The bottom third of
  `band4-4038-meadowhart-15deg.png` and of `hour-22.00.png` is bilinear-stretched
  blur with no detail at all. That is a capture-path/mip artefact, not a
  terrain material.
- **There is a milky haze at the horizon** the owner says the build does not
  have — `band2-2025-burrowback-17deg.png` at y≈265-280, where the far terrain
  goes flat pale and detail simply stops.

**Consequence, and it is not a small one:** none of these frames can support a
claim about how anything reads against the ground — not creature contrast, not
scatter density, not "how empty is the frame", not ground palette. Those are
exactly the claims the lanes made from them. Several of my own criticisms below
would have been aimed at ground/light lanes on this evidence; I am aiming them
at the capture path instead, and re-judging the same subjects on frames I
captured myself.

**And it is not one bad tool.** I captured six fresh Creek Hollow frames myself
on current `main` with a completely different tool
(`tools/_probe_creek_hollow_habitat.gd`, §2). Those frames are cleaner — real
cloud detail, real value range, no milky horizon — but **not one of them shows
grass geometry either.** `creekhollow-rock_overhang.png` and
`creekhollow-open_basin.png` have bushes, reeds, flower clumps and fern cards on
a splat, and no blades. So whatever suppresses grass is in the capture path
generally (or in software GL), not in one lane's script. That is the finding the
capture lane needs: **the harness as a whole may be producing grass-free
evidence**, and nobody reading these frames can tell.

**Routes to: capture/evidence.** Before another lane ships a frame as proof:
the capture harness needs a check that fails loudly if grass instancing is
absent or fog/haze is on when the build's own setting is off. A capture that
silently drops the grass system produces evidence that is not merely weaker
than a real frame — it is evidence for the opposite conclusion.

I have kept my per-item verdicts split accordingly: **build defects** (things
the frame shows that are true of the game) and **capture defects** (things the
frame shows that are true only of the shot).

## 1. T1-CAST evidence — guardian backlight, campsite kit, campfire logs, creature-bed tint

Frames: `ralph/reports/T1-CAST/shots/guardian-den-0830-full.png`,
`shots/camp/01-tent.png`, `01-camp-establishing.png`, `02-camp-close.png`,
`02-campfire-ring.png`, `03-player-bedroll.png`, `04-creature-bed.png`,
`05-workbench.png`, `shots/band24/*.png`.

**Verdict: this evidence does not carry the branch that landed on it.** Two of
the four named claims are not demonstrated by the frames at all (the backlight
does not survive to gameplay distance; the log texture is not wood), one is
demonstrated and is wrong (the creature-bed tint), and the campsite kit has a
style break in it that is louder than any of the four items the lane was
looking at.

### 1a. The camp kit is two art styles bolted together — and this is the biggest defect in the set

`05-workbench.png` beside `02-campfire-ring.png` and `01-tent.png`.

The tent, the beds, the fire-ring stones and the logs are **scanned/photographic
PBR**: real cloth weave visible in the tent panel, granite photo-texture on the
ring stones, cloth wrinkle normal maps on the mattresses.

The workbench is **hand-painted stylised**: specular highlights painted into the
albedo as white streaks along the top planks and down the legs, cartoon
wood-grain swirls, scribbled fake AO in the joints, flat blue-grey metal straps
with painted highlights. Its saturation is far above every other object in the
set — it is more saturated than the fire.

Standing next to each other in `01-camp-establishing.png` these do not read as
one game. Neither family is wrong on its own; having both in a seven-object kit
is. **Routes to: install** (pick one family for the camp kit and re-source or
re-texture the odd one out).

### 1b. The campfire does not read as fire

`02-campfire-ring.png`, and at distance in `01-camp-establishing.png`.

- The flame is **opaque flat-shaded yellow polygonal cones**. No alpha, no
  gradient from a hot white core to an orange tip, no embers, no smoke, no soft
  edge. At the establishing distance in `01-camp-establishing.png` it reads as a
  **yellow crystal cluster**, not a fire.
- This matters more than a prop normally would. The keyart's own NIGHT panel
  puts a warm campfire as the single warm accent anchoring the composition, and
  a campfire is a landmark that has to read at range at night. Reference
  `palworld-05-base-building.jpg` gets warm-source read at distance; this does
  not.
- The fire light is **an unshaped blown-out disc** on the ground with a hard-ish
  circular boundary, brighter at its centre than the sky, **in daylight**. The
  point light has no daylight attenuation.

**Routes to: content** (flame material/VFX) and **light** (fire light
attenuation vs. sun).

### 1c. The campfire logs are not wood — the named "log texture" fix did not land

`02-campfire-ring.png`, foreground.

- **They are boxes.** Rectangular prisms with rounded corners, not cylinders.
  The "cut end" is a flat quad carrying the same noise as the sides, so there is
  no end grain — the cut face and the bark face are the same material.
- **The texture is isotropic mottle.** On a log, bark runs along the axis. Here
  the speckle is the same frequency and has no direction on any face, which is
  why the logs read as **charcoal or granite**, not timber.
- **Two-tone with a hard break.** Sides near-black with orange speckle, top
  faces light tan, and the transition is a hard edge — so they read as painted
  blocks.
- **A visible UV stretch** across the beveled edge of the right-hand log at
  approx. (700-890, 480-560).
- Scale: log diameter ≈0.45m against ring stones ≈0.30m. These are structural
  timbers, laid as two parallel bricks. A campfire reads from crossed thin
  branches, not from two parallel beams.

**Routes to: content/install.**

### 1d. The creature-bed tint is exactly backwards

`04-creature-bed.png`, and both beds together in `01-camp-establishing.png`.

- The frame and lashings are **olive/moss green on green ground** — same hue
  *and* same value as the terrain. The frame disappears; only the mattress
  reads.
- The mattress is near-black (value ≈0.12). Against real Meadows grass that
  will read as **a hole in the ground**, not a bed.
- The tint choice is inverted from what the object needs: the soft part should
  carry the warm inviting note and the frame should carry the silhouette. Here
  the frame is camouflaged and the soft part is a void.
- **It is the player bed retinted.** `03-player-bedroll.png` and
  `04-creature-bed.png` share the same mattress mesh, the same wrinkle
  topology, the same fold at the foot and the same pillow silhouette. In
  `01-camp-establishing.png` you can see the same object twice, four metres
  apart. It also does not communicate creature scale — both beds are the same
  1.8m × 1.0m.

**Routes to: content** (differentiate the creature bed as an object, not as a
hue) and **install** (pick a frame tint that separates from grass in value, not
just hue).

### 1e. The guardian backlight does not survive to the distance the player stands at

`guardian-den-0830-full.png`.

At the framing of this shot — which is a plausible player distance in the den —
the guardian is **a near-black lump** occupying about 8% of frame width against a
dark grey-brown chamber. The rim is present but is a thin, low-contrast edge on
the creature's right side only, roughly 2px wide at this scale. It does not
separate the animal from the wall behind it.

Compare `palworld-01-boss-fight-forest.jpg`: the field boss is read instantly at
the same relative size because it carries **its own internal value contrast**
(bright green canopy mass against a mid-brown body) plus warm rim, not because
of a rim alone. A rim light on a near-black body against a dark wall has almost
nothing to work with.

Also in this frame: **the lit doorway is gone.** `guardian-den-BEFORE-full.png`
has a bright lit door at approx. (355-395, 340-490) that is the only high-value
element in the room and the only thing giving the space a read. In the 0830
frame there is no lit opening anywhere and the room has no value anchor at all.
Whether that is a camera change or a light change, the 0830 frame is a worse
composition than the frame it supersedes.

**Routes to: light** (den key/fill and the door light) and **content** (the
guardian needs internal value contrast, not just a rim). Cross-refs §3.

### 1f. What the band24 frames still show honestly

Discounting the missing grass and the haze (§0), these frames do show scatter
authorship, and it reads procedural:

- `band4-4038-meadowhart-15deg.png`, ridge at (740-1010, 285-320): **seven
  identical dark-green bushes in a nearly straight, evenly spaced row along a
  contour line.** This is the loudest "generator output" tell in the set.
- Same frame: five meadowhart, all the same tan-cream, all in the same standing
  pose, evenly distributed with no clustering. Reads as a spawner grid, not a
  herd. Three of the five sit in pale grass of the same hue and value as their
  own hide and nearly vanish.
- `band2-2025-burrowback-17deg.png`, left: five trees, all the same canopy blob,
  all the same height, all the same rust-red trunk, **with no undergrowth at
  their bases at all** — they plant straight into bare ground. The keyart's oak
  grove panel and `palworld-02-open-field-path.jpg` both put clutter, roots and
  scale variety at the base of every tree.
- `band4-4027-pipwing-15deg.png`: about 60% of the frame is empty ground with
  nothing in it, and the named subject is three 8px white specks at (335,430),
  (945,375), (1075,400). Pure white against pale yellow-green — they read as
  lens dirt.
- `band2-2044-duskhush-32deg.png` (day) and `-NIGHT.png`: **saturated
  violet/magenta discs floating in mid-air above the treeline** at (0-190,
  30-120), emissive blue at night. They are the most saturated thing in either
  frame and they are not attached to anything. Reads as a canopy or flower card
  detached from its parent.
- Same frame, (620-880, 280-340): **the terrain grass splat is painted across
  the vertical faces of the rock cluster.** Triplanar is not being applied, or
  the splat is overriding the rock material. Reads as a bug.
- Same frame: hard straight-line terrain seams, e.g. (110,540)→(500,640), and a
  dotted grid at (1150,600)-(1250,660).

**Routes to: ground** (scatter clustering, tree base clutter, rock/splat
projection, chunk seams) and **install** (the floating violet cards).

### 1g. Tent — good asset, two defects

`01-tent.png` is the best object in the kit: stitched hide, lashed poles, real
pegs, an open door with a floor sheet, believable material. Two things:

- **A floating peg.** At approx. (1060-1110, 520-580) a lashed peg-and-rope
  assembly hovers roughly 0.4m off the ground with no rope attached to it; its
  shadow lands clearly separated below and to the right. **Routes to: install.**
- **Scale disagreement inside the kit.** In `01-camp-establishing.png` and
  `02-camp-close.png` the tent's ridge is roughly at the trainer's shoulder and
  its footprint is shorter than the bed beside it. **The bed cannot fit inside
  the tent.** Two props of the same kit disagree about how big a metre is. The
  workbench has the opposite problem: its top surface sits at about the
  trainer's chest, ≈1.2m, for a bench that should be ≈0.9m, and its footprint is
  larger than either bed. **Routes to: install.**
- In `01-camp-establishing.png` the workbench legs **straddle and interpenetrate
  the creature bed** — the two objects occupy the same volume. **Routes to:
  install.**

## 2. Creek Hollow water-spawn depth

Frames captured in this session with `tools/_probe_creek_hollow_habitat.gd` on
`origin/main` (28265a3a), committed under
`ralph/reports/JUDGE-3/shots/creek-hollow/`:
`creekhollow-{creek_edge,water_edge,far_water_edge,open_basin,rocky_shoulder,grove,rock_overhang}.png`
plus two 4× nearest-neighbour crops I made to read the waterline,
`creek_edge-CROP-waterline.png` and `far_water_edge-CROP-embedded.png`.

**Verdict: the depth fix is real and it reads correctly at the shoreline —
`creekhollow-rock_overhang.png` is the proof, and it is the best frame in this
whole pass. It is not demonstrated on open water, and a separate and worse
placement defect is sitting next to it: creatures embedded flat in the terrain,
on land, with no volume and no shadow.**

### 2a. Where it works — and this is the frame to keep

`creekhollow-rock_overhang.png`. A turtle at (680-780, 335-390) sits on the
beach at the waterline. **Its lower body and flippers are cut by the water
surface, there is a pale wet margin around it, and it sits in the sand rather
than on it.** That is a correct depth read from the bank and it is what the fix
was for. Route: nothing.

This frame also corrects something I would otherwise have written against the
water material from the older evidence: **the pond does have a shallow-to-deep
ramp.** At (700-1280, 290-620) sand is visible through the shallows and the
surface goes opaque with distance, with a foam line along the beach. The reason
the ramp is invisible in `creek_edge` (§2c) is that that stand looks across deep
water at a grazing angle, not that the ramp is absent.

Its remaining defect is contrast, not depth: **the turtle's shell is the same
mottled grey-green as the boulders around it and it sits exactly where a boulder
would sit**, so at 30% it reads as a rock on the beach. Its only creature cue is
the cyan flipper mass, which is the same cyan as the water behind it. A second
creature at (900-930, 285-300) is a cyan blob against cyan water and is not
findable without looking for it. *content* (water creatures need a value or hue
that is not their own habitat's).

### 2b. Where it is worse than a depth problem — creatures flattened into the ground

`far_water_edge-CROP-embedded.png` (4× crop of
`creekhollow-far_water_edge.png`), and repeated in
`creekhollow-rock_overhang.png` at (110-180, 340-370) and (525-555, 370-385).

- The purple/white/blue creature at crop (370-570, 520-575) is **lying
  completely flat with zero volume** — a splayed smear on the grass. It reads as
  a creature whose root is below the terrain so that only the topmost surface
  pokes through, or as a decal. It has no legs, no body depth and no silhouette.
- The brown creature above it at crop (140-280, 100-250) is **sunk into the
  slope from the hindquarters back**, cut off by the ground plane.
- **Neither creature casts a contact shadow of any kind.** Both read as cutouts
  pasted on the hillside.
- These are 100m from any water, so this is not the water-spawn path — it is
  ground placement, and it is a worse read than anything the water fix
  addressed.

*Routes to: ground* (spawn Y / terrain-snap on slopes) and *light* (contact
shadows on creatures).

### 2c. Where it is not demonstrated — open water

`creek_edge-CROP-waterline.png` (4× crop of `creekhollow-creek_edge.png`).

The cyan creature at crop (490-660, 175-295) sits out on open water. **Its entire
body is above the water plane.** No waterline crosses it, no partial submersion,
no displacement ring, no ripple, no reflection, no contact of any kind — the
water surface simply passes behind it. It reads as a sticker on an opaque plane.
Whatever the species is meant to do, that is not a depth read.

Also visible in this crop: the water plane's edge against the far beach at crop
y≈85-105 is **a hard aliased pixel staircase**, not a wet margin — the soft
shoreline that `rock_overhang` has is absent here.

*Routes to: ground* (surface interaction on open water) and *capture/evidence*
(this stand cannot show what it was chosen to show).

### 2d. Two of six habitat stands photograph no creature, and one photographs nothing

Reproduced independently on current `main`, so these are live stand defects, not
one-off bad luck:

- **`creekhollow-water_edge.png` is a black frame.** The vantage-finder put the
  camera inside geometry — near-black bark/rock across the whole image with one
  small tan wedge at bottom-right. This reproduces the failure in the committed
  `T1-CREATURE/shots/water_edge-AFTER.png`, which is shot from inside a tree
  trunk. **The same stand is still broken.**
- **`creekhollow-grove.png` and `creekhollow-rocky_shoulder.png` contain no
  creature at all**, despite both being habitat-cluster stands.

So of six Creek Hollow habitat stands: one black, two empty, one embedded, one
open-water non-demonstration, one good. *Routes to: capture/evidence.*

### 2e. What these frames show about the ground, judged honestly

The owner is right that the haze and the missing grass in the lane frames (§0)
are not the build. My own captures are cleaner — `creekhollow-rock_overhang.png`
and `creekhollow-open_basin.png` have real cloud detail, a real value range and
no milky horizon. But three ground defects survive into my own frames and are
therefore the build's:

- **The splat stretches along slopes.** In `far_water_edge-CROP-embedded.png` the
  ground texture is smeared into streaks that follow the gradient consistently —
  that is UV stretching on a steep face, not blur and not haze. *ground.*
- **Terrain chunk seams are everywhere and they are dotted lines you can read at
  a glance**: `creekhollow-rock_overhang.png` at (700-900, 450-500), (830-880,
  555-700) and (250-450, 430-450); `creekhollow-rocky_shoulder.png` at (900-1100,
  590-620) plus a hard rectangular brightness step at (700-1280, 330-420);
  `creekhollow-open_basin.png` at (720-760, 610-700) with a 45°-edged bright tile
  at (1150-1280, 640-700). *ground.*
- **Distant terrain LOD goes to flat near-white** with no texture at all —
  `creekhollow-rock_overhang.png` at (0-560, 165-260). It reads as snow or as a
  missing texture, and it is a large part of why the far distance in these frames
  looks hazy even when no fog is on. *ground.*

And two scatter/asset defects that are unambiguously the build:

- **`creekhollow-grove.png` is one trunk copied fifteen-plus times.** Same
  silhouette, same taper, same rust bark, same dark vertical smear; only scale
  and rotation change. **Every trunk meets the ground as a flat-cut cylinder** —
  no root flare, no litter, no undergrowth at any base (see (250-350, 620-660)
  and (880-960, 660-700)), and the forest floor is completely empty. The keyart's
  own oak-grove panel is built on root masses and floor clutter;
  `palworld-01-boss-fight-forest.jpg` fills the same depth of frame with
  undergrowth. This is the clearest generator-output frame I captured. Bark
  texture also visibly tiles at roughly 100px intervals on the right-hand trunk,
  (960-1160, 300-700). *ground, install.*
- **Repeated props at even spacing**: three identical flowering purple bushes at
  the same scale along the bank in `creekhollow-rock_overhang.png` at (0-100,
  340-420), (215-330, 300-345), (465-580, 305-345); an even scatter of identical
  fern cards in `creekhollow-rocky_shoulder.png` at (620-980, 360-480); the same
  tree repeated at even spacing across the whole far ridge. The reed clump at
  (40-215, 285-330) in `rock_overhang` is the counter-example and shows the
  project can do a varied clump when it authors one. *ground.*

Credit where it is due: **`creekhollow-rock_overhang.png` would sit beside the
keyart's stream panel without embarrassment** — turquoise water with a real
depth ramp, a sand bank with a wet edge, a believable half-timbered mill,
varied reeds, and warm clouds. It is the strongest evidence in this pass that the
target is reachable.

### 2f. The committed "AFTER" evidence for this item is not usable

`T1-CREATURE/shots/creek_edge-AFTER.png` and `water_edge-AFTER.png`, which are
the standing evidence for this fix, do not photograph their subject:

- **`water_edge-AFTER.png` is shot from inside a tree trunk.** About 70% of the
  frame is brown bark at point-blank range. There is no creature in it and no
  water edge is judgeable.
- **`creek_edge-AFTER.png` clips its subject at the frame edge.** The only
  creature present is a cyan shape at (1200-1280, 370-430), half outside the
  frame. The camera is jammed between a mill wall and a fence; the shoreline the
  shot exists to judge is a thin band across the far distance.

Neither frame can support a depth claim from the bank. **Routes to:
capture/evidence.**

### 2g. What the older `creek_edge-AFTER.png` still shows, honestly

Same stand as my `creekhollow-creek_edge.png`, so these hold on current `main`
too. **Note the correction:** I would have written "the water has no depth ramp"
from this frame alone. `creekhollow-rock_overhang.png` (§2a) shows that it does.
What is true is narrower:

- **Across deep water at a grazing angle the surface is a flat opaque cyan
  plane** — no transparency, no reflection, no visible bottom, and it is the same
  value and near the same hue as the sky, so the far shore has nothing to sit
  against and the pond reads as a hole cut to sky. The keyart's stream panel and
  `palworld-04-plateau-landmark.jpg` both keep a depth read at that angle.
- **A constant-width pale beige beach ring** runs the entire shoreline. Reads
  generated. (The wet margin that makes `rock_overhang` work is absent here.)
- **The creature in frame is the same cyan as the water it lives in** — see §2a
  and §2c; a water creature tinted to its own water is hard to find in the one
  place the player meets it.
- The bridge deck at (350-1150, 380-640) is uniformly dark brown with no plank
  separation while its rails are bright yellow-orange; the deck reads as a hole
  in the bridge.
- The fence rails clip through the mill wall, and the ground strip under the
  fence is near-black with no AO reason to be.

**Routes to: ground** (deep-water surface read at grazing angles, shoreline width
variation), **content** (creature tint vs. its own habitat), **install**
(fence/wall intersection, bridge deck material).

## 3. Guardian silhouette fix (T1-CREATURE, 2026-08-29) in its Warrens context

Frames: `T1-CREATURE/shots/guardian-silhouette-BEFORE-crop.png`,
`guardian-silhouette-AFTER-crop.png`, `guardian-den-BEFORE-full.png`,
`guardian-den-AFTER-full.png`, and `T1-CAST/shots/guardian-den-0830-full.png`.

**Verdict: the crop pair overstates the fix, because the two crops are not the
same shot.**

`BEFORE-crop` is a **rear three-quarter** view. `AFTER-crop` is a **side
profile**, from further back. A bear-shaped animal photographed from behind is
an amorphous lump in any lighting; photographed in profile it has a head, a
back line and legs. **A large part of the apparent improvement is the camera
angle, not the silhouette work.** For this to be evidence, both crops have to be
the same stand. **Routes to: capture/evidence.**

What is genuinely better in `AFTER-crop`, judged on its own: the mossy stone
plates on the back now separate from the body in both hue and value, so the
creature has an internal contour instead of one flat mass. That is a real gain
and it is the right kind of fix — internal value structure, not a rim.

Remaining defects in `AFTER-crop`:

- **The lower half merges into the floor.** Legs and belly are the same value as
  the den floor beneath them and there is no contact shadow, so the animal reads
  as sitting *in* the ground.
- **The face does not resolve.** The white blaze is the only high-value element
  on the creature and it reads as a smear, not a face — no eye is findable at
  this size. Against `palworld-01-boss-fight-forest.jpg`, where the boss's face
  is the first thing you read and its expression is the reason the fight has a
  mood, this is the gap that matters most.
- **The yellow claw shapes float mid-body** at roughly (330-360, 150-200) with
  no leg reading under them, so they read as markings rather than claws.

And the finding that decides the item: **at the distance the player actually
stands, the fix does not carry.** In `guardian-den-0830-full.png` the same
creature is a featureless black lump. The silhouette work is real but it is
tuned at crop scale, and the criterion that matters — readability at small size
— is not met in the den. **Routes to: light** (den key/fill so the animal has a
背 background to separate from; see §1e on the lost door light) and **content**
(the creature needs value contrast that survives to gameplay distance).

## 4. Camp set — bed replacing bedroll

Frames: `T1-CAST/shots/camp/01..05`.

**Verdict: the objects are individually competent and the set does not cohere.**
Full defect list is in §1a–1d and §1g and is not repeated. In summary, ranked:

1. **Style break** — the workbench is hand-painted stylised, everything else is
   scanned PBR (§1a). *install*
2. **Creature bed is the player bed retinted, into the ground's own hue and
   value** (§1d). *content, install*
3. **The fire does not read as fire, at any distance** (§1b). *content, light*
4. **The logs are not wood** (§1c). *content/install*
5. **The kit disagrees with itself about scale** — bed does not fit in tent;
   bench is chest-high; bench interpenetrates the bed (§1g). *install*
6. **Floating peg** in `01-tent.png` (§1g). *install*

One thing this whole set cannot answer: **every camp frame is shot on a
featureless flat green plane.** Nothing here tells anyone whether the kit reads
in real Meadows grass — which, per §0, is the exact thing these captures have
been silently dropping. The camp kit needs one establishing frame pitched in the
world at player eye height before it is called done. **Routes to:
capture/evidence.**

## 5. The four Aspect variants — Nightburrow, Stormtrail, Riftfrill, Ashtusk

Frames: `T1-CREATURE-ART/shots/{nightburrow,stormtrail,riftfrill,ashtusk}-{wide,close}.png`.
(These are the only committed variant renders; `T3-INSTALL/shots/` does not
exist on `main`.)

**Verdict: they read as a retint. The queue asked the question directly and the
answer is unambiguous — one shared decal mask, hue-swapped four times.**

### 5a. The mask is the same mask, and it is broken

Put `nightburrow-close.png` and `ashtusk-close.png` side by side. **The blotch
shapes are the same shapes, in the same places, mirrored the same way.** Only
the hue differs — magenta on one, yellow on the other. `stormtrail` and
`riftfrill` carry fragments of it too.

And the mask itself is not shippable art:

- **Hard pixel staircases.** Every mask boundary is a 90°/45° stair of blocky
  rectangular fragments. `ashtusk-close.png` shows it at full frame: squared-off
  "tetris" chunks, e.g. at (380-520, 120-300) and (800-900, 300-420). It reads
  as **a threshold applied to a low-resolution noise texture**, magnified far
  past its resolution — that is, as a bug, not a marking.
- **Mechanically mirrored.** In `ashtusk-close.png` the left and right halves are
  near-exact mirrors *including every jagged step*, which is what proves it is a
  UV-mirrored texture rather than painted markings.
- **A UV seam crack** runs down the centre line of the face at approx.
  (640,80)→(680,470) where the two shells do not align.
- **The mask is unlit and flat.** In `ashtusk-close.png` the yellow has zero
  material response and casts no light — the yellow patch on the snout does not
  illuminate the nostril beside it. So "ember veins" read as **flat yellow
  paint**, not heat.
- **It bleeds over the eyes.** In `ashtusk-close.png` at (240,470) and (1010,470)
  the yellow sits behind the eyelid geometry, so the eye reads as a glowing
  yellow disc with a floating black pupil. It wrecks the face, which is the one
  thing a creature is read by.

**Routes to: content** (these need painted, form-following markings, authored per
species — the mask as it stands cannot be rescued by hue) and **install** (mask
resolution, mirroring, UV seam, eye-region exclusion).

### 5b. Per variant

- **Nightburrow** (`nightburrow-wide.png`). The badger head is the only authored
  thing in the frame and it is unchanged from the source species — cream blaze,
  black eye, pink nose, all good. Everything distinguishing the *variant* is the
  mask. Body is near-black, so in the night cave it is meant for, **the creature
  IS the pink blotches, floating.** The bubblegum magenta is also off the board's
  own palette strip, which has a violet and a mauve but nothing this hot.
  *content.*
- **Stormtrail** (`stormtrail-wide.png`, `-close.png`). The variant treatment is
  **too small to read** — at 30% this is simply a grey wolf; the cyan occupies
  about 3% of the silhouette and there is no "storm" read at all. In the close-up
  the cyan around the eyes reads as **smudged eyeliner**: a flat unlit blob
  spilling onto the lower lid and cheek with a wavy hand-cut edge. There is a
  stray cyan scribble on the ear at (760-790, 455-480). Separately, the two white
  crescents at (555-600, 570-590) and (680-720, 570-585) sit on the *brow*, not
  in the eye, and there is no catchlight in the eye itself. *content.*
- **Riftfrill** (`riftfrill-wide.png`, `-close.png`). **Both frames are
  unusable.** The wide is at ~0.15 value throughout — material cannot be judged.
  The close crops the head off at the bottom edge with 60% of the frame empty.
  What is visible: the eyes are flat magenta discs with **a stray dark squiggle
  drawn across each iris** at (500-540, 730-760) and (760-800, 730-770), and the
  frill markings are isolated aliased single pixels that read as dead pixels.
  *capture/evidence* for the framing and exposure; *content* for the eyes.
- **Ashtusk** (`ashtusk-wide.png`). The best of the four and the only one where
  the mood lighting works. Silhouette reads, warm rim works, and at ≈1.1m
  shoulder / ≈2.4m long against the 1.80m trainer it reads as a genuinely
  imposing animal — scale is right. Its defect is that the yellow is spread
  evenly across the whole back, so it reads as **camouflage netting** rather than
  ember cracks; embers should follow the form, concentrate in the valleys between
  plates and at the joints, and actually glow. *content.*

### 5c. Two cross-cutting findings

- **The particle emitter is the same emitter four times, recoloured.** Two or
  three small puffs, evenly spaced in a vertical line above the back, unattached,
  no falloff, no motion — `nightburrow-wide.png` (795-885, 160-280),
  `ashtusk-wide.png` (770-800, 165-245), and single stray dots in the other two.
  It reads as a debug emitter, not an aura. *content.*
- **The cast and the creatures are in different art styles, and every wide frame
  shows it.** The trainer is cel-shaded and stylised — oversized eyes, flat hair
  masses, simplified hands. The wolf in `stormtrail-wide.png` is naturalistic
  wildlife — correct canid anatomy, fur-direction shading, realistic muzzle.
  Standing 2m apart they do not read as characters from the same game. The bar
  the owner set is `palworld-0*.jpg`, where the human and the creature are
  unmistakably one design language. **This is a top-three gap and it is not
  fixable by scene tuning.** *content / install — needs an art decision, not a
  parameter.*

### 5d. Limit on this item

These are **purpose-built stage renders on a two-tone backdrop**, not world
placements. The queue asked for the variants "in the world" and that is not what
was committed. In particular, Nightburrow's own stated requirement — confirm it
does not become a silhouette in the real Burrow Warrens at night — is untested,
and given that its body is near-black and its only read is the mask, it is the
one most likely to fail there. **Routes to: capture/evidence.**

## 6. Deep night on the driven clock

Frames: `T1-NIGHT/shots/day_night_after_dawn_keyframe/hour-{20.00,22.00,23.90,00.10,02.00}.png`
(the `_capture_day_night_transition.gd` path, which drives WorldLook's real
clock rather than snapping).

**Verdict: the crimson is gone — that bug is fixed and does not reappear at any
hour. What replaced it is a blue tint over a daytime lighting setup, and the
clock is not driving the shadow-casting light.**

### 6a. The sun is still casting at midnight, and it never moves

- `hour-00.10.png` and `hour-02.00.png` are dominated by **huge, hard-edged,
  long directional shadows** raking across the meadow from the treeline,
  (200,300)→(500,700). At 00:10 there should be no directional shadow at all, or
  a very soft moon shadow. These are sun shadows, tinted blue.
- **The shadow direction is identical in both frames.** Over one hour and fifty
  minutes a driven celestial light rotates visibly; this one does not rotate at
  all. The shadow-casting light is frozen. **The driven clock is changing colour
  and intensity but not the light's direction.** That is the concrete,
  checkable defect and it is the root of the rest of this section.
- `hour-22.00.png` still has **a full-intensity warm yellow-green lit wedge**
  crossing the meadow from lower-left to centre-right at daylight brightness, at
  ten at night.

**Routes to: light.**

### 6b. It reads as day with a filter, not as night

- **The canopies keep their daylight green hue** at 00:10 and 02:00 — value
  drops, saturation does not. Every night convention, and the keyart's own NIGHT
  panel, desaturates and shifts blue.
- **The whole frame sits in a ~0.15–0.35 value band.** There is no black and no
  highlight anywhere. Sky and ground are close enough in value that the horizon
  at (700,255) barely separates.
- **There is no moon and there are no stars.** An open-sky midnight frame with
  obvious directional shadows and no visible moon is a self-contradiction, and
  the keyart's NIGHT panel is built on a moon plus a starfield.
- **There is no warm accent.** The campsite at (520-760, 230-280) is present at
  every hour and **its fire is never lit** — the only warm pixels in the frame
  are a ~20px orange smudge at (640,240). The board's night panel makes a warm
  fire the composition's anchor; the build's own night has none.
- `hour-20.00.png`: **the sky and the ground disagree about the time.** The sky
  is midday blue with white cumulus while the ground is in deep dusk shadow, and
  there is no sunset gradient at all — just a faint pale band at (300-700,
  240-255).

**Routes to: light** (moon as the night key with a real direction; desaturation
ramp) and **sky** (moon disc, starfield, dusk gradient) and **content** (light
the campfire at night).

### 6c. Artefacts visible in this set

- `hour-22.00.png`: a **hard straight-line shadow-cascade seam** from about
  (0,470) to (500,430), with a visible resolution change across it — large soft
  blocks on one side, finer detail on the other. *light.*
- `hour-22.00.png`: **dotted terrain chunk seams** at x≈130 and x≈420, y
  460-600. `hour-00.10.png` has the same at x≈130, y 480-600. *ground.*
- The whole set carries the §0 capture problems — no grass, smeared near ground.
  Any judgement here about how navigable night ground *feels* is therefore not
  supportable from these frames. *capture/evidence.*

## The three things that most separate these frames from the references

1. **The cast and the creatures are not in the same art style, and the creatures
   are not the game's own.** `stormtrail-wide.png` puts a cel-shaded,
   big-eyed trainer two metres from a photoreal-anatomy wolf. In
   `palworld-01-boss-fight-forest.jpg` the human and the Pal share one design
   language — the same shape vocabulary, the same shading model, the same degree
   of stylisation — which is why the creature reads as *belonging to* that world
   and as the thing the game is about. Tetherbound's creatures currently read as
   sourced wildlife with a decal applied. This is the deepest gap and it is not
   fixable by changing the scene.

2. **Value structure is missing almost everywhere.** `band4-4038-meadowhart-15deg.png`
   has five creatures the same hue and value as the ground they stand on, on a
   hillside with essentially no form shading. `guardian-den-0830-full.png` has no
   high-value element in the entire room. `hour-00.10.png` spans about a fifth of
   the available range. Every Palworld reference has a real range in frame —
   `palworld-03-field-boss-meadow.jpg` carries dark tree masses, mid grass, a
   bright sky and specular pops simultaneously, which is what makes a fight in it
   read as an event.

3. **Scatter reads as generator output, not as authorship — and the groves are
   the worst of it.** `creekhollow-grove.png`, my own frame on current `main`, is
   one trunk copied fifteen-plus times, each meeting the ground as a flat-cut
   cylinder with no root flare, over a forest floor with nothing on it at all.
   Alongside: seven identical evenly spaced bushes along one contour in
   `band4-4038-meadowhart-15deg.png`; three identical flowering bushes at even
   spacing on the bank in `creekhollow-rock_overhang.png`; a constant-width beach
   ring around the whole pond. `palworld-01-boss-fight-forest.jpg` fills that same
   depth of frame with undergrowth, and `palworld-02-open-field-path.jpg` puts
   clumped grass at varied scale, a path with a hard value break and rock clusters
   into it. The keyart's grove panel is built on root masses, clustering and
   clearings. This one is not an art-budget gap — it is placement work with assets
   the build already has.

## The two bar questions

**A. Do these frames read as belonging to the world in
`docs/reference/tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`?**

**Not yet — but one frame in this pass does, and that changes the shape of the
answer.**

`creekhollow-rock_overhang.png`, captured this session on current `main`, would
sit beside the board's stream panel without embarrassment: turquoise water with
a genuine shallow-to-deep ramp, a sand bank with a wet edge, a varied reed clump,
a believable half-timbered mill on the far shore, warm clouds, real value range.
That is the target, hit, in the build, today. Nobody should read the rest of this
answer as "the look is unreachable."

What else carries: the sky throughout — `band2-2025-burrowback-17deg.png` and
`band4-4038-meadowhart-15deg.png` have good cloud shapes and a believable blue
gradient, and the daylight meadow hue is close to the board's yellow-greens.
`creekhollow-open_basin.png` has the best light-and-shadow structure in the pass.
`ashtusk-wide.png` shows the project can light a creature well when it tries. The
tent in `01-tent.png` is a board-appropriate object.

What sinks it everywhere else: the board's Meadows is built out of *depth* —
layered canopy, undergrowth at every tree base, clearings, and a warm light
source anchoring each composition. `creekhollow-grove.png` is the counter-case
and it is stark: one trunk copied fifteen times, every one meeting the ground as
a flat-cut cylinder with no root flare, and a completely empty forest floor.
Night is the sharpest miss: the board's NIGHT panel is a moonlit blue world with
stars and a campfire; the build's midnight is an unmoonlit, starless, fireless
blue wash with frozen sun shadows in it.

**Fixable by changing the scene** (no new art needed): the frozen night light
direction and missing moon/star sky (§6a, §6b); the campfire being unlit at night
(§6b); the creature-bed tint (§1d); the fire light's daylight attenuation (§1b);
scatter clustering and the constant-width beach (§1f, §2e, §2g); creatures
embedded flat in terrain and their missing contact shadows (§2b); the rock/splat
projection, slope splat stretching, chunk seams and the far-LOD whiteout (§1f,
§2e, §6c); the floating peg, the bench/bed interpenetration and the camp kit's
internal scale disagreement (§1g); the open-water surface read (§2c); den key/fill
and the lost door light (§1e).

**Fixable, but it is scatter/dressing work rather than a parameter**: root flare,
floor clutter and trunk variety in the groves (§2e) — the assets to do it are
mostly in the build already; they are not being placed.

**Not fixable without art that is not in the build**: the flame VFX (§1b); the
log asset (§1c); a creature-bed that is a different object rather than a retint
(§1d); the Aspect markings, which need painted per-species art rather than a
shared mask (§5a); and the guardian's face reading at distance (§3).

**B. Shown these frames beside `docs/reference/palworld-0*.jpg`, would someone
say these are trying to be the same kind of game?**

**No** — and the reason is narrower than it looks, which is the useful part.

The *world* frames would pass this question on their own. Discount the missing
grass and the haze (§0) and `band4-4038-meadowhart-15deg.png` is recognisably
aiming at `palworld-02-open-field-path.jpg`: same kind of rolling open meadow,
same palette family, creatures loose in it at distance. Given real grass and real
value range, that frame gets there.

What answers no is the creatures and the cast. Palworld's whole proposition is
readable in one frame — a bespoke, expressive creature with a face you read
instantly, standing beside a human drawn in the same language, in a fight staged
as an event. Set `nightburrow-wide.png` or `stormtrail-wide.png` beside
`palworld-01-boss-fight-forest.jpg` and the difference is not polish or budget:
it is that one of them designed its creatures and the other retinted some
animals. `ashtusk-wide.png` is the proof that this is reachable — it has scale,
presence and a silhouette. It is also the one whose surface treatment is a mask
that reads as a texture bug.

**Not fixable by scene changes**: the shared Aspect mask, the style split between
cast and creatures, and creature faces that survive to gameplay distance. That is
the art that has to be made or bought. Everything in the "fixable" list above is
work the existing lanes can do now.

## Method and limits

- Godot 4.7.stable, GL Compatibility (`--rendering-driver opengl3`) under
  `xvfb-run`, which per `D01` is also the shipped renderer — but this box is
  software rendering (llvmpipe). Frame times are meaningless and are not quoted.
  Fine lighting and shadow judgements are safer made on real hardware.
- Static frames only: popping, motion aliasing and traversal feel are invisible
  here.
- No score is given, per the skill. The deliverable is the defect list and the
  fixable/not-fixable split under bar question A.
- Per §0, criticisms that would normally route to **ground** or **light** have
  been routed to **capture/evidence** wherever the frame, not the build, is what
  produced them.
