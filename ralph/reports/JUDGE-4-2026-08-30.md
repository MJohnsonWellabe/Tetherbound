# QUEUE 2 — THE FOUR ASPECT VARIANTS

## Verdict

**JUDGE-3's four named technical defects are fixed. I can see all four fixes in
the frames.** The hard pixel-staircase edges are gone — every emissive now has
a bright core with a real falloff. The bilateral mirroring is gone; the crack
networks are organic and asymmetric. The bleed over the eyes is gone — every
close-up has clean pupils. And the flat unlit look is gone. That was the lane's
brief and the lane met it.

**On the question actually asked — four distinct creatures, or one creature
recoloured? — my answer is two and a half of four.**

- **Ashtusk — distinct.** Unambiguous, at every range, day and night.
- **Nightburrow — distinct.** Clear violet crack network; strongest at night.
- **Riftfrill — marginal.** Recoloured in the right direction but not far
  enough, and not where the sheet says the identity should live.
- **Stormtrail — still reads as a recolour.** The lane says so too, in its own
  words ("the most understated of the four at pure lineup distance, honestly").

**I therefore disagree with the handover's summary line** — *"All four pass the
basic bar the judge's own question sets."* Three do or nearly do. Stormtrail
does not, and the disagreement is specific and checkable: see Q2-D2. The lane
graded its own renders by eye against the boards, which this project's own rule
warns against and which the lane itself acknowledges. This is exactly the
delta a blind pass exists to find, and it is a narrow one.

## Ashtusk — cleared, including the thing JUDGE-3 singled out

**The ember tusks read.** This was the named failure and it is fixed.
`ashtusk-vs-tuskroot-night-wide.png`: the tusks blaze gold-white against a
near-black head and the facial fissures resolve into a glowing mask, with ember
motes above. At that size — the creature is maybe 15% of frame height — you
identify it instantly. `-night-close.png` shows the tusk with a true luminous
core rather than flat paint. `-day-close.png` confirms the close read: cold
cracked basalt with orange fissures and a gold iris rim, against Tuskroot's
warm terracotta and mossy back ridge. Different material family, different
value key, different temperature. **This is the standard the other three should
be measured against**, and it is the one variant that implements its sheet.

**Q2-D1.** The day read is much weaker than the night read
(`ashtusk-vs-tuskroot-day-wide.png`) — the glow competes with a bright sky.
Worth confirming it survives noon in the real world, not only on the stage.

## Stormtrail — the one that does not clear

**Q2-D2 — at lineup distance the variant and its base are not tellable apart.**
`stormtrail-vs-trailpup-day-wide.png` is the frame that decides it: same tan
coat, same head, same ears, same posture, same size. The differentiation is a
patch on one shoulder. Against the sheet, four of five specified changes are
absent:

- *"Darken the coat"* — the coat is still the base's tan. The sheet's
  Stormtrail is a charcoal storm-grey animal; that value drop alone would
  separate them at any distance.
- *"Add lightning markings"* — the sheet's whole design is a **gold lightning
  zigzag running head-to-tail along the spine**, with violet arcs down the legs
  and flanks. The build has a soft gold patch on the left shoulder. The lane
  raised coverage twice and measured the result, so the intent was right; the
  shipped read is still a patch, not a bolt.
- *"Glowing eyes"* (sheet: vivid cyan) — see Q2-D3.
- *"Scale up modestly"* — same size as the base, measured against the 1.80m
  trainer in the wide frame. Out of the lane's scope (`species.json` was
  correctly not touched), so this is a routing gap, not a lane failure.

**Q2-D4 — the marking is on one side only.** It sits on the creature's left
shoulder. From the other half of the angles a player fights from, Stormtrail
is its base species exactly.

## Q2-D3 — the eye glow does not reach the frame, and this is the highest-value
fix available

Every one of the four sheets specifies **glowing eyes**, in a named colour:
Violet Glow, cyan, white-violet, ember orange. The handover states the eye-glow
billboard fires ("Purple flame VFX and eye-glow both fire"). **The committed
frames do not show it.**

`ralph/reports/JUDGE-4/shots/chk-nightburrow-eye-day-vs-night.png` is the
proof, and it is worth looking at directly. Nightburrow's eye in day-close and
night-close, side by side: a black pupil with a **yellow-gold crescent on the
lower lid**. It is the wrong colour for this creature — the sheet says violet —
and, decisively, **it is dimmer at night than by day**, which means it is
diffuse-lit albedo, not emission. An emissive would go the other way. Whatever
the billboard is doing, nothing violet and nothing luminous arrives at the eye
in either lighting condition. Stormtrail and Riftfrill likewise show the base
species' ordinary eyes in every close-up.

Ashtusk is the exception, and probably by luck rather than by wiring: its gold
iris rim happens to match Ember Glow, so its eye reads correctly for its sheet.

This is worth calling out on its own because it is the cheapest, highest-yield
identity cue in the whole queue: eye emission survives to any distance, works
in silhouette, works in darkness, and Palworld leans on it constantly. Making
this one thing work, in the sheet's colour, on all four, would buy more
separation than everything else in the next round combined.

**And there is a likely root cause worth handing to whoever picks this up.**
The lane fixed JUDGE-3's eye-bleed defect by adding a *tight pupil/nostril
exclusion* to the glow generator, plus per-species `z_max` bounds explicitly
described as keeping "the glow and the lightning-marks tint off the muzzle/eye
region". That fix is correct for the defect it targets. But it means the
texture layer is now, by construction, forbidden from lighting the eye — so
the specified glowing eyes can only come from the VFX billboard, and the
billboard is not arriving. **The fix for one judge's defect is standing where
the owner sheet's most valuable cue needs to go.** That is a scope collision,
not an error by either lane, and it needs someone with authority over both.

## Riftfrill — marginal, and a correction to my own blind read

**Q2-D5 — CORRECTED.** My blind note said Riftfrill's body was "the *identical*
cyan as Paddlenewt". That is wrong, and sampling the frames disproves it:

| Patch (`riftfrill-vs-paddlenewt-day-close.png`) | Paddlenewt | Riftfrill |
|---|---|---|
| Lower cheek / jaw | (45,209,218) | (55,129,157) |
| Mid-head | (74,229,233) | (83,167,161) |

The body **is** recoloured — roughly 60–80 units darker in green and blue, and
markedly less saturated. I read a real change as no change, and I withdraw the
claim.

What survives the correction is narrower and still stands: the shift is a
**value** move (a darker teal) rather than the sheet's **hue** move (River Teal
body, *Lilac* frill). The sheet says the frills and patterning should "carry
most of the variant identity", and in the build the lilac is confined to the
crown and the frill tips. `riftfrill-vs-paddlenewt-day-wide.png` shows the
consequence: at lineup distance the pair reads as one axolotl at two
brightnesses. The lane's own claim of a "strong, clearly distinct lilac/violet
frill" overstates what the wide frames show, though the close frames support it
better than I first allowed.

**Q2-D6** — the sheet's emissive filigree markings (glowing swirl patterns over
head, back and frills) are not present.

## Nightburrow — cleared, strongest at night

**Q2-D7.** `nightburrow-vs-burrowback-night-wide.png` is the variant's best
frame and it works: the base's white blaze blazes, the variant is swallowed
into black with a violet rim and drifting motes. Read as different animals. The
violet crack network is visible against the base's moss patches at close range,
and the colour correction the lane made (off bubblegum magenta, onto the
board's Violet Glow) was the right call — it sits in the sheet's palette now.

**Q2-D8 — my blind note here was half wrong.** I faulted Nightburrow for
sharing the base's facial mask. The sheet keeps the badger blaze too, so that
is correct behaviour and I withdraw it. What the sheet does *not* keep is the
eye colour (Q2-D3) and the **purple flame** — the sheet shows amethyst flame
rising off the back, and the build has a small cluster of detached mauve motes
sitting in a gap above the spine, reading as unrelated floaters rather than as
something the animal emits.

**Q2-D9.** By day (`-day-wide`) the separation is thin: two badgers of the same
size and shape at different contrast. The night read is doing most of the work,
and that is the lighting, not the asset.

## Cross-cutting

**Q2-D10 — WITHDRAWN.** I had faulted all four for not changing silhouette.
All four owner sheets explicitly direct silhouette *preservation* — "Preserve
the core boar silhouette", "Keep the body silhouette close to Trailpup",
"Preserve the main silhouette", "Keep armor shapes broad and readable".
Silhouette-identical is the owner's instruction, not the lane's shortcut. The
criticism was wrong.

**Q2-D11 — sheet compliance predicts the verdict, in all four cases.**

| Sheet directive | Nightburrow | Stormtrail | Riftfrill | Ashtusk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Darken body / coat | yes | **no — still base tan** | partial — darker, not re-hued | yes |
| Emissive cracks / markings | yes | **weak — a shoulder patch, not the spine bolt** | **no filigree** | yes |
| **Glowing eyes** (named colour) | **no — gold, not violet** | **no** | **no** | partial — gold rim |
| Signature VFX | partial — motes, not flame | **no — sparse dots** | partial | yes |
| Modest scale-up | **no** (out of lane scope) | **no** (out of lane scope) | n/a | yes |
| **Reads as distinct?** | **yes** | **no** | **marginal** | **yes** |

Read down the columns: the variant that implements its sheet is the variant
that reads. This is not an aesthetic disagreement about how far a recolour can
go — much of the specified recolour is simply not built yet. It is also **not a
T1-VARIANTS failure**: that lane's brief was JUDGE-3's four technical defects
and materials/textures only, and it delivered those. The remaining gap needs a
lane with authority over eye emission, `species.json` scale, and mask
authoring.

**Q2-D12 — the eight "close" frames are mis-framed.** In
`stormtrail-...-day-close.png` and `riftfrill-...-day-close.png` both subjects
are pushed to the frame edges with half the image empty plane; in
`nightburrow-...` and `ashtusk-...-day-close.png` both heads are cropped by the
frame edge. For an A/B the two subjects need to be adjacent, whole, and at
equal distance from camera.

**Q2-D13 — the trainer is unlit in all four night frames.** Flat ambient, no
key, no rim; he reads as a cutout. He is the ruler in these shots and should be
lit like a character.

**Scale.** Measured against the 1.80m trainer in every wide frame, relative
scale is internally consistent and I found no error of the kind this criterion
exists to catch. Boar ≈3.5m long, shoulder at the trainer's shoulder; badger
≈2.3m; axolotl and jackal both under a metre. Each variant matches its own base
exactly — which is itself the Q2-D11 "no scale-up" row.

## Bar questions — Queue 2

> **A. Do these frames read as belonging to the world in the key art?**

**Not answerable from this evidence, by design.** These are turntable frames
with no world in them. Judged as creature art alone: **yes for Ashtusk and
Nightburrow** — the cold basalt with ember fissures and the obsidian-and-violet
badger would both sit in the key art's night panel without argument. Riftfrill's
high-chroma cyan sits outside the key art's palette strip in both forms.
Stormtrail is naturalistic and fits, but fits identically to its base.

> **B. Beside `palworld-0*.jpg`, same kind of game?**

**Yes.** The modelling, shading and material quality on all eight creatures
would sit beside `palworld-01`'s Mammorest without embarrassment, and Ashtusk
in particular would hold its own there.

I had drafted a "no" on the grounds that Palworld does not build a roster by
recolouring one mesh. That is not a fair charge — Palworld does exactly this
for its alpha and variant tiers, and the owner's sheets direct it. The charge
that survives is narrower: Palworld's recolours move **coat value, eye emission
and VFX together**, which is precisely the list these sheets specify and
precisely what Stormtrail did not get. Same kind of game, same intended method,
one of four under-executed.

## The three things that most separate Queue 2 from the references

1. **The eyes.** All four sheets specify a glowing eye in a named colour; three
   of four ship with the base species' ordinary eye, and the fourth is right by
   coincidence. `chk-nightburrow-eye-day-vs-night.png` shows a yellow albedo
   crescent that gets *dimmer* at night where the sheet wants a violet
   emissive. Palworld's variants read at distance largely on eye emission.
2. **Stormtrail's spine bolt.** The sheet's design is a head-to-tail gold
   lightning network on a darkened coat. The build has a shoulder patch on a
   base-tan coat. (`stormtrail-vs-trailpup-day-wide.png`)
3. **VFX that attaches to the animal.** The sheet shows amethyst flame rising
   off Nightburrow's back and arcs crawling Stormtrail's legs. The build has
   small clusters of motes floating in a gap above the body, reading as
   unrelated particles. Ashtusk's embers are the one case where the effect and
   the creature look connected. (`nightburrow-...-night-wide.png`)

## Recommended next round — Queue 2

Not a redesign. The design exists and is owner-supplied. In order of yield:

1. **Eye emission on all four, in each sheet's named colour** — violet, cyan,
   white-violet, orange. Resolve the Q2-D3 scope collision first: the texture
   path is deliberately blocked from the eye, so this has to come from the
   billboard, and the billboard is not currently reaching the frame.
2. **Stormtrail: darken the coat and build the spine bolt.** These two together
   are the whole of that variant's failure.
3. **Riftfrill: push the frill to lilac** rather than darkening the body — the
   sheet puts the identity in the frill.
4. **Attach the VFX to the body** rather than floating it above.
5. **The specified modest scale-up** on the three sheets that ask for it —
   needs a lane that owns `species.json`.
6. **Reshoot in the world**, on grass, at encounter distance, with both
   subjects whole and adjacent, to close the disclosed in-world gap.

  failing to reach the Hall's hill, is the `approach_drain` skin (`#bfb6a0` at

The one finding I would still hand to the fresh pass regardless of what the

**I did not spend budget waiting.** Dispatch a fresh pass when the lane settles;
