## 0. Evidence validity check (done first, per the brief)

### Queue 1 — grass geometry is genuinely present. The fix landed.

I checked every hall frame for real 3D grass geometry before trusting any of
them. It is there:

- `H-01-approach-400.png` — dense grass blade cards, three-leaf clover clumps,
  white and lilac flower props across the whole foreground.
- `H-02-sigil-gate.png`, `H-05-east-flank.png`, `H-06-west-keep.png` — full
  grass field with flower scatter.
- `H-03-ramp-foot.png` / `-golden` / `-night` — grass and broadleaf clumps on
  the verges either side of the ramp.

This is not the flat painted ground JUDGE-3 was shown. **The hall frames are
valid evidence on that axis and I judged them as real.**

**But the field does not reach the building.** In `H-02b-sigil-gate-raised.png`
the entire hill the Hall stands on is bare pale-green/white painted terrain
with no grass instance anywhere on it, and in `H-03-ramp-foot.png` there is a
hard, straight boundary at roughly x=1200 where the grass stops dead and
becomes flat mint-white ground. `H-02b` also has a large bald white patch on
the slope right of the ramp. So the grass field's radius or its authored
exclusion around the structure leaves the chapter's climactic location
standing on undressed ground. That is a new, different defect from the one
that was fixed, and it is visible in the two frames that matter most for
"does this dominate the approach."

### Queue 1 — one frame is corrupt and was written anyway.

`H-04-gate-mouth.png` shows no gate. The camera is below the terrain surface:
the bottom two-thirds is a wall of stone diffuse texture and the top third is
the grass field seen edge-on from underneath, blades rendering as grey unlit
cards against a grey wash. It is a total capture failure. **`tools/capture_check.gd`
passed it.** Whatever the check validates, "camera is not inside/below
geometry" and "the named subject is in frame" are not among them. That is a
gap in the tool, not just a bad frame.

### Queue 2 — these frames are NOT from the fixed world path.

Every variant frame is shot on a bare turntable: a flat untextured green plane,
a hard straight horizon, a flat blue gradient sky, no grass, no terrain, no
props. Nothing about the grass/terrain/weather fix is exercised here.

For an A/B on markings a neutral stage is a defensible choice and I judged the
markings on it. But it means the evidence **cannot answer the question that
actually matters** — whether these four read as distinct creatures *in the
Meadows*, against real grass, at real encounter distance, in real light. A
creature that separates from another only by a shoulder patch on a flat green
void will separate from it less, not more, once there is grass, dappled shadow
and foliage clutter behind it. Queue 2's evidence is therefore incomplete, and
I have graded it as such below.

## QUEUE 1 — THE MEADOWS HALL

### Verdict: **NO. It does not clear the bar.**

Stated plainly, as asked: this is not a deliberately authored, occupied,
climactic stronghold. It is a low-relief box fort with painted-on windows and
no inhabitants, standing on undressed ground, behind a wall of untextured grey
rectangles. It does not dominate the approach at any range in this set. Route
it back to the Hall lane.

This is better than what it replaced — the ramp-foot composition in
`H-03-ramp-foot-golden.png` is a real, framed, intentional approach shot with a
believable processional axis, and the night pass has genuine mood. That is
progress and it should not be thrown away. But progress from "the worst-reading
structure in the world" is not the same as clearing the bar, and the specific
things below are what stop it.

### Defects

**Q1-D1 — Four enormous untextured grey rectangles float behind the Hall in
every exterior frame.** `H-01-approach-400.png` (x≈460–865, y≈305–390),
`H-02b-sigil-gate-raised.png` (four overlapping quads, x≈220–890, y≈225–430),
`H-03-ramp-foot.png` (x≈300–1170, y≈205–290), `-golden` (same, tinted cream),
`-night` (same, tinted pale blue). They are semi-transparent, flat-shaded,
axis-aligned, and they respond to time of day, so they are lit scene geometry,
not a compositing error.

This is the single loudest thing in the evidence and it has to be first. In
`H-02b` these panels are **several times taller and wider than the Hall
itself** and occupy more of the sky than the building does. In `H-01` the
artifact covers roughly 32% of the frame width; the Hall covers roughly 6%.
The critic's eye goes to the bug, not the landmark. Nothing downstream of this
can be judged fairly until it is gone.

**Q1-D2 — The Hall does not read as a landmark at 400m; the pylons beat it and
so does the artifact.** `H-01-approach-400.png`. Measured: the Hall occupies
about 80×31 px of a 1280×800 frame — 6.3% of width, 3.9% of height. It is
rendered in a pale beige at almost the same value as the atmospheric haze
behind it, so it has less contrast against the sky than the oak canopy in front
of it. Meanwhile the cyan tether pylons are brighter, more saturated, repeated
across the ridge, and the one at the right edge is roughly **seven times the
Hall's on-screen height**. The chapter's final destination is the least salient
built object in its own establishing shot.

*Sampled, to put a number on it.* Mean luma of the Hall patch at 400m: **138.5**.
Mean luma of the sky immediately beside it: **147.5**. **Separation: 9 of 255.**
The foreground oak canopy in the same frame sits at luma 82.1 against the same
sky — **65 units of separation, seven times the Hall's.** The building the whole
chapter travels toward has one seventh the figure-ground contrast of a tree the
player walks past. This is the mechanical statement of the defect, and it is
mostly a *value* problem rather than a size problem: raising the Hall's value
separation would help before another metre of height would.

Worse, at that size it does not resolve into one silhouette: the crop shows two
separate pale boxes — a crenellated left block and a plain flat-roofed right
block with a red smear on it — that do not compose. The key art's stronghold
panel reads instantly at thumbnail as one gatehouse mass with two towers and a
dark arch. This does not.

**Q1-D3 — The walls are single flat planes with a painted stone diffuse and no
geometric relief at all.** `H-08-wall-close.png` is unambiguous: every stone is
drawn into the texture, none is modelled. The diagonal shadow crossing the wall
at x≈600–900 falls across the masonry without a single stone self-shadowing,
which is what tells you instantly it is a picture of a wall rather than a wall.
Two adjacent panels in the same frame use **different stone scales** — the
cream face at left has ~0.7m stones, the dark face at right has ~0.35m stones —
so one building disagrees with itself about how big a block is. Same problem
across `H-06-west-keep.png`, where the cream tower cladding is a flat plane
pasted onto a dark box: the seam at x≈870 has no corner geometry and the
cladding's lower edge at y≈595 simply stops in mid-air above the base.

*Sampled.* Lit wall run in `H-05-east-flank.png`: mean **(117,102,78)**,
std-dev **17.5**. A second run right of the banner: mean (138,111,82), std-dev
20.9. Whatever variance is in the diffuse is not surviving to screen at the
distance the player sees the wall from — the surface is close to a flat field.
And the two adjacent faces in `H-08-wall-close.png` measure mean luma **105**
(cream) against **28** (dark): a **3.7:1 value ratio between two faces of one
building under one light**, which is why it reads as separate objects rather
than one mass.

**Q1-D4 — The openings are decals, not apertures.** `H-05-east-flank.png`: the
arrow slits are light-grey rectangles printed on the wall with no recess, no
reveal, no interior darkness, no sill. `H-06-west-keep.png`: the keep's windows
are solid black rectangles printed on the tower face — the same sticker
treatment with the value inverted. A window you cannot see into is the fastest
way to make a building read as a prop. The key art's gatehouse has a deep dark
arch with blue light inside it and recessed embrasures; that depth is the thing
that sells it as enterable.

**Q1-D5 — Nobody lives here.** Across all eleven frames there is exactly one
figure (`H-07-courtyard.png`) and zero occupation props: no crates, barrels,
carts, tents, scaffolding, rope, tools, sacks, laundry, weapon racks, cook
fires, smoke, or refuse. `H-07`'s courtyard is a bare stone box. The key art's
stronghold panel is *dense* with occupation — timber scaffolds up both flanks,
a machine assembly, a sharpened-log palisade, stacked crates, lit braziers,
figures on the steps. `palworld-05-base-building.jpg` makes the same point from
a shipping game: crates, a workbench, a chest, a produce barrel, banners, a
half-built structure, creatures working. Team Tether's forward stronghold, at
the climax of the chapter, currently looks abandoned.

**Q1-D6 — The banners are flat quads and they are the wrong red.**
`H-05-east-flank.png` shows three identical hard-edged rectangles in a
saturated raspberry/magenta-red (roughly #D8305A), hung on plain brown
crossbeams; `H-03-ramp-foot.png` shows the same magenta either side of the gate;
`H-08-wall-close.png` shows one at top as a sub-pixel-thin dark-red slab. None
of them has a sigil, a taper, a hem, a fold, or any cloth behaviour. Two
separate problems: (a) the *shape* is a placeholder, and (b) the *hue* has left
Team Tether's reserved oxblood and gone pink. The key art's banners are a deep
desaturated oxblood carrying a white compass sigil — the sigil is the reason
they exist. The current colour is close enough to the courtyard figure's
magenta hair (`H-07`) that the faction colour no longer reads as faction.

**Q1-D7 — The stronghold is too small to be a stronghold, measured against the
1.80m ruler.** `H-07-courtyard.png` is the only frame with the human in it.
Against her: the courtyard doorway is ≈3.3m and the enclosing wall is ≈5.5m to
its top course. A 5.5m curtain wall is a walled garden. In the key art panel
the gatehouse towers run to roughly 15–20m — eight to eleven times a person.
`H-03-ramp-foot.png` corroborates from the other side: measured against the
1.1m ramp fence rails, the gate mouth is only about 5m tall, and the flanking
towers about 11m. The building never gets the vertical mass that would let it
break a skyline, which is the mechanical cause of Q1-D2.

**Q1-D8 — The courtyard figure is from a different game.** `H-07-courtyard.png`.
Anime/JRPG proportions, a flat-shaded face with painted-on eyes and lashes, a
blown-out white complexion with no shadow terminator, and glossy magenta hair —
dropped into a stylised-realism world. She does not match the trainer in the
Queue 2 frames, who is a much better fit for this project's look, and she does
not match the key art's characters. Judged as the game's look, per the skill:
this asset reads as sourced from elsewhere and is not good enough to be the
character the player confronts at the climax of the chapter.

**Q1-D9 — Floor and wall share a material, so the interior has no depth.**
`H-07-courtyard.png` uses nearly the same cobble diffuse at nearly the same
world scale on the ground plane and on all three walls. With no value or scale
break at the wall/floor junction, the room has no read of its own volume. The
one dark doorway is pure black with zero interior fill, so it reads as a hole
punched in a texture rather than a passage.

**Q1-D10 — Grass geometry punches through the paved ramp.**
`H-03-ramp-foot.png`, along the whole ramp surface: grass blades and broadleaf
clumps grow up through the cobbles at x≈300–800, including in the middle of the
processional axis. Either the ramp is not carving the grass field or the field
is being sampled above the ramp's collision. Reads as a bug, not as
weathering — real weeds would be in the joints and at the edges, not standing
proud of the crown of the road.

**Q1-D11 — Lights with no fixtures; a garrison with no lit windows.**
`H-05-east-flank.png` has an orange pool of light on the wall at x≈810 with
nothing emitting it. `H-06-west-keep.png` has an unexplained cyan vertical bar
at x≈300 hanging off the wall under a small red element — it reads as stray
geometry. And in `H-03-ramp-foot-night.png` — the best-lit frame in the set,
where the braziers finally do warm work and the moon and tether cable both read
well — **not one window in the building is lit.** An occupied fortress at night
with four brazier flames and a hundred dark windows is telling the player
nobody is home.

**Q1-D12 — Two of eleven stands are not evidence.** `H-02-sigil-gate.png` is
framed on a wooden pasture fence in an oak grove; there is no sigil gate in it,
and no part of the Hall. `H-04-gate-mouth.png` is the sub-terrain capture from
§0. That is 18% of the delivered evidence showing nothing of the subject, on
the pass that is meant to close an epic.

**Q1-D13 (adjacent, but it is in these frames).** `H-02-sigil-gate.png`: the
oak canopies are a single high-chroma yellow-green at close to one value, all
at similar heights, so the grove reads as noise rather than as trees. The key
art's oaks have a dark underside, a warm sunlit top, trunk-to-canopy value
separation and real height variety. `H-05-east-flank.png` also puts a hard
emerald tile roof next to the beige stone that does not sit in the palette.

### The three things that most separate these frames from the references

1. **Untextured grey rectangles are the largest object in the establishing
   shots.** The references have no such thing, obviously — but the point is
   sharper than "there is a bug." In `H-01` and `H-02b` the artifact is the
   compositional subject, and the building the chapter builds toward is a
   6%-of-frame beige smudge behind it. `palworld-04-plateau-landmark.jpg` puts
   its landmark tower dead centre, high-contrast, silhouetted against sky, and
   you cannot look anywhere else. (`H-01`, `H-02b`, `H-03` all three times of day)

2. **The reference stronghold is built and inhabited; this one is extruded and
   empty.** The key art's "TEAM TETHER STRONGHOLD" panel earns its climax
   through *accumulated human labour* — scaffold timber lashed up two flanks,
   a machine assembly, a palisade of sharpened logs, crates, braziers alight,
   figures on the steps, moss claiming the old stone. Every one of those is
   absent here. What is present instead is flat planes, printed windows and
   printed banners. (`H-05`, `H-06`, `H-07`, `H-08`)

3. **Neither reference has a surface without relief.**
   `palworld-04-plateau-landmark.jpg`'s stone ruins self-shadow at every joint
   and read as blocks even at that image's low resolution; the key art's
   masonry has broken courses, spalled faces and vegetation in the cracks.
   `H-08-wall-close.png` at arm's length is a drawing of a wall, and it is the
   frame that decides whether the player believes the place is real when they
   walk up to it. (`H-08`, `H-06`)

### Bar questions — Queue 1

> **A. Do these frames read as belonging to the world in
> `tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`?**

**No.**

The *ground* nearly does — the grass, clover and flower scatter in `H-01`,
`H-05` and `H-06` are in the key art's palette and have real density, and
`H-03-ramp-foot-golden.png` has the key art's warm low-sun mood almost exactly.
The *building* does not. The key art's stronghold is dark weathered stone,
oxblood cloth, timber and machinery, heavy and inhabited. This is pale beige
toy masonry, magenta rectangles, printed openings and nobody home. Put the two
side by side and only the meadow matches.

**Fixable by changing the scene:** delete or fix the grey panels (Q1-D1); grow
the grass field over the Hall's hill and kill the bald patch (§0, Q1-D2);
recolour the banners to the reserved oxblood and give them a sigil, a taper and
a hem (Q1-D6); light interior windows at night and give the two orphan lights
fixtures (Q1-D11); carve the ramp out of the grass field (Q1-D10); break the
courtyard's floor material away from its wall material and put fill light in
the doorway (Q1-D9); dress the courtyard and both flanks with the crate/barrel/
scaffold/brazier prop family that already exists in this project (Q1-D5);
increase the building's vertical mass so it clears the ridgeline (Q1-D7);
reshoot the two dead stands (Q1-D12).

**Not fixable by changing the scene — this needs art that is not in the
build:** the wall and tower modules themselves. Q1-D3 and Q1-D4 are not
lighting or placement problems; there is no geometry there to light. Recessed
window and arrow-slit modules, a corner/buttress module so cladding stops at a
corner instead of in mid-air, a plinth module so walls meet ground with a
footing, and a single agreed masonry scale across the kit — those are modelling
work, and no amount of scene tuning substitutes. **This is the reason the
verdict is NO rather than "close".** Q1-D8, the courtyard character, is
likewise an asset problem, not a scene problem.

> **B. Shown these frames beside `palworld-0*.jpg`, would someone say these are
> trying to be the same kind of game?**

**Yes for the exteriors, no for the Hall.**

`H-01`, `H-05` and `H-06` — grass density, flower scatter, sky, palette,
overall chroma — would sit beside `palworld-02` and `palworld-03` and read as
the same ambition. `H-03-ramp-foot-night.png` would too.

The Hall would not. Palworld's built structures at this distance have modelled
relief, real openings, prop clutter and figures. Put `H-05-east-flank.png` next
to `palworld-05-base-building.jpg` and the honest read is "one of these is a
game and one is a blockout with a texture on it."

## QUEUE 2 — THE FOUR ASPECT VARIANTS

### Verdict: **partially cleared. One of four is fixed; one is unchanged in
substance; two are marginal.**

The specific charge — "one shared decal mask, hue-swapped four times" — is no
longer true *as a blanket statement*. Ashtusk is now a genuinely different
material treatment with emissive detail and a VFX signature, and it survives
being shrunk. But two of the four are still exactly what was described, and one
of those is on the same shoulder decal in the same place as its base species.

Answering the question as posed — **shown these four beside their base species,
would someone say these are four distinct creatures, or one creature
recoloured?** — the honest answer is: for **Ashtusk**, four distinct; for
**Nightburrow**, a distinct night form and a marginal day form; for
**Riftfrill** and **Stormtrail**, recoloured.

**Root cause, found after the verdicts and detailed at Q2-D15:** this is not a
question of how far a recolour can be pushed. The owner supplied a reference
sheet for each of these four, each with an explicit four- or five-item build
list. Ashtusk implements its list and passes. Stormtrail implements about one
item of five and fails. The variant that was built is the variant that reads.
The next round is mostly a matter of building the rest of what is already
specified, not of designing anything new.

### Ashtusk — cleared, including the specific thing JUDGE-3 flagged

**The ember tusks now read.** This was the named failure and it is fixed.
`ashtusk-vs-tuskroot-night-wide.png`: the tusks are luminous gold-white against
a near-black head, and the facial fissures resolve into a glowing mask, with
ember motes drifting above. At that size, with the creature occupying maybe 15%
of frame height, you can still identify it instantly and you would never
confuse it with Tuskroot. `ashtusk-vs-tuskroot-day-close.png` confirms the
close read: a cracked cold-grey rock surface with orange emissive fissures, a
gold iris rim, and a warm gradient running up the tusk. Tuskroot beside it is
warm terracotta with soft dark dappling, moss on the back ridge, and a plain
peach tusk. Different material family, different value key, different
temperature, different emissive signature. This is what the other three should
be measured against.

Remaining nit — **Q2-D1**: in `ashtusk-vs-tuskroot-day-wide.png` the daytime
tusk glow is much weaker than at night and competes with a bright sky; the
ember read is carried almost entirely by the night frame. Worth checking that
it survives noon in the actual world.

### Stormtrail — **not fixed. This is still hue-swap.**

**Q2-D2.** `stormtrail-vs-trailpup-day-wide.png` is the frame that decides it:
at wide range the two are indistinguishable. Same tan jackal, same head, same
ear set, same eyes, same nose, same posture, same size. The only difference is
a patch on the shoulder — green on Trailpup, gold-and-blue on Stormtrail — in
**the same UV location, at the same scale, with the same soft-edged shape.**
That is not a variant; that is the same decal with the hue rotated, which is
precisely the finding this round was supposed to answer.

`stormtrail-vs-trailpup-day-close.png` makes it worse, not better, because at
close range you can see the patches are the same brush marks.

**Q2-D3.** The shoulder patch is on the creature's **left** shoulder. At the
angle a player fights from, half the time it is not visible at all, and the
creature reverts to being identical to its base.

**Q2-D4.** At night (`stormtrail-vs-trailpup-night-wide.png`) Stormtrail
separates a little, but only because it goes darker; the gold patch and two
white sparks are all that distinguish it, and the sparks float free of the body
rather than emanating from it. Darkness is not a design.

### Riftfrill — **not fixed. Weakest of the four.**

**Q2-D5.** `riftfrill-vs-paddlenewt-day-close.png`: the body is the *identical*
cyan as Paddlenewt. The violet is airbrushed onto the crown and the tips of the
frills and stops there. Head, eyes, snout, limb colour, belly, flank — all
unchanged. This is the textbook "one creature recoloured."

**Q2-D6.** For a creature whose name and read are its frill, **the frill
silhouette is byte-identical to the base's.** Same six fronds, same splay, same
length, same curl. Silhouette is the first criterion in this project's own
rubric and the variant does not touch it. A frill that fans wider, droops, or
gains a torn/split frond would separate these two at 30% size with no material
change at all.

**Q2-D7.** The violet is confined to the top of the head, so it is visible only
from a high front angle. `riftfrill-vs-paddlenewt-night-wide.png` shows the
consequence: at range, at night, the two are the same blue blob with slightly
different particle counts.

### Nightburrow — **marginal. Works at night, thin by day.**

**Q2-D8.** `nightburrow-vs-burrowback-day-close.png`: the facial marking
pattern is identical to Burrowback's — same white blaze shape, same dark mask
edge, same jaw split. What changed is the value (Burrowback's blaze is white,
Nightburrow's is silver-grey), plus a purple emissive rim along the shoulder
and ear crease and a gold iris. The emissive rim is a real addition and it is
the strongest of the three non-Ashtusk changes, but the underlying mask is
shared and visible.

**Q2-D9.** `nightburrow-vs-burrowback-night-wide.png` is the variant's best
frame and it genuinely works — Burrowback's white blaze blazes, Nightburrow is
swallowed into black with a faint violet rim and pink motes. Read as different
things. But that separation is being done by the *lighting*, not by the asset:
by day (`-day-wide`) they are two badgers of the same size and shape with
different levels of contrast.

**Q2-D10.** The purple motes sit in a detached cluster well above the back with
a clear gap, so they read as unrelated floaters rather than as something the
creature is emitting. Same issue on Stormtrail's sparks and Riftfrill's.

### Cross-cutting

**Q2-D11 — the base species share the decal family too.** Burrowback has a
green mossy patch on its crown; Trailpup has a green mossy patch on its
shoulder; Tuskroot has green moss on its back ridge. Same soft-edged
hand-painted green, same treatment. So the "one shared mask" observation is not
only about the four variants — the *bases* are being differentiated from each
other by the same device, in different places. Whatever is decided for the
variants should probably be decided for the bases at the same time.

**Q2-D12 — WITHDRAWN.** My blind note here read: *"all four variants
differentiate by surface only; none by silhouette — per this rubric's first
criterion, four variants invisible at 30% are not four creatures."*

After the verdicts I opened `docs/art/reference/creature-expansion-2026-08-30/`,
which is **owner-supplied reference art** and therefore canon above my rubric
reading. All four sheets specify the build path as `VARIANT RECOLOR + VFX` and
all four explicitly direct silhouette *preservation* — "Preserve the core boar
silhouette", "Keep the body silhouette close to Trailpup", "Preserve the main
silhouette", "Keep armor shapes broad and readable". Silhouette-identical is
the owner's instruction, not the lane's shortcut. I withdraw the defect.

It is replaced by Q2-D15, which is the same evidence read correctly.

**Q2-D15 — three of the four variants do not implement their own owner sheet,
and the item they all skip is the one that would have worked.**

This is the finding that should drive the next round, and it only became
visible once the reference sheets were open. Each sheet's Pipeline Note is a
short explicit list. Scoring the frames against them:

| Sheet directive | Nightburrow | Stormtrail | Riftfrill | Ashtusk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Darken body / coat / fur | yes | **no — still base tan** | **no — still base cyan** | yes |
| Emissive cracks / markings | partial — a shoulder rim, not cracks between plates | **no — a soft blob on one shoulder, not the spine bolt** | **no — none** | yes — fissures on head and tusks |
| **Glowing eyes** | **no — built gold; sheet says Violet Glow** | **no — plain brown; sheet says cyan** | **no — plain black** | partial — gold iris rim |
| Signature VFX | partial — detached motes, not purple flame | **no — two white dots, not arcs** | partial — a few motes | yes — ember motes |
| Scale up modestly | **no — same size as base** | **no — same size as base** | n/a (not specified) | yes |

Read down the columns and the round explains itself. **Ashtusk implements its
sheet and is the one variant that reads as a distinct creature. Stormtrail
implements roughly one item of five and is the one that reads as a pure
recolour.** The correlation is exact, across four cases, and it means this is
not an aesthetic disagreement about how far a recolour can go — the recolour
that was specified has not been built.

Two specifics worth calling out on their own:

- **Every one of the four sheets asks for glowing eyes. Three of four shipped
  with the base species' ordinary eyes.** It is the cheapest identity cue
  available, it survives to any distance, it works in silhouette and in
  darkness, and Palworld leans on it constantly. `stormtrail-...-day-close.png`
  shows plain brown eyes where the sheet's detail inset shows vivid cyan;
  `riftfrill-...-day-close.png` shows the base's flat black eyes where the
  sheet shows luminous white-violet. Doing only this, on all four, would
  buy more separation than everything else in this round combined.
- **Stormtrail's spine bolt is missing entirely.** The sheet's whole design is
  a gold lightning zigzag running head to tail along the spine with violet arcs
  down the legs and flanks. The build has a soft gold patch roughly 40px across
  on the left shoulder. That is not an under-delivered version of the sheet; it
  is a different idea. Q2-D2/D3/D4 above are all downstream of this one thing.

Note also that this reframes Q2-D8 in the lane's favour: Nightburrow keeping
the badger blaze is *correct* — the sheet keeps it too. What the sheet does not
keep is the eye colour and the purple flame, and those are the misses.

**Q2-D13 — the framing of the eight "close" shots wastes them.** In
`stormtrail-vs-trailpup-day-close.png` and `riftfrill-vs-paddlenewt-day-close.png`
both subjects are pushed to the frame edges with half the image empty green
plane; in `nightburrow-...-day-close.png` and `ashtusk-...-day-close.png` both
heads are cropped by the frame edges. For an A/B comparison the two subjects
need to be adjacent, whole, and the same distance from camera.

**Q2-D14 — the trainer is unlit in the night frames.** He renders as flat
ambient with no rim and no key, reading as a cutout. He is the ruler in these
shots; he should be lit like a character.

### Scale (measured against the 1.80m trainer, who is in every wide frame)

Relative scale is internally consistent and I found no error of the kind this
criterion was added for. Boar ≈3.5m long with its shoulder at roughly the
trainer's shoulder; badger ≈2.3m long, shoulder at chest height; axolotl and
jackal both under a metre. Each pair matches its own base exactly. Whether a
badger *should* be bear-sized is a design decision and not mine.

### Bar questions — Queue 2

> **A. Do these frames read as belonging to the world in the key art?**

**Unanswerable from this evidence, and that is itself the finding.** These are
turntable shots on a flat green plane with no grass, no terrain, no props and a
gradient sky. Nothing here can be compared to the key art's world because there
is no world in the frame. Judged as *creature art alone*: Ashtusk yes,
emphatically — the cold cracked rock with ember fissures would sit in the key
art's night panel without argument. Paddlenewt/Riftfrill's high-chroma cyan is
outside the key art's palette strip in both forms. Burrowback and Trailpup are
naturalistic in a way that fits.

> **B. Beside `palworld-0*.jpg`, same kind of game?**

**Yes.** The modelling, shading and material quality on all eight creatures
would sit beside `palworld-01`'s Mammorest without embarrassment — these are
good assets, and Ashtusk in particular would hold its own in that company.

I had drafted a "no" here on the grounds that Palworld does not build a roster
by recolouring one mesh. That is not a fair charge: Palworld does exactly that
for its alpha and variant tiers, and more to the point the owner's own sheets
direct it. The charge that survives is narrower and is Q2-D15 — Palworld's
recolours change *coat value, eye emission and VFX together*, which is
precisely the list these sheets specify and precisely what three of the four
did not do. Same kind of game, same intended method, three of four
under-executed.

## Routing notes added after the verdicts

*(Written after the verdicts above were final. Nothing below changed a verdict.)*

- **Correcting my own note here.** I first wrote that the grey panels appeared
  unnoticed rather than accepted. That is wrong and unfair to the design lane.
  `HALL_DESIGN_2026-08-30.md` §11 is an explicit numeric acceptance list, and
  the panels violate its item 9 by name: *"no untextured mass anywhere in
  H-01..H-08."* They are a stated, checkable failure of the lane's own gate,
  not an oversight nobody had thought about.

- **Four more of the design's own §11 criteria fail on the delivered frames.**
  Measured, so the lane can re-check them directly:
  - **Item 7** — *"wall patch std-dev ≥ 35; lit wall mean in [150,185] — not
    (212,203,185)"*. Measured on `H-05`: std-dev **17.5**, half the floor;
    mean luma **104**, some 46 units *below* the floor. The over-bright
    maquette problem has been over-corrected into an under-lit, low-variance
    one. Both fail the same criterion from opposite sides.
  - **Item 8** — *"wall stone ≥ ground cobble scale, ratio ≤ 1.5:1 at H-08"*.
    The two adjacent wall panels in `H-08` are themselves about **2:1** to each
    other, so the criterion cannot be satisfied on both faces at once.
  - **Item 4** — *"gate has frame, reveal and depth (H-04)"*. `H-04` is the
    corrupt sub-terrain frame. **The single stand designated to prove the
    openings have depth is the one that shows nothing**, which is also the
    stand that would have answered Q1-D4. Reshooting `H-04` is the cheapest
    high-value action available to this lane.
  - **Item 2** — *"roofline breaks ≥ 4 times at H-01"*. Unverifiable: at 9
    luma units of contrast against sky (Q1-D2) the roofline does not resolve

  pass it, and running §11 against them before handing off would have caught
