# JUDGE-5 — blind visual verdict, 2026-08-30

Frames read directly out of `ralph/T1-HALL-REBUILD`, `ralph/T1-STORMWALL` and
`ralph/T1-VARIANTS-2` without merging. References: `docs/reference/tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`
and `docs/reference/palworld-0*.jpg`. No score. No design docs, handovers or lane
notes were opened.

Pixel coordinates below are on the 1280×800 frame, origin top-left.

---

## SET 1 — The Meadows Hall (`ralph/T1-HALL-REBUILD`)

### Does it read as a fortified seat of power?

**No. It reads as a dressed greybox.** The rebuild put a real meadow around the
Hall and left the Hall itself almost untouched. Ground, vegetation, sky and the
ramp approach are markedly better than `hall0830`. The fortress is not.

The reference panel labelled TEAM TETHER STRONGHOLD (MEADOWS HALL) in the key art
is doing four things none of these frames do: it stacks masses at different
heights so the roofline breaks in five places; it hangs sigil banners that read as
cloth; it wraps the stone in timber scaffolding and a palisade so the wall has
depth and occupation; and it puts weathering — moss, staining, broken crenels —
on every surface. The build has one flat wall height, flat quad banners, no
scaffolding, and uniform clean stone.

### Did the rebuild improve on `T1-HALL/shots/hall0830/`?

**Yes, substantially, but only outside the walls.**

Improved:
- **H-01-approach-400** — `hall0830` had bare noise-textured ground from y≈450 down.
  The rebuild adds grass, flowering shrubs, a worn path and pebbles. Genuinely better.
- **H-02-sigil-gate** — same story: mown-blur ground replaced by real grass, wildflowers,
  a dirt path with scattered stones at (250-350, 640-750).
- **H-03-ramp-foot** — the biggest single gain. `hall0830` was a bare chocolate ramp
  between two empty green banks. The rebuild adds stone parapets, timber rails,
  braziers on plinths, banners flanking the arch, and dense foreground planting.
- **H-05-east-flank** — foreground hill goes from smeared green blur to real grass
  and flower scatter.
- **Time-of-day coverage is new and worth keeping.** `H-03-ramp-foot-golden` is the
  best frame in the set: the low warm key gives the parapet stone real form and the
  ramp finally has a value range.

Not improved — identical or near-identical to `hall0830`:
- **H-04-gate-mouth** — still a broken capture (see D1).
- **H-07-courtyard** — the architecture is unchanged frame-for-frame. Same walls,
  same doorway at (335-530, 265-465), same cyan bar at (0-280, 460-520), same
  shadow on the left wall. Only the character standing in it changed, and that is
  a regression (D2).
- **H-06-west-keep** — the broken keep mesh is untouched (D7).

### Defects, this set

**D1. `H-04-gate-mouth` is not a frame of the gate.** The camera is inside/below the
ramp: the lower two-thirds is the underside of the cobble deck, the upper third is
grass and pebble billboards clipping through it edge-on at y≈0-215, and there is a
pure-black unlit column at x≈400-880. The gate is not visible. `hall0830`'s version
of this stand is broken the same way. This is the threshold the player walks through
to fight the Warden and there is still no image of it.

**D2. The courtyard is an empty box and the figure in it is from a different game.**
In `H-07-courtyard` the Warden arena is four untextured-looking tiled stone walls and
a cobble floor — no crates, no braziers, no banners, no apparatus, nothing that says
anyone occupies it. The character at (560-740, 355-790) is cel-shaded anime: flat
white blown-out face, painted eye and mouth, hair as a solid magenta mass. The world
around her is painted stylised-realism. She does not share a rendering language with
the wall behind her. Her cast shadow at (700-820, 640-740) is a hard blob thrown to
her right while the large soft architectural shadow on the left wall at (0-130, 30-330)
falls the other way — two light directions in one frame. The `hall0830` grunt in the
same stand, in oxblood with a shoulder sigil, sat in the world far better; swapping
him out cost style cohesion and cost the Team Tether colour identity.

**D3. The Hall has no landmark silhouette.** In `H-01-approach-400` the entire
stronghold is a pale ~60px smudge at (620-700, 375-405) — smaller and lower-contrast
than the tether pylons flanking it at (700-900, 350-430), which read first. The rubric
asks whether landmarks are visible from distance; at the approach stand the answer is
that the pylons are and the fortress is not. `H-02b-sigil-gate-raised` shows why: the
Hall is a single symmetric mass on a bald hill, one roofline, no towers breaking
upward, no mass at the flanks. Palworld's `palworld-04-plateau-landmark.jpg` puts its
landmark spire against sky with clear negative space around it; the Hall is buried in
its own hill.

**D4. The "sigil gate" has neither.** `H-02-sigil-gate` is a three-rail farm fence with
a small yellow padlock at (465-480, 545-570). No sigil, no banner, no gatehouse, no
Team Tether mark of any kind. Nothing tells the player they have crossed into hostile
ground. `H-02b` shows the same fence line as a thin white dash across the ridge.

**D5. The walls are single planes.** `H-05-east-flank`, wall face (320-900, 200-460):
the masonry texture tiles visibly at roughly 2m repeat, the wall has no batter, no
buttress, no parapet thickness, and the crenellations at y≈195-215 are identical cubes
at identical spacing. The dark timber crosses at (455-745, 260-360) have no structural
logic — they are not hoardings, brattices or scaffolds, they are crossed beams stuck on
a flat face. There is an unmotivated warm glow blob at (780-830, 300-340) with no light
source in frame.

**D6. Banners are flat quads in the wrong red.** `H-05` at (480-520, 285-355) and
(665-720, 285-350) — pure-fill magenta-crimson rectangles, no cloth shape, no sag, no
sigil. `H-06` at (700-730, 200-460) is a pink strip clipping through the tower corner.
`H-08` at (710-850, 180-210) is a dark-red stub cut off by geometry. The key art's
banners carry the compass sigil and hang in folds. The hue is also wrong: the day
frames read hot magenta, not oxblood — compare the same banners in
`H-03-ramp-foot-golden` where they sit correctly at a deep muted red. Under the day
key the Team Tether colour has drifted bright enough to read decorative rather than
dangerous.

**D7. `H-06-west-keep` is a broken mesh, unchanged from `hall0830`.** The keep at
(690-1280, 0-660) is two materials butted with a hard vertical seam at x≈875 — pale
cream facing on the left, dark brown block on the right, no trim, no shared lighting.
The pale facade's bottom edge stops in mid-air at y≈590 with the dark base visible
below and behind it. Windows are pure black rectangles with no recess, frame or glass.
There are flat grey quads lying on the grass at (95-400, 630-700) that read as
untextured decals. The cyan tether line at (295, 380-460) is a vertical glowing bar
that terminates in nothing.

**D8. `H-08-wall-close` shows the wall has no close-range material.** All stone form is
painted into the diffuse; there is no normal map, so at this range the wall is mushy
and flat. Top-left, a pale block with rectangular holes at (0-500, 0-160) intersects
the grey-green slab above it with a raw boolean edge and no trim. There is a hard
material seam at x≈935 between tan and near-black wall with no transition. At the far
left edge (0-15, 270-790) sky and grass are visible **through** the wall — a gap
between segments.

**D9. Far terrain is bald and seamed.** In `H-02b` and both StormWall frames everything
past the treeline is flat pale mint with zero scatter, there is a white untextured
splat at (740-940, 405-470), and vegetation stops dead along a line at y≈490. In
`H-03-ramp-foot` the right bank has the same washed pale surface with a hard diagonal
splat edge running (600,600)→(1050,450).

**D10. Ground scatter is on a grid.** `H-01-approach-400`: the shrubs across
(0-1280, 450-800) sit at near-regular intervals in loose rows, all the same species,
all the same scale. Palworld's `palworld-02-open-field-path.jpg` clusters its grass and
leaves real clearings; this reads as a density value, not a placement.

**D11. Foliage clips through the ramp.** `H-03-ramp-foot`: plants growing out of the
cobble deck at (490-520, 470-510) and (700-780, 395-425).

**D12. Night is unresolved.** `H-03-ramp-foot-night`: the moon at (300-450, 105-215) is
a soft white blob with no disc edge. The braziers are lit, which is right, but they
throw no pooled light on the deck. The key art night panel is deep blue with a readable
landscape and firelight; this is mostly black.

**No trainer appears in any Hall frame**, so the rubric's 1.80m ruler is unavailable
for the architecture. Using the courtyard figure in `H-07` as the stand-in, the wall
stones read at roughly 30cm — plausible — but the doorway at (335-530, 265-465) is
barely wider than she is, which makes the fortress interior feel like a house.

## SET 2 — StormWall behind the Hall (`ralph/T1-STORMWALL`)

### Does the storm formation read as weather, or as flat grey slabs in the sky?

**It reads as weather now. The slabs are gone. This is the cleanest fix of the three
sets.** But it has overshot into the opposite failure: it reads as a distant smoke or
haze bank, not as a storm.

`defect1-before-...png` is unambiguous: four hard-edged translucent grey rectangles at
(60-920, 140-410) with visible corners, visible depth-sort overlap where they cross,
and a flat bottom edge. Nothing about it is weather. The same slabs are what is
sitting behind the Hall in `H-01`, `H-02b`, `H-03-ramp-foot` (left 300-450 and right
850-1180, y 200-330), `H-03-ramp-foot-golden` (cream rectangles at 295-460 and
850-1180) and `H-03-ramp-foot-night` (pale blue rectangles brighter than the sky
around them). **The Hall set was captured before this fix and still carries the slabs
in five frames.**

`defect1-after-...png` replaces them with a soft-edged dark band at roughly
(280-900, 250-400). Edges are soft, the shape is irregular, it sits behind the terrain
horizon correctly. That is real progress.

Remaining defects:

**D13. It has no internal structure.** The band is a single near-uniform neutral grey
with almost no value variation across its whole extent. A storm front has a defined
top — an anvil, a shelf, a lit upper edge — and darker rain shafts below it. This has
a diffuse top that just fades out around y≈250 and a flat bottom cut along the horizon
at y≈378. It reads as a smoke plume from something burning behind the ridge.

**D14. It is colourless and does not light.** Pure neutral grey, no cool shadow side,
no warm rim where the sun would catch its top edge. And the Hall directly in front of
it at (515-720, 310-450) is in full warm sunlight with no darkening, no cast shadow
approaching, no colour temperature shift. A storm front that large and that close
would change the light on the thing in front of it. Because it does not, it reads as
a decal pasted behind the Hall rather than a body of air in the world.

**D15. It sits under the cloud layer.** The warm cirrus at y≈180-235 runs above and
across it, so the storm occupies a band lower in the sky than ordinary fair-weather
cloud. That inversion is a large part of why it reads as ground haze.

**D16. It is too small to be an event.** It spans about half the frame width and stops
well short of the right side. In the key art's sunset panel the sky does most of the
dramatic work across the full frame. Here the sky is 55% of the image and mostly empty
pale blue.

`defect2-H-04-gate-mouth-known-bad-frame.png` is the same broken capture as D1 — the
camera is buried in the ramp. I judged it as an image and it contains no scene.

## SET 3 — Aspect creature variants (`ralph/T1-VARIANTS-2`)

### Do these read as distinct creatures, or as palette-swapped recolours?

**Distinct enough to tell apart in a fight; still one silhouette per pair.** Judged
against `T1-VARIANTS`, this is a large, real improvement — but it is differentiation by
material, not by creature.

The `T1-VARIANTS` set was in places a literal palette swap and in one case not even
that. `VARIANTS/stormtrail-vs-trailpup-day-wide.png` shows **two identical grey
wolves** — the only difference is a small moss patch on one shoulder and a faint
yellow smudge on the other. That is indefensible. `VARIANTS-2` makes Stormtrail
near-black with yellow markings and glowing motes. Now they separate instantly. Same
for Ashtusk, which gains emissive orange fissures and gold tusks against Tuskroot's
warm tan and mossy back. The `VARIANTS-2` camera also pulled back far enough to show
the whole animal, where `VARIANTS` close-ups were cropped inside the head and could not
be judged at all. Both changes were correct.

Remaining defects:

**D17. Every pair shares one mesh, one pose and — with one exception — one size.**
Ashtusk/Tuskroot, Riftfrill/Paddlenewt and Stormtrail/Trailpup are the same model
twice, standing identically, at the same height. Squint any `-day-wide` to 30% and the
only surviving difference is value: light animal, dark animal. Palworld's roster in
`palworld-04-plateau-landmark.jpg` separates its creatures by outline — wing shape,
limb count, body mass — before colour does any work. Nightburrow/Burrowback is the one
pair that gets this right: Nightburrow is visibly the larger animal (crown at y≈255 vs
y≈305 on the same ground line) with a broader shell over the shoulders. That is the
model the other three pairs should follow.

**D18. Riftfrill/Paddlenewt is still essentially a hue shift.** `riftfrill-vs-paddlenewt-day-wide`:
same mesh, same pose, same size, and the change is violet on the frills and crown over
an otherwise identical cyan body. It is the weakest of the four and the one most
likely to be read as a recolour.

**D19. Nightburrow has four eyes.** `nightburrow-vs-burrowback-night-close`: two pink
glow sprites sit on the forehead at (790-850, 265-320) and (1000-1050, 270-315), while
the animal's actual eyes are below them — the gold-iris eye is clearly visible at
(855-880, 450-475). The glow sprites are also visibly hexagonal rather than round, so
they read as polygon billboards. This is the loudest single artefact in the creature
set.

**D20. Trailpup is a stock grey wolf.** `stormtrail-vs-trailpup-day-wide`, left figure:
naturalistic coyote proportions, naturalistic grey-tan coat, no exaggeration, no
designed marking, no fantasy language anywhere on it. Beside the trainer — who is
stylised, big-eyed, cel-lit — it looks like an animal from a different asset library.
The bar for this project is bespoke expressive creature art; this reads sourced. The
boars, badgers and newts all clear that bar; this one does not.

**D21. Some markings read as damage rather than design.** Stormtrail's yellow patches
at (850-880, 465-505) and down the forelegs are irregular blotches with soft edges that
look like texture bleed. Nightburrow's chest patch at (855-905, 490-540) is a scribbly
grey smear. Ashtusk's emissive fissures, by contrast, are placed on anatomy and read as
intentional — that is the standard to match.

**D22. The roster occupies only two size bands, and the two large species collide.**
Against the 1.80m trainer: the boars stand roughly 2.1m at the crest, and Nightburrow
stands roughly the same. A badger and a tusked boar reading as the same mass removes a
distinction the player would otherwise get for free at a glance. The canids sit around
0.9m at the shoulder and the newts around 0.55m. Nothing occupies the space between
0.9m and 2.1m.

**D23. The night stands are a black void and cannot support a lighting judgement.**
All four `-night-` frames: the sky above y≈410 is near-black with no moon and no stars,
the ground is a flat vertical blue ramp, and there is a hard horizontal seam where the
two meet (clearest in `riftfrill-vs-paddlenewt-night-close` at y=380). The emissives
read well against it, which is the useful information; nothing else in those frames is.

## The three things that most separate these frames from the references

**1. The reference stronghold is occupied; this one is empty.** The key art's MEADOWS
HALL panel is covered in evidence of people — timber scaffolding lashed to the stone,
a palisade, banner masts, apparatus bolted to the walls, stacked material at the foot
of the stair. `H-07-courtyard` has four bare walls and a floor. `H-05-east-flank` has a
flat wall face and two crossed beams. `palworld-05-base-building.jpg` does the same
thing the key art does: the structure is dressed with the stuff of its use. Nothing in
the Hall set is.

**2. The references break their silhouettes; this one does not.**
`palworld-04-plateau-landmark.jpg` reads at a glance because its landmark has a
distinct outline against open sky. The key art stronghold has five separate mass
heights. `H-02b-sigil-gate-raised` shows the Hall as one symmetric block, one roofline,
sunk into a bald hill, with a wall whose crenellations repeat at a single height —
and at `H-01-approach-400` range it loses to the pylons in front of it.

**3. The references have a value range; these are mid-tone.**
`palworld-01-boss-fight-forest.jpg` runs from near-black foliage through a fully lit
creature to blown gold sparks, and the fight is legible because of that spread. Almost
every Hall frame here sits in a narrow band of tan-brown and mid-green under a flat
overhead key — `H-05`, `H-07`, `H-08` in particular have no true darks and no true
lights. The one frame that breaks this, `H-03-ramp-foot-golden`, is immediately the
best-looking frame in the set, which shows the geometry can carry it once the lighting
gives it form.

## The two bar questions

### A. Do these frames read as belonging to the world in `tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`?

**Partly — and for the first time the answer is not a flat no.** For the meadow, yes.
The ground in `H-01-approach-400`, `H-02-sigil-gate` and `H-03-ramp-foot` — grass,
wildflowers, worn paths, pebbles, layered planting — now sits in the key art's world.
`H-03-ramp-foot-golden` would not look out of place beside the sunset monolith panel.
The creature set also belongs: the boars, badgers and newts share the key art's warm
stylised palette and its readable shapes.

For the fortress, **no.** The Hall is not the building in that panel and is not
currently in the same world as its own meadow. And `H-07-courtyard` breaks the world
outright — the figure standing in it is drawn in a different idiom from everything
around her.

### B. Shown these frames beside `palworld-0*.jpg`, would someone say these are trying to be the same kind of game?

**Yes for the creatures. No for the Hall.**

The `VARIANTS-2` wides, with the trainer standing beside the roster on clean ground,
are recognisably the same genre as `palworld-03-field-boss-meadow.jpg` — comparable
creature appeal, comparable proportion language, comparable readability. That is a real
pass and it was not one in `T1-VARIANTS`.

The Hall frames are not. Someone shown `H-05-east-flank` or `H-07-courtyard` beside
`palworld-01-boss-fight-forest.jpg` would say the first is an in-progress level and the
second is a game. The gap is occupation, silhouette and value range, not resolution.

### What is fixable in-scene, and what needs art that is not in the build

**Fixable by changing the scene — density, palette, lighting, composition, scatter,
placement:**
- D1 gate-mouth capture — move the camera stand out of the geometry.
- D3 landmark silhouette — raise/stagger the existing masses, cut the hill back so the
  Hall stands against sky, move the pylons out of the approach sightline.
- D9/D10 far terrain and grid scatter — the scatter system clearly works; extend it
  past the treeline, cluster it, vary scale, fix the splat seams.
- D12/D23 night — this is a lighting setup, not an asset problem. Add moon, sky
  gradient and brazier pools.
- D13-D16 storm — structure, colour temperature, cloud-layer ordering and a light
  response on the Hall are all scene work.
- D5's tiling, D6's banner hue, D11 clipping, D7's material seam and floating facade,
  D8's wall gap, and the stray cyan tether bars are all fixes in the scene or the
  material, not new art.
- D22 relative sizes — a scale value per species.
- D2's contradicting shadow and blown-out character lighting.

**Needs art that is not in the build:**
- **A dressed fortress kit.** Scaffolding, palisade, hoardings, banner masts, wall-top
  props, gate furniture, damage and moss decals. Without these the walls stay flat
  planes no matter how they are lit. This is the single largest gap in the Hall set.
- **Cloth banners with the Team Tether sigil.** The current quads cannot be fixed into
  cloth by material work.
- **Courtyard props** for the Warden arena — the room is empty because there is nothing
  to put in it.
- **A close-range wall material** with a normal map. D8 is a texture-authoring gap.
- **A humanoid for the courtyard that matches the world's rendering idiom** — or the
  `hall0830` grunt back. This is a style decision, not a scene fix.
- **Trailpup**, and silhouette variation for the three same-shape variant pairs.
  Nightburrow/Burrowback shows this can be done with scale and a shell overlay rather
  than a new mesh, so it may be cheaper than it looks — but it is asset work.
