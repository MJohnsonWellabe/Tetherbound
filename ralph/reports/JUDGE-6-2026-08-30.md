# JUDGE-6 — Meadows Hall, blind visual pass

**Date:** 2026-08-30
**Branch judged:** `ralph/T1-HALL-3`
**Stand judged:** `ralph/reports/T1-HALL-3/shots/` (11 frames) + `ralph/reports/T1-HALL-3/D11-causeway-footprint-ab.png`
**References used:** `docs/reference/tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png` (esp. the TEAM TETHER STRONGHOLD panel), `docs/reference/palworld-0*.jpg`
**Read before writing this verdict:** nothing else. No handover, no design doc, no prior judge report, no backlog.

---

## Capture validity

All 11 shots contain a scene. None is a buried camera, an empty sky, or a uniform-colour fill.
`H-08-wall-close.png` fills the frame with wall, but it is a deliberate material close-up — the wall
corner and a sliver of roof and sky are visible at the top edges, and the surface is correctly lit and
textured. I judged it as a material inspection shot, not a broken capture. The grass field is bound in
every frame that should show it.

---

## Answer to the question this pass exists for

The previous blind pass said *"a dressed greybox… the rebuild left the Hall itself almost untouched."*

**That is no longer accurate as written, and I want to say so plainly before I criticise anything.** The
fortress now has towers, a curtain wall with a parapet, a gatehouse, a keep mass, a courtyard with an
interior doorway, and a built stone causeway. That is real structure, not boxes.

But the failure changed shape rather than going away. It no longer reads as a greybox. **It reads as a
stock asset-pack castle** — a cream, clean, freshly-built, symmetrical medieval castle kit with a jade
pagoda roof — dropped into the Meadows. Almost none of the specific design language on the owner's own
stronghold panel is present in it: no overgrowth, no ruin, no timber-and-iron retrofit, no monumental
banners, no arch. That is a different criticism and I think a more useful one, because "it's a
greybox" implies *finish it*, and what I actually see implies *it is finished, as the wrong building*.

## What is genuinely good

I want this on the record because it is real and some of it is the best work in the frame set.

1. **`H-03-ramp-foot-night.png` is the strongest frame here, by a wide margin.** The fortress finally
   has a silhouette — a dark, complex mass against a moonlit sky. The four brazier flames read as warm
   points against the cool blue, the cyan cable arcing off to the right reads as menace rather than as
   debug geometry, and the warm rim on the tower tops (x 465–590 and 680–810, y 40–110) is exactly the
   right note. **This frame proves the geometry is capable.** Whatever is wrong in daylight is not
   wrong with the model.
2. **`H-03-ramp-foot-golden.png`** — the low raking sun gives the left parapet (x 0–560, y 280–560)
   genuine form, and the fence-post shadows falling across the causeway (x 780–1080, y 300–400) are the
   single best piece of lighting in the set.
3. **The one-point composition of all three ramp-foot frames.** The causeway converging on the centred
   gate is a real, confident, authored shot. Nothing procedural about it.
4. **The Team Tether grunt in `H-07-courtyard.png`.** Oxblood tunic, gold cross-strap, sigil cap, red
   chest gem, masked face. This is on-brief, distinctive, and reads as a designed faction uniform. It
   holds up against Palworld's character work better than anything else in the set does against
   anything else in the references.
5. **The proper banner asset** (both banners in `H-07`, and one at x 1140–1180 in `H-05`): sigil, gold
   hanger rail, notched hem. That *is* the board's language.
6. **Oxblood discipline is intact.** Red appears only on Team Tether property. It has not leaked onto
   a fence, a plant, a path or a friendly prop anywhere in the set.
7. **Near-field ground scatter** (`H-02`, `H-03`, D11 panels 1 and 3) is dense, varied and has real
   species mix — grasses, broadleaf bushes, white and orange flowers, pebbles. Inside about 40 m the
   meadow is good.

## Defects, ranked by cost to the player's impression

### 1. The reveal has no silhouette. The fortress is the same value as the ground it stands on.

`H-02b-sigil-gate-raised.png` is the establishing shot — the moment the player first sees the place
they will fight the Warden. Measured luminance in that frame:

| region | mean L | sd |
|---|---|---|
| fortress mass (600,350)–(720,420) | **137.6** | 26.6 |
| bald hill immediately right of it (760,430)–(900,500) | **162.5** | 54.7 |
| bald midground left (200,420)–(500,500) | **143.1** | 29.5 |
| near foreground grass and trees (0,600)–(400,800) | 41.1 | 32.0 |

The fortress is within **5 luminance points** of the ground beside it and is *darker* than the hill to
its right. There is no figure/ground separation at all. The keyart stronghold panel, measured the same
way, puts the fortress mass at 72 against 104 for the hill behind it — a 32-point separation, with the
fortress as the **dark** shape. That is what makes it a landmark.

The consequence is visible without any measurement. At thumbnail size:
- **`H-01-approach-400.png`: you cannot find the fortress.** It is a 25×35 px pale nub at (630, 390).
  In the same frame, an ordinary meadow tree at x 645–675 is *taller on screen than the entire
  fortress*. The climax location of the chapter loses a silhouette contest to a tree.
- **`H-02-sigil-gate.png`: the sigil gate in the foreground out-reads the fortress behind it.** The
  gate's red banners are the highest-contrast element in the frame; the fortress at x 615–800 is a pale
  smudge against a pale hill under a grey haze band.

This is the most expensive defect in the set, because it fails at exactly the three moments whose only
job is to make the player want to go there.

### 2. Everything beyond roughly 60 m is bald.

`H-02b`, and confirmed in `H-02` and `H-03`. From the treeline outward, the terrain is a uniform,
near-white, desaturated yellow-green (`H-02b` right side, x 900–1280, y 380–560) with **no grass, no
bushes, no rocks, no props of any kind**, marked only by faint horizontal contour banding that reads as
map contour lines rather than landform. There is a hard vegetation cull line: in `H-02b` there is not a
single tree above y ≈ 560 across the whole 1280 px width, and below it there are dozens.

The D11 A/B stand shows why: the middle panel is the ground with scatter removed, and the base albedo
underneath is a very pale washed chartreuse. That colour is what the entire mid-distance is made of.

Palworld-04, the closest reference shot (landmark at distance), has foliage and rock right up to the
base of the far cliff, with atmospheric haze doing the depth work. Here the depth cue is *absence of
content*, which reads as an unfinished level, not as distance.

Related, same frame: along the far ridge (x 480–1280, y 400–420) there is a hard dark rim with **evenly
spaced identical pale posts** along it. Regular intervals plus identical props is the textbook
procedural read, and combined with the hard rim it says "edge of the map".

Also in `H-02b`: a stark near-white patch at x 745–800, y 430–470 beside the ramp reads as snow or as a
hole in the terrain texture.

### 3. The stone material is a tier below everything around it, and it is used at four scales at once.

`H-08-wall-close.png` is the clearest evidence and it is unambiguous. The wall material is a
hand-painted cartoon stone in which **the highlight and the shadow are baked into the albedo**: every
stone carries a hard cream rim on its top-left edge and a hard black band on its bottom-right, and
those are identical regardless of where the stone sits on the surface or which way the surface faces.
Compare the painted shadow under the stone at (400,430)–(520,470) with the one at (250,200)–(350,230) —
same shape, same value, different part of the wall. There is no normal map; the surface is flat. The
stones have no grain, no chips, no lichen, no colour variation between them.

The tile repeat is visible at normal viewing distance: the stone at (460,110)–(680,220) recurs at
(900,300)–(1040,400), and the block at (250,320)–(500,410) recurs at (400,620)–(620,760).

And in that single frame there are **three UV scales meeting at two hard vertical seams** — the main
face at x 250–1280, a third-scale version at x 0–180, and a different strip at x 180–250. This repeats
everywhere:
- `H-04-gate-mouth.png`: tower cladding stones ~70 px (x 210–360), gate wall behind ~25 px (x 520–770),
  causeway ~55 px, parapet kerb ~70 px. One material, four scales, one frame.
- `H-06-west-keep.png`: the near tower (x 660–900) is at roughly **4× the wall's scale** and the stones
  become 120 px soap-smears (x 730–880, y 30–130) with airbrushed white streaks. It reads as wet clay,
  not masonry.
- `H-07-courtyard.png`: the left wall and the right wall (meeting at x ≈ 830) are two different stone
  materials with a hard vertical seam and no transition.

Beside palworld-04's and -05's stone buildings — quiet, low-contrast, real bevel, dirt in the joints —
this is the loudest single "different tier of production" signal in the set.

### 4. None of the stronghold panel's design language is on the building.

> **Two rows of the table below are corrected in the Reconciliation section at the end** — the merlons
> do vary, and a thin parapet moss fringe does exist. The blind text is left as written; read the
> correction before acting on either row.

Going down the board's own stronghold panel and checking each element against the frames:

| board element | in the frames |
|---|---|
| heavy ivy/moss overgrowth cascading down the walls | **absent everywhere** |
| broken, decayed wall tops | absent — every merlon is an identical sharp cube at identical spacing (`H-04` x 225–345 and 730–870; `H-05` x 400–880) |
| timber-and-iron industrial retrofit — scaffolds, wheels, pipes, chimney | absent. `H-05`'s dark crossbars (x 460–720, y 260–460) are the closest thing and they are flat strips with no thickness |
| monumental banners, several, at varied size | present but **postage-stamp sized**. The good banner in `H-05` (x 1140–1180) reads ~1.5 m on a ~12 m wall; the board's are 6–8 m |
| stone arch with keystone over the gate | absent — the gate is a flat rectangular lintel (`H-04` x 520–770; `H-07` x 340–590) |
| cold blue glow inside the gate | present, but as a hard-edged aliased cyan quad rather than a glow (see #9) |
| sharpened palisade | replaced by a clean pale post-and-rail **farm fence** (`H-02` x 250–780; `H-03` both sides) |
| foreground clutter — carts, stumps, an altar, crates | absent (see #7) |

Two additions actively contradict the board: **the jade-green tile roof** (`H-05` x 0–260, y 190–280;
`H-06` x 500–670, y 315–380) is the only strongly saturated hue on the whole building and there is
nothing like it anywhere in the reference palette strip; and **the smooth untextured tan cone turret**
(visible in `H-02`, `H-02b`, `H-03` ×3, `H-04`) is a flat gradient triangle with no shingles and no
ridge. It appears in five frames and it is the worst individual asset in the set.

The wall also **never meets the ground.** `H-05` x 200–800, y 455–490 and x 950–1280, y 500–570: the
grass rises and is simply cut by the wall plane — no plinth, no footing, no rubble skirt, no dirt, no
darkening. `H-02b` x 100–300 at the left tower base: a straight horizontal cut with a thin dark line.
The fortress is pasted onto the terrain rather than founded in it.

### 5. Nothing touches the ground — contact shadows are missing across the whole set.

This is what makes the good scatter in #7 of the "good" list still read as stickers.

- D11 panels 1 and 3: the pale pebbles at (185,40), (240,250), (405,490), (450,470) have **zero**
  darkening at their base.
- `H-05` x 770–990, y 350–580: three trees standing on grass, no contact darkening on any of them.
- `H-02b` x 480–560 and 600–660: dark tree clumps on bright pale ground with no contact at all — they
  float visibly.
- `H-04` x 0–140, y 410–560: a stone block sitting on the parapet with no darkening at the join.
- `H-07`: the grunt's cast shadow (x 640–820, y 620–800) is a soft blob that **starts about 60 px to
  the right of their feet and never touches them.** The character is visibly detached from their own
  shadow.

Palworld's tight dark contact under every object is doing enormous work in those five reference shots,
and its absence here is a large part of why the frames feel lighter and flatter than they should.

### 6. Two hard rendering bugs, both plainly visible.

- **`H-05-east-flank.png`, x 855–915, y 395–540: a tree rendering as near-black.** Sampled RGB at
  (880,430) is (0, 9, 0) and at (820,430) is (2, 22, 3). The tree immediately to its right at x 920–990
  — same asset, same depth, same light — is bright saturated green. Two identical trees side by side,
  one lit and one not. This is a bug, not a style choice.
- **`H-06-west-keep.png`, x 900–1280, y 0–620: about a quarter of the frame is a flat near-black
  slab.** The keep's shadowed face retains essentially no bounce light and no visible texture. Compare
  palworld-04's shadowed cliff faces, which keep colour and detail. Here shadow equals black.
  In the same frame the tower's plinth (x 655–1010, y 570–800) **floats**: at x 660–720, y 545–580 a
  cobble ledge juts into space with no support under its left end, and at x 1000–1080, y 610–680 you
  can see grass rendering behind and under the plinth end with a visible gap.

Also in `H-06`, x 0–400, y 620–715: four pale grey rounded rectangles lying flat on the grass
(x 30–115, 130–215, 300–360, 335–400). They have no thickness and no shadow. Whatever they are meant to
be — rubble, stepping stones — they read as decals or z-fighting planes.

### 7. The causeway is 530 px of empty stone in every frame that shows it.

`H-03` ×3 and `H-04`. From y ≈ 800 up to y ≈ 270 there is unbroken cobble with **nothing on it** — no
rubble, no crates, no barricade, no Tether machinery, no scorch, no wear track. The most important
approach in the chapter is a bare ramp. The board's stronghold foreground is littered — a wheeled
cart, cut stumps, an altar block, scattered rock; palworld-05's base approach likewise. Empty ground
in a game reads as "the level isn't dressed yet".

The causeway also has a razor-straight diagonal terrain-material boundary running from about (620,800)
to (1010,430) in all three ramp-foot frames. In the plain and night versions it shows as a hard-edged
pale wedge; at golden hour the material on the right of it fails to pick up the warm light at all while
everything left of it goes orange. Whatever it is, a straight diagonal edge cutting the foreground of
the hero shot reads as a bug.

And in `H-04` the gatehouse has **no gate** — no portcullis, no doors, no bar. The enemy stronghold's
front entrance is a permanently open hole, and looking through it (x 590–700, y 290–490) you see a wall
about two metres in. There is no courtyard reveal and no light at the end.

### 8. Props and the character disagree about how big a metre is.

`H-07-courtyard.png`. The trainer is 1.80 m; the grunt is close enough to use as the ruler.

The **bench at x 930–1130, y 490–550** is the outlier and it is unmissable. Its seat is at y ≈ 497 and
its feet at y ≈ 550 — 53 px for a seat height. At that depth 0.45 m ≈ 53 px would make 1 m ≈ 118 px.
The grunt, standing *nearer* the camera and therefore needing a larger px-per-metre, measures roughly
470 px head to foot. Even on the most generous reading of the depth difference that puts the grunt at
3 m and more likely near 5 m — or, read the other way, puts the bench's seat somewhere below the
grunt's ankle. Look at it directly in the frame: the bench top (y ≈ 497) against the grunt's knee
(y ≈ 690), with the bench *further away*. Nobody could sit on it.

The floor cobbles disagree with the bench too: at ~55 px across near the grunt's feet they imply the
grunt is 2–3 m depending on whether you call a cobble 0.2 or 0.3 m. The doorway (x 340–590, y 265–490)
is generously sized and fine. So it is specifically the small props and the paving that are off, not
the architecture — but they are off from each other as well as from the character, which is the part
that shows.

Elsewhere, the merlon module changes size between pieces: `H-05` left tower merlons (x 215–345,
y 65–105) are ~30 px while the curtain wall's at similar depth (x 400–880, y 195–215) are ~18 px.

### 9. Team Tether's colour hierarchy is inverted, and the cable has no anchor.

The faction's colour is oxblood. In `H-02b` the most saturated, highest-chroma objects in the entire
1280×800 frame are the **cyan tether pylons** at x 805–830, 930–945 and 1235–1250 — bright cyan-white
crystals against an otherwise desaturated world. They pull the eye harder than the fortress does. In
`H-02` the cyan cables (x 620–1090, y 370–460) read at that distance as **dashed wireframe debug
lines** hanging in mid-air.

In `H-03` ×3 and `H-04` the cyan cable arcs across the top-right of the frame (`H-03` x 850–1280,
y 60–215) with **no visible anchor at either end and no sag variation**. It reads as a stray line
rather than as a cable under tension.

In `H-07` the cyan is a hard-edged aliased emissive quad running along the wall base (x 0–540,
y 400–490) that passes *under* the black doorway sill and keeps going — a clipping artefact — with a
second sliver poking through the wall behind the right banner at x 895–905, y 325–370.

### 10. Flat cutouts standing in for cloth and timber.

- **Banners.** The good asset (`H-07`) is a perfectly rigid rectangle with a **rectangular notch cut
  out of the bottom** — no cloth curve, no ripple, no thickness, no wind, and it casts no shadow of its
  own onto the wall despite standing off it on a rail. It reads as a laminated sign. The bad asset —
  used in `H-02`, `H-02b`, `H-03` ×3, `H-04`, and two of the three visible banners in `H-05` — is a
  **flat untextured red rectangle with no sigil at all**. The faction's identity mark is missing from
  most of the places it appears.
- **Timber.** `H-05` x 460–720, y 260–460 and the vertical posts at x 1075–1090 and 1235–1250: sampled
  RGB (59,53,42) and (55,49,38), and **perfectly uniform along their entire length** with no lit face,
  no cast shadow, and no visible thickness. They read as black tape on the wall. One post in the same
  frame (x 1095–1115) does have a lit and a dark face — so the shading is inconsistent between
  instances of the same thing.
- In `H-04`, brown slabs at x 400–450 and 830–880, y 275–500 **intersect the towers and the banners**.

### 11. Sky and ground disagree about the weather, in daylight.

In `H-01`, `H-02` and `H-02b` there is a desaturated dark slate haze band across the horizon
(`H-02b` y 260–400) while the sky above it is clear blue and the ground is lit by a warm sun. `H-05`
has a muddy blue-grey overcast sky (y 0–200) over a wall carrying a warm sunlit glow (x 780–900,
y 290–380). The frames do not agree with themselves about the time of day, and the haze band is dark
enough that it swallows the fortress in `H-01` and `H-02` rather than separating it.

`H-02b` also has faint vertical dark streaking descending from the haze band at x 200–560 and
x 730–760, y 330–420, which does not reach the ground. If that is rain it is not reading as rain.

### 12. Unmotivated lights, and fire that does not light anything.

- `H-06` x 100–175, y 375–425 and `H-05` x 780–900, y 290–380: warm orange glows on the wall with no
  lamp, brazier or window anywhere near them.
- `H-06` x 300–310, y 385–465: a bright cyan emissive rectangle on the wall with no fixture.
- `H-03-ramp-foot-night`: the four braziers are lit, but the towers immediately behind them (x 465–520
  and x 760–810, y 90–260) are uniformly dark with **no falloff pool at all**. The flames are unlit
  sprites. The keyart's night panel gets a great deal out of a single campfire pooling warm light on
  the ground; here four fires light nothing.
- The moon (`H-03-night`, x 300–440, y 95–235) is a soft blurry ellipse with no disc edge — it reads as
  a lens bloom rather than an object — and there is a second unexplained bright bloom at x 750–870,
  y 0–90.
- `H-03-golden`: at golden hour with a very low sun, the crenellations at x 465–590 and 680–810,
  y 40–110 cast **no shadow down the parapet below them**. The fortress does not appear to be casting
  shadows onto itself at all, which is a large part of why it stays flat in every daylight frame.

## The three things that most separate these frames from the references

**1. The reference makes its fortress the darkest, most complex shape in the frame; these frames make
it the same value as the dirt.** Keyart stronghold panel: fortress mass L=72 against a hill at L=104,
with a broken, ivy-tangled, irregular top edge biting into a bright sky. `H-02b`: fortress L=138
against ground at L=143–163, with a flat regular top edge against a grey haze band. The reference earns
its landmark with value contrast and silhouette complexity, and neither is present here.

**2. The reference's world is full to the horizon; these frames stop having content about 60 m out.**
Palworld-04 puts foliage, rock and prop detail right up to the base of the far cliff and uses haze for
depth. `H-02b` puts a bald near-white surface between the near treeline and the horizon and leaves it
completely empty, then terminates it in a hard rim with evenly spaced identical posts. The keyart's
meadow panels likewise carry scattered trees, hedgerows, fences and buildings all the way back.

**3. The reference's stone is a surface; this stone is a picture of a surface.** Palworld-05 and -04's
masonry has real bevel, real occlusion in the joints, dirt, and no visible repeat. `H-08` is flat
albedo with the highlight and shadow painted on, no normal map, a repeat visible within one frame, and
three UV scales meeting at hard seams. This one gap is what most makes the fortress read as an
asset-pack building rather than the board's stronghold.

## The two bar questions

### A. Do these frames read as belonging to the world in `tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`?

# NO

**The meadow does. The fortress does not, and the fortress is the subject of all eleven frames.**

Carried it: the near-field ground — grass, wildflowers, broadleaf scatter, the oak-ish trees, the warm
light in `H-03-golden` — is genuinely the board's Meadows. The banner sigil, the oxblood discipline and
the grunt's uniform are the board's Team Tether.

Sank it: put the frames beside the board's own TEAM TETHER STRONGHOLD panel and almost nothing carries
over. The board's building is an **ancient grey ruin reclaimed by ivy, with Team Tether's timber-and-iron
industry bolted onto it and huge crimson banners hung down its face**. What is in the frames is a
clean, cream, symmetrical, freshly-built castle with a jade pagoda roof, a smooth cone turret, tiny
banners, no ivy, no ruin, no industry, and a farm fence where the palisade should be. The one place the
board's identity does land — the sigil banner — is used at a fraction of the size the board gives it,
and in most frames is replaced by a blank red rectangle.

### B. Shown beside `palworld-0*.jpg`, would someone say these are trying to be the same kind of game?

# NO

This is closer than A, and I want to be exact about where it turns.

Carried it: `H-03-ramp-foot-golden` or `H-03-ramp-foot-night` shown *alone* beside palworld-02 would
pass. Same third-person framing, same stylised-not-realistic register, same saturated-natural palette,
same cozy-with-menace mood. `H-07`'s character work would pass too.

Sank it, decisively, on two shots:
- **`H-02b` beside palworld-04.** These are the same shot — landmark seen across country — and one has
  a dense, textured, hazy, populated middle distance with a silhouetted tower, and the other has a bald
  putting green with a pale nub on it. Nobody would call those the same production.
- **`H-08` beside palworld-05's stonework.** Baked painted highlights and a visible tile repeat against
  real material response. That is a generation gap, not a style difference.

Whole-frame value structure supports it numerically too: palworld-04 has a luminance sd of 70.7 and
palworld-03 of 53.2; `H-02b` has 46.8, and spends most of what it has separating a near-black
foreground from a pale midground rather than modelling the subject.

## Fixable in the scene vs needs art that is not in the build

The split matters because it decides what becomes a task and what becomes a purchase order.

**Fixable by changing the scene — density, palette, lighting, composition, scatter, scale:**

- Silhouette contrast on the fortress: darken and cool the stone tint, and/or push a lighter warm haze
  behind it so it reads as the dark shape. (#1)
- Raise vegetation cull distance and scatter density across the mid-distance; darken and saturate the
  terrain base albedo so objects stop floating on it. (#2)
- Normalise UV scale across all uses of the stone material; remove the hard seams. (#3, partial)
- Enable/repair contact shadows and AO globally. (#5)
- The near-black tree in `H-05` and the near-black keep face in `H-06` are lighting bugs to chase. (#6)
- The floating plinth and the flat grey slabs in `H-06`. (#6)
- Dress the causeway with props that already exist elsewhere in the build. (#7)
- Rescale the bench and the small courtyard props; rescale the banners up 3–4×. (#8, #10)
- Anchor the cyan cables to visible pylons, dial the cyan chroma down below the oxblood, fix the
  emissive quad clipping in `H-07`. (#9)
- Reconcile the sky/haze band with the sun direction; enable self-shadowing on the fortress. (#11, #12)
- Give the braziers a real light with falloff; replace the moon bloom with a disc. (#12)
- Replace the blank red rectangle banner with the good sigil asset everywhere. (#10)
- Add a footing/plinth and a rubble-and-dirt skirt where wall meets terrain — this can be scatter and
  terrain painting, not new meshes. (#4)

**Needs art that is not in the build:**

- **A real weathered stone material** — normal map, per-stone colour variation, moss and lichen in the
  joints, no baked highlight. This is the single highest-value purchase and it fixes #3 outright and
  most of #4's "reads as a kit" problem.
- **Ivy, moss and overgrowth** — decals and geometry to cascade down the walls. Without this the
  fortress cannot read as the board's reclaimed ruin, no matter how it is lit.
- **Team Tether retrofit props** — timber scaffolds, iron machinery, pipes, wheels, a chimney. The flat
  dark strips in `H-05` are standing in for this and they cannot carry it.
- **A broken/decayed wall-top set** to replace the identical cube merlons.
- **An arched gate with a keystone, and a portcullis or door.**
- **A roof asset** to replace both the smooth cone turret and the jade tile roof.
- **A banner with cloth shape** — sculpted folds and a curved hem at minimum; the current rectangle
  with a notch cut in it cannot be dressed into looking like cloth.
- **A courtyard dressing set** — crates, barrels, racks, cages, a forge. The courtyard is currently a
  paved yard with one bench.

### Where I was wrong, and I am correcting it

**The crenellations are not identical cubes.** I wrote that in §4 and it is wrong. The lane's D5 fix
landed. Magnified 4–6×, the merlon row in `H-05` (x 350–930, y 180–240) varies in width (I measure
merlons from ~60 to ~95 px at constant depth), varies in height, varies in pitch (gaps of ~55, ~110 and
~100 px between successive merlons), and has a genuine missing merlon in the run before the right
tower. `H-04`'s near merlons vary visibly even without magnification.

The accurate criticism is narrower and I would still make it: **the variation is real but it is tuned
below the threshold at which it reads at frame scale.** At 1280×800, and certainly at the distances of
`H-01`, `H-02` and `H-02b`, the row still scans as regular. The board's ruin gets its irregularity from
*collapse* — whole sections gone, stubs at wildly different heights — not from ±20% jitter on an intact
row. This is a tuning note now, not a "you didn't do it".

**There is some overgrowth.** I wrote "absent everywhere". There is a narrow fringe of green moss cards
along the parapet tops, visible in `H-04` at x 400–500 and x 1030–1130, y 45–60. It is real. But it is
a thin edge trim on horizontal surfaces only, and the board's language — ivy cascading down the
vertical wall faces, grass in the ledges, the building visibly being eaten — is still absent. Restated:
overgrowth exists as parapet trim; it does not exist as the reclamation the board is built on.

### Where I agree with the lane, independently

- **D8/D5 close-range wall material** — declared not fixed, routed to a texture lane. This is my
  defect #3, found blind, and I rank it third.
- **D9 far terrain bald, D10 scatter on a grid** — declared not fixed, routed to `T1-GROUND-3`. This is
  my defect #2, and I rank it second.
- **D12 night moon and brazier light pools** — declared not fixed, routed to `T1-LIGHT`. This is my

I found all three without knowing they were open. They are the right three to have deferred in the

said out loud.

### Where I disagree with the lane

**1. D3 "no landmark silhouette" is recorded as fixed. It is not fixed, and I think the fix was aimed
at the wrong axis.**

The lane treated D3 as a *massing* problem and solved it as one: heights laddered from 10.8 to 37.7,
the spire relocated to the north-west corner so the approach can see it, with a stated deviation past
`HALL_DESIGN` §3's height cap to get there. The massing genuinely is better — `H-02b` shows a stepped
profile rather than one roofline.

But the defect is a **value contrast** problem, and raising a pale object standing on a pale hill does
not create a silhouette. The measurement in my §1 is the whole argument: fortress mass L=138, ground
beside it L=143–163. The board's stronghold reads at L=72 against L=104. Until the fortress is the dark
shape in its own frame, another 10 m of height buys very little.

**2. And the spire — the thing D3's fix was built around — is the worst asset in the set.**

I did not know it was the spire when I wrote §4. Zoomed 8× (`H-02b` x 640–760, y 325–420), the tallest
mass in the Hall is a slender tower capped by a **smooth, untextured, flat-gradient tan cone** that
flares wider than the tower beneath it. It is the shape that now dominates the fortress's skyline read
from every approach frame. It is also, at RGB (197,170,134) against the grey haze, the single
highest-contrast thing the fortress has — so the one element that does separate from the background is
a party hat.

There is a real irony here worth naming: the lane spent its top-ranked effort making sure the player can
see this object from the approach, and succeeded, and the object is a flat-shaded cone. **Height was
delivered; a landmark was not.** The cheapest large win available on this whole list is probably to
give that cap a real roof material and a broken or complex crown, and to drop the fortress's stone
value.

**3. D6 "banners are flat sigil-less quads" is recorded as fixed. It landed in the courtyard and
almost nowhere else.**

The generated compass sigil is present and correct in `H-07` — I praised it blind. But of the banners
visible elsewhere: `H-02`, `H-02b`, `H-03` ×3 and `H-04` show flat red rectangles with **no sigil at
all**, and two of the three visible banners in `H-05` do too. Only `H-05`'s x 1140–1180 carries it.

And there is a distance failure I can now name: in `H-02b` at x 675–690, y 371–395 the keep wall
carries a patch of **pink diagonal hatching** — I logged it blind as "a decal that failed to resolve".
Magnified, it is a banner's sigil degrading into diagonal stripe noise at range. So the device does not
merely fail to appear at distance; where it does appear it appears as an artefact.

On the hue half of D6: the magenta is gone, which was the point — sampled banner cloth is now
(175,88,72) in `H-05` and (173,82,67) in `H-07`, with R>G>B as intended. But that is a mid brick /
terracotta, noticeably lighter and warmer than the faction's `#7a2430` oxblood. The correction appears
to have overshot past the target rather than landed on it.

**4. D7 "facade ending in mid-air" is recorded as fixed with foot shafts. `H-06` still floats.**

At `H-06` x 660–720, y 545–580 a cobble ledge juts into space with nothing under its left end, and at
x 1000–1080, y 610–680 grass renders behind and under the plinth end with a visible gap. Whether this
is a module the shaft pass missed or a new float introduced by it, the west keep elevation — the exact
stand D7 was raised against — still shows the defect.

**5. D2 "the courtyard is an empty box" — the props went in and the room still reads empty.**

The handover says 18 props were added hugging the walls, with the fight floor deliberately clear. That
is a defensible design constraint and I would not overrule it. But in the frame the lane shipped, I
count roughly five objects: a bench, a crate, a sack, a basket, a ground ring. The read is still an
empty paved yard. And one of the props introduced a new defect that is louder than the emptiness it was
meant to cure — **the bench is at roughly a fifth of the scale it should be** (my §8), which is the
kind of error the owner spots instantly.

If the fight floor must stay clear, the dressing has to go *up* the walls — racks, hanging gear,
shuttered openings, banners at real scale — rather than sitting in a low band the camera barely sees.

### One thing the lane got right that I want to reinforce

Reverting `officer_b` back to the oxblood grunt was correct. I praised that character blind, without
knowing it had been swapped and swapped back, and it is the strongest piece of character art in the
set. The handover's open question — whether to revert `stronghold_outer_watch` and
`stronghold_checkpoint` too — has an easy answer from where I sit: **yes.** If the grunt reads this
well and the officer read as "from another game", cohesion is worth more than per-individual silhouette
variety, and variety can come from the badge/palette layer as the handover itself notes it already does.

### The headline that should not be carried forward

**"The rebuild left the Hall itself almost untouched" is no longer true and repeating it would send the
next lane in the wrong direction.** The Hall has been substantially rebuilt. My answer is still No, but
for a different reason: the fortress is now *built* and is not *the board's fortress* — it is a clean
castle kit where the reference is an overgrown, retrofitted ruin — and it is clad in a material and set
on a ground that are both a tier below it. Told "it's still a greybox", a lane would add geometry. What
this needs is material, overgrowth, retrofit props, and a value drop.
