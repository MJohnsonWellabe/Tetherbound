# Village Inn R11 — independent visual verdict

## Verdict: POLISH — retain, not a strict named-location PASS

R11 preserves the accepted Inn identity, built approach, central room hierarchy,
and day/night usability from R10. It does not satisfy either explicit R10 promotion
condition: the service counter remains box-panel construction, and the guest bed is
still openly exposed to the common room.

### Evidence integrity: PASS

- `manifest.json` is complete with 12/12 unique records and zero failures.
- All 12 distinct native PNGs are present at the declared 1280×800 resolution;
  each camera has a genuine day/night pair.
- The receipt discloses the production Meadows scene, authored village NPCs and
  clock, fixed camera, hidden HUD/overlay, and the hidden grounded player used for
  production NPC facing. It discloses no art, pose, weather, progress, encounter,
  or lighting injection.

### Strict findings

- **Exterior identity / built approach: PASS.** The tall half-timbered mass,
  masonry base, signed porch, lit windows, bench, barrel, vine, and attached wing
  read immediately as a public Inn from all three exterior views. Night retains a
  clear door and porch destination, although the upper timbers become very dark.
- **Materials and day/night usability: PASS.** Plaster variation, wood grain,
  masonry, roof tiles, and interior fittings retain sufficient surface separation.
  Exterior and interior routes remain readable at night; the interior is warmly,
  evenly usable without losing its hierarchy.
- **Bar/service focal point: POLISH blocker.** Bram, bottle shelves, mugs, books,
  stool, barrel, and sign establish service purpose, but `04` still centers a broad
  rectangular counter/island with a straight slab top and repeated blank front
  panels. Its silhouette and joinery read as a texture-treated box or desk, not a
  convincingly built working bar. The added foreground mass also competes with the
  otherwise stronger counter behind Bram.
- **Room hierarchy and lived service: PASS.** The runner, opposed long tables,
  unequal table contents, tools, crockery, baskets, and Bram sightline give the
  common room a navigable public aisle and recognizable service use.
- **Guest-bed privacy: POLISH blocker.** In both `05` and `06`, day and night, the
  purple mattress and bed frame are plainly visible beside and above the open timber
  screen. The screen marks a zone but does not form a private lodging boundary; the
  bed still reads as furniture placed directly in the taproom.

Retain R11, but do not promote the Inn ledger row to PASS. Fresh evidence must show
a materially constructed, non-box bar silhouette and a guest bed genuinely screened
from ordinary common-room views while preserving the current route and lighting.
