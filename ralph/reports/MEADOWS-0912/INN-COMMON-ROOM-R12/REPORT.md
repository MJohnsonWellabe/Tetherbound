R12 preserves the accepted exterior identity, material treatment, room
hierarchy, lived service, and day/night usability while resolving both R11
promotion blockers. Promote the Village Inn named-location ledger row to
**PASS**.

## Strict findings

- **Exterior identity and arrival: PASS.** The tall half-timbered mass, masonry
  base, signed porch, lit windows, bench, barrel, vine, and attached wing read
  immediately as the public Village Inn. Front, corner, and doorway views each
  retain a clear built approach at both clocks.
- **Material depth and night usability: PASS.** Plaster variation, wood grain,
  roof tiles, stone courses, shutters, fittings, and ground treatment remain
  separated in daylight. Night keeps the sign, porch, door, windows, masonry,
  and interior route legible without flattening the whole building into one
  exposure value.
- **Bar/service focal point: PASS.** R11's broad straight slab and repeated
  blank box panels are gone. The customer face now uses unequal timber bays,
  open lower rails, posts, diagonal braces, and varied plank courses under a
  continuous working top. Bram, bottle shelves, mugs, books, stool, cask, tap,
  and the `ROOMS - ALE - STOCK` sign complete a convincing service hierarchy.
- **Room hierarchy and lived use: PASS.** Opposed long tables, unequal table
  contents, tools, crockery, baskets, rugs, windows, and the central aisle give
  the room distinct service, dining, circulation, and lodging zones. The new
  screen does not block the ordinary path to Bram or compress the aisle.
- **Guest-bed privacy: PASS.** In both `05` and `06`, day and night, the former
  exposed purple mattress and bed frame are fully hidden behind a floor-to-head
  timber-and-cloth partition. The framed opaque panels read as a deliberate
  lodging boundary rather than the old token open screen, while leaving the
  public side visibly furnished and navigable.
