# The Old Quarry R20 — independent named-location verdict

## Verdict: FAIL — reject R20 evidence; do not promote

I reviewed the R20 `manifest.json`, both surviving 1280×800 production frames, the
complete R19 evidence/report, and the Meadows key art and five Palworld reference
frames. I did not inspect implementation or run Godot.

R20 cannot clear a named-location visual gate. Its own manifest says `complete: false`,
lists six refused captures, and contains only `01-arrival-day` and
`01-arrival-night`. The missing matched views are exactly the evidence needed to judge
the worked floor, conduit head, final cut, repaired slab, and whether the night floor
still crushes. Each was refused because all sampled R20 extraction faces, tool courses,
benches, and floor-to-wagon apron were blocked by the quarry cut-face collisions. This
is failed production evidence, not a complete visual round.

The surviving arrival pair does show the quarry destination rather than hiding it
behind the rejected central-tree camera, so arrival occlusion is not the blocker in
these two frames. It does not establish the claimed terrace improvement: at small size
the pale central mass still reads as one smooth, rounded, modular mound with only a few
shallow top steps. The right-edge trunks remain visually heavy, and at night the lamp
isolates a doorway-like face while the worksite floor and surrounding forms fall into
near-black.

## Three strongest gaps from the references

1. **Complete authored destination evidence — missing `02-*`, `03-*`, and `04-*`.**
   The key art and Palworld frames present the route, activity surface, and defining
   landmark together. R20 supplies no admissible interior or final-cut view, so it
   cannot demonstrate that relationship at all.
2. **Excavation silhouette and material language — `01-arrival-*`.** The references
   build landmarks from layered silhouettes, surface breaks, and terrain integration.
   R20's visible quarry remains a uniformly pale, rounded module with token cap steps,
   not a convincing sequence of worked extraction terraces.
3. **Night value hierarchy — `01-arrival-night`.** The references retain traversable
   terrain planes and landmark depth after dark. Here most of the floor, worker, wagon,
   rocks, and trees collapse together while one small lamp makes the cut resemble a
   dark doorway.

## Binding bar questions

**A. Does this belong to the world in the Meadows key art? No.** The oak grove, grass,
wildflowers, and sky share the broad palette, but the named quarry lacks the key art's
layered landform hierarchy and readable terrain integration. The incomplete evidence
also prevents a whole-location judgment.

**B. Beside the Palworld references, would someone say this is trying to be the same
kind of game? No.** The colorful third-person setting points in that direction, but a
smooth blockout-like destination, crushed night values, and six missing production
views do not meet the same shipping-quality presentation bar.

The remaining gaps are scene- and capture-fixable: make the required cameras valid
against production collisions, expose repeated benches and cut planes in the actual
composition, break up the quarry material/form, connect cut to worked floor, and
preserve major planes at night. This evidence does not establish a need for a new asset
family.

R20 is **FAIL**. R19 remains the latest complete **POLISH** round. The authoritative
named-location ledger and aggregate count are unchanged.
