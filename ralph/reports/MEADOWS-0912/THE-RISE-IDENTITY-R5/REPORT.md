# The Rise identity R5 — independent named-location verdict

## Verdict

**POLISH — retain R5, but do not promote The Rise to strict named-location
PASS.** The matched production frames are valid and the wind-shaped crown is a
recognizable local landmark. R5 also resolves R4's reversed/unstable day-night
exposure. The submitted pixels still do not show one unmistakable, walkable climb
from the maintained road through the contour shelf to the crown, and they do not
show the promised overlook payoff once the player arrives.

## Evidence integrity

- `manifest.json` reports `complete: true` with no failures, and all eight listed
  1280x720 PNGs are present as four matched day/night pairs.
- The disclosure identifies the production Meadows scene, ordinary trainer, live
  Terrain3D, authoritative scatter, props, encounters and both authored roads. It
  states that locomotion and clocks alone are frozen and that no scene content,
  light, material, pose or progression is injected.
- Frame roles, player positions, target positions and measured hero/fork distances
  are populated. The images contain no obvious camera clipping or invalid evidence.

## Visible findings

- `01-road-climb-approach`: the brown maintained road, fence and crown tree establish
  a credible approach. The road ends visually at the foot of a broad mottled slope,
  however; no tread chain carries the eye from the road to the crown. The tree is
  identifiable at 63.71 m, but it reads as a tree on a hill rather than proof of a
  built destination.
- `02-road-end-trailhead`: this is the clearest ordinary-player road view. Pale stone
  patches mark the road edge and one warm waylight survives at night, but the route
  continues straight past the hill while the intended climb is not visually
  announced. The steep right-hand face has isolated rocks and grass rather than a
  legible branch, stair or contour path.
- `03-west-foot-climb`: the hero tree remains visible above the shelf, but the frame
  is dominated by a steep diagonal grass/rock face. Only a small tread fragment is
  visible at the bottom edge; it does not connect to another tread or demonstrate a
  traversable ascent. At small size this reads as hillside and trees, not The Rise.
- `04-crown-arrival`: the wind-shaped crown has a clean silhouette in both lighting
  states, but a large bare foreground mound hides the approach and consumes most of
  the frame. The camera faces uphill, so neither an overlook vista nor the location's
  optional discovery/trainer/ecology payoff is visible.
- Day/night continuity is materially better than R4: every day frame is now clearly
  daylight and each night mate remains judgeable. Night terrain is cool and dark but
  no longer reverses the pair's value hierarchy. This closes the exposure blocker,
  not the route/destination blocker.

## Strict promotion boundary

PASS requires the actual pixels, without manifest labels, to connect the road end,
fork, repeated treads/waylights and crown as one plainly walkable authored approach.
At arrival it also needs a readable reason this is *The Rise*—at minimum the first
overlook payoff or its local discovery/encounter context—rather than only a hero tree
above a bare slope. R5 remains a valid **POLISH** destination because its approach and
crown identity exist, but the route is visually discontinuous in all four views.

## Reference-bar check

The three largest gaps from the supplied references are:

1. **Route legibility (`02`, `03`):** the references use continuous paths, stairs,
   walls or clustered props to pull the eye to a destination; R5 offers isolated
   edge stones and a nearly unmarked steep face.
2. **Destination payoff (`04`):** the key art and Palworld plateau view open onto
   layered country and recognizable activity; R5 ends on a blank mound below one
   tree and never presents the overlook.
3. **Authored density (`01`, `04`):** reference landmarks combine terrain,
   vegetation and built/local story elements into a hierarchy; broad undifferentiated
   terrain still occupies the centre of these frames.

**A. Key-art world fit: no.** The palette and pastoral tree/road vocabulary belong
to Meadows, but the route hierarchy, layered overlook and inviting destination
density do not reach the project's board.

**B. Palworld-kind-of-game fit: no.** The broad stylized landscape is directionally
related, but the marked destination lacks the visibly authored approach, encounter
presence and arrival payoff of the comparison frames. These blockers are scene and
capture-composition work; the evidence does not establish a need for new art.
