# The Rise identity R6 — independent named-location verdict

## Verdict

**POLISH — retain R6, but do not promote The Rise to strict named-location
PASS.** The new crown-facing frame closes R5's missing arrival payoff: the summit now
opens onto a strong, readable village-country overlook in both clocks. The submitted
ordinary-route pixels still do not connect the maintained road to the claimed
switchback, or show the full switchback as an unmistakably broad, walkable ascent to
the crown. R6 therefore resolves one of R5's three blockers, not the location gate.

## Evidence integrity

- `manifest.json` reports `complete: true`, no failures, and four matched 1280x720
  day/night pairs; all eight listed PNGs are present.
- The disclosure identifies the production Meadows scene with the ordinary trainer,
  live Terrain3D, authoritative scatter, props, encounters and both authored roads.
  It says only locomotion, clocks, HUD and the independent submersion overlay are
  controlled, with no scene content, lighting, material, pose or progression injected.
- Frame roles, player positions, target positions and measured fork/hero distances are
  populated. The evidence is valid for visual review.

## R5 blocker disposition

- **Road to fork: still open.** In `01-road-climb-approach`, the maintained road leads
  toward the base of the hill, but no distinct branch, repeated treads or waylight chain
  carries the eye onto the slope. In `02-road-end-trailhead`, the broad road continues
  straight into the trees while the right-hand hill reads as an unmarked steep bank.
  Pale stones embedded in the road do not establish a turn or trailhead.
- **Walkable climb: still open.** `03-full-switchback-climb` shows a narrow diagonal
  shelf and a few separated dark/stone fragments across a steep mottled face. It does
  not show a continuous lower turn and return, enough tread width, or a connected path
  reaching the crown. At small size the claimed eighteen-piece route reads as scattered
  terrain breakup, not a safe authored switchback. The night mate loses even more of
  that shelf into the hillside; the tiny practical does not preserve a route chain.
- **Summit overlook: closed.** `04-crown-overlook` is a material improvement and a real
  destination payoff. The foreground ledge opens onto the fenced village, buildings,
  road, creatures and layered country, with the objective beam giving a strong vertical
  landmark. The night mate remains readable and keeps both settlement and route visible.

## Visible findings

- `01` establishes the crown tree clearly, but the large uninterrupted slope between
  road and tree still makes the landmark look inaccessible from the ordinary approach.
- `02` is a credible road view, yet its strongest directional cue is forward along the
  existing road. Nothing comparably strong announces that the player should turn toward
  the Rise.
- `03` is dominated by steep terrain and sky. The hero tree survives in both clocks,
  but the purported route has insufficient continuity and scale cues to read as a
  traversable climb rather than a narrow erosion shelf.
- `04` supplies the missing authored payoff and is the strongest frame in the set. The
  village, fence circuit, creatures and distant terrain produce foreground/mid-ground/
  distance layering absent from R5's uphill-facing arrival.
- Day/night matching is stable. Night is judgeable overall, but `03`'s already weak
  route hierarchy collapses before the crown silhouette does.

## Strict promotion boundary

PASS still requires ordinary pixels that visibly hand the player from the maintained
road into a distinct fork/trailhead, then show repeated broad treads or another
continuous route language through both switchback legs to the crown. This is a
scene/composition problem, not evidence for new art. Preserve R6's outward-facing
summit view; it successfully closes the arrival-payoff requirement.

## Reference-bar check

The three largest remaining gaps from the supplied references are:

1. **Route hierarchy (`01`, `02`):** the key art and Palworld field/path references use
   continuous roads, edge structure and clear destination pulls; these frames visually
   send the player straight past the hill.
2. **Traversable landform (`03`):** the references separate walkable ground from cliff
   faces through width, contour and value. Here the route merges into one steep,
   low-contrast slope, especially at night.
3. **Authored approach density (`01`, `03`):** the overlook is populated and layered,
   but the climb itself lacks clustered route furniture or repeated tread rhythm that
   would make the ascent feel deliberately built rather than procedurally rocky.

**A. Key-art world fit: no.** The crown silhouette, pastoral palette and especially
`04`'s village overlook belong to the Meadows board, but the location's approach lacks
the board's clear path hierarchy and inviting traversable terrain.

**B. Palworld-kind-of-game fit: no.** The broad landscape and populated overlook point
in the same genre direction, but the main traversal sequence is not as legible or
authored as the comparison frames. The remaining gaps are fixable through route
geometry, placement and framing with assets already in the build.

## Review-only scope

This review adds only `REPORT.md`. The ledger remains unchanged because strict PASS was
not earned. No gameplay, capture, test, source-art or manifest file was edited, and no
Godot process was launched.
