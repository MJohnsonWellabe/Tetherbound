# The Rise identity R7 — independent named-location verdict

## Verdict: FAIL — reject R7 evidence; retain R6 POLISH

R7 does not earn strict named-location PASS. The eight production frames are present,
but the manifest reports `complete: false` and records the real player blocked before
waypoint 2 after reaching only two waypoints. Its disclosure says the player traverses
the entire switchback, while the embedded receipt proves the opposite. Safe continuous
collision traversal is therefore disproved by this submission, not established.

The pixels also leave R6's two route blockers open. The crown overlook remains a strong
arrival payoff, but the maintained road still does not visibly hand the player to a
fork, and the broadened hillside treatment does not read as one continuous, walkable
lower turn and return to the crown.

## Frame findings

- **`01-road-climb-approach-*`:** The fenced brown road is the strongest directional
  line and carries the eye to the hill's foot, then stops. No contrasting branch,
  repeated edge treatment, or visible tread chain turns toward the crown tree. At
  night the road-to-slope handoff disappears further into the dark foreground.
- **`02-road-end-trailhead-*`:** Pale paving clusters strengthen the maintained road
  but reinforce travel straight ahead. The right-hand Rise is still a steep bank with
  no readable trail mouth; the small warm light lies forward on the main road rather
  than marking a turn. The trainer's cropped head supplies scale but no route cue.
- **`03-full-switchback-climb-*`:** R7 adds broader, repeated dark ledges, but from the
  ordinary camera they read as overlapping rock shingles/strata on a steep face. They
  do not resolve into a continuous lower tread, a generous turning platform, and a
  connected return leg. No player-scale figure stands on the alleged path. The route
  hierarchy is weaker at night, where the ledges merge with the slope.
- **`04-crown-overlook-*`:** The village, fenced road, creatures, objective beam, and
  layered country still provide the set's only convincing destination payoff in both
  clocks. The huge pale canopy cards at frame right are a new dominant intrusion,
  flattening depth and competing with the village; this does not erase the payoff but
  is a visible regression from R6's cleaner edge.

No new interface defect is exposed because the HUD is hidden. Day/night matching is
stable, and no obvious seam or z-fighting appears. Scale cannot be validated on the
climb itself because `03` lacks a readable trainer or creature ruler; the distant
population in `04` supports world scale but not tread width.

## Three strongest gaps from the references

1. **Continuous route hierarchy (`01`, `02`, `03`).** The key art and Palworld path
   frames use an unbroken path silhouette, clear edges, and destination pull. R7 asks
   the eye to infer a turn from a road that visibly continues straight, then infer a
   switchback from disconnected hillside ledges.
2. **Walkable landform and scale (`03`).** The references distinguish travel surface
   from cliff through width, contour, value, and human/creature scale. R7's dark slabs
   merge into one steep face and provide no convincing safe tread or turning bench.
3. **Authored, inhabited ascent (`01`–`03`).** The reference scenes pair routes with
   clustered vegetation, people/creatures, landmarks, and purposeful roadside detail.
   This ascent is empty and materially coarse; only the crown view (`04`) supplies
   population and layered composition.

## Binding bar questions

**A. Does this read as belonging to the Meadows key-art world? No.** The pastoral
palette, crown tree, and especially the village overlook point toward that world, but
the defining approach lacks the key art's inviting, legible path hierarchy and layered
traversable landform.

**B. Beside the Palworld references, would someone say this is trying to be the same
kind of game? No.** The colorful third-person landscape shares the broad genre, but the
empty approach, ambiguous cliff-like route, coarse repeated surface forms, and absent
character/creature focus fall short of the shipping-quality comparison.

The blockers are scene- and evidence-fixable with assets already present: establish a
visually dominant fork off the maintained road, make both switchback legs and the turn
continuous and player-scaled, preserve their value chain at night, clear the crown-view
canopy intrusion, and recapture only after the production player completes the full
collision route. This round does not establish a need for new art.

The authoritative named-location ledger remains unchanged. R6 remains the latest
complete **POLISH** evidence and its crown payoff should be preserved.
