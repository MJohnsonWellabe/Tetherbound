# First Ironwood — independent production visual judgment

**Verdict: PARTIAL / HOLD. The owner's washed-out-white approach complaint is
visually closed and the approved scale is retained, but this set does not close the
full Tier 2 #8 visual row. Night fissure dominance and the detached workyard remain
visible defects. The story reason also still requires its separate runtime receipt.**

The manifest is complete (`complete: true`, 8/8 expected PNGs, no failures). All
eight 1280 × 800 production frames were inspected at original resolution. The
capture is sufficient to judge the current state; its framing does not, however,
prove a successful tree-to-workyard composition.

| Criterion | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Approach colour and texture | **PASS** | `01-long-road-world-tree-day`, `02-southwest-world-tree-day`, and `03-root-district-day` retain brown trunk planes, green canopy variation, darker branch separation, and visible atlas/mottling detail. The hero remains deliberately paler than ordinary trees, but it no longer reads as an untextured white mass. |
| Colossal scale | **PASS** | Both long views give ordinary mature trees and the player as rulers; the Ironwood remains a city-scale canopy and trunk without relying on a distorted close camera. `03-root-district-day` preserves the scale at a normal route distance. |
| Night material hierarchy | **HOLD** | In `02-southwest-world-tree-night` and especially `03-root-district-night`, near-black bark loses most of its brown/material response while cyan-white fissures become the principal internal read. The crown silhouette survives, so this is not a visibility failure, but the trapped source overwhelms the tree material instead of illuminating it. This is the previously retained “night exposure / trunk-heavy lightning network” concern, not merely theoretical polish. |
| Crown lightning distribution | **PARTIAL** | Cyan reaches some upper branches and scattered crown edges, but the brightest continuous network remains concentrated in the central trunk and main crotches. Day frames are restrained; night frames still pull the eye to a vertical glowing core before the canopy. |
| Workyard relationship | **HOLD** | `04-ironwood-workyard-day/night` show a readable human-scale fence, rack/platform, lamps, and stock, but no part of the colossal Ironwood is visible and no strong route, root, material, or processing chain connects the yard to it. The frame reads as a generic isolated timber pen. Keeping the hero physically clear of gameplay is correct; making the yard visually unrelated is the older “detached workyard” defect, not a pass. |
| Workyard night readability | **PARTIAL** | The player and main platform remain readable in `04-ironwood-workyard-night`, but pale flat timber and two point-like lamps carry most of the structure while smaller process props disappear into the grass. The frame proves basic visibility, not a compelling Ironwood craft identity. |
| Story reason | **NOT PROVEN BY PIXELS** | These frames cannot prove the production Juno → Halder dialogue/objective explanation. `tests/smoke_ironwood_story_0912.gd` is the appropriate runtime receipt and remains separate from this visual judgment. |

## Required follow-up evidence

After reducing the cyan/white dominance enough that bark and canopy material remain
present at night, and after visually tying the workyard to the First Ironwood while
preserving its safe physical separation, recapture is required. Preserve the long
day/night approach pair and root-district pair. Replace or supplement the isolated
workyard close-up with a mid-distance day/night composition that includes both the
workyard process and a recognizable portion of the colossal trunk/root or its
continuous arrival route in the same frame. That combined view is the minimum proof
that the yard is integrated rather than merely nearby.

No source conclusion should be inferred from this HOLD: the approach colour fix and
scale are independently accepted and should be preserved through any follow-up.
