## Three strongest gaps from the references

1. **Far-bank landform language — `03-overlook-west-*` and
   `07-bank-wander-*`.** The Meadows key art layers rolling banks, vegetation,
   rocks, and broad height changes around water. R4's opposite shore still presents
   a long level rim over a sheer face, so the reach reads partly as an engineered
   canal rather than a naturally seated river.
2. **Bank surface hierarchy — `01-*`, `03-*`, and `07-*`.** The reference
   landscapes break large slopes with shelves, exposed material, roots, rocks,
   shore plants, and softer transitions. Here one repeated terrain surface covers
   most of the tall face; isolated rocks and crest plants do not explain the cut or
   integrate it with the waterline.
3. **Night landform separation — `02`, `04`, and `08`.** The new palette restores
   usable near-ground visibility, but the opposite wall and tree line still merge
   into a long dark band. Reference night scenes retain more internal value and
   silhouette breakup in their major destination forms.

## Binding bar questions

**A. Does this belong to the world in the Meadows key art? No.** The bright pastoral
palette, wooded path, flowers, and broad water corridor are directionally aligned,
and R4's arrival now has a useful traversal composition. The dominant opposite
shore still lacks the key art's layered, naturally eroded landscape language.

**B. Beside the Palworld references, would someone say this is trying to be the
same kind of game? No.** The colorful third-person exploration frame and distant
landmark are compatible, but the canal-like bank, repeated terrain material, and
limited night breakup keep the named location below the references' authored,
shipping-quality destination read.

The Meadows key art and Palworld comparisons are the binding bars. BOTW's stylized
water-and-bank integration is supplementary only. A passing revision needs a visibly
uneven crest and two or three broad, separated erosion intervals that reach through
the face to the waterline—not additional narrow grooves—plus material/rock/plant
transitions that make those intervals read as natural shoreline construction in
both clocks.

R4 remains **POLISH**. The authoritative named-location ledger and its aggregate
count are unchanged.
