# Old Mill Crossing `final-old-mill-04` independent review

**Verdict: FAIL**

## Evidence integrity

`manifest.json` is complete: eight distinct 1280x720 frames, four matched day/night
pairs, production Meadows, ordinary player and live ecology, with no recorded capture
failures. The disclosed wildlife reset/freeze keeps authored bodies at authored homes
and does not invalidate this location review. No gameplay or capture source was
inspected.

## Strict named-location judgment

| Criterion | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Built approach and route | **PASS** | `01` and `04` retain a clear dirt-road arrival, crossing axis, bridge and mill silhouette. The location is navigable and identifiable from ordinary travel views. |
| Mill/crossing identity | **POLISH** | The tall mill and bridge remain unmistakable, but the working wheel is substantially hidden behind the building and flume in `02` and `03`; the arrival views do not establish how the mill works. |
| R3 bright-blue trough blocker | **POLISH** | R4's water is less saturated and less opaque than R3's trough. It no longer dominates the daylight frame as a debug-blue strip, but it remains a narrow, abrupt slab with hard rectangular edges and is still conspicuously bright at night. |
| Source -> wheel -> tailrace continuity | **FAIL** | No frame shows an unbroken, physically legible water path contacting the wheel and discharging below it. In `03`, the visible channel rides above a broad dark slab; the wheel is mostly occluded at its side, and the water below reads as a separate river surface rather than a tailrace. |
| Structural credibility | **FAIL** | The R3 void has been filled, but the replacement is a very large smooth gray rectangular mass with sharp box corners and little material breakup. The broad dark headrace underside still projects beyond it without visible supports. In `03-day/night` and `04-day/night`, these shapes read as unfinished blockout geometry rather than masonry foundation, buttresses and supported millrace construction. |
| Day/night hierarchy | **POLISH** | The mill silhouette, windows and route remain readable at night, but `03-night` spotlights the gray blockout mass and black slab while the wheel/water mechanism remains obscure. `01-night` also keeps much of the travel foreground very dark. |

## Conclusion

R4 partially addresses one R3 symptom by reducing the trough's blue saturation and
placing mass beneath the suspended mill. It does not resolve the underlying production
defect: the new mass reads as an untextured rectangular placeholder, the headrace still
looks unsupported, and the evidence still cannot prove a believable
intake-to-wheel-to-tailrace mechanism. Old Mill Crossing remains below the named-location
PASS bar. The Meadows ledger and counts are therefore unchanged.

The next repair should replace the rectangular fill and slab with stepped, textured
masonry and visibly load-bearing timber or stone supports; expose enough of the wheel
for its water contact to read; and stage one ordinary three-quarter view in which the
intake, moving channel, wheel contact and downstream discharge are visible as a single
continuous system.
