# Old Mill Crossing `final-old-mill-09` independent review

## Verdict: FAIL

### Evidence integrity

I inspected `manifest.json` and all eight 1280x800 PNGs at native resolution,
alongside the binding Meadows key art and five Palworld reference frames. The four
required day/night pairs are present. The manifest reports `complete: true`, no
automatic failures, and the expected production-scene disclosure. The named-node
projection receipts and low reported wheel/foundation overlap (`0.081` day, `0.065`
night in `03`) establish that geometry was projected into the camera; they do not
establish that the hydraulic process is visually understandable.

R9 visibly removes R8's giant boulder cluster and pale hanging channel shell. That is
a real cleanup. It exposes, however, an assembly whose remaining silhouette and
material handoffs still do not read as a believable working watermill.

### The three largest gaps from the references

1. **Immediate process silhouette — `03-day/night`.** The reference frames preserve
   their central action or landmark at small size through separated silhouettes and
   strong value grouping. Here, an edge-on wheel, dangling trestle parts and a huge
   dark trough merge into one ambiguous shape; the water path cannot be traced through
   it.
2. **Grounded authored construction — `03-day`, `04-day/night`.** The key art seats
   buildings, paths, banks, rocks and water into continuous terrain compositions, and
   the Palworld base frame makes constructed pieces read as an assembled place. R9's
   cantilevered mill edge, plain gray pier, isolated rectangular blocks and unresolved
   support feet still read as placed blockout parts rather than a battered mill built
   into the crossing.
3. **Value and depth hierarchy — `01-night`, `02-night`, `03-night`.** The references
   keep the destination/action readable through foreground, subject and distance
   separation. R9's arrival is half occluded by a dark near tree, and its night wheel,
   trestles and trough collapse into the same dark value, removing both destination
   emphasis and mechanical clarity.

### Bar questions

- **A — Does this belong to the Meadows key-art world? No.** The timber mill, bridge,
  rolling green banks, broadleaf woodland and day palette point toward the correct
  world. The proof set is sunk by the unresolved block geometry, weak terrain/support
  handoffs, obstructed arrival and unreadable night mechanism. Those are scene,
  material and composition repairs; they do not require a different Meadows identity.
- **B — Beside the Palworld references, is this trying to be the same kind of game?
  No.** The third-person landscape and stylized architecture share the broad genre,
  but this named landmark lacks the instantly legible action silhouette, coherent
  constructed assembly, material finish and foreground-to-subject hierarchy of a
  shipping-quality real-time location. Most of this gap is fixable in the scene by
  rebuilding the race/support silhouette, exposing a broad connected water surface,
  seating supports and masonry into the bank, choosing a wheel-readable view, and
  restoring local night separation. Higher-detail bespoke mill hardware could improve
  finish, but the present failure is primarily composition and construction, not
  missing art.

R9 is a meaningful cleanup of R8's largest occluders, but it does not close the
binding hydraulic-sequence, grounded-mill, wheel-readability or night-proof gates. Do
not promote Old Mill Crossing or update the authoritative Meadows ledger from this
evidence.
