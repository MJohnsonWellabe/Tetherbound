# Old Mill Crossing `final-old-mill-11` independent review

## Verdict: FAIL

### Evidence integrity

I inspected `manifest.json` and all eight 1280x800 PNGs at native resolution,
alongside the Meadows key art, all five Palworld reference frames, the binding visual
rubric and the R9 FAIL report. The four required day/night pairs are present. The
manifest reports `complete: true`, no automatic failures, an unchanged production
fixture disclosure, a grounded south-bank hydraulic stand, and measured projection,
broadside, downhill and continuity receipts. Those receipts prove that the named
geometry is present and projected; the pixels remain authoritative for whether it
reads as a believable mill.

R11 materially improves R9's most important silhouette defect: `03-day/night` now
shows a large, round, broadside wheel whose rim and paddles can be recognized at a
glance. It does not close the grounded-support or continuous-water-process gates.

### The three largest gaps from the references

1. **A legible cause-and-effect water silhouette — `03-day/night`.** The references
   keep their central landmark or action readable through continuous shapes and
   separated values. R11 has a recognizable wheel, but its intake, contact,
   discharge and river remain disconnected cyan accents and dark slabs rather than
   one traceable process.
2. **Grounded authored construction — `03-day/night`, `04-day/night`.** The Meadows
   key art seats buildings, paths and waterfront edges into continuous terrain; the
   Palworld base frame groups constructed parts into a coherent worksite. R11's thin
   cantilever, monolithic piers, abrupt chute and isolated road-edge blocks still
   resemble unfinished blockout geometry.
3. **Destination-first route composition — `01-day/night`.** The references use a
   clear path-to-landmark hierarchy even amid dense foliage. R11's ordinary arrival
   is dominated by a foreground tree, leaving only a sliver of the mill and, at
   night, little readable road progression.

### Bar questions

- **A — Does this belong to the Meadows key-art world? No.** The half-timbered mill,
  green banks, woodland, bridge and broad water use the right broad vocabulary. The
  obstructed arrival, suspended structural silhouette and unresolved water/material
  handoffs keep the landmark below the key art's grounded, inviting, authored-world
  standard. These are fixable scene, composition, geometry and material problems.
- **B — Beside the Palworld references, is this trying to be the same kind of game?
  No.** The stylized third-person landscape is genre-adjacent, and the improved wheel
  is a useful focal asset. The location still lacks the shipping-quality references'
  immediate action read, coherent construction hierarchy and clean ground/contact
  finish. Most of the gap is fixable by exposing the arrival, rebuilding the race as
  visibly contained descending water, joining its discharge to the river, and
  replacing the slab-and-pier silhouette with grounded battered supports. Bespoke
  mill hardware could raise finish later, but missing art is not the present blocker.

R11 is a real wheel-readability improvement over R9, but automatic completeness and
projection do not close the binding hydraulic-chain, grounded-mill, arrival or finish
gates. Do not promote Old Mill Crossing or update the authoritative Meadows ledger
from this evidence.
