# Stronghold Approach — `final-stronghold-approach-01` independent visual judgment

## Verdict: POLISH — retain, not a strict named-location PASS

The approach has a coherent late-game sequence: powered pylons lead north, faction
standards and watch beats repeat along the road, a stone threshold frames the final
procession, and the Hall eventually resolves as a large fortified destination. This
is authored enough to remain above FAIL. It does not earn strict PASS because the
canonical 560 m arrival still reads primarily as a pylon run across open meadow, the
middle evidence is disrupted by a huge cropped creature, and the Hall loses most of
its mass and facade readability at night.

### Strict visual findings

- **Outer arrival (`01`): POLISH blocker.** The road, tree wall, repeated crystal
  pylons, cyan cable, and pale boulder rhythm create a clear controlled corridor.
  At the recorded 560 m distance the Hall is not visually recoverable; the image
  reads first as infrastructure in a broad meadow rather than the arrival to an
  occupied stronghold. At night the cyan network survives but the road and watch
  dressing fall close to black.
- **Road drop (`02`): evidence/composition blocker.** The forked road and repeated
  oxblood standard are present, and a distant Hall fragment appears at far right.
  A very large Skyrill is cropped through the entire left edge in both lighting
  states and becomes the dominant subject. The tiny standard/fire and off-axis Hall
  cannot compete, so this pair does not prove a deliberate middle approach beat.
- **Processional threshold (`03`): PASS with limits.** Twin stone piers, vertical
  faction cloth, fenced road, repeated downstream standard, and the Hall beyond
  finally form one legible destination axis. The gate is broad and grounded rather
  than a signboard across the road. In the night frame the threshold and path remain
  readable, but the Hall becomes a nearly black cutout with only small internal
  lights and a pale gap behind it.
- **Hallward overlook (`04`): strongest pair, still POLISH.** The castle mass,
  processional ramp, banners, pylon/cable, fence and approach path make the intended
  destination unmistakable in daylight. The bright, tall pylon competes strongly
  with the Hall, woodland hides much of its lower base, and the site reads sparsely
  occupied rather than under active Team Tether control. At night the pylon and
  banners retain color while most Hall architecture collapses into black.
- **Road hierarchy and continuity: PASS.** Across the set, dirt-road curvature,
  repeated cyan infrastructure, oxblood markers, and progressively larger Hall
  views communicate forward travel. Nothing visible suggests the path is blocked.
- **Faction occupation: POLISH.** Pylons, cables, standards, fence wings, cages and
  small supply/watch accents establish ownership. Ordinary frames show no strong
  guard, work crew, muster, machinery operation, or concentrated road wear; the
  occupation is communicated mainly through repeated markers rather than activity.
- **Terrain/ecology integration: mixed.** Tree masses and meadow ground cover frame
  the road naturally, but repeated pale boulder clusters and isolated pylon pads sit
  as placed rows on otherwise broad, smooth slopes. The stronghold base/ramp meets
  the land more convincingly in `04` than the infrastructure does in `01`.
- **Named-location identity: POLISH.** Frames `03` and `04` unmistakably show a
  Stronghold Approach. The full sequence does not sustain that identity from the
  authored outer arrival through night, so the evidence cannot support strict PASS.
