the road-drop pair now shows a continuous powered processional line, and the final
two pairs make the threshold and Hall unmistakable. It remains POLISH because the
canonical 560 m arrival still has no readable Hall, crown, wall, or fortified

## Strict findings

- **`01-outer-watch-arrival`: POLISH blocker.** The curving dirt road, tree edge,
  crystal pylons, cyan cable, and pale watch stones form a readable controlled route.
  At the recorded 560 m Hall distance, however, no stronghold silhouette survives.
  Day reads as infrastructure across meadow; night reduces most of the road and
  occupation dressing to near-black while the oversized moon and cable dominate.
- **`02-road-drop`: improved, but not sufficient.** Removing the R1 creature
  occlusion reveals the forked road, repeated pylons, distant palisade/crown, and a
  coherent forward axis. The foreground pylon occupies roughly a quarter of the
  frame and the distant fortification is still a small, low-contrast horizon detail,
  especially at night.
- **`03-processional-reveal`: PASS.** The gate, twin faction banners, fenced road,
  downstream markers, and rising Hall mass make the destination axis clear. The
  left tree block is heavy, but it frames rather than defeats the approach.
- **`04-hallward-overlook`: PASS by day, POLISH by night.** The castle, ramp, road,
  standards, fence, pylon network, work lights, and ordinary creature establish the
  intended late-game destination and credible scale. At night the keep becomes a
  largely black cutout; isolated windows and cyan infrastructure do not describe its
  facade, base, or occupancy strongly enough.
- **Continuity and intentionality: PASS.** The four positions now form an honest
  progression from controlled outer road to gate to fortified ramp. Repeated oxblood
  and cyan language is authored and route legibility remains intact.
- **Named-location identity: POLISH.** The back half is unmistakably Stronghold
  Approach, but the full evidence sequence does not sustain that identity from its
  canonical outer arrival through both clocks.

## Three largest reference gaps

1. **Landmark hierarchy (`01`, `02`):** the key art and Palworld plateau frame put a
   destination silhouette above route furniture; here pylons dominate before the
   stronghold becomes recoverable.
2. **Night modelling (`01`, `04`):** the key art uses warm local pools and separated
   terrain/building planes; these frames crush the road, woodland, and keep toward
   blue-black while the moon, cables, and banners remain bright.
3. **Occupation and surface richness (`02`, `04`):** the references layer guards,
   work clusters, worn ground, and materially varied fortification. This approach is
   coherent but sparse, with smooth meadow and repeated markers doing most of the work.

## Reference-bar verdict

**A. Meadows key-art world fit: yes.** The rolling meadow, broadleaf edge, dirt road,
fortified Hall, faction colour, and day/night mystery belong to the intended pastoral
fantasy world. The weak outer landmark read and crushed night values prevent strict
location closure, not world fit.

**B. Palworld-kind-of-game fit: yes.** The traversable third-person route, production
creature, large built objective, and readable hostile infrastructure clearly pursue
the same creature-led open-world language. Material richness, occupation density, and
early landmark hierarchy remain behind the reference.
