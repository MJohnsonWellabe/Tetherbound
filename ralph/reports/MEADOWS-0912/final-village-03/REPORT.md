# Independent visual verdict — `final-village-03`

**Overall: FAIL**

The manifest is complete (`complete: true`, 14/14 expected PNGs captured, no failures). I inspected all 14 production frames at original resolution. The civic center is substantially more authored than a placeholder settlement, but this capture does not meet the full 09/12 village acceptance bar.

| Requirement | Verdict | Capture evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Coherent street/layout | **FAIL** | `02-well-path-south-*` establishes a useful well-centered bend, but `03-grandpas-home-square-*` and `04-west-street-to-well-*` do not make the west street leg read continuously. Houses, fencing, planters, and the cart fragment or conceal the route. `07-south-street-from-well-*` cannot establish the south leg because foreground structure fills the view. |
| House/fence/NPC relationships | **FAIL** | The gate ensemble in `06-*` has a credible threshold relationship, but the fence/planter band in `04-*` visually cuts across the intended approach. Several houses dominate or occlude the street, and the villagers are distant/scattered rather than legibly tied to a home, shop, or task. The capture does not clearly prove road-facing doors or purposeful NPC placement. |
| South-leg continuity in both directions | **FAIL** | `06-south-street-from-trail-gate-*` gives a partially legible gate-to-well view, although crossed timber heavily obstructs it. The reverse `07-south-street-from-well-*` pair is unusable: roof/canopy, chains, signs, and a large pale panel obscure nearly the entire road. |
| Grounding/intersections | **FAIL** | Most houses and props appear grounded, but `07-*` places the camera/player behind or inside the foreground canopy/sign assembly, producing an obvious composition intersection. The blank panel across the lower third and the occluding overhead geometry are acceptance-blocking. |
| Day/night readability | **FAIL** | Windows, moonlight, and several landmarks survive at night, especially in `02-*`, `05-*`, and `06-*`. However, street edges, fences, and NPC relationships collapse into dark values in `01-*`, `03-*`, and `04-*`; `07-*` remains unreadable in both lighting states. |
| Mira trade/house presentation | **FAIL** | In `05-mira-trade-crest-*`, the well, direction sign, and oversized green **GRANDPA'S VILLAGE** board dominate. Mira is tiny/partly hidden and neither her house nor a trade identity is the clear subject. The sign reads as a town label, not a convincing Mira shop sign. |
| Mira crest composition | **FAIL** | The named `05-*` view does not reveal a distinct crest/overlook silhouette or a clear Mira-on-the-crest hierarchy. The foreground roof/sign mass and well sculpture carry the composition instead. |
| Finished sign/banner geometry | **FAIL** | The large unmarked pale face in `07-*` (and a smaller blank face at the right edge of `06-*`) reads as unfinished plane/board geometry. This conflicts with the no-placeholder sign/banner acceptance standard. |
| No obvious paper flags | **PASS** | No visible red flag reads as a flat paper cutout in these 14 frames. The small oxblood elements on the trail-gate posts appear mounted/dimensional. This pass is limited to the flags actually visible in the capture. |

## Acceptance blockers

1. Rebuild or reframe the well-to-gate view so the south road is visible from the well without canopy/sign/camera intersection, then prove the same continuous leg in reverse.
2. Make the west leg read as a street between Grandpa's area and the well; fencing, raised beds, and cart staging should reinforce its edges instead of visually crossing it.
3. Give Mira a dedicated, unobstructed house/trade composition and an identifying sign that belongs to her rather than the village generally.
4. Remove or finish the conspicuous blank sign faces and lift nighttime route/NPC separation enough to read the same relationships as daytime.

This verdict is based only on visible capture evidence, not source intent or earlier reports. It does not clear collision/traversal behavior or any out-of-frame flag treatment.
