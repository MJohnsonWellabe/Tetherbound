# Burrow Warrens approach — independent visual judgment

**Verdict: HOLD / not accepted for OWNER-0912 Tier 2 #5.**

The five files declared by `manifest.json` are present, the manifest reports
`complete: true`, `5 / 5` captured frames and no capture failures, and every PNG was
inspected at its native 1280 × 800 resolution. The new composition is a material
improvement over a small anonymous hole: the distant day frame has a clear destination,
the two ruts lead to it, and the mound/tree silhouette is recognizable. It does not yet
earn a visual PASS. Visible material/readability problems remain, and the submitted
camera set omits the one exterior angle needed to decide the redesign's central claim.

This is an evidence judgment, not a source or traversal judgment. The manifest's very
small player-ground deltas establish that the player/camera stands were seated; they do
not establish that the new ribs, windfall pieces or rut surfaces are correctly seated.

## Required criteria

### Road-to-mouth hierarchy

**Day arrival: provisionally successful.** In `01-arrival-day.png`, the dark paired
ruts converge on the mouth, the pale brow separates the opening from the mound, and the
opening holds the center of the picture. The mouth reads at ordinary road distance.
The large left foreground rib competes with the route but does not fully steal it.

**Night arrival: only partially successful.** The lit aperture remains findable in
`01-arrival-night.png`, but the mound, ribs, windfall and much of the route collapse
into one near-black mass. The bright white pickup/cache effect at far right becomes the
strongest local light and competes with the dungeon entrance. The route is locatable,
but the authored approach composition is not readable as a composition.

### Asymmetry versus portal-like framing

The overall skyline is asymmetric: the rear mound steps left, trees vary in height, and
the large side pieces are unequal. The entrance itself still reads as a centered,
nearly complete circular ring around a black opening. From the only exterior camera,
the side ribs flatten into dark slabs and the root fringe becomes decoration around a
portal rather than a clearly excavated, laterally uneven bank.

The set contains no mid-oblique exterior view. It jumps from the same centered frontal
arrival stand in day/night directly to a camera inside the brow. Therefore it cannot
show whether the three strata ribs actually step in depth, whether the windfalls bridge
stone into meadow convincingly, or whether the mouth stops reading like a freestanding
portal when its side profile is visible. **That missing mid-oblique angle prevents a
positive judgment of the redesign's primary asymmetry claim.**

### Visual and material continuity

Continuity is not yet convincing. The field is mottled olive photographic ground, the
bank is a broad smooth near-black green mass, the approach ribs are flat dark
brown/black faceted solids, and the mouth ring is a much lighter mossy stone. The
windfall/root language helps connect the site to the surrounding trees, but it does not
resolve the abrupt value and surface changes between field, ribs, bank and brow.

At the threshold, the pale exterior brow gives way immediately to extremely dark flat
interior faces. `03-den-arrival-day.png` then presents a hard rectangular stone doorway
and box corridor. The manifest correctly discloses that the interior is untouched, so
this is not charged as a regression from the exterior pass; it is still evidence that
the approach-to-den material/form transition remains abrupt rather than continuous.

### Day and night readability

- `01-arrival-day.png`: readable destination and usable hierarchy; visual polish still
  required.
- `01-arrival-night.png`: entrance is findable, but most approach massing loses all
  internal separation.
- `02-threshold-day.png`: fails presentation readability. Most of the frame is black;
  roots and shell edges overlap into an indistinct canopy, while the resident blocks a
  large part of the already narrow view through.
- `02-threshold-night.png`: fails. Almost all threshold form disappears. Only part of
  the resident, a small distant opening and a patch of foreground light remain.
- `03-den-arrival-day.png`: the destination resident is visible, but is partly occluded
  by the rectangular doorway and the image mainly documents the unchanged box-like
  interior rather than the new approach.

The approach needs enough controlled local value separation for the brow, roots, ribs
and traveled floor to remain distinguishable at night. It need not become bright, but
the current threshold-night frame is not usable visual evidence.

### Seating, intersections, grass bleed and z-fighting

No gross floating object is proven in the centered arrival frame, but the critical
bases are hidden by the frontal overlap and grass. The left foreground rib and tall
right rib have hard, prop-like silhouettes; their contact patches cannot be inspected.
Branches overlap the brow and side slabs heavily, and the threshold frames show bright
sky/terrain wedges through the upper side assembly. Those gaps read as intersecting or
unfinished shell layers rather than a clean root-fringed cut.

The paired rut meshes read as dark ribbons laid over the field. Grass remains visibly
inside or directly across parts of their road-distance footprint, and their serrated
edges are conspicuous against the terrain. The threshold floor also contains isolated
grass/green tufts against the black shell. A still image shows no conclusive moiré or
duplicate-surface pattern, so this report does **not** claim proven z-fighting; however,
the low-lift ruts and missing oblique/motion evidence mean absence of z-fighting is not
established either.

## Evidence sufficiency and minimum reproof

The manifest is mechanically complete but the framing set is not acceptance-complete
for the new exterior layer. `03-den-arrival-day` is useful continuity context, not a
substitute for viewing the exterior construction from the side.

Minimum reproof:

1. Add one daylight mid-oblique exterior frame, roughly 10–15 m off the route axis and
   far enough back to include the full mouth, all three rib bases, both windfalls and
   most of the paired ruts. This must expose depth, burial and side-face seams rather
   than repeat the favorable centered silhouette.
2. Re-present the threshold at night after it retains readable brow/root/floor
   separation without flattening the cave into daylight.
3. Use the oblique frame, and preferably a short moving traversal receipt, to establish
   that the rut surfaces do not float, grass does not cut through their traveled strips,
   and no coplanar flicker occurs.

Until that evidence exists and the visible black-collapse/material discontinuity is
repaired, Tier 2 #5 remains **Source-complete-awaiting-runtime-or-visual**, not Complete.
