## Acceptance criteria

### Road-to-mouth hierarchy — PARTIAL

The distant frontal day/night pair now provides a clear route target. That is a real
improvement and should be preserved. The route surface itself is not accepted: two
uniform near-black strips with crisp parallel boundaries read as rails or decals, not
compressed soil. Their contrast is too severe for the surrounding olive field, and
the oblique frame confirms their flat surface treatment.

### Asymmetry versus portal framing — FAIL

The overall debris skyline is asymmetric, but the entrance remains a nearly complete
pale circular arch centered on a black aperture. The new side view does not dissolve
that portal read; instead it reveals unrelated slabs and wedges arranged around it.
Asymmetry alone is insufficient when the parts do not form a believable excavated
bank, collapsed root system, or laterally weighted brow.

### Earth/moss material continuity — FAIL

The mound has a dark mottled earth/moss response, but it meets pale yellow-green arch
stone, flat rust-brown boards/slabs, black roots, and angular olive panels with abrupt
value and roughness changes. The facade does not share one earth/root family. The
large brown slab in `02-mid-oblique-day` is especially incompatible and dominates the
intended mouth.

### Day/night threshold readability — FAIL

The added practical is visible but far too localized. It lights a small floor patch
and dot on the wall while leaving the shell construction black. Night does not need
daylight exposure, but it must retain brow/root/floor separation and a readable path
through the mouth; `02-mid-oblique-night` and `03-threshold-night` do not.

### Seating, seams, grass, and z-fighting — FAIL / motion still unproven

The oblique evidence shows bright open wedges between upper shell layers and hard
faceted bases that do not blend into terrain. The rut strips retain hard, perfectly
parallel edges and sit visually above the surrounding field response; grass crowds
their margins and the traveled-earth transition is absent. No still frame proves
active coplanar flicker, so this report does not claim observed z-fighting. A short
moving receipt is still required after the geometry/material repair.

## Minimum repair and recapture

1. Remove or fully re-author the dominant rust-brown facade slab and the upright
   blockout panels; build one asymmetric, laterally weighted earth/root brow whose
   side profile visibly grows from the mound.
2. Close the bright upper shell wedges and seat every visible rib/root/windfall base
   into the same earth/moss material family.
3. Replace the black parallel rut strips with irregular, terrain-seated compressed
   earth whose value and edges feather into the road and locally exclude grass.
4. Broaden the restrained threshold practical/fill enough that brow, roots, traveled
   floor, and first interior wall remain distinguishable at night without making the
   facade glow.
5. Recapture the same three exterior day/night pairs. The present 12 m mid-oblique
   angle is useful and should be retained; no additional still-camera category is
   required. Add a short low moving approach/threshold receipt to rule out rut or
   shell z-fighting that PNGs cannot establish.

Until those pixels pass, Tier 2 #5 remains visually incomplete even if traversal and
interior mechanics continue to work.
