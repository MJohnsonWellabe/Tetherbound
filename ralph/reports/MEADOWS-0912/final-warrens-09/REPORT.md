## Three largest gaps from the references

1. **Integrated landform:** `02-mid-oblique-day` separates the grey mound and brown
   sawtooth fan into visibly assembled layers. The key art and Palworld references blend
   soil, rock and vegetation into coherent terrain cuts.
2. **Organic construction:** `04-den-arrival-day` is dominated by a rectangular stone
   portal and straight box passage. The references avoid primitive-kit framing at hero
   destinations through irregular silhouettes and layered natural edges.
3. **Surface and depth richness:** `01-arrival-day`, `03b-threshold-inside-day` and
   `04-den-arrival-day` rely on large smooth planes with sparse wear, embedded stone,
   root structure or clustered detail; the references sustain multiple surface scales
   and stronger foreground/middle/distance separation.

## Bar questions

**A. Does this belong to the world of the Meadows key art? No.** The pastoral route,
palette and creature context point toward the setting, but the applied triangular fan,
smooth bank cones and box-built den do not share the board's integrated, weathered
landform language. The blocking gaps are scene-fixable: shoulder topology/material
transition, seam removal, irregular opening surrounds and a genuinely curved passage.

**B. Beside the Palworld references, would this be read as the same kind of game? No.**
The readable third-person scale and large creatures help, but this hero location remains
visibly primitive-built and materially shallow beside the references' authored terrain
cuts, surface layering and spatial depth. No new creature art is required to address
this R9 failure.

Retain the broad bank, removal of facade roots/crown, rounded near threshold, grounded
traversal receipt and live creature context. Replace the exterior sawtooth fan with a
continuous, non-pointed earth-to-mouth transition, and obscure or rebuild the stone
rectangle and straight corridor so the final den view is actually irregular in
silhouette, not merely hooded in front.
