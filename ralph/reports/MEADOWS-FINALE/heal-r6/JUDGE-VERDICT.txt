Q. Does the land visibly heal between each -a and -b, readable untold?
======================================================================
YES — in three of four pairs the land itself reads as healed; in the fourth
(h04) what reads is "life came back", not "the land changed".

h01 (Old Quarry)  — YES, clearly. -a is a parched straw/ochre floor with
  scattered grey tufts; -b is the same floor shifted to a live yellow-green,
  with fresher grass cards. The two cyan-crystal pylons are gone from the
  skyline — one now lies toppled across the mid-ground (x~650-780, y~320-345)
  — and the glowing cyan panel in the gate frame (x~425, y~290) is dark. A
  new broad-leaf plant has sprouted beside the yellow turnip. Weakness: the
  bare dead trees on the right (x~850-1000) are unchanged in -b, so the
  "healed" claim is contradicted in the same frame.

h02 (Approach)    — YES, the strongest pair. The cyan tether beams that slash
  across -a are gone; the tall pylon is down; the ochre field turns green.
  Readable at thumbnail size. Weaknesses: (1) the fallen pylon leaves a dark
  brown dome/half-cylinder (x~490-570, y~250-310) that reads as an unexplained
  bunker or boulder, not wreckage; (2) a dark cable still runs from it to a
  short surviving pylon (x~635), so the network looks partly alive; (3) the
  heal stops at the near field — the far hills and middle distance are
  identical between -a and -b, so it reads as a local tint, not a region.

h03 (Works)       — YES, but the weakest land change. The lit slope on the
  right goes from dry straw to green and the overhead cyan beam disappears.
  The shadowed foreground and path are virtually identical, so most of the
  frame does not change. -b also introduces a large flat pale beige wedge
  (x~845-1010, y~200-300) that reads as untextured ramp geometry rather than
  anything healed. The comparison is confounded by HUD: the TEAM 4/5 panel
  covers the left of -a and is absent in -b, so "new" boulders at x~0-120 in
  -b are just revealed, not grown.

h04 (Highfield)   — PARTIAL. The ground is already green in -a and is the
  same green in -b; the land does not visibly heal. What changes is a herd of
  ~8 deer and a boulder-backed beast (x~200-240, y~290) appearing — readable
  as "wildlife returned", which is a good story beat, but not a land heal.
  Again the team panel present in -a / absent in -b makes the left third
  non-comparable.

======================================================================
Defects, ranked
======================================================================
1. f03-f16: each pylon falls as a single rigid piece pivoting at its base —
   no breakup, no crystal shatter, no sparks or discharge when the power
   dies, no ground reaction (grass never flattens, no crater, no debris
   scatter). It reads as a prop being rotated, not a structure failing.
2. f15-f19: the dust is one flat, low-opacity beige card; it has no volume,
   no rising column, and never occludes the wreck. Palworld-03's event VFX
   (bright radial streaks, particulate) is the comparison — this is a
   whisper.
3. f00-f24: the land does not change at all in the fall sequence — the grass
   is equally green at t=0 and t=12 s. The one event on screen and the heal
   are disconnected; nothing spreads outward from the fallen pylon.
4. h02-b: dark dome remnant (x~490-570) and surviving dark cable to a short
   pylon; reads as unexplained geometry and "not fully shut down".
5. h03-b: large flat pale beige wedge (x~845-1010, y~200-300) looks like
   untextured blockout geometry; it is the brightest shape in the frame.
6. h01-a/b, h04-a/b: the cart (h04 x~415-510, h01 x~455-520) and fence runs
   (h04 x~780-980; h01 x~360-610) are pure flat white with no texture or
   shading — they read as missing materials, and are the highest-value
   shapes in the frame, pulling the eye off the event.
7. h04-b: the deer are identical clones (same size, tint, pose), pale and low
   detail; they read as stock placeholder fauna, not creatures of this world.
8. h03-a/b: the badger creature and the Hall walls carry a crunchy,
   pixelated low-res texture (visible stair-step on the black/white face
   stripe and wall stones) that clashes with the smooth painted trainer and
   grass.
9. h01-b: bare dead trees on the right remain dead after the heal.
10. h02, h03, h04: heal is near-field only; distant hills (h02) and shadowed
    ground (h03) do not change, so the heal reads as a colour grade around
    the camera rather than the region recovering.
11. All pairs: HUD state differs between -a and -b (team panel, alpha toast,
    one caption box), so each pair has regions that are not comparable.
    This is a capture defect, not an art defect, but it weakens the evidence.
12. f12-f16: the falling spire passes directly behind the trainer at close
    range with no reaction and no shake — scale of danger is lost.
13. All frames: the horizon is a pale, desaturated grey mountain card (h02,
    f00) with little value separation from the sky; distance reads as fog
    rather than landscape.

Scale check (trainer = 1.80 m): pylons ~6-8x trainer — plausible for a
landmark; badger (h03) is bulkier than the trainer and reads as a large
companion — consistent; deer (h04-b) shoulder roughly at trainer chest at
range — plausible; orange rabbit-creature (h04) ~0.5 m — plausible for a
small wild creature. No scale defect found.

Colour/identity: oxblood appears only on the Hall's banners (h03) — reserved
correctly. Cyan marks the Tether tech and is removed by the heal — a clean,
readable colour language for the event.

======================================================================
Three things that most separate these frames from the references
======================================================================
1. The event has no spectacle (f03-f19). Palworld-03 makes a field event
   loud: bright VFX streaks, particulates, a creature silhouette dominating
   the frame. Here a rigid mesh tips over with a faint dust card. The
   story's climax payoff is visually the quietest thing in the sequence.
2. Materials/art consistency (h01, h03-b, h04). Palworld-05's props (fence,
   crafting bench, rocks) are all textured and lit in one style; the
   keyart's fences and carts are weathered wood. Here pure-white untextured
   carts and fences, a blank beige ramp, pixel-crunchy creature/Hall
   textures and clone deer sit beside a smoothly painted trainer — four art
   styles in one frame.
3. Ground and depth structure (h02, h04, f00). Palworld-02 breaks the ground
   into readable dirt paths, short grass, rock and cliff with strong value
   breaks and a clear mid-ground. These frames fill the lower half with an
   even carpet of identical flower/grass cards at regular spacing, and the
   distance collapses into a pale grey mountain card; the keyart's village
   and mountain landmark language is absent from every view.

Bar questions
======================================================================
A. Belongs to the key-art world: YES.
   Carried by: the post-heal palette (h01-b, h02-b, h04) lands on the
   keyart's greens, olive and warm yellow; the Hall (h03) with oxblood
   banners is recognisably the "Team Tether Stronghold" panel; oak-like
   groves and wildflower meadows match; the heal turning ochre to green is
   exactly the keyart's "welcoming region" mood. Held back by the white
   props, the grey horizon card and the absence of settlement/landmark
   silhouettes in the distance.

B. Same kind of game as Palworld: YES.
   Carried by: third-person trainer in a creature-populated open meadow, a
   companion creature beside the player, Palworld-style HUD layout (objective
   top-right, bars bottom-left, hotbar), a wild herd in h04. Nobody would
   mistake the genre. It does not meet the bar on execution (gaps 1-3).

Fixable by changing the scene (no new art):
  - Event staging: split the pylon into its existing sub-parts or add a
    snap point, crystal-shatter/discharge particles, a rising dust column,
    grass flattening/decal at impact, camera shake (gap 1, defects 1-2, 12).
  - Make the heal spread visibly from the fallen pylon and reach the middle
    distance; include the dead trees and shadowed ground (defects 3, 9, 10).
  - Remove/replace the dark dome remnant and the leftover cable (defect 4).
  - Assign existing wood materials to the white cart/fences; texture or
    remove the beige ramp (defects 5-6, part of gap 2).
  - Vary deer scale/tint/pose and stagger their placement (part of defect 7).
  - Break up grass/flower scatter into clusters and clearings with dirt and
    rock patches; push horizon colour/value and add a distant landmark
    silhouette from installed assets (gap 3, defect 13).
  - Capture pairs with identical HUD state (defect 11).
Needs art not in the build:

    existing mesh does not yield convincing pieces.
