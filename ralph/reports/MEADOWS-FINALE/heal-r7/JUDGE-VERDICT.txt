F05#7 heal-r7: blind visual judge verdict
Inputs: the heal-r7 frames only (the pair and fall sheets, all eight h01-h04 frames at full size, f00/f03/f09/f17/f24 at full size); docs/reference/tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png; docs/reference/palworld-0*.jpg. No source, history or docs read beyond the rubric.

QUESTION 1: Does the healed ground read as green grass?
YES. In every "-b" frame the ground reads immediately as living green meadow turf, not tinted dirt. The ground texture keeps its blade-streak detail, and the grass cards, flowers and shrubs share its hue, so ground and foliage read as one surface. Caveat (defect 1 below): in h02-b and h03-b the green is close to one saturated lime value everywhere, so it reads more as a mown lawn than as the key art's mixed meadow.

QUESTION 2: Does the land visibly heal, readable without being told?
YES in the before/after pairs, NO in the fall sequence. Every pair shows dry straw/ochre ground become green, and the Team Tether machinery goes dead or down, so the cause and effect are clear without text. h04 adds wildlife returning. The f-sequence shows no ground change at all: the grass is the same green-olive in f00 and f24. What it shows is the pylons going dark and falling. Nothing in that vantage tells you the land healed; it only tells you the towers fell.

PER PAIR
h01 quarry: YES. -a is a clearly sick clearing: a dead brown straw floor, a lit pylon and cyan tether line. -b has a lush green floor, the pylon toppled beside the rock (dark debris at ~700,330), the tether line gone and the socket dimmed. The trees, rocks and props stay identical, so the ground is plainly the thing that changed. It is the strongest pair. The green is warm and varied, with shadow and value break-up under the trees. Weak point: the bare dirt path strip and the white fence/plank props (360-620, 220-420) are unchanged and still read as raw, untextured white blocks.

h02 approach: YES, with the biggest before/after swing. -a has an ochre field, two lit pylons and cyan lines to the Hall. -b has a vivid green field, the pylons down (a dark brown dome plus debris at ~400-570,250-330) and the lines dropped to a thin dark cable. Defects: the distant hill band (60-560, 170-230) stays straw-yellow in -b, a hard boundary that reads as an unhealed zone or a terrain-material seam rather than natural variation. The fallen pylon reads as a brown blob or dome, not a toppled tower. A faint grey diagonal line survives in the sky at top right (~1040-1070, 0-150), a leftover tether filament. The foreground green is uniform and over-saturated.

h03 works: YES, but weakest. -a already has green grass on the right half, so the change there is a saturation boost, not dry-to-green. The left and mid-ground go from brown-dark to green, and the cyan line across the sky disappears. Defects: a large pale beige slab or ramp (~850-1010, 150-295) becomes the most prominent surface in -b. It is flat and untextured, and reads as a missing-material plane beside the new green. A second badger (left edge, ~0-130, 250-340) appears in -b in place of the moss-shelled creature, which reads as a swap rather than a heal cue. The foreground right quadrant is a single lime value with almost no variation.

h04 highfield: YES, and the most legible story beat. The flat khaki ground (-a) becomes green meadow with extra orange/yellow flowers, and a herd of deer appears. Without text this reads as "life came back". Defects: the deer are a near-identical beige colour and pose, spaced like a spawned group. A pale band or rope around the big tree trunk (~770-830, 205-215) persists in both frames. The white cart and fence (-a) read as untextured white geometry, partly hidden by deer in -b.

f-sequence (f00-f24): the ground does not heal on screen. f00 already shows the same green-olive meadow as f24. The event reads as: crystals go dark (f01-f03), lines drop to dark cables (f03), the towers tilt (f04-f12), dust (f13-f18), then wreckage lies in the grass (f24). The collapse itself reads well and in time order. Defects: the ground, sky and light are identical across all 25 frames, so any "healing" here is invisible. The dust clouds (f13-f18) are flat brown sprite sheets whose edges show against the sky. The wreck at right (~1110-1280, 340-380) contains a pale, flat, grey-blue slab that reads as a floating plane. At f24 the fallen towers are low dark bars that are hard to identify at 30% size.

DEFECTS RANKED
1. h02-b, h03-b (and h04-b foreground): the healed green is one high-saturation lime value over whole quadrants, with little patchiness, dry tufts, clover or dirt. It reads as a lawn or a filter, not a meadow. The key art mixes olive, yellow-green, ochre and wildflower drifts.
2. h02-b: the distant hill stays straw-yellow with a hard edge against the green. It reads as an unhealed chunk or a material seam.
3. f00-f24: no ground change is visible in the event sequence, so the heal is only legible as a cut between -a and -b.
4. h03-b: the untextured pale ramp/slab is the brightest non-sky surface. f24 has a pale floating slab in the wreck at right.
5. h02-b, f13-f24: the fallen pylons read as blobs, domes or bars. The dust is flat sprite cards.
6. h02-b top right: a leftover grey tether filament in the sky. h04: a band around the tree trunk.
7. h01, h04: raw white fence, plank and cart props look untextured and unlit next to painted terrain.
8. h04-b: the deer herd is uniform in colour, size and pose, so it reads as spawned.
9. h03: the badger creature is shorter than the 1.80m trainer's head in this framing, as far as a still allows; the depth offset makes it hard to measure exactly. The moss-shelled creature swaps for a second badger between frames.

THE THREE THINGS THAT MOST SEPARATE THESE FRAMES FROM THE REFERENCES
1. Ground variation (h02-b, h03-b, h04-b). Palworld-02/03 grass is a desaturated natural green broken by worn earth, clumps, value shifts and shrubs, and the key art mixes olive, yellow and ochre. These frames switch whole areas to one flat saturated lime, so the healed land looks painted over rather than grown. Fixable in the scene: palette/tint variation, patch masks, dry tufts and dirt breakup.
2. Prop and structure art at the same finish as terrain (h01, h03-b, h04, f24). Palworld's props, buildings and debris are fully textured and lit like the ground. Here white fences, carts and planks, a flat beige ramp, pale slabs and brown-dome pylon wreckage read as untextured or placeholder geometry next to detailed grass and trees. Partly fixable: materials and assignments on existing meshes, and hiding or replacing slabs. Convincing wreckage and dust FX likely need art not in the build.
3. Life, event and silhouette readability (h04-b, f-sequence). Palworld frames are anchored by distinctive, expressive creatures with strong silhouettes, and events carry clear, bright FX. Here the heal moment has no visible VFX or ground transition, the wildlife is a uniform deer herd, and the fallen towers are dark bars at small size. Fixable in the scene: a staged ground-colour sweep during the fall, placing wreckage in lit and clear spots, and varying the herd. Expressive bespoke creature variety needs art not in the build.

BAR QUESTIONS
A. Belongs to the key-art world: YES (the -b frames). Rolling green hills, oak-like trees, pastel sky, wildflowers, a ruined stone Hall with banners, and dry-to-lush contrast all speak the key art's language. h01-b and h04-b are closest. What holds it back is the lime lawn uniformity and the untextured props, not the world concept.
B. Same kind of game as Palworld: YES. It is a third-person trainer in an open, green, stylised meadow with creatures nearby, a similar HUD layout and grass density. Someone would group them together. What sinks it relative to the bar: flatter prop finish, uniform over-saturated ground, weaker creature appeal and variety (the deer herd, the badger), and an event (the tower fall) that lacks FX punch.
Fixable by changing the scene: ground palette and patch variation, the unhealed far-hill seam, the heal transition visible during the fall, removing leftover filaments and bands, materials on white and beige props, placement and readability of wreckage, and herd variation.
Needs art not in the build: convincing collapse wreckage and dust/impact VFX at Palworld finish, and more distinctive, expressive creature models to populate the healed land.
