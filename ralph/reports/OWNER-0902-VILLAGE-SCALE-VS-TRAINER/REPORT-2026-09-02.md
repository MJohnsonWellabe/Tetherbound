# FINDING — "villagers look small next to our own character" is a camera-depth effect, not a height/fit bug

**Task as handed in:** the owner rejected the prior session's "it's the
creatures that grew, not the villagers" explanation outright ("small
villagers have nothing to do with larger creatures. bullshit. it's compared
to our own character NPCs look small"), and that prior session had shipped
with **zero render verification** (no Godot binary was available to it).
This session's job: find the real cause with an actual rendered, measured
frame — not a third plausible-sounding guess.

**Verdict: no code or config bug. `character_model.gd`'s `_fit()` and
`render_bounds.gd` scale every rig to its declared `art.json` height
correctly — verified below to within ~1%, which is noise at this render
size. What actually produces "villagers look small next to me" is the
trailing third-person camera's own geometry: whenever the player stands
close to and faces a villager (which is how a player would ever be in a
position to compare their own height to one), the villager sits measurably
farther from the camera than the player's own body does, and at typical
talking distance that alone shrinks the villager to roughly 73% of the
trainer's apparent screen height — a ~25 percentage-point swing that has
nothing to do with either character's declared height.**

## Method

Built a small standalone capture tool, `tools/_capture_trainer_villager_scale.gd`,
following the existing bare-stage pattern in `tools/capture_village_npcs.gd`
and `tools/_capture_character_cast.gd` (both already read in full before
writing this) rather than paying the ~2.4s/frame cost of the full
`meadows_playground.tscn`. It builds the trainer and villager rigs through
the real `CharacterModel.build_from_config()` / `art.json` path — the same
code the shipped world uses — against a flat magenta backdrop over a grey
ground, so a separate Python pass (`/tmp/.../measure_scale.py`, not
committed — a throwaway measurement script) can find each figure's actual
on-screen silhouette top/bottom by colour alone and report real pixel
heights, independent of any of the engine's own claims about scale.

Render command:

```
xvfb-run -a -s "-screen 0 1280x800x24" "$GODOT" --path . \
  --rendering-driver opengl3 --resolution 1280x800 \
  --script tools/_capture_trainer_villager_scale.gd
```

Three shots, all against the same magenta/grey stage so silhouette
measurement is directly comparable across them:

- **Shot A — equal distance** (`shots/shot_a_equal_distance.png`): trainer,
  `villager_keeper`, `villager_farmer` standing in a row, identical Z from
  camera. Isolates whether `_fit()`/`render_bounds.gd` scale each rig to its
  declared height correctly when camera distance is held constant.
- **Shot B — gameplay-realistic, facing** (`shots/shot_b_gameplay_realistic.png`):
  the real third-person camera geometry from `data/config/movement.json`
  (`distance=5.2m, height=1.75m, pitch=-12deg, fov=70`), trainer at the
  origin facing away from the camera (the standard over-the-shoulder
  framing), `villager_keeper` placed 1.8m in front of the trainer facing
  back — i.e. exactly where a player stands to talk to, or simply look at,
  an NPC.
- **Shot C — gameplay-realistic, side-by-side** (`shots/shot_c_gameplay_side_by_side.png`):
  same real camera, but the villager stands shoulder-to-shoulder with the
  trainer (same Z, just offset sideways) instead of farther from the camera.
  Isolates whether the real camera itself uniformly shrinks NPCs (it should
  not — both figures are ~equidistant from the lens here) versus the
  specific "walked up to face them" framing in Shot B being what produces
  the gap.

## Measured pixel heights (silhouette top-to-feet, real render pixels)

| Shot | Figure | Declared height | Camera distance | Measured px height | Measured ratio to trainer | Declared ratio to trainer |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | trainer | 1.80m | 7.66m | 262px | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| A | villager_keeper | 1.78m | 8.03m | 262px | 1.000 | 0.989 |
| A | villager_farmer | 1.75m | 8.03m | 257px | 0.981 | 0.972 |
| B | trainer | 1.80m | 5.82m | 194px | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| B | villager_keeper | 1.78m | 7.45m | **141px** | **0.727** | 0.989 |
| C | trainer | 1.80m | 5.82m | 194px | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| C | villager_keeper | 1.78m | 6.04m | 188px | 0.969 | 0.989 |

**Shot A confirms the fit code is correct.** At equal camera distance, both
villagers' measured pixel-height ratios track their declared `art.json`
height ratios to within ~1 percentage point (262/262 vs. declared 0.989,
257/262=0.981 vs. declared 0.972) — well inside the noise floor of a
~260px-tall silhouette (hairstyle volume alone is worth a pixel or two).
There is no hidden second scale multiplier, no `_fit()` regression, nothing
for `render_bounds.gd` to have gotten wrong here. This directly rules out
the "code/fit bug" branch of the brief's own investigation order.

**Shot C confirms the real camera does not uniformly shrink NPCs.**
Side-by-side at (nearly) the same depth, the villager still measures 96.9%
of the trainer's height — close to its declared 98.9%, the small remaining
gap explained by the villager sitting a further 0.22m from the camera
purely from standing 1.6m to the side of a camera that looks straight
ahead, not at an angle.

**Shot B is the actual complaint, reproduced and measured.** The *same*
`villager_keeper` rig, at the *same* declared height, standing where a
player would actually stand to face and talk to them (1.8m in front of the
trainer, which puts them ~1.6m farther from the trailing camera than the
trainer's own body), collapses to **73% of the trainer's on-screen height**
— a dramatic, visually obvious size gap that both screenshots below show
directly, not just numbers:

- `shots/shot_b_gameplay_realistic.png` — the villager is unmistakably,
  strikingly smaller than the trainer.
- `shots/shot_c_gameplay_side_by_side.png` — the same villager, same
  distance-in-front-of-camera pose, at (nearly) the same depth as the
  trainer, reads as essentially the same size.

The only variable that changed between B and C is depth from the camera.
That is a direct, render-verified demonstration that the "small villager"
effect is a **camera/perspective framing consequence of the trailing
third-person camera**, not a model, fit, or config defect — and it is worst
in precisely the situation the owner is actually judging it from: standing
near and facing a villager, since that is the only way to visually compare
"our own character" to one in the first place. The effect gets *more*
pronounced, not less, the farther the NPC stands from the player during
that comparison (a simple inverse-distance projection check against the
measured trainer distance puts the ratio at ~0.85 at a 1.0m gap and ~0.65 at
a 3.0m gap) — so this is not an artifact of the specific 1.8m chosen here,
it is the general shape of the effect.

## What this rules out, and what it does not fix

This is **not** a repeat of the rejected "compared to bigger creatures"
framing (nothing about a creature appears anywhere in this investigation),
and it is **not** the "hidden height regression" the first response to this
complaint should have checked for and didn't — Shot A checks that directly
and it is clean.

It also does not fully explain itself away as "just how cameras work and
nothing to fix" — a design decision genuinely is available here (see
below), it is just not a safe one for this task to make unilaterally per
`CLAUDE.md`'s "ask instead of inventing" list, which already names "changing
traversal philosophy" as exactly this class of call. Concretely, the design
question is: **should the trailing exploration camera (or a dedicated
dialogue/close-approach camera) hold an NPC at closer-to-equal apparent size
when the player is standing near and facing them, the way Shot C already
does structurally?** That is a camera-behavior change (e.g. a
context-aware camera profile the way combat already swaps in a narrower-lens
profile via `camera_rig.gd`'s `set_target(profile)`), not a one-line data
fix, and it changes how every NPC interaction in the game reads, so it
needs an owner call rather than a guess.

**Separately, and much smaller:** Shot A's own frame visibly shows
`villager_farmer` (the `villager_female` base rig) reading stockier/more
head-heavy than the trainer even where its measured height ratio matches
its declared config almost exactly — this is the *already open*,
independently-flagged question from the deleted `BLOCKED.md`, carried
forward in `ralph/BACKLOG.md` §1: "are villager NPC rigs meant to read as
adults or youths, since their proportions don't cleanly support either."
This investigation did not re-derive that finding new; it just confirms
the same rig proportions are visible in this render too, and that they are
a real but secondary contributor (worth low single-digit percent, per
Shot A's own measured ratios) next to the ~25-point camera-depth effect
that dominates Shot B.

## What this branch does not touch

No code or data changed. `tools/_capture_trainer_villager_scale.gd` is a
new diagnostic tool (kept, in case this needs re-measuring after a camera
decision lands), committed alongside this report and its three renders. No
`art.json` height was touched — Shot A already shows the declared heights
are not the dominant driver, and nudging them up further would be exactly
the kind of unauthorized, unrequested design change the prior session
correctly declined to make for a different (wrong) reason.

**What this needs from the owner:** a decision on whether the exploration
camera should behave differently when the player is near and facing an
NPC (closing some or all of the depth gap Shot C shows is achievable), or
whether the current behaviour — an NPC farther from the camera than the
player reads smaller, which is true to how the camera actually sees the
scene — is acceptable as-is. Nothing here is guessed; it is measured
directly off real rendered frames, referenced above by path.
