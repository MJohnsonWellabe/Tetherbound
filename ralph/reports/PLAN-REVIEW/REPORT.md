# Tetherbound plan review

Read-and-report audit, 2026-09-19. No implementation, new branch, game execution, test execution, asset generation, or fixes were performed. The only authored artifact is this report.

**Audit baseline:** `origin/main` at `b8eda885810c577bc46e6cf096b01ea36531690f`, the consolidation commit. The workspace root is a directory of checkouts; `D:/tetherbound/source` itself is on `codex/all-branches-integration-0913` at `cd385bfa0`, with substantial pre-existing local changes. I read the six requested documents and the committed source through `git show`/`git grep` against the main revision, rather than pretending that checkout was current main. I also read the newer, unmerged combat and Cloudreach branch reports. Those results are identified separately below. Existing tests were inspected, not run; existing captures/verdicts are evidence with their original limitations, not a fresh playtest.

File references below refer to that main revision unless another branch/commit is named. Counts are recomputed JSON record counts, not claims of reachable, discoverable, enjoyable content. I consulted official Godot documentation only to check the renderer premise.

## 1. What game this actually produces

**An authored, chapter-based creature action RPG with expedition maintenance and light building.** More specifically, a monster-team campaign played through large connected regions. Calling it a Palworld/Valheim-style open-world survival sandbox sets an expectation the rules and geography do not support.

You start at Grandpa's, choose a large companion, learn to fight by becoming that creature, catch enough others to form a team, do preparation chores, and qualify for a tournament. You then work down a geographically gated journey: bridge, quarry and Warrens, river occupation and relay, captains and Sigils, Hall and Warden. Your recurring decision is whether to fight another encounter, spend recovery supplies, replace a teammate, or stop and prepare. Camps are service stops; the world is primarily the route to the next authored challenge. The climax asks whether a new legendary deserves one of the five places you have been filling with history. [Sources: `data/progression/objectives.json`; `data/config/tournament.json`; `data/config/bands/*/trainers.json`; `data/config/stronghold_climax.json`; `scripts/combat/combat_manager.gd`; `autoload/party.gd:19`.]

Later chapters change the traversal problem: flying through cliffs, traversing a storm forest and its arches, swimming between islands. There is substantial code for all four, not merely a Meadows prototype plus three written pitches. But traversal novelty and another liberation finale do not, by themselves, produce four different kinds of play. [Sources: `scripts/world/cloudreach_world.gd`, `stormwood_world.gd`, `water_world.gd`; `data/config/fly_traversal.json`, `stormwood_arches.json`, `water_swimming.json`.]

The player most likely to love it wants a small, memorable team, direct control of fantastic animals, understandable progression, gentle domestic preparation, and a finite adventure. Their useful comparison set is **a Pokémon/Monster Hunter Stories-style team journey with real-time piloting**, plus the companionship and camp atmosphere they enjoy in survival games. This is an audience hypothesis, not tested positioning. The Palworld player who chiefly enjoys accumulating creatures and assigning jobs, or the Valheim player who chiefly enjoys self-directed settlement, systemic danger and long-term world transformation, is not automatically this audience.

The strong idea is the five-companion expedition. The weak claim is that the current campaign structure produces the same freedom as its sandbox references.

## 2. The subtraction problem

First, **five owned at once does not mean five species**. The base catalogue contains 57 species definitions. The five-slot cap removes accumulation, not encounter variety. That distinction matters. [Sources: `data/creatures/species.json`; `autoload/party.gd::MAX_CREATURES`.]

**The argument that this is a complete game:** collecting everything is not necessary if deciding which five to carry is rewarding. Directly piloted creature combat can replace human shooting with a distinct skill. Injury, supplies, mounts, learning moves, evolution and permanent release can give exploration a purpose. A finite campaign does not need a factory or starvation to be complete. If an unfamiliar encounter changes how I fight, a detour changes what my team can do, and the next expedition tests the changes, the loop is whole.

**The argument that the main loop has been removed:** all the expensive replacement responsibilities fall on combat, authored encounters, creature appeal and rewards. There is no collection backlog, labour economy, factory expansion or severe survival pressure to create an independent reason to continue. Once I like my five, catching can turn into an invitation to destroy prior investment. Once my camp works, additional gathering can turn into replenishment. Once I have seen the opponent's pattern, the next fight can turn into XP maintenance.

The present design amplifies this risk:

- Bond asks for **50 wild wins, 3 discoveries, 4,000 metres, 4 rests and 10 feeds**, in an ordered ladder. A late replacement gives up accumulated progress precisely when the criteria demand that catching remain attractive. No live document specifies a satisfactory late-recruit recovery curve. [`data/config/bond_milestones.json`; `scripts/creatures/bond_milestones.gd`; `ACCEPTANCE.md` §5.2.]
- Only one evolutionary family exists: Mudsnout into Tuskroot or Ashtusk. Traits are chiefly flavour; stronger individuality is a numeric inducement. This is a thin supply of qualitative team changes for four chapters. [`species.json` evolution links; `data/traits/traits.json`; Bible §2.1.]
- Each creature has two equipped attack slots. **Those attacks are mechanically differentiated already**: Pebble Toss has range 9, cone 26°, power 0.85 and no lunge; Stone Rush has range 3.2, cone 80° and lunge 7.5. The Bible's description of moves as flavour/naming understates what exists. But shaped attacks are still a limited replacement for an entire collection/production economy. [`data/moves/moves.json`; `combat_manager.gd::_move_profile`, line 2288.]
- The 31 Meadows trainer rows all have `rechallenge: false`. Nonrenewable resources and one-time discoveries further constrain a long-term loop. Repeat play consequently falls back heavily on wild fights, alternative teams and player-made building goals. [`data/config/bands/*/trainers.json`; `scripts/world/harvest_node.gd`; archived D72.]

**My answer:** the design can be a complete game, but it has not yet earned its replacement loop. It is not inherently a creature collector missing its point. It is an action RPG whose combat mastery, team attachment and authored discoveries must do almost all the work. The current plan too often treats those as polish around an already complete loop. They are the loop. If this fails, a prettier landscape full of additional copies of existing encounters will expose the failure for longer.

## 4. The hard ceilings and whether the visual bar is achievable

**The complete §4 bar is aspirational under the present asset and approval plan.** Some individual requirements can be met; there is no credible basis to promise all of them across every creature, biome, pose, light state and gameplay distance using tuning alone.

However, the renderer explanation contains a factual error. **Compatibility does support real directional shadows.** Godot's renderer documentation distinguishes unsupported *PCSS directional soft shadows* from ordinary shadows. This source enables the sun's shadows in `world_look.gd:855`, uses two parallel splits at line 864, and defaults its shadow distance to 220 m. `project.godot:99` sets a 2048 directional shadow size. SDFGI, volumetric fog and SSR are unavailable in Compatibility; ordinary directional shadows are not Forward+-only. The “no real directional shadows” sentence in `TECHNICAL.md` §1 should not drive planning. [Official Godot 4.7 renderer comparison](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/rendering/renderers.html).

The actual ceilings are more specific:

1. **Geometry and rig defects cannot reliably be repaired by colour.** A face with poor anatomical structure, an inadequate silhouette, an unsuitable rest pose, or a tree with the wrong crown/trunk proportions eventually needs mesh/rig/animation work. Material edits may improve the read without supplying missing form. §4 requires every creature to be a distinct designed animal; the Bible explicitly specifies shared-mesh aspect variants. Those clauses need an explicit lineage exception or they can reject intended designs forever. The four model-path reuse pairs are Burrowback/Nightburrow, Trailpup/Stormtrail, Paddlenewt/Riftfrill, Tuskroot/Ashtusk. [`species.json::species.*.placeholder.model`; Bible §2.1; Acceptance §4.3.1.]
2. **The creature scale decision creates camera and terrain costs.** The actual base ladder is **1.90–7.20 m**, not the Bible's small-tier figures. Terrapup is 3.85 m, Ripplet 3.60, Pipwing 1.90, Veridian 5.80; Abyssal Guardian is 7.20. The Bible still calls Veridian at 3.60 m the game's tallest. Large bodies need wider arenas, meaningful attack distances, slope contact, doorway clearance and camera space. A fixed 3 m burst cannot be assessed independently of those bodies. [`species.json`; `combat.json::burst`; `COMBAT-0919/RESULTS.md`.]
3. **Rendering features do not substitute for production art.** Compatibility's missing dynamic GI and volumetrics constrain lighting approaches; stylised sky, restrained ambient light, baked lighting where suitable, material value design and carefully placed lights remain available. Baking is additional asset/pipeline work, especially in worlds built procedurally at runtime. It is not a free renderer checkbox.
4. **Draw-call caps are not a handheld performance acceptance test.** A scene under 4,000 draws can still fail from overdraw, shadow passes, vertex load, texture bandwidth or frame spikes. Increasing shared instance density is not free just because draw-call count stays flat. The plan lacks a binding device mode, resolution, frame-time target, percentile and traversal/camera scenario matrix. [`TECHNICAL.md` §14; `ACCEPTANCE.md` §7.]
5. **The current two bar questions are weaker than the quality ambition.** In `ralph/combat-depth-0919:ralph/reports/COMBAT-0919/VISUAL-VERDICT-02.md`, the critic answers A and B **yes**, while explicitly saying the frames do not meet Palworld's finish. I inspected that existing contact sheet: the animal's bright graphic surface, busy ground and partly obscured opponent support the stated concern. This was not a new blind judgment. “Belongs in this world” and “trying to be the same kind of game” measure identity and genre, not commercial polish.

To meet the ambitious reading of §4 requires an approved final creature/cast style, an asset-by-asset retain/retexture/rerig/remodel disposition, owner reference art or explicit permission for blocked subjects, authored locomotion/attack/rest standards, a coherent environment kit, and native motion/performance acceptance. Keep Compatibility unless new device evidence justifies changing it. **A renderer migration would not solve missing art direction, geometry or content.**

Recent evidence is unfavorable: Cloudreach's 0919 R1 had local improvements, an Observatory regression and both bar answers “no”; R2 still traded improvements for Three Bells regressions; R3 at `8fe8abb2d` has structural tests but no new capture. No location promotion followed. That is a concrete example of local tuning failing to converge on the whole scene, not proof that another grass parameter is the missing answer. [Branch `ralph/cloudreach-visual-production-0919`, `ralph/reports/CLOUDREACH-0919/RESULTS.md`, `CANDIDATE-R3.md`.]

### Hours and cost

**I cannot honestly assign actual authored hours per biome from these files.** No fresh run was undertaken, and neither placement count nor word count measures engagement. At 200 words/minute, all the recorded dialogue would take roughly 30/12/18/14 minutes to read per chapter, even if a player saw every conditional line. That establishes its compactness, not a playable-time estimate.

There is an existing adverse pacing signal: `chapter_curve.json::_comment_measurement_pacing` records an older model at **2.35 h raw / 4.71 h projected**, against the 3–4 h Meadows target. This is a historical estimate with a crude ×2 factor, not a current measured run. Nevertheless, the plan has no grounds to assume that adding content will cure pacing. The same config says Band 2 wild levels 6–8 are below expected entry level 9, and Band 3's 9–12 wild band meets an expected entry level 12. More low-challenge fights can add minutes while weakening the loop.

At six activities per principal region, the four-chapter requirement means **138 minimum; 230 at ten each** across 23 region groups. If each Water island counts as a major region, it becomes **174–290**. Meadows alone means **30–50**. The acceptance document never resolves that denominator or how substantial one activity must be.

The cost is not writing that many JSON rows. Each worthwhile activity needs a distinct reason to investigate, a visible or audible cue, a traversable approach, an interaction/encounter, a reward with remaining utility, and saved/multiplayer-correct completion. Then it needs daylight/night readability and inclusion in the regional pacing pass. Reusing present systems can make small discoveries cheap; distinctive fights, geography and creature rewards are the expensive work. I would budget this as **dozens of designed content packages and their integration**, not a scatter pass. There is no observed production throughput here that warrants an honest calendar or dollar estimate.

**The density target is plausible as a production goal, unsupported as a description of the current game, and dangerously easy to satisfy numerically without making exploration good.**

## 6. Holes that appear after the easy work

1. **Retention versus replacement has no resolved economy.** Full bond before leaving Meadows, attachment to permanent companions, genuine new temptation in every region and catching through the last hour pull in opposing directions. A late catch may be exciting as a model but expensive as a teammate. Define how it becomes useful without making earlier investment disposable. The fixed 50-win first bond milestone makes this more than a prose concern. [`bond_milestones.json`; Acceptance §5.2.]
2. **The open-world complaint is topological.** Meadows is an 8 km long, 2 km wide authored corridor with sequential barriers. Loops help local freedom but do not remove the progression spine. The river's steepness is explicitly tied to preventing gate bypass. An art lane naturalising its banks can damage progression; a content lane adding side pockets cannot independently solve the whole complaint. [`terrain_playground.json::world_bounds`, `river`, `trail`; `scripts/world/world_perimeter.gd`; archived D50 and D75.]
3. **Care pressure and combat skill may cancel each other.** Better dodging can remove the injury pressure meant to justify camps; increasing unavoidable attrition can make skilled play unrewarding. The new criteria do not say how often a prepared, competent player should need recovery or what care decision feels good. In the unmerged combat branch's corrected Mira sample, reader HP cost is 2.59% versus masher 23.99%, both winning 24/24. That is promising mechanical differentiation, but it is not proof of an expedition economy. [`COMBAT-0919/RESULTS.md`.]
4. **Rewards need sinks and marginal value.** Dozens of pickups do not stay enticing when the player already has sufficient recovery and no production economy to expand. Permanent resource removal plus co-op raises depletion and late-arrival questions. Which resources renew, which rewards are per-player, and what keeps each tier useful must be checked together. [`bands/*/pickups.json`; `scripts/world/harvest_node.gd`; `data/progression/flag_scopes.json`; `scripts/net/`.]
5. **Some nominal exploration is already reduced to bookkeeping.** River Nest's material hand-in and the instant herd-sighting completion illustrate how fulfilling a content label can lose the activity implied by the label. Adding similar rows would hit counts faster than it adds memorable play. [`objectives.json::local` and its explanatory comments.]
6. **The final-five climax does not scale straightforwardly to co-op.** Stormwood reserves its legendary for the first valid nearby stable character; Water has a comparable one-recipient journal. Four players can share a campaign in which only one receives the companion that supposedly forces the emotional climax. The live Bible describes the solo fantasy but omits this outcome. This is a product/fairness question, not merely replication correctness. [`stormwood_ending.gd:5–10`; archived `D-water-guardian-ceremony-ownership.md`.]
7. **The real source seams are more complicated than the technical map.** Water adds namespaced species and separate recipe/encounter adapters. There are 17 scene files, 433 `test_*.gd` and 265 `smoke_*.gd` files in the main tree, not 12 scenes and 248/180 tests as documented. The current save version is 24, not 16. A contributor following the compressed map can edit the wrong catalogue or miss a realm-specific consumer. [`creature_species.gd:27`; `water_species_catalog.gd`; `water_encounter_runtime_data.gd`; `save_game.gd:258`; `git ls-tree` census.]
8. **“Built” is repeatedly confused with “accepted,” in both directions.** Stormwood's Dynamo, legendary release and aftermath are built and mounted by `stormwood_world.gd:147–154`, contrary to State §2.4. Conversely, their existence and focused smokes do not prove the whole earned journey. The continuous coverage report explicitly documents manufactured chapter-entry parties/flags. The later Stormwood prefix proves meaningful earned progress from such a seam, not a fresh four-chapter clear. [`ralph/reports/FOUR-BIOME-BUILD/CONTINUOUS-COVERAGE-GAPS.md`; `stormwood-continuous/REPORT.md`.]
9. **The combat prerequisite is still open.** State calls rungs 1–3 substantially built, which is fair as source status. The newer combat lane discovered zero-poise restaggering, cooldown closing and accumulating movement impulses; its last camera/ring edits lack fresh capture, and its broad two-seed diagnostic reports 38 unmet criteria. Those fixes are not evidence that main is already safe to treat foundational combat as finished. They are unmerged findings with disclosed limitations. [`ralph/combat-depth-0919`, `RESULTS.md`, `HANDOFF.md`.]
10. **Acceptance cannot infer emotion from state changes.** A roster diff does not prove attachment; a forced release does not prove meaningful regret; following a beacon does not prove understanding geography; an automated walker taking a detour does not prove voluntary curiosity. A1–A11 need observed player choices and a small amount of explanation from players. Banning reliance on a survey does not make internal motivation observable from telemetry alone. [Acceptance §§1–2, 5, 10.]
11. **Several standards are contradictory or unmeasurable as written.** “≥1.5:1 value/hue” has no defined hue-ratio metric, sampling region, exposure or distance. “No clipping in any authored pose” across slopes needs a bounded matrix. Every species reachable needs an exception for mutually exclusive starters and runtime aliases. Three vegetation layers in every biome needs a deliberate exception for exposed cliffs and open water. Generic no-placeholder language conflicts with the stand-in policy. [Acceptance §4.3; Bible §§1.3, 2.1.]
12. **No owner exists for integrated play quality or target performance.** All four lanes can meet local criteria while the combined game has poor pacing, unreadable motion, depleted resources, expensive travel and bad frame spikes. Gate F should stay demoted as daily activity; that does not remove the need for milestone integration and independent player/hardware evidence. “No gate waits on a human” and owner-only performance/LAN checks also need a practical evidence owner. [State §§2.5, 3; Acceptance §§7, 10; Technical §14.]

**B for reducing navigation overhead; D for authoritative accuracy and lossless preservation.** The six-document shape is useful. The assertion that every binding decision survived is false.

Not everything old should remain binding. For example, obsolete 4–7-hour prose and old low creature heights should lose to later decisions. **Compression must preserve the winning decision and its dependencies, not preserve whichever old summary is easiest to condense.**

The archive is therefore still load-bearing. Declaring it non-authoritative before verifying migration creates an incentive for future agents to repeat mistakes already settled there. The fix would be a traceable reconciliation inside the existing live documents, not a return to hundreds of competing entry points. This audit makes no such edits.

## 8. Priorities: the four co-equal lanes are the wrong dependency model

**The domains are sensible; “none blocks another” is false.**

Combat decides injury and supply consumption. Those decide camping and the value of rewards. Creature size, animation and camera decide whether combat is readable. Route geometry determines both visual composition and how content is discovered. Shared lighting and materials affect Meadows and Cloudreach. The plan itself acknowledges system-based ownership in Acceptance §4.3, then splits its principal visual lanes by biome in State §3.

I would order the work this way:

1. **Establish a trustworthy source/evidence baseline.** Reconcile the false clock/storage/Stormwood/Water claims and the altered combat contract inside the existing documents. Inspect outstanding branches and integrate only accepted fixes. This is a bounded correction, not another documentation campaign.
2. **Own one representative Meadows expedition as a playable whole.** It must contain an optional lead the player notices, a worthwhile detour, a fight improved by deliberate play, a genuine roster or preparation choice, and a reason to rest or return. Use actual controller play. Decide the combat/attrition/reward relationship before multiplying it across the map.
3. **Run creature/combat presentation and shared world-art work with explicit ownership.** Put the blockers visible in every minute first: readable fighters, coherent creature materials, motion, camera, ground hierarchy and scale. Assign shared pipeline changes to one owner; use Meadows and Cloudreach as regression subjects, not competing implementations.
4. **Expand content by regional deficit once that expedition works.** Count distinct optional experiences, not placements. Couple each batch to reward usefulness, travel time and tested sightlines. Start with weak later Meadows bands, while preserving the deliberately short finale approach.
5. **Give integration, saves/co-op and handheld performance an explicit cross-cutting owner.** Milestone checks, not perpetual Gate F repair. Include chapter handoffs and four-player consequences, not merely a pair of avatars connecting.

Cloudreach can receive independent, bounded environment work while this happens. **It should not have equal claim on scarce judgment, final-asset production or render time while the core Meadows expedition remains unproven.** Polishing the second chapter's grass is especially low-value when the first chapter's creature camera and rewards still determine whether anyone wants the second chapter.

Stormwood and Tidewake need honest maintenance/integration status even while feature expansion is deferred; their code is already part of the game. Ignoring them is not the same as keeping them safely out of scope.

The plan fails if it produces four greener ledgers without proving why a player voluntarily chooses the next encounter or detour. The promising product is a focused creature expedition RPG. Finishing that requires fewer independent polish claims and more ownership of how its decisions fit together.
