## Phase 1 findings

### 1. What the current plan actually produces

A player leaves Grandpa's farmhouse with one named companion, fills a five-creature belt, repeatedly takes direct control of a large creature in short bounded fights, gathers supplies, camps, defeats a succession of named gatekeepers and follows physical routes into four authored chapters. They occasionally replace somebody they have travelled with. Freeing a legendary changes the landscape and asks whether another goodbye is worth it. This is an **authored creature action RPG with expedition preparation and small-scale building**. It is not an open-ended survival sandbox, however large its terrain bounding boxes are.

The player who loves the intended game likes a persistent small party, expressive creatures, readable action, excursions with friends and a campaign they can finish. They compare the relationship fantasy with Pokémon, the preparation/expedition rhythm with Valheim, and visible creatures/traversal/spectacle with Palworld. The player who wants a collection checklist, automation, elaborate bases or an endless server has the wrong product. This is positioning judgment, not demonstrated audience demand.

One good thing: five permanent companions and voluntarily freeing a legendary support the same theme. The problem is that a cap creates loss aversion by itself; it does not create attachment, expressive combat or satisfying rewards.

### 2. The subtraction problem

**The case for it:** removing guns, factory work and creature warehousing creates room for piloting and relationships. Fifty-seven base species are enough to produce choices for five slots. A finite RPG does not need an infinite acquisition economy. A new traversal route, a move that changes how a favourite plays, a useful camp and a transformed landscape can replace the payoff of another stored catch. Light survival avoids punishing players who are here for creatures.

**The case against it:** once the five are comfortable, ordinary catches become threats to investment. Many later species share moves (the eleven electric additions use `spark_bite`/`arc_lash` in `data/creatures/species.json`). The present replacement rewards are much stronger on paper than in play. Short fights, generic loot and routine feeding cannot carry twelve hours merely because they are repeated across different scenery. Removing survival pressure while also leaving camping unnecessary deletes its reason to exist. Adding strain, five menus and arbitrary gates can restore friction without restoring desire.

**My answer:** the hard rules can support a complete game; **the present specification does not yet supply a convincing replacement loop**. I would not sell it as a reduced Palworld. I would build and prove a creature expedition RPG whose repeatable reward is mastery and a stronger relationship with a chosen team. Combat, distinct creature roles, useful detours and a satisfying ending are production gates. Extra geography and additional unproven systems are not substitutes. The new plan is a design bet, not a claim that the bet has already paid off.

### 4. Hard ceilings

The inherited statement that Compatibility has **no real directional shadows is false**. `project.godot` sets directional shadow size2048; `scripts/world/world_look.gd::_apply_sun()` enables shadows and uses two splits. [Godot's renderer comparison](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/rendering/renderers.html) distinguishes ordinary shadows from unsupported directional PCSS, and confirms no SDFGI, volumetric fog or SSR here. The previous Vulkan freeze is a real reason not to casually switch renderers; it is not evidence that all lighting is impossible.

The commercial visual bar is **aspirational on the present assets and evidence**. Every creature being acknowledged as a stand-in while new meshes require owner references creates an external production dependency. Texture polish cannot repair a buried face, missing silhouette, poor topology or unsuitable attack animation. The remedy is a selected final creature style, a three-creature proof at real camera scale, an owner-approved reference/asset pipeline, rig/animation repair, consistent environment families and measured Ally budgets. Existing permissions are narrow named exceptions, not an unspent general Meshy allowance. The bar stays open until evidence passes; this plan does not rename prototype art 'finished'.

Huge bodies magnify camera, arena, animation and terrain-support problems: source heights range1.90–7.20m; `combat.json` still contains metre-sized-creature comments, radius11 arena, body-clearance multiplier2.75 and size-dependent camera pullback. Increasing saturation or draw density cannot fix those geometric conflicts. Camera/arena/attack-volume and asset proportions must be reviewed together.

### 6. Holes that become expensive later

* **Roster identity:** dual Water IDs and aliases can disagree on stats, moves, mounting and rewards. Define semantic identity and migration before adding more content. Repeated electric loadouts make new animals weak rewards.
* **Attachment versus replacement:** three newly discovered landmarks were credited globally; late catches need per-creature visit credit. Fifty wild wins is a tunable historical example, not immutable owner law. Preserving investment must not make every later catch irrational.
* **Starter promise:** Galewisp is promised Fly and Ripplet Teleport; current `fly_traversal.json` capability is Galecrest/loaner. Do not silently substitute an extra permanent creature slot for the starter's promised payoff.
* **Care:** C4's full strain/night system was specified, not implemented. Implementing every multiplier together would penalize weak players twice and produce a rest tax. Need one legible attrition rule, affordable recovery and acceptance of real choices rather than forced harm totals.
* **Combat geometry:** attack data, body spacing, root motion and camera use different size assumptions. Stagger repetition, hitstop/network time and switching resource reset can defeat the economy. Do not accept 'wind built' without full team readers versus mashers and manual feel tests.
* **Economy:** a finite world with permanent resource nodes and shared pickups needs a four-player solvency calculation; a recipe in JSON with no reachable ingredient or grant is not content. Primary-type-only TM compatibility needs an explicit dual-type decision. Reusable shrine relics are not one-shot consumables.
* **Finale:** the Water contract closes its chapter but never specifies the end of the four-chapter product. Eight-force cosmology otherwise sells a half-finished eight-region story. Need a regional resolution, consequences and credits, not an unearned fifth-biome tease.
* **Persistence and authority:** every reward needs world-versus-character ownership. A late joiner cannot be permanently denied a traversal unlock; a unique legendary cannot be cloned by reconnect. Off-screen realm residency, interior/exterior identity, finalized death and rollback are core content dependencies.
* **Audio/accessibility:** functioning generated audio is not a final mix; remote cues are not all spatial, ambience reads Meadows bands and later-chapter identity needs integration. Combat cues need shape/sound, not colour alone. Controller input contexts currently lack the proposed Y skill; coop revive hold3s contradicts literal 'no held buttons'. Resolve that explicitly to tap-start/proximity progress, with no held chord.
* **Performance:** draw-call ceilings did not predict Ally FPS. Loading previously took336s; large grass/tree/creature passes can regress time and memory even if captures improve. Source boot is not exported package proof.
* **Acceptance ambiguity:** 'cares', 'fun', 'looks like Palworld' and a species counter are not mechanical tests. Separate necessary instrumentation from a declared human playtest protocol. A passing screenshot cannot establish motion, a seeded suffix cannot establish earning, and a green docs-only CI cannot certify the game.

### 8. Priority disagreement

Four co-equal lanes will not converge. Two environment-polish lanes can consume the same asset/render budget while combat remains unproven and campaign seams remain broken. Content authored before the reward/creature roles are settled creates rework. The missing lanes are **product proof, character/asset quality, and campaign integration/performance**.

Sequence: (1) reconcile design and source; (2) prove a45–60min Meadows expedition with three distinct creature styles of play, readable fights, useful detour and chosen rest; (3) finish and prove Meadows from fresh save, with honest art/hardware gates; (4) apply the proven contracts to Cloudreach, Stormwood and Tidewake in progression order, allowing independent asset/audio production; (5) earned campaign/co-op/package/hardware acceptance and release. Cloudreach art can work in parallel only within proven shared budgets. Stormwood/Water are integrated foundations requiring completion, not greenfield chapters and not discarded sunk work.

## Phase 2 central decision

**Tetherbound is a 12–16-hour, four-chapter creature expedition action RPG for Windows and ROG Ally, playable alone or with up to three friends. You leave home with a named companion, choose at most five creatures to keep, directly pilot their distinct real-time fighting styles, and prepare small camps for journeys through authored regions. The pleasure is getting better with a team you remember, taking detours that change what that team can do, and physically reopening a world divided by Team Tether. The campaign ends in Tidewake with a regional victory and a homecoming; it does not promise an endless collection, factory economy or eight playable biomes.**

### Hard rule changes I'm requesting

None for the five-owned cap, non-fighting human, real-time piloting, no shields/blocking/held combat inputs, or no-starvation satiety. No blanket new-mesh permission is assumed. The art bar remains a dependency on owner references and accepted production assets where installed assets cannot meet it. Among the lower-level disagreements, the design explicitly replaces three proposals:6–10 activities per region becomes6–10 per chapter; C4's stacked attrition becomes one bounded injury/recovery model; the proposed loud type chart stays at the current1.25/.80 until evidence warrants a retune. These disagreements are documented in their destination specifications. Storefront 'open-world survival' positioning is rejected. The regional Tidewake ending is new authored closure, not a claim that the archive already specified one.
