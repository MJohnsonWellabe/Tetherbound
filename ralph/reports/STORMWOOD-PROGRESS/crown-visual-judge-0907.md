# Stormwood Crown visual judge — 2026-09-07

Blind visual pass of the supplied Compatibility-renderer survey fixture. This is a rendered survey, not a playthrough. It is judged against the supplied Stormheart boards A and B, the visual bible, the Meadows key art, and the Palworld reference frames.

## Frame-specific defects

- **01-entry:** At sheet size, the only legible things are a few oversized pale trunks, near-black ground and identical crimson shrubs. The crown/stronghold has no readable presence. The dead trees and living trees use the same smooth, swollen trunk language, while the even intervals and repeated round crowns read as a placed asset row rather than an electric forest. The low, blurry ground has no grass, root, stone, litter, or path layer to establish scale or a route.
- **02-struck-sentinel:** This is another open field of isolated trunks, not a sentinel or a struck landmark. The one bare tree is small, central, and visually interchangeable with the living trunks around it. Its silhouette does not carry lightning damage, energy, or story. The player is readable only because they sit on an empty patch; they are not framed by a destination or encounter.
- **03-glowmoss:** The enormous cropped foreground trunk blocks the view without creating depth, and the object that should identify the frame as glowmoss is a small yellow-brown cap cut off at the lower right. There is no visible glow, bioluminescent colour, or local lighting response. The red shrub and lime plant repeat the same isolated-scatter grammar from the entry frame.
- **04-conductor:** The small house and freestanding roof frame are cleanly visible but have no visual relationship to a storm conductor, the forest, or a Team Tether occupation. They appear dropped on bare terrain. Their warm tan/white materials are much brighter than the ground and therefore become the only subject, but they do not make the frame feel inhabited: no path, fence, platform, cable, banner, props, creature, or character supports the premise.
- **05-crown-overlook:** This frame contains a hard white rectangular geometry/material failure at the right edge and a distant, flat-topped green landform with a conspicuously straight silhouette. Those artefacts dominate over the nominal overlook. The horizon is empty and hazy rather than layered; no landmark pulls the eye through the frame. This is a release-blocking visual defect in a survey capture.
- **06-deepwood:** The cottage, well-like roof frame, cyan creature, and far gate are all scattered across a bare clearing with equal visual weight. The cyan creature is the only creature shown, but it is too small and too distant to read as a companion or a wildlife encounter; it also has a colder, more saturated material language than the earthy environment. The red shrubs repeat almost one-for-one across the clearing and compete with it as red dots, so the scene reads as a fixture layout rather than a place.
- **07-stormheart-approach:** This is the only frame where the crown is visible. It reads as a pale, skeletal tower with a black spiral wrapped around it, not a colossal lightning-split living tree. The approach offers no scale bridge from the trainer to the crown: no platforms, bridges, roots, carved entrance, banners, lamps, waterfall, core light, or occupied foreground. The pale upper foliage washes into the sky, making the landmark weaker at small size than the neighboring ordinary green trees.
- **08-treebase:** The low-angle close-up reveals the crown's strongest defect. The black spiral is a smooth, uniform tube with no believable material, supports, attachment logic, or destination; it reads as debug/course geometry. The pale vertical trunks behind it are thin and disconnected from the dense living canopy. The central blue drops form a weak dotted line rather than the bright, branching lightning core called for by board B. There is no warm practical light, electric-root detail, platform language, or Team Tether fabric/metal grammar.

## Rubric findings

**Silhouette and small-size readability:** No. At contact-sheet size, the crown disappears in 01–06 and is only a pale vertical mass in 07–08. Trees are readable as trees, but their shared bulbous crowns and pale twisted trunks collapse into one repeated silhouette family. The trainer and cyan creature in 06 are identifiable only because the clearing is empty.

**Colour and value structure:** No. The frames share a green/brown/blue daylight palette, but almost all terrain is a dark, low-detail midtone while tree foliage is a similar dark green. Crimson shrubs are the most saturated recurring accent, yet do not communicate danger, storm energy, or ecology. The Stormheart boards reserve bright blue and gold for an unmistakable core; 07–08 supply only faint cyan marks, without enough contrast to command the scene.

**Intentionality:** No. Repeated tree meshes, round shrub clumps, red shrub spacing, and isolated lime plants produce a regular scatter-field look throughout 01–07. Frame 04's two isolated structures and 06's evenly separated landmarks add to the fixture impression. There are no composed clearings, route edges, habitat clusters, or focal arrangements that make the woodland feel authored.

**Lighting:** No. The sky is readable and the tree shadows ground the near objects, but shadowed terrain goes muddy and textureless. No frame communicates electric illumination, a core glow, warm lantern contrast, or a time-of-day event appropriate to the Stormheart. The stated Compatibility-renderer limit may temper fine-shadow claims, but it does not explain the missing focal light and value hierarchy.

**Horizon and depth:** No. 01–06 repeatedly open onto a near-flat horizon with no distant forest mass, cliffs, stronghold, or atmospheric separation. Frame 07 finally points toward the crown, but its silhouette is too porous and pale to hold against the sky. Frame 05 has an obvious distant geometry seam/plateau and a white object failure.

**Interface:** Yes. The sparse status label at upper left stays within the safe area and does not hide the scene. It is too small to establish much hierarchy, but it is not the visual problem in these frames.

**Artefacts:** No. Frame 05 has a bright white rectangular failure at the right edge and a hard, artificial distant plateau boundary. Frame 08's uniform black coil reads as unintended/unfinished geometry; frame 07 shows the same issue at distance. Several foreground trunks in 01 and 03 are cropped so tightly that they look like accidental capture obstruction rather than deliberate framing.

**Scale agreement:** No. Individual large trees plausibly exceed the 1.80 m trainer, but their very thick, smooth trunks carry small, near-spherical crowns, giving them a mushroom/lollipop proportion rather than a massive old forest form. In 07–08 the proposed regional landmark does not dominate the nearby trees by enough to read as a monumental stronghold; it needs a clearer height, width, and platform-scale hierarchy. The creature in 06 is too distant for a precise size judgment, which is itself a framing failure for a creature-adventure survey.

## The three largest gaps to the references

1. **The Stormheart has no landmark identity (07, 08).** Boards A and B depict a colossal, broad, lightning-split living tree whose branching crown, interior core, platforms, bridges, banners, lamps, and waterfalls make it recognizable from forest distance. These frames show narrow pale trunks, a featureless black helix, and a faint cyan dotted line. This is partly scene work (composition, contrast, platform/route dressing, lighting, approach framing) and partly absent art/geometry (a convincing split trunk, branching silhouette, core energy, and material detail).
2. **The forest is an empty scatter field (01–07).** The key art and Palworld references layer grasses, shrubs, rocks, roots, paths, creatures, and distinct foreground/midground/distance masses. These frames expose a repetitive tree-and-red-shrub grid on a low-frequency ground texture. Density, clustering, route shaping, foreground dressing, and landmark framing are scene fixes; richer tree canopy/branch structure, ground-cover assets, and a coherent creature material language need art that is not visible here.
3. **There is no creature-adventure event in view (01–08; especially 06).** Palworld's frames make creatures expressive, large enough to read, and embedded in interaction or combat. The survey has one small cyan creature in 06; its silhouette and role cannot be read, and every other frame is creatureless. Adding placed creatures, encounter staging, companions, and readable activity is scene/content work. Creature assets with distinctive Stormwood silhouettes and materials will also be needed to reach the reference bar.

## Bar questions

**A. Do these frames read as belonging to the world in `tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png` — the project's own art direction? — NO.** The blue sky and stylised tree treatment point toward the same broad genre, but the key art promises lush layered ground, route composition, mature oak silhouettes, inhabited scenes, and landmark-led views. The surveyed crown forest is sparse, flat, repetitive, and almost entirely eventless. Scene changes can fix much of this: dense ground layers, authored clusters and clearings, paths, roots/rocks/props, creatures, a stronger approach view, and electric/warm focal lighting. A credible Stormheart silhouette, true old-growth tree forms, a strong core effect, and appealing readable Stormwood creatures require art not presently visible in the build.

**B. Shown beside `palworld-0*.jpg`, would someone say these are trying to be the same kind of game? — NO.** The frames use a third-person stylised outdoor vocabulary, but the commercial bar shows full, layered locations, strong creature scale and appeal, visible encounters, varied terrain, and landmark depth. These shots look like an early environment fixture with a player placed in it. Density, composition, activity, encounter staging, landmark contrast, and removal of visible artefacts are scene fixes. Bespoke or substantially improved tree, crown, ground-detail, and creature art is required for the remaining gap in material richness and character appeal.
