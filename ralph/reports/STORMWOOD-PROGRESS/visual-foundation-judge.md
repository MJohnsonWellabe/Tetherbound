# Stormwood foundation — blind visual judge

**Evidence reviewed:** `_sheet.png` and `01-entry.png` through `08-treebase.png`; owner Stormheart boards A and B; the five supplied Palworld comparison screenshots. Renderer stated for this capture: native Forward+ Vulkan on an NVIDIA GTX 1060. No implementation, source, or change history was inspected.

## Read of the contact sheet

At sheet size the images read as one night forest, but not as eight useful views of a region. A very large, close brown boot blocks roughly a quarter to half of seven frames. It pulls the eye away from every intended subject and makes the capture resemble a broken camera placement rather than third-person gameplay. `08-treebase` avoids that particular failure and is the only frame that offers a composed upward view.

The forest has a limited but legible silhouette language: pale, twisted trunks stand below very dark, round canopy clumps, with occasional red foliage and red shrubs. At small size the trunks still read as trees, and the red canopy in `08-treebase` gives a momentary crown focal point. The repeated lollipop-like dark canopy masses, however, make the wood read as a sparse asset scatter. There is no visible landmark tree, lightning scar, hollow trunk, platform, bridge, banner, lantern, crystal, or electrical event. Therefore neither the named Stormheart nor a route toward it can be read from this evidence.

Ground is a nearly uniform brown-green blur, with isolated bright green plants, pale mushrooms, and red shrubs sitting on it. Those accents do not form path edges, clusters, or a route hierarchy. `02-struck-sentinel` and `05-crown-overlook` have broad open ground but no path silhouette or mid-ground destination; `04-conductor` has the strongest tree rhythm but still reads as a repeated row. The distant teal land in `05-crown-overlook` is a flat band rather than atmospheric depth.

The palette is too compressed and too dark for the supplied boards. The boards use rich living greens, warm timber and lantern pools, stone, blue electric light, and a high-value sky to separate layers. These frames instead reserve almost all readable value for the boot, with dark maroon foliage against a blue-black sky. The single white moon in `08-treebase` is brighter and more legible than anything in the supposed Stormwood scene. No storm-blue or gold energy establishes the biome's electric identity.

Material readability is weak. The ground texture repeats as a soft, low-contrast smear; foliage becomes black masses; the pale trunks have a usable carved/twisted form but lack enough light separation to hold against the canopy. The boot is detailed enough to dominate every view, which magnifies the scale problem. With no full player body, creature, platform, or landmark visible, there is no reliable 1.80 m ruler and no meaningful scale hierarchy for the world. `08-treebase` suggests the central red crown may be larger than surrounding trees, but it is still a compact canopy cluster, not a colossal tree that could contain the board's bridges and ascent.

## Three things that most separate these frames from the references

1. **No Stormheart landmark or electric event — `07-stormheart-approach`, `05-crown-overlook`, `08-treebase`.** Boards A and B make a lightning-split, colossal tree the scene's immediate and distant organizing silhouette, with a bright blue/gold core and visible built platforms. The approach frame shows only ordinary trees and low groundcover; the crown frame does not establish a trunk, split, core, or construction. This is the largest gap because the region's destination is absent, not merely underlit.

2. **Broken gameplay framing and route readability — `01-entry` through `07-stormheart-approach`.** The Palworld frames keep the player, creature, encounter, path, and terrain readable together. Here the foreground boot obscures the play space, while paths do not separate from the terrain and no mid-ground objective carries the eye forward. The scenes need a clear gameplay camera composition, a visible route, and a subject that is not occluded by the player model.

3. **Dark, sparse, repeated forest instead of layered electric woodland — `02-struck-sentinel`, `03-glowmoss`, `04-conductor`, `06-deepwood`.** The boards use canopy depth, vines/moss, foreground plants, warm built materials, and blue energy to make the forest feel living and inhabited. The frames offer repeated dark canopy blobs over a nearly flat ground texture with a few widely spaced accent plants. The missing layering, lighting/value structure, and ecological clustering make this read as a nighttime test field rather than a designed Stormwood.

## Top addressable defects

1. **The near-camera boot consumes the frame.** It dominates `01-entry`, `02-struck-sentinel`, `03-glowmoss`, `04-conductor`, `05-crown-overlook`, `06-deepwood`, and `07-stormheart-approach`. Correct the gameplay/capture camera collision or player-model visibility so the foreground shows route, vegetation, and an on-screen player silhouette instead of a close-up boot.

2. **The intended landmark never appears.** `07-stormheart-approach` is the clearest named failure: its distant view contains no distinguishable Stormheart. Establish a large split-trunk silhouette above the normal canopy, make its blue/gold electrical core visible through the forest, and place readable approach architecture or route cues at the base. `08-treebase` can then use its upward composition to show the actual split and internal scale rather than an ordinary red crown.

3. **Night lighting hides the material and palette hierarchy.** `03-glowmoss` and `06-deepwood` have no visible glowmoss or deep-forest lighting event despite their names. Lift local value with blue electrical spill and selective warm practical pools, retain dark canopy negative space, and use those pools to reveal bark, moss, path surface, and foreground-to-distance separation. The goal is not blanket brightness: it is a readable hierarchy where electric light identifies Stormwood.

## Required bars

### Internal visual bible fit: **No**

The frames share a forest palette and have a small amount of twisted-trunk silhouette character, but they do not meet the bible's required lush layering, composed foreground/mid-ground/distance structure, landmark readability, path hierarchy, or inhabited-world presentation. The Stormheart boards are especially decisive: the defining landmark, constructed ascent, and blue/gold electric identity are not visible. The camera occlusion also prevents the frame set from demonstrating player readability or scale.

### External gameplay screenshot quality: **No**

Beside the Palworld screenshots, these do not look like the same level of finished creature-adventure gameplay. Palworld's frames show readable player and creature silhouettes, a clear event or destination, dense layered terrain, usable value range, and an image composition that explains what to do. This set contains no creature, encounter, visible player, path, landmark, or stronghold activity, and its near-black foliage/ground leaves little readable gameplay space. The principal gaps are scene and presentation work: camera framing, landmark staging, path and vegetation composition, and lighting/material separation. The Stormheart's detailed tree architecture, platform kit, banners, electric roots, and crystal/core language also require visible art in the build; their absence cannot be solved by exposure alone.
