## Bar questions

**A. Do these frames read as belonging to the world in `tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`? No.** They share a broad natural-green / blue-sky palette, but not the key art's layered, sunny, inhabited adventure world. At sheet size, the frames become the same brown-green ground plane and repeated tree silhouettes. There is no route, creature, landmark, or mid-distance subject to carry an adventure scene.

**B. Beside `palworld-0*.jpg`, would someone say these are trying to be the same kind of game? No.** The references are dense, lit playable spaces with a distinct activity, creature scale hierarchy, and a clear focal event. These frames present a sparse test grove. The player is visible, but the world gives them nothing readable to approach or interact with.

## The three biggest gaps to the references

1. **The promised Stormheart landmark is absent from every gameplay-facing view.** `01-entry`, `02-struck-sentinel`, `04-conductor`, `05-crown-overlook`, `06-deepwood`, and `07-stormheart-approach` all frame only ordinary trees on a shallow rise. The Stormheart boards use one colossal, split, electrically alive tree as the region's instant silhouette, with visible platforms, bridges, banners, energy, and vertical destination. The pale narrow form in the far centre of `07-stormheart-approach` does not survive at small size or read as a destination. This is primarily a **scene/composition failure**: the landmark must be visible, framed, and supplied with an approach sightline. If the intended tree is present but cannot distinguish itself from the surrounding trees at this distance, its silhouette and landmark kit are also an **asset limitation**.

2. **The forest reads as an evenly scattered asset field, rather than an authored electric woodland.** `02-struck-sentinel` has large, regular gaps between near-identical trees; `04-conductor` and `06-deepwood` repeat the same trunk-to-puffball form across the horizon. The red shrubs, lime ferns, pale mushroom stacks, and occasional rocks occur as small isolated tokens on a continuous blurry ground texture. The boards and Palworld references use overlapping vegetation layers, clustered species, route edges, roots, rocks, and foreground occluders to make spaces feel navigable and lived in. Clustering, a readable path, ground-layer transitions, and intentional clearings are **scene fixes**. The trees' very similar swollen bases, sparse branch structure, and rounded canopy language remain an **asset limitation**; scale alone will not make them carry the board's ancient-oak silhouette.

3. **Value, composition, and visual hierarchy do not establish a playable event.** In `01-entry`, `04-conductor`, `06-deepwood`, and `07-stormheart-approach`, the dark trunks and near-black foliage sit against a uniformly blue sky while the terrain remains a low-contrast brown-green smear. The focal point is usually the trainer's small silhouette or a single foreground trunk. `08-treebase` is the clearest individual image but frames a generic trunk and a blown-out moon, not the tree's power, scale, or place in the world. The references reserve high-contrast light, saturated energy, warm lamps, and creature/character silhouettes for a clearly legible destination or encounter. Lighting placement, value separation, terrain material breakup, framing, and real destination dressing are **scene/render fixes**. A convincing Stormheart also needs an **asset-level focal structure**: the current ordinary-tree material and shape cannot by themselves represent the split, inhabited, electrically charged stronghold shown on both boards.

## Scene work versus asset limits

The immediate, addressable scene work is to make one approach view read in a second: frame the Stormheart against sky from farther back; create a route into it; cluster the surrounding forest into foreground, mid-ground, and distant masses; and place visible electric, architectural, and inhabited cues around the destination. Replace the continuous empty ground between trees with designed ground-cover transitions and set pieces that guide the eye.

The ceiling visible in this capture is the ordinary tree family. Its repeated bulbous trunks, rounded canopy clumps, and dark, nearly identical materials do not have the distinctive split trunk, exposed inner core, platforms, bridges, or electrical silhouette required by the Stormheart boards. Those cannot be established through scatter and camera placement alone. No creature, combatant, or encounter object appears in these frames either, so the creature-adventure bar cannot be demonstrated by this set.
