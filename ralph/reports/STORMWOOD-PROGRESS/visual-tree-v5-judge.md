# Stormwood foundation tree v5 — blind visual judge

**Evidence viewed:** `shots/stormwood-foundation-tree-v5/_sheet.png` and all eight
individual frames, against `docs/VISUAL_BIBLE.md` and the two owner Stormheart Tree
boards dated 2026-09-06.  The capture log header identifies **Vulkan 1.3.280,
Forward+, NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1060 3GB**.  The generic contact-sheet footer is stale
and was not treated as evidence of a Compatibility/software capture.

This is an incomplete foundation, but the owner boards remain the bar.

## What the frames show

### Silhouette, scale, and landmark readability

- In `01-entry`, `02-struck-sentinel`, `03-glowmoss`, `05-crown-overlook`, and
  `06-deepwood`, the woodland still reduces at small size to a repeated line of the
  same lollipop tree: a vertically striped trunk, a rounded green blob, and occasional
  dead variant.  There are no readable groves, root masses, layered limbs, or distinct
  canopy silhouettes to make Stormwood feel electrically old or dangerous.
- The object presented as the Stormheart in `07-stormheart-approach` is a thin,
  straight tower wrapped in a black coil.  It is smaller in visual authority than the
  nearby ordinary trees and does not read as a lightning-split colossal tree, a built
  stronghold, or a regional landmark.  `08-treebase` confirms the same problem from
  below: it reads as a dark spiral frame with a dotted cyan line, with the moon and
  foreground canopy doing more compositional work than the target.
- The trainer provides a clear ruler in frames 01–07. Ordinary trunks are broadly
  plausible beside them, but the supposed region-defining tree has no trunk/crown mass
  or scale relationship that distinguishes it from scenery.  The player cannot infer
  a climbable interior or an ascent from these silhouettes.

### Colour, value, atmosphere, and lighting

- The boards use bright blue-and-gold electrical energy as a focused beacon inside warm
  bark, moss, built timber, and a deep but legible forest.  `07-stormheart-approach`
  offers only a thin pale-blue dotted stripe; `08-treebase` loses even the supporting
  trunk/platform structure into nearly black shapes.  The energy neither illuminates
  nearby form nor communicates a charged core.
- Across the entire sheet, the blue gradient sky and very dark green canopy repeat
  while the ground stays a brown-green, low-detail midtone.  It reads as one flat,
  lightly populated field under a clean sky, rather than the layered electric forest
  called for by the boards.  Red shrubs and neon green ground plants are isolated,
  repeated accents, not a controlled Stormwood palette or ecological pattern.
- `04-conductor` and `06-deepwood` introduce a warm cottage and a well-like timber
  frame. Their familiar pastoral materials pull the scene toward an ordinary meadow
  settlement. They do not establish Team Tether occupation, engineered platforms, or
  the dark timber/aged metal/electric-root language on the boards.

### Composition, density, and intentionality

- The foreground/midground/distance stack is absent in `01-entry`, `02-struck-sentinel`,
  `03-glowmoss`, and `05-crown-overlook`: sparse trees and scattered identical red
  shrubs sit on an open, texture-blurred plane with a hard empty horizon. The eye has
  no route, encounter, landmark, or designed clearing to move toward.
- `04-conductor` and `06-deepwood` frame the trainer between a cottage and an isolated
  prop, yet neither is embedded in a path, platform, root system, or inhabited pocket.
  This reads as placement of unrelated assets, rather than authored stronghold approach
  dressing.
- `07-stormheart-approach` is the one frame that aims the player at a subject, but the
  symmetrical flower/shrub scatter creates a garden aisle to a small tower instead of a
  dense forest opening onto an enormous, occupied tree.  Add root buttresses, layered
  approach vegetation/rock, visible walkways/platforms, and a landmark large enough to
  dominate the midground.

### Ground, materials, artefacts, and interface

- Every ground-heavy frame has a smeared, repeating brown-green terrain texture with
  almost no grass, leaf litter, roots, stones, moss, or terrain breakup. It fails the
  visual bible's layered ground coverage requirement and makes the trees appear planted
  on a flat test field. The pale stepping stones visible in `01-entry` and `07-stormheart-approach`
  are too sparse and disconnected to read as a path.
- The hard horizontal terrain/sky edge in `01-entry`, `02-struck-sentinel`,
  `03-glowmoss`, `05-crown-overlook`, and `06-deepwood` exposes the scene's finite,
  empty construction. A distant tree mass, terrain relief, fogged depth, or forest wall
  is needed before these can function as outward-facing world views.
- `03-glowmoss` has a large dark, flat-sided foreground obstruction at right and a
  cropped mushroom at bottom right; both look like camera collision/placement artefacts.
  `08-treebase` has similarly unreadable black coil geometry against the sky. These
  prevent clean inspection of the intended subject.
- No interface is visible. That is acceptable for an environment capture, but these
  frames provide no player-facing interaction cue for the tree, core, or climb route.

## The three things that most separate these frames from the references

1. **The Stormheart landmark has not arrived visually.** Board A's calm exterior and
   Board B's charged exterior show a colossal, split, living tree with a beacon-like
   core, broad roots, platforms, bridges, banners, and a visible ascent. In
   `07-stormheart-approach` and `08-treebase`, the feature is a small dark spiral/tower
   with a thin cyan dotted line. Build the landmark's tree mass and readable lower
   approach before tuning surrounding forest dressing.
2. **The environment is a sparse meadow test field rather than a layered Stormwood.**
   The boards and visual bible use foreground roots/foliage, dense varied midground,
   deep forest masses, moss, rock, water, and vertical structure. `01-entry` through
   `06-deepwood` show repeated trees, isolated shrubs, an empty horizon, and blurred
   ground. This is primarily scene work: compose groves and clearings, add ground and
   terrain layers, establish a forest wall and depth, and make the route legible.
3. **The material and prop language does not tell the intended story.** The references
   combine warm living wood, dark bark, aged metal, moss, cloth, deliberate lamps, and
   blue/gold electricity. `04-conductor` and `06-deepwood` instead read as a generic
   cottage-and-well settlement, while `08-treebase` is black-on-blue and loses form.
   Purpose-built/appropriate landmark art and a coherent electric-stronghold material
   pass are required; scene placement alone cannot make the current core read as the
   board's Stormheart.

## Bar questions

**A. Do these frames read as belonging to the world in
`docs/reference/tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png` — the project's own art direction?**
**No.** The stylised tree-and-trainer baseline is compatible in broad category, but the
flat ground, empty horizon, repeated canopy forms, and thin, unlit electric feature do
not deliver the lush, layered, composed, inhabited creature-adventure world. Scene work
can fix density, ground layers, terrain depth, grove composition, and landmark framing.
The Stormheart's missing colossal split-tree silhouette, climb architecture, and unified
electric stronghold material language need art absent from these frames as well as scene
work.

**B. Shown beside `docs/reference/palworld-0*.jpg`, would someone say these are trying to
be the same kind of game?** **No.** The frames lack the foliage/ground density, layered
distance, authored focal point, life, and strong encounter-scale landmark needed for that
shipping-quality creature-adventure reading. Vegetation clustering, terrain breakup,
route composition, lighting/value separation, and purposeful prop placement are fixable
in scene. The present generic tree forms and the inadequate Stormheart feature require
new or substantially different art to reach the board's silhouette and material bar.
