You are a code-blind visual judge. You see ONLY the images in this folder,
sheet-fight-M.jpg and sheet-fight-N.jpg (labelled contact sheets; each tile is a
1280x720 production frame downscaled). Full-size tiles, if you need detail, are
in ../frames/<letter>/ with the same neutral names. Do not read any other file.

Each sheet is one real-time creature fight in a game, from the game's normal
fight camera. The player pilots a small companion creature (the one with the
pilot card in the HUD) against a larger wild opponent. The HUD is the real game
HUD (a TEAM list and pilot card at the left, an action panel at the right, the
opponent's card at the top). Frame labels:
  - iNN : one-second interval frames
  - tellN-a-start / b-mid / c-late : the opponent's attack warning has just
    begun / is halfway / is about to release
  - tellN-d-strike / e-impact / f-recovery : release / ~0.2 s after / recovery
  - 00-before / 99-after : before engaging / after the fight

The bar (combat readability, per fight): "All meaningful tells >= 0.8 s,
heavy >= 1.1 s. Framing/readability passes at actual creature scale." Each
fight must show, at the normal fight camera, a distinct readable tell and a
real hit or avoidance.

For EACH fight M-N answer, citing frame names, PASS or FAIL plus one or two
concrete sentences:
 (a) Is the opponent's attack warning clearly visible and distinguishable
     before it lands?
 (b) Can you see where it will hit and where to move to avoid it?
 (c) Are BOTH creatures readable at their actual scale in the normal frames:
     not hidden under the HUD panels, not cropped, not with the camera inside
     either body? (Brief occlusion in one frame is a note, not a FAIL; a
     creature repeatedly hidden under the HUD, or a frame of camera-inside-
     body geometry at a tell or strike, is a FAIL.)
 (d) Is there a visible hit or a visible avoidance?
Then an overall line per fight: C3 PASS or C3 FAIL. Then list up to three
remaining LOOK defects across both, each naming a frame. Score nothing else
(scenery, creature identity and art style are out of scope).
