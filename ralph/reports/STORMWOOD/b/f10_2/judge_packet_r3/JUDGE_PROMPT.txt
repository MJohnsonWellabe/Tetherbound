You are a code-blind visual judge. You see ONLY the images in this folder:
sheet-fight-X.jpg and sheet-fight-Y.jpg (each is a labelled contact sheet;
each tile is a 1280x720 production frame downscaled). If the operator gives
you full-size copies of individual tiles, they carry the same neutral names.
Do not read any other file in the repository.

Each sheet is one real-time creature fight in a game, captured from the
game's normal fight camera. The player pilots a small companion creature
(the one near the bottom-left of the frame, with the HUD card) against a
larger wild opponent. The on-screen HUD is the real game HUD. Frame labels:
  - iNN            : fixed one-second interval frames
  - tellN-a-start  : the opponent's attack warning (tell) has just begun
  - tellN-b-mid    : halfway through that warning
  - tellN-c-late   : late in that warning, just before the attack
  - tellN-d-strike : the attack is released
  - tellN-e-impact : about 0.2 s after release
  - tellN-f-recovery: the opponent's recovery after the attack
  - 00-before / 99-after : before engaging / after the fight ends

The bar these fights must pass:
  "All meaningful tells >= 0.8 s, heavy >= 1.1 s. Framing/readability passes
   at actual creature scale." Each fight must show, at the normal fight
   camera, a distinct readable tell and a real hit or avoidance.

Intended visible identity of each fight (one line each; shuffled, do not
guess which is which from this order):
  - A diving alpha: "a visible route line shown for about a second BEFORE the
    strike warning begins, then the strike warning, then a dive along that route."
  - A crown guardian: "Crown glass and a stationary frontal guard stance: the
    area in front of it that its blow covers is shown while it winds up."

For EACH fight X and Y answer, citing frame names:
