# Code-blind detour-lure judgement: storm-forest road, views V to Z

I judged these from the five frames only. The question for each: "Would an ordinary player walking this road notice a side place worth a detour here?"

## View V: PROBABLY
- **What draws the eye:** On the right, at middle distance, two warm iron lanterns hang from a wooden post beside a gnarled grey tree. A tall, pale yellow-green column of light rises behind the lanterns. It is the only vertical light source in the frame.
- **Detour or road:** It mostly reads as a spot beside the road. A thin pale stone or water edge runs right from the road toward the lanterns, which hints at a little pocket. But the post stands close to the road's shoulder, so it could also be read as a road lamp.
- **What hides or competes:** The glowing gold line down the middle of the road is brighter and runs straight to the horizon, so it pulls the eye forward, past the lanterns. The green column is flat and looks like an effect. It could be read as a glitch or a quest beam rather than a place.

## View W: YES
- **What draws the eye:** On the right, near to middle distance, a large hollow-looking gnarled tree fills the right third of the frame. It has a bright gold shaft of light in its trunk and two lanterns hanging from its branches. A ring of white stones at its base sets out a small plot.
- **Detour or road:** It reads as a place off the road. The stone edging leaves the road on the right and wraps the tree, so it looks like a threshold you step off the road to reach. The road carries on past it to a small blue marker far ahead.
- **What hides or competes:** Three bright gold streaks in the road near the camera are the brightest thing in the frame and pull the eye down and forward. A pale boulder far off on the left is a small secondary distraction. Otherwise the lure is well framed.

## View X: AMBIGUOUS
- **What draws the eye:** Ahead-right, far away, a gallows-style lantern post with two lanterns glows strongly next to a bare dark tree, with a soft light shaft behind it. Far off on the left, small teal crystal spikes stand on the horizon.
- **Detour or road:** The lantern post stands on the road's right shoulder and the road runs straight toward it, so it reads as a lamp marking where the road goes, not a separate place. The low stone wall running right along the hill slightly suggests a side area, but it is not clear. The teal crystals on the left are the only real "off to the side" hint, and they are tiny.
- **What hides or competes:** The gold crack in the road near the camera is the brightest element in the frame. The camera looks slightly downhill and the lure is small and far off in the upper right. The teal crystals are too small and low-contrast to register at normal play distance.

## View Y: YES
- **What draws the eye:** Several things.
  1. On the left, at middle distance, a tree with a gold light shaft and two lanterns on a post. A white cobbled path clearly branches left off the road toward it.
  2. On the right, near, an ornate dark pylon with a glowing cyan crystal top and a rust-coloured cable running off to the right.
  3. Far ahead, a red tent-like structure and blue shapes that look like a camp.
- **Detour or road:** The left lanterns read clearly as a detour, because the cobbled spur leaves the road at a visible junction. The right pylon also reads as a roadside object you could walk up to. The far camp is where the road goes.
- **What hides or competes:** There are three lures at once. The right pylon is closer and higher-contrast than the left lanterns, so it may win the glance. The road's gold streaks are bright again. The frame is busy but readable.

## View Z: AMBIGUOUS
- **What draws the eye:** Straight ahead, near to middle distance, two dark trees frame a gateway. Two cyan lanterns hang in it and a strong cyan beam of light rises between the trees. On the right, at middle distance, there is a smaller cyan-topped pylon with a cable, a pale boulder, and at the far right edge a violet crystal cluster.
- **Detour or road:** The main lure is where the road itself goes. The road runs straight into the lit gateway over a pale stone threshold, so it reads as the route's destination, not a side place. The pylon, boulder and violet crystals on the right are true side interest, but they are dim, small and pushed to the edge of the frame.
- **What hides or competes:** The central gateway dominates the frame and takes attention away from the side dressing on the right. The dark tree canopy on the right hides that area.

## Across all five views
- Every lure uses the same idea: a pair of hanging lanterns plus a vertical light shaft. After two or three of these, a player may stop treating them as special. They also look like road lamps whenever they sit right on the road's shoulder (V and X).
- The glowing gold cracks in the road are the brightest thing in almost every frame. They pull the eye forward along the road and away from anything to the side.
- The frames that clearly read as detours (W and Y) show a visible ground-level cue, a stone ring or a cobbled spur, leaving the road. Where that cue is missing or faint (V, X, Z), the lure reads as road furniture or as the road's destination.

Tally: 2 of 5 YES (V PROBABLY, X and Z AMBIGUOUS)

Overall: The lures can be seen but look too much alike, and the glowing road outshines them. They only read as a detour where a stone path or ring visibly leaves the road.
