# Judge: lightning ground-warning sequence I (t000–t170)

I judged these pictures only (19 frames). Scale: the trainer is about 175 px tall at foot depth (y≈355–530), so about 97 px per metre at that depth.

**Q1. Danger zone: PASS.**
From t000 there is a bright magenta ring with a darker magenta fill on the dirt path. The trainer stands inside it, toward the far rim. At the trainer's foot depth the ring measures about 640 px across (x≈320–960), which is about 6.5 m. That makes the radius about 3–3.5 m, which matches the 3 m intent. The ring is the most saturated colour in the lower half of the screen, and neither the grass nor the path hides it. Small rim tick marks appear at t000–t020 and turn into crack lines, which helps the zone read as ground that is about to break.

**Q2. Time left: PASS, with a caveat.**
Progress reads clearly. At t000–t020 the cracks are short ticks at the rim. From t030 to t080 they grow inward, and by t090 they reach the trainer's feet as a full web. At t100 the fill turns a pale, bright pink. At t110 the rim and cracks are near-white. The cracks meeting at t090 is a good "full, time's up" cue. Caveat: the strongest "last chance" change, the fill brightening, only comes at t100, 0.2 s before impact, which is too late to act on. Between t040 and t080 each step is subtle (the cracks just get a little longer). The cracks work as a meter, but they don't show a fraction of time left the way a closing ring would.

**Q3. Impact reads as lightning striking the zone: PASS.**
At t120–t130 a jagged white bolt with side branches comes down from the top of the frame and lands exactly on the trainer's feet, where the cracks meet. Small arcs spark around the trainer, the trainer is tinted red and pink, and the ring and cracks turn white-hot. It clearly reads as lightning hitting this spot. At t140 the ring and cracks fade to faint lines, and by t150 nothing is left. There is no scorch mark or afterglow, so the "it hit here" moment is gone almost at once. That is minor, but a short scorch would help.

**Q5. Competition: FAIL.**
Three large, bright, jagged white lightning bolts stay frozen in the sky in every frame from t000 to t170. They look identical in every frame, including after the strike:
1. A big bolt in the top centre-right (x≈700–990, y≈0–300) whose lower branch reaches down toward the pylon/lamp structure on the right.
2. A bolt at the left edge (x≈0–30, y≈170–220), plus fainter horizontal forks at x≈120–430, y≈50–230 behind the trees.
3. A bolt at the right edge (x≈975–1010, y≈230–260).

These use the same white-core, violet-glow style as the real strike and are brighter than the warning ring. Throughout t000–t110 they look like lightning that is already hitting somewhere else. The big one points down at the glowing pylon, which suggests a second strike location. At t120 the real bolt starts from the same patch of sky and crosses the frozen bolt (around x≈700, y≈90), so the two form one tangle. The real strike stands out only because it is the one that reaches the ground.

Smaller distractions: steady yellow-orange glow streaks beside the trainer's hands and on the path (x≈580–720, y≈440–470), and a warm fire-like glow on the far path behind the trainer (x≈580–610, y≈395–410). Neither looks like a strike.

Is the struck area the same as the warned zone? Yes. The bolt lands inside the ring, at the point where the cracks meet (t120–t130). That point sits slightly toward the far rim of the ring's visual centre, but it is clearly inside the same zone.

**Q6. Photosensitivity: PASS.**
There is no full-screen flash, white-out or strobe in any frame. The brightest changes stay small: the pale fill at t100–t110, and the bolt with its white ring at t120–t130. The overall scene brightness hardly changes. The sky bolts are frozen and never flicker in these frames (sampled every 5–10 ms).

**Overall: PARTIAL.**
The zone, its size, the progress and the impact all read well. It fails the "nothing else competes" test because large, frozen, ground-reaching lightning bolts fill the sky for the whole warning. The single most useful change: remove the persistent jagged sky bolts during a ground warning, or replace them with brief, diffuse flashes inside the clouds, so the only jagged lightning on screen is the one that lands in the warned circle.
