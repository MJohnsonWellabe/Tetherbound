# Lightning ground warning, Reduced Motion ON: visual judgement (judged from pictures only)

I opened all 19 frames in order, t000 to t170.

**Q1. Zone and size: PASS**
At t000 there is a solid magenta ring on the path, and all of it is in frame. Short notch marks sit on its rim. The trainer stands inside it. Measured against the trainer's height (1.80 m), the ring is about 6.5–7 m across, which is close to the intended 3 m radius. Magenta is easy to tell apart from the purple sky, the green grass, the brown dirt and the red rope. It does not use the oxblood colour. The ring stays the same size and position through t110, so the zone never looks as if it moves.

**Q2. Time left: PASS (with caveats)**
The warning changes in clear stages:
- **t000:** only the rim notches show.
- **t010–t050:** the notches grow into forked cracks that run inward.
- **t060–t090:** the cracks join and meet at the centre, at the trainer's feet.
- **t100:** the inside of the ring turns pale pink.
- **t110:** the inside turns near-white lavender and the rim turns white-pink.

This reads as a burning fuse followed by a final "about to fire" state. The forked shape also hints at lightning before the strike lands.

Caveats:
- The cracks are thin lines, 2–3 px, and the heavy rain streaks partly hide them.
- Between t050 and t090 each step is small, so reading the time left at a glance is rough.
- The point where the cracks meet is hidden behind the trainer's legs (t070–t110), so the fuse's end is covered by the player's own character.
- The strong "last moment" cue (the fill lightening) only appears in the last 0.2 s.

**Q3. Impact: PASS**

- **t125–t130:** the bolt holds the same shape and stays readable.
- **t140:** the bolt is gone and a faint scorched outline of the ring remains.

Minor point: at t120–t130 one upper branch reaches right towards the crystal pylon (it ends near x≈850, y≈270). It could briefly suggest a link to the pylon.

**Q4. Reduced motion: PASS**
I see no strobe, rapid pulse, flicker or white-out at any sampled time:
- The sky and overall exposure stay the same in every frame, including t120.
- The bolt looks identical at t120, t125 and t130. It holds, then fades out (t140).
- The rim brightness is steady from t000 to t090.
- The brightest area is the pale fill at t110. It stays inside the ring, and the trainer, the ground detail and the surroundings all remain readable.

Limit: the frames are 50–100 ms apart, so they cannot rule out a flicker shorter than that gap.

**Q5. Competition (t000–t110): PASS**
- There is no lightning, bright flash or forked shape in the sky or anywhere else before t120.
- Nothing looks like a second warning ring.
- Two small steady yellow glows sit on the path either side of the trainer, and faint warm sparks sit on the horizon behind them. Both are present in every frame, before and after the strike, so they read as scenery, not as a hazard.
- The dense bright rain is the busiest thing on screen. It takes some attention away from the thin cracks, but it does not look like lightning.

Outside the window: at t160–t170 a large cloud-to-cloud bolt appears in the upper-right sky, with a faint one at the left edge. It does not flicker (it is the same in both frames). But it arrives only 0.4 s after the strike, and it could make players think another strike is coming or that one is landing somewhere else. Consider holding off sky bolts for about 1 s after a ground strike, or keeping them away from where a warning just was.

**Overall: PASS**
The warning is well placed, easy to see from the first frame, shows clear stages, and ends in a strike on the warned spot, with no strobing under Reduced Motion.

Suggested polish:
1. Make the cracks thicker, or add a sweep around the rim, so the time left is easier to read mid-countdown.
2. Stop the crack end being hidden behind the trainer.
3. Keep sky lightning away for a short time after a strike.
