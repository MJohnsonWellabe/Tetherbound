# Visual judge — Stormwood f10_4 (r2)

Frames reviewed: `_sheet.jpg` and all fifteen 1920x1080 frames (`forest_*`, `giant_*`, `glass_*`, `rod_line_*`, `stormheart_*`, each in `_calm`, `_break`, `_aftermath_calm`).

## Criterion-specific read

**1. Forest — deep old storm forest, not open parkland?**
Mostly **yes**, with a caveat. `forest_calm` and `forest_aftermath_calm` show closely spaced, thick dark trunks with a genuinely closed canopy overhead and a dense flowering understory at the trainer's feet — this reads as old forest, not parkland. `forest_break` is the exception: the rain layer and near-zero ambient light drop the whole background to flat black silhouette, so canopy structure and understory both disappear; you can tell "forest" only from the tree outlines, not that it's a *closed-canopy, deep* one.

**2. Giant trunks — is a giant old trunk clearly the subject of the giant/stormheart views?**
**No** for `giant_calm` / `giant_break` / `giant_aftermath_calm`: composition, trunk spacing, canopy color, and camera height are all essentially identical to the `forest_*` row. Nothing in the frame signals "this specific trunk is the point" — no distinct scale jump, no unique bark detail, no reveal of how high the crown goes. Flipping between the forest and giant columns on the sheet, they read as the same location.
**Yes** for `stormheart_calm` / `stormheart_break` / `stormheart_aftermath_calm`: the huge trunk-tower with the vertical glowing seam, plus the small cottage at its base for scale, reads clearly as a singular giant landmark distinct from ordinary trees.

**3. Glass scars — readable as scars in the land?**
**No.** `glass_calm`, `glass_break`, and `glass_aftermath_calm` all show tall, pointed, translucent teal-blue crystal spikes standing vertically out of the grass, roughly person-height. That's a crystal-prop silhouette, not a scar: a scar (per the chapter intent, "glass-fused scars") implies something embedded and flattened into the terrain, discoloring the ground itself. These read as decorative gem/rock-candy formations dropped into a meadow, not as damage to the land.

**4. Rod line — readable as a destination line?**
**No / marginal.** `rod_line_calm`, `rod_line_break`, and `rod_line_aftermath_calm` each show exactly one clear pylon close to the player, a second, much smaller pylon silhouette far down the path, and a single cable exiting the top-right of frame. Two data points and a barely-visible wire isn't enough repetition to read as an engineered "line" of scaffolds pulling the eye toward a destination — it reads as two isolated found objects on a path, not infrastructure.

**5. Restored sky — does the aftermath column read as the same place, lighter rain, no lightning, scars still present, distinct from the storm columns?**
**No.** `forest_aftermath_calm`, `giant_aftermath_calm`, and `stormheart_aftermath_calm` are, at a glance and at full size, essentially identical to their `_calm` counterparts — same rain density, same brightness, same everything. They are clearly distinct from `_break` (which is much darker and rainier), but the intended three-state read — storming-calm vs. storming-peak vs. storm-lifted-aftermath — collapses to two states because calm and aftermath don't differentiate. The only frames where aftermath shows any visible change are `glass_aftermath_calm` and `rod_line_aftermath_calm`, where the amber ground-crack glow is visibly fainter than in their `_calm`/`_break` versions — a good, correct touch, but it's the only place the "storm lifted" idea actually reads.

## Specific, addressable defects by frame

- `forest_break`, `giant_break`: rain density + near-zero fill light flattens the entire background to solid black. Trunk bark, canopy leaf shape, and the far tree line all disappear — a rendering choice that reads as "night" rather than "storm peak," and fails silhouette readability outright.
- `giant_calm`/`giant_break`/`giant_aftermath_calm`: no compositional distinction from the forest row (see criterion 2).
- `glass_calm`/`glass_break`/`glass_aftermath_calm`: the cyan spikes read as gems/crystal props, not ground scarring; also, of the two visible creatures in this frame (a dark blob-like shape and a blue spider-like shape), neither is doing anything — no combat stance, no readable silhouette detail — they sit as static, undetailed props rather than the "bespoke, expressive" creatures the bar demands.
- `rod_line_*`: only two pylons in frame, one of them tiny/distant — too sparse to read as a "line."
- All `_calm` and `_break` frames: trunks in the forest/giant rows are rendered as flat, near-black silhouettes with no visible bark form or local shading — terrain/props have very little modeled form under this lighting; this is closer to flat-lit than the "form from cool moss-light from below" the chapter intent calls for.
- No frame in this set shows a fight, a creature reacting to the player, or a "safe, calm, grounded" camp space — the whole set is empty traversal/exploration shots.

## The three things that most separate these frames from the references, ranked

1. **Storm-break blackout vs. legible drama.** Bar B's Palworld references stay fully legible even in their busiest combat moments — value range, saturation, and silhouette separation are always intact. `forest_break` and `giant_break` instead go to near-solid black-and-white-streaks, losing the "silhouette and readability at small size" test outright. Nothing in either reference bar goes this dark and stays this flat.
2. **No monumental "giant" read outside the Stormheart.** Board A/B's Stormheart key views (`stormwood-stormheart-tree-stronghold-board-a/b.png`) sell scale through crown height, split-trunk silhouette, banners, and walkways dwarfing the figure — every key view is instantly "this one tree is different." Our `stormheart_*` frames get partway there (good use of the cottage for scale), but the dedicated `giant_*` row is indistinguishable from plain forest, which the boards never allow: every "big tree" view in the reference is staged to prove its own scale.
3. **Props read as generic crystal decor, not authored scars/infrastructure.** The boards show bespoke, detailed material language — carved wood, aged metal, banner fabric, "electric roots" worked into the bark. `glass_*`'s crystal spikes and `rod_line_*`'s bare pylons are comparatively plain, low-detail geometric primitives that don't carry the same sense of a authored, damaged landscape; they read as inserted set-dressing rather than the land itself having been hurt.

## Bar A — does this belong to the Tetherbound art direction (`tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png` + the Stormwood boards)?

**No.**
What carries it: the palette commitment is real and consistent — purple-violet storm sky, dark old-growth trunks, a flowering understory, and copper-toned cabling are present in every frame and match the chapter intent's "always a purple storm" instruction rather than defaulting to the Meadows' sunny green. The Stormheart silhouette (tower-trunk + vertical glowing seam + small building at its base) is a legitimate, recognizable echo of the boards' "colossal tree, glowing core, landmark visible across the region" idea.
What sinks it: the boards sell craft — carved archways, banners, bracketed platforms, braided rope, a genuinely towering split trunk with lightning branching through the canopy. What's in these frames is comparatively bare: flat-black trunks with no visible bark modeling, a plain cylindrical Stormheart with a single dashed light strip instead of a dramatic lightning-split crown, and crystal spikes standing in for what should be fused, damaged ground. The storm-break blackout is also a bigger departure from "vibrant, readable colours" than the always-storm premise requires — even a dark, tense scene in the reference material keeps color and form legible.

## Bar B — shown beside the Palworld screenshots, is this trying to be the same kind of game?

**No.**
What carries it: ground and flower density in the meadow foreground is genuinely comparable to Palworld's grass density, and the flat-shaded, saturated-highlight art style on the flowers/grass is in the right family.
What sinks it: none of these fifteen frames show a fight, a creature reacting, or the player's own creature — the two creatures visible (in `glass_*`) sit inert and undetailed, nothing like Palworld's expressive, bespoke pals captured mid-combat. Value range is also far narrower here: Palworld's shots (even the boss fight) keep strong light-to-dark separation and saturated color; these frames, especially the `_break` variants, sit in a narrow dark-desaturated band. A viewer would say this is a moodier, quieter game, not "the same kind of game."

## Scene-fixable vs. needs-new-art

**Scene-fixable** (lighting, density, tint, VFX, camera, composition):
- Raise the ambient/fill light floor during `_break` so trunks and canopy keep some visible form instead of crushing to black (`forest_break`, `giant_break`).
- Differentiate the `giant_*` camera/framing from `forest_*` — pull back or tilt to reveal more crown height, or move the shot to a spot where the trunk visibly dwarfs its neighbors (`giant_calm`, `giant_break`, `giant_aftermath_calm`).
- Add 2-4 more pylons along the visible path, at decreasing scale into the distance, so `rod_line_*` reads as a line instead of two isolated props.
- Make `_aftermath_calm` visibly lighter/calmer than `_calm` — thin the rain particle count further, brighten exposure slightly, or add a becalmed-light/haze pass — so all three per-row states are distinguishable, not just break-vs-the-other-two.
- Give trunks in the forest/giant rows a visible bark-form light response (even a faint rim or moss-glow per the "cool blue-green moss from below" line) instead of flat black silhouette.

**Needs new art:**
- The `glass_*` crystal spikes are a shape problem, not a lighting one — reading as "scars in the land" needs geometry that lies into/along the terrain (fused, cracked, embedded) rather than standing crystal props.
- The creatures visible in `glass_*` (dark blob shape, blue spider-like shape) are undetailed relative to the bar; making them expressive/bespoke is an asset-quality issue, not a scene setting.
- The Stormheart's dramatic "split trunk with branching lightning through the crown" from the reference boards is a modeling/VFX-authoring gap beyond what relighting the current cylinder-plus-light-strip mesh can achieve.

## TOP FIXES

1. **Fix the `_break` blackout** (`forest_break`, `giant_break`, and to a lesser extent `stormheart_break`) — raise the ambient/fill floor or add a faint moss/lightning-flash fill so trunks and canopy stay legible at small size during the storm peak. This is the single biggest readability failure in the set and the most damaging to Bar A/B both.
2. **Make `_aftermath_calm` visually distinct from `_calm`** across all five views, not just `glass_*`/`rod_line_*` — thin the rain further, lift exposure slightly, or add a becalmed-haze pass, so the three-state story (storming-calm / storm-break / aftermath) actually reads as three states instead of two (`forest_aftermath_calm`, `giant_aftermath_calm`, `stormheart_aftermath_calm` currently read as duplicates of their `_calm` frames).
3. **Give the `giant_*` shots their own identity** distinct from `forest_*` — reframe the camera to show more crown height or a visibly wider trunk base than the forest row, so "giant" reads as a distinct, monumental subject rather than the same forest shot repeated (`giant_calm`, `giant_break`, `giant_aftermath_calm`).
