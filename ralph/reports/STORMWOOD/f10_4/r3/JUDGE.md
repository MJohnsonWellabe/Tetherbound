# Stormwood visual judge — r3

## Criterion-specific read

1. **Forest — deep old storm forest, not open parkland?** Yes. `forest_calm.jpg` and `forest_break.jpg` have a genuinely closed canopy (thick dark trunks filling the whole upper frame, only slivers of sky between them) and a dense flower/grass floor the trainer stands knee-deep in — it reads as enclosed woodland, not a lawn. Caveat: the floor is a flat wildflower meadow with almost no fallen logs, roots or fungus breaking it up (`giant_calm.jpg` has one small mushroom cluster; nothing else does), so it reads more like a planted meadow under trees than a wild forest floor.

2. **Giant trunks — clearly the subject of the giant/stormheart views?** Split. **No** for `giant_calm.jpg`, `giant_break.jpg`, `giant_aftermath_calm.jpg` — the trunks there are the same scale and shape as the ones in the plain `forest_*` frames; nothing marks them as an exceptional "giant old trunk," they're just more forest. **Yes** for `stormheart_calm.jpg`, `stormheart_break.jpg`, `stormheart_aftermath_calm.jpg` — the Stormheart itself is unmistakably a single colossal trunk, dwarfing the trainer and the cottage at its base, with a lit seam climbing its full height. The rubric's own "giant" row fails at the one thing the "stormheart" row does correctly.

3. **Glass scars — readable as scars in the land?** No. The blue spikes in `glass_calm.jpg`, `glass_break.jpg`, `glass_aftermath_calm.jpg` read as crystal props standing on unmarked grass — there is no scorching, cracking, glassy sheen or discoloration of the ground itself. They look like spawned crystal geometry (indistinguishable in silhouette from the blue creature standing next to them), not fused glass left by a strike.

4. **Rod line — readable as a destination line?** Weak/no. `rod_line_calm.jpg` and `rod_line_break.jpg` show exactly one nearby ornate pylon plus one small, hard-to-read silhouette further down the path, joined by a single cable arcing off-frame. Two elements with no visible repetition toward the horizon reads as "a landmark and a smaller landmark," not a line of scaffolds guiding you onward. The cable does at least point a direction.

5. **Restored sky — same place, lighter rain, no lightning, scars remain, distinct from storm columns?** Mostly yes, with one real problem. All five `*_aftermath_calm.jpg` frames correctly drop the rain and lightning bolt, and the glass spikes / rod pylons / Stormheart's lit seam all persist (nothing was scars-removed). But `forest_aftermath_calm.jpg` and `giant_aftermath_calm.jpg` shift the foliage to a bright, saturated, warm cartoon-daylight green that reads as a different biome/time-of-day rather than the same purple storm calmed down — this contradicts "Stormwood is always a purple storm; it has no day/night look." `glass_aftermath_calm.jpg`, `rod_line_aftermath_calm.jpg` and `stormheart_aftermath_calm.jpg` hold the purple sky and cooler palette much better and read as the intended "storm lifted, scars remain" beat.

## Full verdict

**Specific defects by frame:**
- `glass_calm/break/aftermath_calm.jpg` — crystal spikes sit on clean, unmarked grass; no scar, burn or glassing of the terrain under them, so "glass-fused scars in the land" doesn't land; a blue creature nearby is the same silhouette family as the crystals, which muddies what's a hazard vs. what's scenery.
- `rod_line_calm/break/aftermath_calm.jpg` — only one pylon is close enough to read; the "line" is asserted by a single cable, not by repeated structures receding into the distance.
- `giant_calm/break/aftermath_calm.jpg` — misnamed in effect: nothing distinguishes these trunks from ordinary forest trees at the same camera distance; no scale cue (no reference object, no unusually wide base) marks them as "giant."
- `forest_aftermath_calm.jpg`, `giant_aftermath_calm.jpg` — foliage saturation/hue jumps to a warm sunny green that breaks Stormwood's cool blue-green/purple identity; reads as a palette swap rather than "same place, calmer."
- All frames — the described "copper vines," "black reflective pools," and "Stormglass arches" are not visible anywhere in this set; what's present is grass, flowers, tree trunks, blue crystal spikes and one dark metal pylon/altar shape.
- All frames — art style is flat-shaded/low-poly (blocky flowers, simple foliage clumps, unshaded trunk bark) with a plain, generic humanoid model, next to references that are painterly, high-detail and atmospheric; this is the single biggest style gap and it appears in every frame.

**The three things that most separate these frames from the references, ranked:**
1. **Material/identity language is largely missing.** The Stormwood boards and chapter intent call out copper vines, black reflective pools, glass-fused scars, and Stormglass arches as the defining texture of this biome. None of that is visible in `glass_*.jpg` or `rod_line_*.jpg`; what's there instead is a bare crystal-spike prop and a single unornamented dark pylon. The frames could currently belong to almost any generic purple-storm forest, not specifically Stormwood.
2. **Overall render style vs. the boards.** `docs/reference/boards-2026-09-06/stormwood-stormheart-tree-stronghold-board-a.png` and `-board-b.png` are painterly, volumetrically lit, richly detailed (bark texture, glowing energy veins, warm lantern pools, banners). Every Stormwood frame here is flat-shaded and comparatively bare — this is the gap a still frame shows most plainly, most visible in `stormheart_calm.jpg` next to the board's "front view."
3. **"Giant" as a named view doesn't deliver its own premise.** `giant_calm.jpg`/`giant_break.jpg` promise a giant trunk and show ordinary forest instead, while `stormheart_calm.jpg` two rows down proves the game already has the asset language to do this correctly.

**Bar A — do these frames read as belonging to `tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png` / the Stormwood boards?** **No.** What carries it: the purple storm sky, the giant Stormheart trunk with its lit vertical seam, and the overall cool-forest palette during the storm columns are recognizably Stormwood and match the boards' basic silhouette language. What sinks it: the flat cel-shaded rendering next to the boards' painterly detail, the absence of copper/glass/reflective-pool materials the chapter intent explicitly names, and the aftermath's swing into a warm generic-daylight green that undercuts "always a purple storm."

**Bar B — beside the Palworld shots, would someone say these are trying to be the same kind of game?** **No.** What carries it: reasonable ground/flower density in the meadow floor, a readable third-person trainer silhouette, a real value contrast between dark trunks and lit sky. What sinks it: Palworld's shots show a shaded, detailed, real-time game with bespoke creature and character art (fur, cloth, distinct silhouettes) and layered depth (distant mountains, haze, midground activity); these frames have one generic, unshaded low-poly creature glimpsed once (`glass_calm.jpg`), a plain trainer model, and comparatively little depth beyond "trees, then purple sky" — the visible fidelity and content density are well below the Palworld bar.

**Scene-fixable vs. needs-new-art:**
- Scene-fixable: ground scarring/discoloration under the crystal spikes; adding more rod pylons along the path to make a legible line; re-staging the `giant_*` views around a suitably oversized trunk (the Stormheart asset already exists); regrading aftermath lighting/foliage color to stay in the cool storm palette.
- Needs-new-art (or an unverified existing asset this reviewer cannot confirm from pictures alone): copper vine dressing, black reflective pool props, and a distinct "Stormglass arch" silhouette for the crystal formations, since what's on screen now reads as a stock spike shape rather than an arch; a higher-fidelity trunk bark/foliage shading pass to close the style gap with the painted boards is also an art-level change, not a placement one.

## TOP FIXES
1. **Regrade `forest_aftermath_calm.jpg` and `giant_aftermath_calm.jpg`** to keep the cool purple-green storm palette (lighter rain, brighter but not warm/saturated) instead of the current sunny cartoon-green, so the aftermath reads as the same calmed storm rather than a different biome.
2. **Restage `giant_calm.jpg`, `giant_break.jpg`, `giant_aftermath_calm.jpg`** around an actually oversized ancient trunk (reuse the Stormheart's giant-trunk silhouette or an equivalent already-built large-tree asset) so a giant trunk is unmistakably the subject, matching what `stormheart_calm.jpg` already achieves.
3. **Add ground scarring under the crystals and extra pylons along the path** in `glass_calm.jpg`/`glass_break.jpg`/`glass_aftermath_calm.jpg` and `rod_line_calm.jpg`/`rod_line_break.jpg`/`rod_line_aftermath_calm.jpg` — scorched/glassy dark patches under each spike, and two or three more pylons receding toward the horizon — so "glass-fused scars" and "rod line" both read as intended rather than as isolated props.
