# Code-blind visual judgement: Stormwood Deepwood

I looked at images only. I read no code, config or reports. I also made brightened crops of the eye-level band of each view, to see what sits behind the trunks.

Side note: view_Q.jpg is byte-identical to forest_road_calm.jpg.

## Part 1: forest readability

### View P: EDGE OF WOOD
- **Trunks.** There are good, massive, dark trunks. The foreground trunk on the right fills about a quarter of the frame, which gives a strong sense of scale against the 1.8 m trainer. But the stand is only about two rows deep. Behind the second row there are no more trunks.
- **Canopy.** It is closed overhead across the top third of the frame, which is good.
- **Open land and horizon at eye level.** This is the failure. A continuous purple sky band runs behind the trunks from the left edge to about x=1400. It is visible in every gap between trunks, just above the grass line. With the brightness lifted, the view reads as a grove standing on open ground, with sky on the far side of it. The floor is also an even, mowed-looking meadow of bright grass with a flat far edge. A player standing here would see the trees end about 30 to 40 m ahead.
- **Depth.** The layering is shallow: foreground trunk, a mid row, then sky. Nothing fades into darker, denser trunks. It feels like a large wood you have just walked into, not the deep interior of one.

### View Q: INSIDE DEEP FOREST (marginal)
- **Trunks.** Dark, broad trunks form walls on both sides of the road. Behind the right-hand trunks there is solid darkness, which reads as more forest. The left side also layers back into shadow.
- **Canopy.** It is about 90% closed. The two crowns almost meet over the road, leaving only a narrow slot of sky. This is the strongest "under the trees" read of the three views.
- **Open land and horizon at eye level.** There is one exposure: the road opens at its far end (x about 800 to 1050) onto a lit, flat plain with a clear horizon line. It is small in frame and sits on the path axis, so it reads as "the road leads out of the forest over there" rather than "I'm at the edge".
- **Depth.** The depth is good. The pylon line recedes, the trunks recede, and the road narrows to the vanishing point. The weakness is that the trees line the road evenly, a little like an avenue or allee. The lit plain at the vanishing point caps how "deep" the place can feel.

### View R: EDGE OF WOOD
- **Trunks.** They are big and plentiful in the mid-ground, with a heavy trunk on the right edge. The trainer's scale reads well against them.
- **Canopy.** The top half of the frame is dense and closed.
- **Open land and horizon at eye level.** This is clearly visible, and it is the worst of the three. On the left (x from 0 to about 500), a misty purple open field with a ground plane and distant objects shows under the crowns. On the right (x from about 1300 to 1450), a pale, bare hill or plain is plainly visible through a gap. A thin line of fence or rope crosses open ground in the centre background. The foreground is a flat, open grass lawn with flowers.
- **Depth.** There is only a single band of trees between the camera and open country. This reads as standing at the tree line looking out, with the wood behind you.

### Common cause (P and R)
In both views the tree mass is a band about two trunks deep. At eye level, beneath the canopy, open sky or plain shows straight through it. The canopy is doing its job. What is missing is occlusion at eye level: more trunks behind the second row, or trunks that fade into darkness or fog rather than into lit purple sky. The flat, lawn-like grass floor adds to the parkland read, though that is a lesser factor.

## Part 2: storm states (forest road)

**(a) Can you tell Break from Calm? Yes, easily.**
- Break has dense, slanted, heavy rain streaks across the whole frame. The scene is clearly darker: overall mean luminance is about 20 against 30 for Calm. The foliage loses its green and goes blue-black, and the golden road crack glows more saturated against the dark.
- However, it reads as "downpour", not "lightning peak". There is no visible bolt, no flash-lit frame and no hard lightning shadow. The only hint is a paler lavender streak in the sky slot at the top.
- If Break is meant to be the lightning peak, a still frame does not show lightning. (A flash may exist in motion that one frame missed.)

**(b) Does the aftermath read as the same place, with lighter rain and no lightning? Mostly yes, but with extra changes beyond the owner's rule.**
- **Same place:** yes, unambiguously. It has the identical road, fern, mushrooms, pylon line and trunks.
- **Sky:** it stays purple, as the rule requires, now with more visible cloud structure.
- **Rain:** it is lighter, with fewer and fainter streaks than Calm. The difference from Calm is modest, though. Rain alone would not make the aftermath obvious.
- **Lightning:** none, as required.
- **Beyond the rule:** the whole scene is noticeably brighter and greener than Calm (mean luminance about 45 against 30). The foliage goes from dark, stormy green to vivid mid-green, and the trunks and road are lit more warmly. This reads as "the weather has cleared, afternoon light". The owner's rule says the aftermath shows only through lighter rain, no lightning and remaining scars. This lighting lift is an extra, fairly large change.
- **More changes:** the pylon crystals have gone from glowing cyan to dead grey, and the golden road crack has faded to a faint dull line. Those may be intended story consequences, but they are further differences beyond "lighter rain".
- **Scars:** I cannot identify any "scars that remain" from the image. There is no visible burn, broken trunk, scorch or storm damage. If the faded road crack is meant to be the scar, it reads as the feature having faded, not as a scar left behind.

**(c) Is the trainer findable in all three? Yes.**
- The trainer is centred on the road, about 1/5 of frame height, with a warm orange backpack and blue sleeves against a brown road.
- **Calm:** clearly readable.
- **Break:** still findable, but the weakest of the three. The trainer's region is darker, and rain streaks cross the silhouette. The saturated gold road crack runs directly under and behind the legs and competes with the figure. The backpack is still the anchor.
- **Aftermath:** the most readable. It has the brightest lighting and the least rain, and the trainer separates cleanly from the road.

## Summary
- **Views P and R do not read as deep forest.** At eye level, sky or open plain shows through a tree band only about two trunks deep.
- **View Q passes, marginally.** It works because of the closed canopy tunnel and dark side depth, but the lit exit at the end of the road limits it.
- **Break vs Calm:** clearly different, but it reads as a downpour without visible lightning.
- **Aftermath:** it keeps the purple sky, rain is lighter and there is no lightning. But it also brightens and greens the whole scene and turns off the pylon and crack glows, which goes beyond the owner's "only" rule. No remaining scars are visible.
- **Trainer:** findable in all three frames. Break is the weakest.
