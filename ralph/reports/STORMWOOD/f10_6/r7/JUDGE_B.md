## Visual-judge verdict

Creatures and character come first. The only character in frame is the trainer. He is a competent stylised figure with a readable backpack and a good silhouette, and he holds up next to the Palworld protagonist as a cousin. He is small and has fairly plain shading. He is not a gap. There are no creatures in frame apart from one indistinct shape at left in FB and FC. The bar this project set is "a fight that looks like an event" and creature art, and these six frames give me nothing on either. That is a gap in the evidence, not a pass. I will not infer creature quality.

Defects by frame:
- FB (forest, Break). Severely underexposed. Trunks are flat near-black, and there are hard black wedges at the left and at the right of centre. The right wedge looks like an unlit cutout, the left like a render or shadow-cascade edge. The trainer is barely lit. Rain streaks are the brightest thing in the frame. There is no value range, so the forest reads as a void, not a place. Scale: trunks are huge relative to the 1.8 m trainer, which is fine and gives grandeur, but they are featureless slabs.
- FC (forest, Calm). Better, and the trainer reads. The left wedge of black remains, and the right side is still black behind the HUD. The trunks are smooth uniform columns with no bark detail. The far-left creature is a low-contrast brown shape lost against the dark.
- GB (glass field, Break). This is the strongest mood frame: purple sky, silhouetted dead tree, glowing lantern-like lights and cyan crystal spikes as landmarks. The grass is dense and varied with flowers. Defects: the dead tree's lower half is a flat purple silhouette and dissolves into the sky; the slope crest is a hard, empty diagonal band; the crystals sit oddly on a thin white line, which reads like a stray render artefact; the foreground is mid-dark and everything is one green-brown value.
- GC (glass field, Calm). Near-identical to GB. Break versus Calm reads mostly as a small drift in sky brightness and the "Calm/Break" tag. The two do not read as different states without the tag.
- RB (rod line, Break). The best frame of the set and the only one with a real value range: bright gold lightning on the path, saturated purple sky, dark trees, a lit rod tower with a red cable as a distance landmark, and a clear path leading the eye. It has legible depth to the horizon. Defects: the trees are lollipop canopies on similar trunks at regular intervals and heights, which reads as generator output; the path is very wide and bare; the gold path streaks are so bright they overpower the trainer and could be mistaken for a hazard.
- RC (rod line, Calm). Almost identical to RB with a slightly brighter sky, and the bolt in the sky is gone. The gold path streaks remain, so "calm" still looks electric. The Break/Calm difference is too small.
- Oxblood: the rod line cable is a dark red-brown. That is a small leak if red belongs to Team Tether; check what the line is. It reads as a hazard line, so it may be intentional.
- Across frames: rain is the same density in every frame and adds noise at handheld size. The 7-inch sheet shows rain streaks as competing white lines, most harmfully in FB and GB.

Three biggest gaps from the references, ranked:
1. Creature and fight content is absent (Palworld 01, 03). Palworld frames a huge, colourful, expressive creature against a bright field with effects. Not one of these frames shows a creature or a fight, and the only visible shape is an unreadable low-contrast blob in FB and FC. I cannot pass a creature-expedition game on that.
2. Value and exposure (Palworld 01, 02). Palworld is bright and saturated with a wide value range and strong subject-to-background separation. FB and FC crush to black, and GB and GC are one mid-dark tone. Only RB has range. The trainer is near-lost in FB.
3. Foliage and prop craft, and density variety (Palworld 02). Palworld's ground and trees carry scale variety, rock and cliff landmarks, and layered depth. The trees here are smooth, uniform lollipop shapes at regular spacing (RB, RC), the trunks in the forest frames are bare columns (FB, FC), and the land is empty of landmarks other than the rod line and the crystals.

Bar A (mood-only, no reference available): NOT JUDGEABLE against the named key art and boards. As a mood read against the brief ("permanently purple storm forest"), RB, GB and GC carry it with purple sky, gold lightning and a lit landmark. FB and FC sink it. A yes/no on the real reference cannot be given.

Bar B: NO. RB has the closest shipping-quality feel, and the trainer is credible. It is sunk by no creature or fight in frame, the black forest frames, the generator-regular trees, the flat trunks and the small Break/Calm difference.

Split of gaps:
- Scene-fixable: exposure and fill light in the forest frames (FB, FC), including the black wedges; tree spacing, height and tint variety in RB and RC; a larger Break/Calm gap in sky, rain density and path glow; reducing the rain to something less noisy at handheld size; dimming the path streaks so they do not out-shout the trainer; a rim light on the trainer in dark frames; landmark clustering on the empty slope in GB and GC; moving the clock to a contrasting plate; distinguishing the five move glyphs.
- Needs new art or content: creature and fight frames (not present); bark and trunk texture on the forest trees; more distinct tree species and canopy shapes; a real creature at a readable size.

## TOP FIXES

1. Fix exposure in the forest frames. Add fill and ambient so the value range is visible, remove the black wedges at the left and right, and give the trainer a rim light so he is found in under a second in FB.
2. Re-render with a fight and a lightning-ring strike so the device-profile questions can be answered. Until then questions 1 (enemy name, tell text, team list), 2 and 3 (creature, opponent) and 4 (fight action) stay open.
3. Handheld HUD tuning. Give the clock a backing plate, brighten the FOOD label, make the five move glyphs distinct, and reduce rain density and path glow. Widen the Break/Calm contrast so the state reads without the tag.
