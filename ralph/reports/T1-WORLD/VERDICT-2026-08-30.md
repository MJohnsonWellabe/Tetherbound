# T1-WORLD — the whole-board visual pass

**Branch:** `ralph/T1-WORLD` off `origin/ralph/LAND-0830I` (109 commits ahead of
`main`; the first branch carrying the second grass species, the visible stream,
the seam fix, golden hour, the Hall rebuild and the StormWall fix together, so
the first branch on which a whole-world pass shows the current game).

**Date:** 2026-08-30. **Judged against:** `docs/reference/tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`
and `docs/reference/palworld-0*.jpg`, under `.claude/skills/visual-judge`.

---

## The two bar answers

> **A.** Do these frames read as belonging to the world in
> `tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`?

**No** — narrowly, and on **composition**, not on palette. The palette is
genuinely close now: the greens, the terracotta path, the sky blue and the
half-timber village all sit inside the key art's own colour strip, and the
village frames could go on that board almost unedited. What sinks it is that
the key art gets "cozy and inviting with hints of mystery" from framing —
trees at the edges, dappled light on a path, a focal landmark in *every*
panel. Four of the seven day frames have no focal object at all.

> **B.** Beside `palworld-0*.jpg`, would someone say these are trying to be the
> same kind of game?

**No.** The genre framing lands — over-shoulder camera, a trainer walking a
path, an NPC to talk to. Three things stop it: the frames are far emptier
(one depth layer where the references have four), the character art is a
visible tier below, and **no creature appears in any frame** — see the
honesty note in §5, because that last one is partly an artefact of the
capture tool and must not be read as a claim about the game.

Both were **NO** before this pass and both are **still NO**. The movement is
real but it is inside "no": B is described by the blind critic as reading like
"an early open-world walking prototype with a nice palette", where the previous
round's standing verdict was further out than that.

## Does the Meadows currently look like one game?

**Yes for six of the seven stands, and the seventh is the one that matters.**

Locations 00–05 share a sun, a sky, a grass shader, a path material, a terrain
palette and a scatter vocabulary. Section J's own test — do band 2 and band 4
look like the same world under the same sun — passes cleanly and on the
evidence: the two agree on trunk colour, grass hue and blade length, the orange
sedge, the flower scatter, path beige, sky gradient and cloud texture. What
differs is **density, not tuning**, which reads as an emptier part of the same
map rather than as two regions authored by different people.

**Location 06, the stronghold, breaks the set three separate ways** (§2). It is
darker than the rest of the board at every time of day, unreadable at night, it
is the only place the trees' near-LOD material is visible and it does not match
the far-LOD the rest of the board shows, and the stronghold itself is not in
either eye-level framing.

So: the Meadows is one game up to its own climax, and then stops being one at
the climax. That is the opposite of the failure this pass was expecting to find
and it is a better problem to have, because it is localised.

## 1. What was captured, and why it can be trusted

`tools/_capture_ground_and_sky.gd`, extended here from five bands to the seven
authored stands (`tools/perf_site_survey.gd::VIEWS`' own coordinates, so these
frames and the perf numbers are the same points on the map), in three framings:

| framing | what it answers |
|---|---|
| ground (2.0m up, aimed 4.5m ahead) | what the ground is made of |
| **vista** (1.72m, horizon in frame) — new | whether the regions are one world |
| elevated (`--elevated=24`) | macro ground variation, distance |

75 frames: 7 stands × day/golden/night × 3 framings, plus 3 weather presets at
band 2, 3 water bodies at two framings each, and one landmark stand.

**Every shutter ran `tools/capture_check.gd`** — added to this tool here, at one
choke point so a framing added later cannot skip it. This is not a formality.
Of 75 frames, **74 passed and 1 failed**, and the failure was real:
`water-02-river-grazing` was shot from *below the terrain*, the JUDGE-4
`H-04-gate-mouth.png` defect exactly. It was re-rendered after the fix and
passes. No verdict on this page rests on a degraded frame.

The cause is worth recording because it was guaranteed rather than unlucky: the
tool seated the grazing camera at `ground + 0.15`, and `0.15` is *exactly*
`capture_check.gd::GROUND_CLEARANCE_M`, whose test is `<=`. Every grazing shot
over water below its own bank was going to fail forever. The two also read
different surfaces — a collision raycast here, the baked heightfield there —
which this tool's own header records disagreeing by up to 22m at a river
channel. Floor raised to `ground + 0.45`.

`grass_field.gd::_follow_camera` did its job: the grass field rebound to the
capture camera on every stand and the check confirmed it drawing in all 75.
**The grass-free evidence era is over on this tool.**

## 2. The three breaks in the board

### BREAK 1 — location 06, the stronghold (the one that matters)

- **Tree material disagrees with itself inside one frame.** In
  `ground-06-stronghold-day-vista.png` the trunks at ~4m are dark chocolate with
  a hand-painted bark stripe and metre-wide flat leaf cards; at ~25m in the
  *same frame* they are pale smooth salmon with clumped small-leaf canopy. This
  is the only stand where the near-LOD is visible at all, so 06 is the only
  place the mismatch shows — and it reads as a different game.
- **Exposure is a stop or more below the rest of the board** at all three times
  of day. `ground-06-stronghold-night-vista.png` is effectively black; every
  other stand's night frame is a legible blue moonlit read.
- **The landmark is not in the shot.** Neither eye-level framing at 06 contains
  a stronghold; both are forest interior. Only the elevated frame shows the
  keep, and there it sits on the far ridge *inside the haze band*, pale beige,
  at nearly the same value as the sky.

The third item is partly mine — the stand at `(0, 7420)` aims into a wood — but
only partly: the elevated frame proves the keep is genuinely buried in aerial
haze at its own approach, which is a world defect and not a camera one. §4's
aerial fix targets exactly that.

### BREAK 2 — location 00, the village: fidelity mismatch

The half-timber houses are by a wide margin the highest-frequency and most
saturated objects on the whole board — crisp beam work, clean plaster, hot
orange terracotta. Against the soft flat-shaded trees, untextured boulders and
flat-ribbon grass everywhere else they read as a bought village pack dropped
into a lower-fidelity world, and the orange roof out-competes the player for
attention. Not fixable by moving anything; it is a levelling problem, and the
cheaper direction is lifting the world rather than dulling the houses.

### BREAK 3 — location 03, the crossing: a measurable scale error

In `ground-03-band3-crossing-day-high.png` the half-timber house on the right
bank stands roughly **twice the height of the mature trees beside it**, with a
footprint about two-thirds the width of the river. The plank bridge next to it
pins the scale at a normal ~1.5m, which puts that house's door at about 4m. The
same asset family sits at correct cottage scale at location 00, so this is one
instance scaled ~2.5×. **Loudest single scale error on the board**, and a data
fix, not an art one. *Logged, not fixed in this pass — see §6.*

Secondary: the tree beside the leftmost village house is ~7m; the band 2 trees
are ~18m, same silhouette and same material. Reads as an error, not two species.

## 3. Open item: "ground scatter reads procedural"

Raised against pre-grass-fix captures and never re-judged on good ones.

**It survives the re-judgement. Confirmed BAD, and now fixed.**

The blind critic named the mechanism without seeing the shader: *"the broadleaf
rosette repeats across the entire right-hand meadow at a near-constant ~1m
interval. There is no clustering, no bare patch, no density gradient, no
drift."* Also: the path pebbles are laid **along the centreline at even
intervals**, reading as a dotted line rather than debris.

**The obvious diagnosis is wrong and worth recording.** The *baked* scatter is
not the culprit — `scatter_rules.gd` places clumps of `per_clump` items on a
sqrt-sampled disc, which genuinely clusters, and its corridor fill does the
same. What dresses the near field in every frame is the camera-following
**cover tiers**, and `cover_tier.gdshader` keeps each **lattice** site with
probability `drift * density_gain`. That is a Bernoulli thinning of a jittered
2m grid. A thinned grid *cannot* cluster: it has a minimum spacing by
construction and no mechanism that ever puts two plants touching with bare
ground around them. Density alone was never the lever — lower is sparser and
just as even, higher is denser and just as even.

Three numbers had to move together, per tier:

| | was | now | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| `drift_contrast` | 0.88 | 1.0 | at 0.88 the drift floor is 0.12, so every square metre keeps a non-zero chance of a plant and genuinely **bare ground is unreachable** |
| `drift_scale` | 0.04–0.09 | 0.15–0.22 | a ~20m noise wavelength over a 2m lattice is flat *within* any one patch |
| `density_gain` | 0.30–0.70 | 0.62–0.85 | so **inside** a patch most sites are kept and it reads as a thicket |
| `size_jitter` | 0.3–0.6 | 0.7–0.85 | the other half of the finding — "no seedlings and no large specimens" |

**This costs no draw calls and does not grow the instance buffer.** `count` is
the number of lattice slots in the ring and is unchanged; the shader discards
the sites it does not keep, so a higher keep fraction fills slots already
allocated and already paid for. `docs/PERFORMANCE_BUDGET.md` §3.3 is the
general form — under Compatibility, draw calls track MultiMesh **batches** in
frustum, not the instances inside them.

**The variety half is not config-fixable and is not fixed.** The critic counted
the entire meadow vocabulary at **about four meshes** — broadleaf rosette,
orange sedge, purple flower blob, grey pebble — across seven locations, with
near-zero colour spread. Placement clumping makes four meshes read better; it
does not make them read as six or eight. That is an art ask (§6).

## 4. Aerial perspective — the second-largest gap, and fixed

Both the critic and this lane found it independently. *"Every high-day frame
ends in a flat milky grey-green band with a hard top edge and nothing in it …
distance currently reads as the world stops, not as depth."*

`aerial_fade_start_m` 45 + `aerial_fade_range_m` 160 puts the gradient **fully
saturated at 205m**. Past that, every pixel of ground gets identical treatment
however far away it is — so the one thing aerial perspective exists to do stops
happening exactly where distance starts to matter. This is why the stronghold
keep has no value separation from the sky behind it at its own approach.

`terrain_playground.json`'s own `_comment_aerial` predicted this axis would be
the one that overshot. It was right about the axis and **wrong about the
direction**: the *range* was too short, not the strength too high — which is
why halving the strength would never have been the fix. `aerial_fade_range_m`
160 → 900, so the gradient keeps describing distance out to ~945m, the band a
player actually reads ridgelines in.

Deliberately **not** a fog change. R9.4 rejected that axis twice and that
history stands. Nothing nearer than 205m moves toward *more* haze — every
distance in that band now gets less — so near-field creature readability
(VISUAL §15) is untouched.

## 5. The loudest artefact on the board — and a night-only regression

`ground-04-band4-ironwood-night-vista.png`: the player is correctly darkened to
near-silhouette by moonlight; the trainer NPC three metres away is at **full
daylight brightness**, warm tan coat and near-white hair, the brightest object
in the frame — brighter than the moon. Same shot, same sun. The blind critic
ranked it the loudest single artefact independently.

Cause: `character_model.gd`'s additive emission floor. It is a **constant**
additive lift that keeps a dark-uniformed NPC out of the ACES tonemap's toe, and
a constant cannot be right at more than one light level — at night everything it
competes with has dropped an order of magnitude and it has not, so a term meant
to rescue a figure from a *daylight shadow* makes it self-lit after dark.

Two things beyond the fix are worth recording:

- The floor's own comment reasons that *"the trainer/Grandpa/villagers/captain/
  Warden"* are skipped because their tint luminance is always ≥ 0.95. That holds
  for the **rank** palettes and not for **per-instance overrides**: band 4's
  `captain_field` carries `palette: {"*": "#d9a878"}`, luminance **0.686**, so
  the gate fires on a character the comment concluded it could not reach. The
  gate tests the *effective* tint, not the rank's.
- The comment also records the night case being measured (day body luma
  30.6/35.4, night 51.7/59.5) and read as acceptable. A night frame beside a
  correctly lit subject is what that number actually looks like. This is not a
  criticism of the measurement; it is the argument for judging day **and** night
  in the same pass, which is what this one did.

Fix: the floor is now scaled per time of day from `art.json`, blended like every
other look key, with an explicit base default of 1.0 so the blend ramps rather
than snaps (the bug `_comment_adjustment_defaults_t1_sky` describes). **Daylight
is byte-identical** — the product is what renders and the base scale is 1.0, so
every measurement in that comment still holds. Night and dawn cut to 0.25.

### Honesty note: there are no creatures in these frames

The critic's largest single item is that **no creature appears in any of the 75
frames**, and that a creature-training game cannot be judged against a
creature-game bar without them. That is correct as a statement about *this
evidence* and **must not be recorded as a finding about the game**: this tool's
own header says it settles only long enough for terrain streaming and is
explicitly "not a creature census", where `_probe_corridor_survey.gd` carries
`SETTLE_FRAMES` so the encounter director can populate. The bar question B
answer above is therefore **partly unearned** — it is a real "no" on emptiness
and character art, and an *unanswerable* on the thing the references are
actually about. A whole-board pass that settles for encounters is owed.

## 6. Open item: the Warrens exterior mound

Logged previously as needing **geometry rather than another material pass**.

**Confirmed, and the earlier sub-finding is now stale.** Frames:
`landmark-01-warrens-exterior-{day,golden,night}.png` — a new stand, squared up
on the outcrop's own approach at 40m, derived rather than eyeballed
(`burrow_warrens.json` sites the cave at (-357, 2610) yaw 315, and
`_build_mound` treats local −Z as the mouth, so the approach bearing is world
(+0.707, −0.707)).

**The material is fine and needs nothing.** One coherent warm-grey granite
across every boulder, sitting believably with the grass. The old "three
unrelated rock languages" and mint-green findings are gone. The
`golden` frame renders it as nearly pure silhouette against a bright sky, so
texture contributes nothing there — **and it still fails**, which is the proof
that this is shape.

**The previously-logged "chamfered cubes" is fixed and should not be reopened.**
`_place_rock`'s per-axis stretch landed; individual boulders are rounded now.
The defect has moved up a level, to the **arrangement**:

- `_build_mound` walks the perimeter as **four straight edges of a rectangle** at
  fixed 3.0m spacing in three courses, and roofs each chamber with a **regular
  grid** at that chamber's ceiling height. The ±1.2m jitter softens a line; it
  cannot break a rectangle.
- So the silhouette is a **horizontal row of a dozen near-identical lozenges
  with a flat top**, ending in vertical faces at both ends instead of tapering
  to ground. No dominant mass, no vertical accent, no overhang, no negative
  space — not one gap you can see sky through. It reads as a heap, not as rock.
- All boulders sit in one size class (`perimeter_scale` 2.2–3.6), so there is no
  big-at-the-base gradation.
- One noise frequency at the same texel density on a 12m boulder and a 3m one,
  which flattens the scale read. (Secondary; triplanar would fix it.)

**What would actually fix it**, in impact order — all of it placement and
modelling of the *same three `Rock_Medium_*` meshes*, so **no new mesh and no
Meshy generation**:

1. **Cut horizontal bedding planes across the whole formation.** Strata running
   continuously through several boulders is what makes separate masses read as
   one outcrop. Single highest-value change.
2. **One dominant vertical** — a leaning slab or fin at ~2–2.5× the current
   tallest lump, off-centre, so the silhouette gets a peak and reads
   asymmetrically from any approach.
3. **Crown the roof and taper the ends** — dome the roof grid toward the centre
   instead of a flat plane; extend the perimeter outward past the footprint with
   progressively smaller, lower rocks so the mass meets the ground.
4. **Negative space** — one through-gap or overhang near the top.
5. **Frame the entrance** — a lintel slab, two flanking uprights, a 2–3m recess,
   sized to read at ~3× the trainer from the approach. Currently a small dark
   rectangle punched in at ground level with no depth.

**Cost caveat, and it cuts against the headline.** These are individual
`MeshInstance3D`s, not a MultiMesh. `PERFORMANCE_BUDGET.md`'s "added density is
nearly free" applies to MultiMesh **batches** and **does not transfer here** —
every rock added to this mound is a draw call. Items 1–4 should be done by
*moving and re-scaling* the rocks already placed, not by adding more.

### Known limits, logged with reasons

- **Location 06 stronghold (exposure, LOD mismatch, landmark siting).** Three
  distinct defects in one location, two of which (the tree near/far LOD material
  mismatch, the stronghold's own exposure) are changes to landed lane work —
  the Hall rebuild and StormWall lanes on this branch. Deliberately not touched
  here: this pass's job was to judge the board and it has now produced the first
  evidence that 06 is the break, which is the input those lanes need. The aerial
  fix in §4 addresses the *third* item (the keep buried in haze) directly.
- **Band 3 oversized house instance.** A one-line data fix, but it is one
  instance in a settlement config owned by another lane, and changing a
  building's scale without seeing that lane's own framing risks breaking a
  composition somebody authored. Logged with the measurement so it is a
  five-minute fix for whoever owns it.
- **Scatter variety (~4 meadow meshes).** Needs 6–8 more meadow meshes with real
  scale and *desaturated* colour spread, plus a second textured rock family.
  `CLAUDE.md` forbids spending a Meshy generation without owner reference art,
  and these are environment meshes rather than creatures, so this is a sourcing
  decision, not something to invent here.
- **Character art quality, and the empty-frame gap vs the references.** Both
  need art that is not in the build — distant skyline landmarks (a peak, a
  tower, a ruin) do not exist at all.
