# Visual Judge Report — Band 1 Survey (blind, code-unseen)

Reviewer had no access to code, config, or prior conversation. Judged purely from:
- `docs/reference/tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png` (project art direction board)
- `docs/reference/palworld-0*.jpg` (five files, the owner's stated quality/density bar)
- 18 in-game frames: `shots/` (5), `shots_places/` (5), `shots_composition/` (8)

Rubric applied per the visual-judge skill: silhouette/readability, colour/value structure,
intentionality, lighting, horizon/depth, interface (n/a — no HUD present), artefacts, scale
agreement (trainer = 1.80 m ruler). No numeric scores; defects only, each naming its frame.

## 01-spawn-outward

- (a) Eye lands first on the two grey boulders on the skyline (highest-contrast, highest
  silhouette on the hilltop), second on the player character standing just below them.
- (b) Subject: the player's immediate spawn surroundings — a hillside garden/paddock with an
  elder NPC and a flower bed, read as "here is your starting dooryard."
- Silhouette: boulders and player read cleanly; the elder NPC's white hair blends into the
  bright sky behind her, softening her silhouette at small size.
- Artefact: a hard-edged, near-black cross/plus-shaped shadow patch dominates the lower-mid
  frame. Its shape does not match any single caster in view (the rocks and fence are up-slope,
  off to the sides) — reads as a shadow-map or decal bug, not a soft cast shadow.
- Intentionality: the flower/clover ground clumps are spaced with visible regularity (near-equal
  gaps, similar clump size) — reads as scatter-tool output rather than natural growth.
- Value structure: mid-tone green dominates; the only true darks are the artefact shadow blob
  and character silhouettes. Sky is a flat two-tone gradient with soft, low-detail clouds.
- Scale: boulder-to-player looks plausible (~1.3–1.5× player height). Trees on the left are
  fine relative to the player, but one is a full leafy tree next to an oddly bare, thin dead
  tree of similar height — an intentional variety note, but the two read as mismatched species
  rather than an authored pairing.

## 02-valley-floor

## 03-rise-overlook

- (a) Eye lands first on the cluster of red rooftops (village) at the centre of the valley —
  it's the only saturated colour in an otherwise green-on-green frame — second on the two
  brown/grey foreground boulders.
- (b) Subject: the overlook payoff shot — "here is the whole valley and your village in it."
- Horizon/depth: this is the frame where the depth problem is most visible. Past roughly the
  midpoint of the valley, the terrain flattens into a featureless green-grey wash with a hard,
  visible tonal seam running horizontally about a fifth of the way up the frame — it reads as a
  fog/LOD cutoff line, not atmospheric perspective. There is no tree cover, no elevation change,
  and no colour shift (no blue-shift toward the horizon) to sell distance.
- Intentionality: the valley floor is almost entirely blank — no groves, no rock outcrops, no
  paths visible between here and the village — which reads as unpopulated space rather than an
  authored landscape.
- Lighting: the foreground hilltop (the one true bright spot of the survey) has real terrain
  form — visible slope shading, patchy dry-grass texture, distinct value steps. This is the one
  frame where "does terrain have form" clearly passes, but it's undercut by the flat valley
  behind it.
- Scale: village buildings read as small huts at this distance, consistent with the extreme
  distance implied by the vantage — no scale complaint here.

## Overall verdict

### 1. The three things that most separate these 18 frames from the references, ranked

1. **Tree-line silhouettes read as instanced props, not a grown grove — the single biggest,
   most repeated gap.** The key art's groves show canopies of clearly different heights and
   widths clustering together with gaps and overlaps; every treeline shot in this survey
   (**place2-the-rise**, **place5-bridge-approach**, **comp7-pond-reveal**, **comp8-bridge-rim**,
   and the right-horizon line in **place1-gate-meadow**) shows the same canopy shape at the same
   height repeated at even spacing — literally "one lollipop, repeated" as the rubric warns
   against. Palworld's own reference shots (`palworld-02`, `palworld-04`) show similarly stylised
   trees but with real height and clumping variety that this set lacks almost everywhere except
   the left-hand grove in **place1-gate-meadow** and the framing trunks in **comp4-rise-look-back**.

2. **Ground cover is laid out on a visible grid, not scattered.** The small white-flowered clover
   clumps recur in nearly every frame (**01-spawn-outward**, **02-valley-floor**,
   **place1-gate-meadow**, **place4-long-field**, **comp1-village-approach**,
   **comp2-route-out**, **comp5-pond-arrival**, **comp7-pond-reveal**) at even spacing and
   near-identical size, forming diagonal rows that are especially visible in
   **place1-gate-meadow** and **comp1-village-approach**. Both the key art and the Palworld
   references show dense, irregular, overlapping ground foliage with no visible pattern; this
   set's foreground reads as tiled rather than grown.

3. **Value range and atmospheric depth are compressed — the world reads as one green midtone
   with a flat sky, and distance is cut off rather than faded.** Most damaging in
   **03-rise-overlook**, where the valley beyond the foreground hill goes flat and featureless
   with a visible tonal seam at the horizon instead of atmospheric colour-shift, and in
   **place4-long-field**, which is almost entirely one green value with no landmark to anchor it.
   The key art and Palworld references both use strong local darks (tree shadow, water,
   silhouetted rock) against saturated highlights to build real value structure; this set's only
   comparable darks are the (likely-unintended) hard shadow-blob artefacts in
   **01-spawn-outward** / **05-spawn-low-sun**, not intentional value design.

### 2. The two bar questions

**A. Do these frames read as belonging to the world in `tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`?**
**No, partially.** The palette (green fields, warm dirt paths, red-roofed cottages, standing
stones) and the subject matter (rolling meadow, oak grove, pond with a mill-adjacent building,
village on the horizon) are unmistakably the same *place* as the key art — a viewer would
correctly guess these are the Meadows. What breaks the belonging is craft register: the key
art's trees have individually-readable canopy clumps and real height variety within a single
grove, its water has visible reflection and colour, and its distances fade with real atmospheric
colour shift; none of that survives into these frames. It reads as the same map at a much
lower art fidelity, not a different world.

**B. Shown beside `palworld-0*.jpg`, would someone say these are trying to be the same kind of
game?** **Yes, with reservations.** The stylised-realism intent, the third-person over-the-
shoulder framing, the mix of open field + grove + water + settlement, and the general density
of small foreground foliage are close enough that a viewer would place this in the same genre
and would likely name Palworld/similar games as the comparison point unprompted. What would
give a viewer pause: Palworld's frames are built around a creature or a fight in nearly every
shot (`palworld-01`, `palworld-03`, `palworld-04`) — none of these 18 Tetherbound frames contain
a creature at all, only the player and one NPC, so the survey cannot demonstrate the thing the
game is named for. Palworld's terrain also carries real rock outcrops, cliff faces and elevation
breaks (`palworld-04`); this set's terrain is almost uniformly rolling grass with the one
conspicuous exception of the smooth, primitive-looking dome hill in **comp1-village-approach**
and **comp2-route-out**, which if anything reads as a step backward from Palworld's terrain
variety rather than toward it.

**What carried it:** consistent palette, readable dirt-path wayfinding, a couple of genuinely
strong compositions (**comp4-rise-look-back**'s trunk-framed village view;
**place3-pond-pocket**'s water arrival).

**What sank it, and the fixable/not-fixable split:**
- *Fixable by changing the scene* (density, palette, lighting, composition, scatter — no new
  art required): the ground-cover grid pattern (randomise placement/rotation/scale and vary
  clump size); the flat, seamed valley haze in **03-rise-overlook** (push atmospheric fog colour
  toward blue/desaturated rather than a hard cutoff); the disconnected fence props in
  **comp6-bridge-approach** (either connect them into a line or remove); the unexplained hard
  shadow-blob artefact recurring in **01-spawn-outward** / **05-spawn-low-sun** (a rendering bug,
  should be diagnosable and fixable without new assets); adding a few taller "peak" trees into
  the uniform groves to break the canopy-height repetition, if taller tree variants of the
  existing tree asset already exist in the installed set.
- *Not fixable without new art*: individually-varied canopy silhouettes on the tree asset itself
  (if only one canopy shape/size exists as a mesh, no amount of scattering variety fixes the
  "one lollipop" read — this needs either a second tree mesh/material variant or a shader-level
  canopy-shape variation, per `CLAUDE.md`'s no-new-mesh constraint this likely means sourcing
  variation from materials/scale rather than new geometry); the water's flat, low-detail shading
  compared to the key art's reflective stream (needs a better water shader/material, not scene
  changes); the smooth primitive-dome hill silhouette in **comp1-village-approach** /
  **comp2-route-out** (this is a terrain-mesh/sculpting issue, not a placement one); confirming
  whether a windmill mesh with visible sails exists at all for **place3-pond-pocket** — if it
  does not, that is an asset gap, not a camera problem.
