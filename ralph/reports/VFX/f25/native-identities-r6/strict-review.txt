Verdicts after mapping disclosure

Root disclosed 00/01/02 as Pebble Toss mastery 1/3/5; 03/04/05 as Rock Throw 1/3/5; 06/07/08 as Fireball 1/3/5; and 09/10/11 as Thunder Break lightning 1/3/5. Root supplied the assertion that these are native 0/12 cases, 36 PNGs, at source state 08be. I did not independently establish that source state, capture procedure, case coverage, or mastery metadata.

F25#2 literal identity: PASS for the four sampled families, with each required component considered separately. Pebble Toss shows several small stones in flight. Rock Throw shows one boulder in flight. Fireball shows an orange burning-looking orb, an orange flame-like trailing wake, and a localized enlarged fiery cloud with bright core and sparks that reads as a small explosion. Thunder Break shows a jagged branching yellow bolt reaching the creature from above. These are narrow still-image identity findings. The Fireball explosion is soft and generic, and the lightning is extremely thin; those weaknesses prevent a craft pass but do not erase their literal identities.

F25#5 sampled mastery progression: NOT A COMPLETE PASS. Pebble Toss is partial: mastery 1 to 3 visibly increases the count from three stones to four, but mastery 3 to 5 does not give a sufficiently clear further size/count/trail upgrade at native viewing. Its somewhat stronger dust is not a strong substitute for the requested projectile progression. Rock Throw passes the sampled size progression: the single boulder and especially its dust response grow through the triple, although trail growth is not consistently clear. Fireball passes the sampled size progression: the orb and fiery burst grow visibly through 1/3/5, while trail differences are modest and inconsistent. Thunder Break provides limited sampled count progression through added branches; that supports a narrow branching/count upgrade, but I would not claim a substantial increase in strike strength or impact craft. The overall mastery requirement remains unproved because the small-stone triple is weak and ranks 2 and 4 were not supplied.

Full craft bar: FAIL for all four sampled effect families and for the presented creature/world combination. Literal identity is only the first rung. The rocks show useful silhouettes, material distinction, and shadows, but the small volleys have little visual force and the boulder impacts depend on enlarged soft dust. The fire orb has a clear warm identity but looks like a mottled glowing sphere with a soft orange plume and cloud. Lightning reads as a narrow yellow branched filament with almost no distinct impact response. None has the confident layered shape hierarchy, deliberate accent placement, material variation, and integrated light response needed to establish polished Palworld/Animo effects. The creature is recognizable but the visible surface and leaf treatment do not establish the requested creature craft. The plain plane and empty backdrop cannot establish Valheim-like light, atmosphere, or world integration.

Localization and target readability are broadly successful in these isolated stills: impacts sit beside the snout or at the upper back, and the body remains readable. The largest dust/fire clouds partly veil the face. These findings are helped by the empty background and cannot establish readability amid foliage, terrain, multiple creatures, overlapping attacks, or gameplay UI. Solid rock depth is the strongest; fire has weaker grounding, and lightning mostly presents as a planar line silhouette.

Evidence limits: Only the twelve disclosed cases and their three supplied stills were judged. Other archetypes, mastery ranks 2 and 4, different targets, viewing distances, and integrated game-world scenes remain unproved. Stills cannot establish actual projectile travel, timing, acceleration, impact synchronization, motion quality, animation, audio, effect lifetime, cleanup, or performance. The near-similarity of lightning contact and impact frames is a visible limitation of this evidence, not proof of a runtime lifetime defect. A synthetic world cannot prove integrated-world quality. No full-bar claim is justified by these samples.
