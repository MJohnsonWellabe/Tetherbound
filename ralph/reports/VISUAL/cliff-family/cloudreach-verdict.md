# Cloudreach cliff-family comparison — independent visual verdict

Reviewed all 12 matched native 1920x1080 PNGs in each packet (24 images total), directly through the image viewer. A = `shots/cliff-family-baseline/region/cloudreach`; B = `shots/cliff-family-candidate/region/cloudreach`. Filenames match between packets. The supplied fixtures are staged location/clock/party, HUD hidden and companion parked. The initial manifest records were read for capture context. No production source, configuration, implementation explanation, engine run or production edit was used.

**Retention verdict: B does not demonstrate a meaningful overall visual improvement worth retaining on this evidence.** Windscar is the only viewpoint with large unobstructed cliff faces. B adds conspicuous regular corrugations to those faces without resolving the larger smooth, extruded-wall appearance. I prefer A for Windscar day and night; dusk is effectively tied at whole-frame scale. The other three viewpoints give no material whole-frame preference and limited cliff evidence. This is not acceptance of A's cliff quality: both remain substantially below the intended weathered, layered landform finish.

## Acceptance failures and next useful change

1. **Cliff structure still reads as long smooth extrusions.** In 004–006 the tall pillar and far right wall have very long largely uninterrupted vertical planes; the center plateau has an obvious green cap with a sharp zigzag boundary. B changes face rhythm, but does not visibly establish broad ledges, varied fracture depth, convincing broken shoulders or a natural sequence of large/mid/small rock forms. Repair class: landform/asset silhouette and surface structure. A stronger next change is to give one exposed Windscar pillar and adjacent wall a few substantial, irregularly spaced terraces, broken corner masses and recessed fractures, then use restrained surface detail to support those forms. Simply increasing the current ripple strength would emphasize its regularity.

2. **The candidate pattern is too regular to carry geological scale.** B004's stacked highlights and B006's repeated zigzags are an added finish regression in the only strongly informative view. The source mechanism is unknown; the visual result looks corrugated. Repair class: visible surface/geometry rhythm; images alone cannot assign shader versus mesh cause. Acceptance requires the face to read as weathered pale stone at this actual camera scale without a ladder/zipper rhythm in daylight or moonlight.

3. **Pale daytime and golden-hour cliffs lack material separation.** A and B005 turn the major faces into broad beige masses; 004's far-right wall is pale and relatively uniform. Cool distance remains useful, but the large planes need enough warm/cool and plane-to-plane separation to retain stone structure. Repair class: material/value/lighting balance after the form repair, not a global contrast increase.

For a decisive next witness, retain 004–006 as the fixed whole-frame comparison and add a reachable near-face three-quarter view plus a medium-distance side view where cliff shoulders and ledges cross the sky. The current 001–003 and 007–009 are useful non-regression controls but poor cliff acceptance views. The small exposed cliffs in 010–012 cannot certify near material quality. Do not interpret hidden faces as absent content.

## Whole-frame observations and boundaries

No material regression was identified in the trainer, buildings, main route, sky or overall day/dusk/night composition across the other paired views. Grass/particle differences between captures are not treated as evidence of a cliff change. The painted sky and strong altitude premise remain strengths. Windscar's large distant masses establish height and inaccessible upper country even while the face construction is underfinished.

Several unchanged issues still limit the whole-frame result: Windscar's large wooden arch and plain hanging banners dominate the reveal, with masonry-patterned foreground boulders contrasting with the smooth distant stone; the shrine views are dominated by elementary cylindrical pillars and bright blue/gold rings; Cliffhold's approach has an extensive bare plateau and little framing or edge transition. These are residual scene observations, not candidate regressions or proof of what lies outside the camera.

## Bars A and B

**Bar A — identity: partial, still open.** Open sky, dramatic elevation, pale cliffs, green tops and stone/timber habitation belong to the intended Cloudreach premise. The reference board's irregular stacked rock masses and buildings integrated with terrain are not achieved by this candidate's more regular face pattern. These four staged viewpoints do not establish full chapter identity.

**Bar B — genre/finish: not met in this slice, still open.** Both sets retain large primitive-looking landform planes and sparse approach composition relative to the gameplay reference. B does not supply a clear finish advance and introduces the Windscar surface-rhythm concern. No motion, ordinary traversal, earned route, hidden-HUD usability, device performance or whole-game acceptance follows from these stills.
