## Retention and acceptance are different verdicts

**Worth retaining: yes, as a modest and localized form improvement over A.** B004–006 give the tall left Windscar pillar larger, irregular interruptions in its vertical faces. Its upper and middle portions have more distinct planes and breaks instead of several nearly uninterrupted narrow vertical strips. The right wall also has some visible local breaks. The result carries a little more rock structure at actual frame scale and does not visibly damage the scene's altitude, palette, route or subject hierarchy.

**Final cliff form: does not pass. Final cliff material: does not pass.** This is a small advance in the useful Windscar witness, not a finished cliff family. Long smooth vertical walls still dominate; several new breaks are very sharp planar cuts, and the major masses still resemble tall extruded shapes. The surface remains broadly uniform and lighting-dependent. The nine other pairs do not demonstrate a meaningful improvement; they are largely controls or weak cliff witnesses.

## Remaining failures, ranked by effect on the exposed cliff scene

1. **Large-scale geological construction remains too simple.** B004–006 retain a very tall largely parallel-sided pillar, a near-continuous right-hand wall and broad flat cliff faces. Some middle-sized plane breaks help, but do not establish the reference's hierarchy of shoulders, ledges, recesses, broken corners and irregular stacked masses. The center plateau's sharp sawtooth green cap remains conspicuous. Repair class: asset/landform geometry and composition, rather than merely adding fine texture.

2. **Surface/plane treatment still reads unfinished.** B004's left pillar has visible hard horizontal and diagonal cut boundaries, including an abrupt transverse step around its middle; these can read as joined sections rather than a natural fracture network. This is a residual quality concern in the improvement, not a proven open seam or hole. B004's right wall remains mostly smooth, and B005's broad faces wash toward uniform pale beige. B006 loses much of the wall's internal structure into blue. Repair class: integrate the visible forms and then organize material/value variation around them. The images cannot identify the implementation mechanism.

3. **The cliff family is not yet visually coherent at different distances.** The near masonry-patterned boulders in 004–006 and textured ledge on the right have a substantially different surface scale from the smooth towering faces. The contrast accentuates the distant landforms' lack of weathered stone detail. Repair class: coherent asset/material family at near, medium and far scale.

**Next useful change/witness:** retain these larger irregular breaks, then finish one exposed Windscar pillar and adjoining wall with a few substantial, connected ledges/recesses and broken shoulders. Soften or integrate abrupt section boundaries into that structure; use restrained material variation that follows planes rather than replacing form. Re-capture 004–006 plus a reachable near-face three-quarter view and a medium side view showing the cliff shoulder against sky. A good result should read as pale weathered rock in day, dusk and night, maintain broad value hierarchy, and avoid both featureless walls and conspicuously manufactured joins.

## Whole-frame findings and capture limitations

No material new regression was observed in trainer readability, main paths, houses, shrine foreground, sky, grass distribution at scene scale or the day/dusk/night mood. Minor grass/particle differences are not assigned to the candidate. The bright open sky, height, distance layers, clear trainer silhouette and legible route cues are strengths to preserve.

The useful cliff witness is Windscar. Gate/Waycamp shows almost no exposed cliff; shrine framing occludes most of the background; Cliffhold views expose mostly distant cliff tops. These limitations mean the packet supports a bounded retention judgment, not biome-wide form/material acceptance. Hidden content is not presumed missing.

Whole-frame residuals remain visible in both sets: the large wooden arch and plain banners dominate Windscar; the shrine reads as large simple pillars and colored rings; Cliffhold sits beyond an extensive flat, sparsely dressed approach. None is evidence of a regression caused by B. The cliff change has limited effect on those compositions.

## Separate reference bars

**Bar A — identity: partial and directionally improved at Windscar, still open.** B preserves open sky, elevation, pale stone and grassy upper surfaces. The more interrupted pillar planes move toward the Cloudreach premise. The stacked weathered landforms and convincing terrain/architecture integration of the approved board are not yet achieved.

**Bar B — genre/finish: remains below target and open.** B is preferable in the exposed cliff view, but smooth large walls, abrupt planar breaks, simple surface treatment and sparse approaches still separate these frames from the finished creature-adventure comparison. Preference does not close final form, final material, full chapter or whole-game acceptance.
