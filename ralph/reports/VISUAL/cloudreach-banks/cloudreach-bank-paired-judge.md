# Cloudreach anonymous paired image judgment

Reviewed 2026-09-27. Image-only comparison of sets A and B. Inspected all five overview sheets in each set and all 32 supplied native 1920×1080 frames: pairs 01, 05, 12, 13, 21, 22, 25, 26, 28, 29, 31, 32, 33, 34, 35 and 36. References inspected: Cloudreach Sky Aviary stronghold board, Cloudreach creature-roster board, and all five Palworld gameplay references. Applied visual-judge and the ART_DIRECTION shared-world, chapter-identity and subject-presentation targets. No source, implementation history or prior judgments were inspected. No performance inference enters this verdict.

**Overall preference: A, for bounded terrain/material continuity. Neither set passes the full-scene identity or genre/finish bar.** The preference is strongest in 13 and 21. Several smaller improvements are visible elsewhere, but the foreground, route design, landmarks and creature presentation usually remain the same. This is not region acceptance.

The creatures are visibly central, but their presentation still falls short of the reference register. The blue companion has a readable beak, eye and raised-wing silhouette, yet its rear body repeatedly becomes a broad pale-blue mass of overlapping fins/feathers with dark irregular surface marks (01, 12, 26, 34). In rear views it reads as a dense fan before it reads as an animal (22, 25, 36). This is a form/material clarity problem visible in both sets. The trainer's blue sleeves, pale collar and backpack provide useful colour blocks, but the companion overlaps the head and upper body in 31–32. The creature-roster board and Palworld references retain cleaner face/body separation and more coherent large colour masses. Different wild-creature positions and poses are not credited as environmental gains or regressions.

## Regressions and limits of the preference

No clear environmental regression in A relative to B is established by these pairs. A does lose green area on steep banks, but in the affected views that loss is appropriate to exposed cliff faces and improves continuity. It is not a loss of plausible walkable vegetation.

The important qualification is that A often exchanges an implausible green plane for a more plausible grey plane. Long straight wall tops, triangular intersections and flat faces remain obvious in 13, 21, 28 and 34–35. Do not mark those banks fully resolved because their colour/material family is better. The night comparison in 31 is especially modest and should not carry a broader acceptance claim.

Overview sheets show the same chapter-wide problems in both sets: repeated narrow grassy corridors; isolated grass strips rather than graded verges; similar thin-trunk trees; and sparse landmark surroundings. Some sheet-only close views are almost entirely blocked by stone structures. Those are whole-scene composition failures, separate from the 16 paired native judgments.

## Three largest gaps from the references, ranked

1. **Terrain and route construction is still conspicuous.** In 13, 21, 28 and 34–35, broad planar walls, straight horizontal edges and abrupt material intersections read as constructed barriers. In 01, 21–22 and 25, the path reads as an edged strip over repeated mottled ground. Palworld's open-field and plateau frames use irregular rock/soil/grass interfaces and variable verge coverage to make traversable ground part of the land. The Cloudreach board uses broken, layered cliffs with supported ledges and vegetation following them. A removes the most distracting green vertical faces but does not reach that continuity. This needs scene/terrain shape, material transitions and deliberately placed edge detail; blanket grass density would not fix it.

2. **Creature form and presentation do not sustain the intended polish.** In 01, 26 and 34, the companion's face is readable but the torso-to-tail structure dissolves into layered blue fragments; rear views 22 and 36 are especially ambiguous. In 31–32, the trainer overlaps the body so the relationship is less legible. Palworld's creature silhouettes and colour masses remain immediately comprehensible in a busy environment; the roster board likewise gives each animal clear facial and anatomical hierarchy. Camera/spacing can address overlap. The rear-body silhouette and noisy material/form relationship require asset-level art work, not ground dressing. The images cannot establish what suitable replacement assets are available.

3. **The aviary and settlements are insufficiently integrated as destinations.** The board's aviary is rooted in cliff architecture, terraces, bridges and planted levels; its approach communicates sanctuary and altitude together. Frame 29 supplies a recognizable dome but surrounds it with a flat, sparse apron, scattered containers and exposed hardware. In 34–35, two close bare walls compress the approach and conceal most of the landmark; 33 does not provide the intended distant reveal. Cliffhold in 25 reads as a few houses beside an isolated tower on turf. Palworld's plateau/base references tie buildings to coherent ground, surrounding activity and vegetation. This needs scene composition, foundations, thresholds and inhabited detail. The dome's architectural massing and support language may also need authored structural art; materials alone cannot supply that silhouette.

**Bar A:** Both have useful identity ingredients: open sky, visible height, pale cliffs, grass-topped routes, blue flying creatures and a recognizable domed aviary. A connects exposed banks to stone more convincingly. Across the full survey, however, the repetitive corridors, slab-like escarpments, thin destination integration and limited warm/cool depth structure do not consistently read as the authored Cloudreach world of the chapter board. The judgment concerns identity/composition, not concept-art pixel fidelity.

**Bar B:** Both plainly identify a third-person creature game, but the actual bar includes its polished production register. The large blank or repetitively textured ground areas, conspicuous terrain construction, sparse lived-in detail and unresolved creature form/material clarity keep both below it. The references show coherent, inhabited gameplay spaces with clearer creature anatomy and natural terrain transitions. A's local bank improvement does not close any of those full-scene gaps.

Scene-fixable work is terrain shape and transitions, irregular route verges, landmark reveals, foundation/threshold dressing, graded vegetation clusters, depth/value separation and trainer/companion separation. Asset-level work is needed where the creature's rear-body structure and the aviary's architectural silhouette cannot be expressed by existing visible forms. No claim is made about the unseen asset catalogue. Static captures cannot certify motion, contact through animation, traversal or performance.
