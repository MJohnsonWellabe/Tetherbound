# Cloudreach current matrix — independent visual judgment

Reviewed 2026-09-27. Evidence: all 36 native 1920×1080 frames in `visual-acceptance/shots/cloudreach-current-matrix/`, the full contact sheet and four subsidiary sheets, the Cloudreach Sky Aviary board, Meadows key art, and all five supplied Palworld gameplay references. Judgment uses the visual-judge rubric and art-direction targets. No source, changes, or previous review reports were inspected. Findings concern visible pixels; filenames identify intended views, not proof of distance or game state.

**Bar A: NO. Bar B: NO.** The creature-adventure premise is unmistakable, and blue companions, open sky, green plateaus, bridges and the dome supply recognizable ingredients. Their presentation does not yet form the coherent, inhabited, naturally grounded world or polished gameplay register of the references.

## Subjects first

The blue bird companion is identifiable, has a readable eye/beak in side views, separates strongly from the ground, and is convincingly larger than the trainer. It still does not hold up as the finished central subject beside Palworld: its body is covered in busy mottled markings, while its tail becomes large cyan slabs and spikes with an inconsistent feather read (01, 02, 06, 07, 23, 27). At sheet size the head becomes a small pale hook attached to a dominant wing/tail bundle. Palworld's subjects retain clear face, torso and appendage colour masses even in busy encounters. This needs subject art/material and possibly feather-form refinement, not a global scene saturation change. These stills cannot determine the underlying topology or rig cause.

The more immediately damaging problem is staging: the companion repeatedly overlaps the trainer's head/torso and the forward route (02, 04, 06, 07, 09, 27, 31, 32, 34, 35). In 21 and 32, three large creatures crowd the route, with the purple creature and ram overlapping each other and the bird blocking the trainer. In 10 the ram is cropped into an enormous foreground head. In 20 the bird appears to enter the column's silhouette. Preserve creature scale; give subjects lateral separation and clear camera space. The trainer's backpack, sleeves and trousers otherwise supply readable large blocks; these rear views do not establish facial or expression quality.

## What carries the images

09–10 provide the clearest height-specific authored place: a visible timber crossing, rope rails, stone supports and gold bells. 05 has a coherent architectural family. 24 and 29 have recognizable landmark motifs. Consistent sunlight and shadows ground many props and the trainer; main routes are easy to pick out in ordinary travel frames. The green/ochre lower route and straw-coloured upper route form a modest regional progression. These strengths should survive the repairs.

## Three largest gaps, ranked by visible impact

1. **Restore subject and camera separation.** Palworld's travel references let the player, companion and navigable environment coexist. Here the bird repeatedly eclipses the trainer/route (02, 04, 06, 07, 32), while 16/18 erase gameplay altogether and 20 cannot function as a vista. Fix camera obstruction and lateral staging first; separately refine the bird's noisy body/cyan tail art. Do not solve this by shrinking creatures.
2. **Make the land and route continuous authored surfaces.** The Cloudreach board uses coherent stacked cliff masses and terraces; Palworld's plateau/path frames tie worn ground, vegetation edges and rocks together. These frames expose turf walls, straight path borders, smooth bare carpet and disconnected rock treatments (13, 21, 22, 26, 28, 32). This affects nearly every view and is chiefly terrain, material and placement work, with scoped cliff/foliage art needed where existing forms cannot carry the silhouette.
3. **Turn landmarks and settlements into inhabited destinations with a visible ending.** The boards integrate buildings into the heights and connect approaches to useful spaces; Palworld's base view communicates occupation through activity and purpose. Cliffhold remains a house cluster (25), the aviary arrives late as an isolated shell (29, 33, 34), and the aftermath is unreadable (35, 36). Repair approach composition, terrace/threshold construction, civilian staging and comparable post-finale cues. Richer landmark architectural forms may require new or adapted art beyond dressing.

## Separate acceptance verdicts

**Bar A — NO.** Cloudreach's sky/stone/grass/blue/gold ingredients relate to the board, and the dome is recognizable. But the clipped shrine/perch views, turf-wall terrain, weak architecture-to-cliff integration and sparse habitation prevent the journey from reading as the inviting, layered world of the Meadows key art extended into the sky. Scene work can repair staging, density transitions, approach reveals and aftermath. Uniform cliff primitives, overly coarse shrine cylinders and the aviary's limited supporting architectural silhouette need geometry/art treatment where placement cannot suffice.

**Bar B — NO.** These are plainly aiming at a creature-adventure game, but beside the supplied gameplay references the present register remains unfinished: central subjects overlap, materials and vegetation differ visibly in finish, large ground regions lack authored transitions, and destinations lack enough visible life and hierarchy. Creature colour-block/form refinement and coherent environment art are necessary alongside scene changes; simply increasing scatter would not close this bar.

HUD was intentionally hidden, so UI is unjudged. No combat event is established by this matrix; fight spectacle cannot be accepted from travel stills. Still images cannot establish locomotion, fly behavior, collision robustness in motion, vegetation motion, popping or temporal stability. They also cannot verify claimed 400 m/100 m distances. All failures above are limited to what these staged production-camera frames actually show.
