# Cloudreach cards v1 — independent visual verdict

Scope: five supplied contact sheets and all eight individual 1920×1080 PNGs in `visual-acceptance/shots/cloudreach-c2-cards-v1/`. References inspected: the Meadows key art, all five `palworld-0*.jpg` gameplay references, the Sky Aviary stronghold board and Cloudreach creature-roster board. Rubric: `visual-acceptance/.claude/skills/visual-judge/SKILL.md`; visual targets: `visual-acceptance/docs/design/ART_DIRECTION.md`.

Evidence boundary: no source, git, separate reports, conversation history or prior screenshot verdict was inspected. A read of ART_DIRECTION returned its whole text, including its embedded status/provenance sections, rather than only the requested visual targets. That is a disclosure about the blind-review procedure; findings below are from the pictures and make no before/after claims. No performance conclusions are drawn. This subset cannot close full C2, motion, the full roster, weather, other settlements, or the full chapter matrix.

## Creature and trainer finding

The large blue-white bird companion is legible, recognizably a creature subject, and substantially taller than the 1.80 m trainer in the useful comparison views. Its pale breast and blue wings separate from the brown-green environment. The trainer's hair, blue sleeves, white collar, tan pack and dark trousers make a coherent travel silhouette. These are real strengths.

The bird does not yet match the clean designed material blocks of the roster board or the Palworld creatures. In `09` its breast, face and upper legs read as irregular mottled patches; the wing edges have crisp layered feather geometry but the body reads soft and blotchy. `05`, `12`, `23` and `26` show the same material inconsistency. This needs local creature texture/material art and inspection of feather/body transitions, not more environmental saturation. The stills do not establish that a replacement body or rig is necessary. The tilted-head pose in `05`/`09` is expressive, but its oversized wings and stance consume the bridge corridor in `09`.

In `34`, the pale winged lion subject loses internal form into near-white highlights and overlaps another pale wing/body silhouette. At this distance its face, body boundary and wing ownership are hard to separate. Re-stage and relight this reveal before deciding whether its topology needs replacement. No other species should be accepted from these frames: the distant creatures are too small or occluded for face/material judgement.

## Ranked defects by frame

Priorities below rank visible impact, not implementation cost. Short identifiers refer to the exact filenames in the inspected-frame list.

1. **`34` — the finale is a narrow bare chute, not a sanctuary built into the heights.** Two enormous almost featureless sloping walls occupy most of the view. The dome is recognizable, but its stone base, entrance and relation to the cliff are compressed into the distant gap. Four narrow straight grass strips underline the artificial corridor. There is no cloud sea or side silhouette to establish its elevated perch. The Sky Aviary board uses terraces, masonry integrated with stone, broad sky separation and an unmistakable entry. **Scene work:** open/recompose the arrival reveal, vary and dress the banks, expose a cliff/cloud side, make the approach terminate in an intelligible threshold, and separate the pale creatures from each other and the stone. **Art work:** if the current rock set cannot form ledges and broken banks without these giant smooth planes, make/adapt suitable cliff modules. The visible dome already provides the required broad silhouette; these images do not prove it needs replacement.

2. **`26` — the named stronghold approach has no readable stronghold destination.** A nearly continuous beige cliff wall fills the middle distance; the path exits right, and the tiny object on the crest cannot communicate an aviary or entrance. The left ramp and repeated thin outcrops are geometric terrain construction, not a persuasive ruined skyroad or major ascent. **Scene work:** establish a visible dome/entry silhouette or an intermediate unmistakable route landmark, reveal the ascent's connection, and break the continuous wall through setbacks and staggered masses. **Art work:** the enormous smooth extruded faces and thin protruding ledge tabs need more convincing rock/strata shape and surface variation. Density alone will not repair that geometry.

3. **`09`, `12`, `01` — cliff and cloud treatments disagree about material and scale.** Near cliffs are tan angular slabs, distant cliffs become smooth pale folded sheets, and cloud banks have repeated upright tree-like/crystalline edges. In `09`, broad featureless white voids below the bridge alternate with jagged cloud outlines. In `12`, the large pale background forms look melted compared with the abrupt brown foreground blocks. `01` shows all three treatments in one view. Altitude is unmistakable, but the coherent airy limestone landscape of the board is not. **Scene work:** separate cloud layers in depth, soften the abrupt white field, arrange near/mid/far masses with deliberate overlap and hue progression. **Art work:** author or adapt cloud silhouettes with rounded billows and a consistent cliff family with broken vertical buttresses and strata. A blue tint or increased cloud quantity cannot fix tree-shaped cloud contours or folded-sheet rock silhouettes.

4. **`05` — Galefoot reads as an ordinary flat village with its altitude hidden.** The house family is cohesive and the lane leads toward a person, fire/camp elements and a small central building. Those support settlement readability. But the two foreground roofs fill the sides, the far skyline is almost empty, and no overlook, anchored structure, cliff-supported terrace or recognizable skyroad establishes why this is a Cloudreach settlement. The road/grass boundary is a hard green-edged strip; broad foreground lawn and plaza remain sparse. **Scene work:** retain the coherent houses, expose an overlook and upper/lower terrain relationship, connect settlement props into occupied thresholds and work areas, and blend the road edges. The bird's right wing also disappears into the roof edge, while a support pole crosses the body area; move its stopping position. **Art work:** only necessary if no installed/adaptable support, anchor or terrace pieces can express the height-aware setting. A new house family is not justified by this image.

5. **`01` — strong gate read, weak ground integration.** The path clearly aims at the arch; its banners, tree, wagon and cliffs give the eye distinct landmarks. But a straight dark edge runs beside the path, the lawn shows repeating blur/checker texture, and dense grass lies in rectangular pads separated by almost empty turf. The gate is pressed against a bulky spiral-like rock stack and its top is cropped, weakening the full silhouette. The foreground speckled grey boulder has a different material register from the tan cliffs. **Scene work:** naturalize verge transitions, reshape grass pads into irregular edges and clearings, reconcile the foreground rock treatment, and show the gate's upper silhouette and landing. **Art work:** improve the close ground/rock material set where simple re-use cannot reconcile their surface scale.

6. **`12` — the route is legible but its foreground vegetation looks like an unrelated asset family.** The saturated lime-green broad blades at lower right are flat, huge and sharply outlined beside fine grass and small flowers. Sparse black-green blade clusters across the lawn look like isolated strokes rather than a grounded ecological layer. Trees repeat a narrow trunk with small high foliage masses, making the view feel placed rather than naturally wind-shaped. **Scene work:** remove or restyle that clump, cluster verge cover with stones and worn edges, vary tree grouping and reserve the distant creature as a clear lure. **Art work:** improve the grass/leaf material and silhouette family if existing assets cannot be made consistent. The main road itself remains obvious.

7. **`23` — upper-route reverse view has useful layering but little regional narrative.** The winding path and successive rocks create the strongest ordinary-route depth in this subset. The left cliff and clouds communicate height. Yet broad angular grass-topped slabs and clipped rectangular grass masses dominate; the one large tree is isolated on a block like a display prop. Beyond the green glow, the reverse view lacks a distinctive previously visited landmark that would connect the journey. **Scene work:** establish that landmark, integrate the tree into a convincing pocket of soil/vegetation, and vary the stepped rock arrangement and verge shapes. **Art work:** the same cliff/ground family repair described above applies; this frame does not require a unique new hero asset.

8. **`30` — night is distinct and navigable, but the lighting hierarchy is inverted.** The trainer's legs and path remain readable and the blue distance is coherent. The gate's cool lamps provide a destination. However, grass pads glow almost mint-white while the gate stone and surrounding terrain are very dark, making the vegetation the brightest large objects. The cyan-lit gateway has little of the warm inhabited refuge seen in the key-art night or aviary night view. The flat bright crescent/partial moon is clipped at the top. **Scene/material work:** reduce grass brightness relative to its ground, create restrained warm inhabited light at the threshold, preserve cool moonlit distance, and adjust moon/camera framing. This is not evidence for needing a new scene-wide night asset set.

## Additional bridge and scale observations

`09` clearly demonstrates exposure, a continuous timber crossing, rope rails and a distinct bell gateway. These are good Cloudreach cues. The companion entirely blocks the central travel line and obscures the far landing; move its bridge travel/idle staging and provide a usable reveal beyond it without shrinking the creature. The deck reads as a smooth repeated wood surface, with little visible individual plank or rope construction at this close distance. Local bridge material/detail art would improve it. Still images cannot prove collision, usable width or traversal behaviour.

The trainer and companion maintain broadly plausible relative scale across these frames; no evidence supports shrinking either. The oversized cliff geometry is an articulation problem, not inherently a metres problem. The distant pale lion's relative size cannot be reliably measured against the trainer because they are at different depths.

## Small-size and set-level judgement

On the contact sheet, the blue companion, trainer, gate, path and dome remain identifiable. `05` reads village, `09` reads bridge, and `30` reads night. The ordinary route frames are very similar brown-green lanes flanked by the same bird and blocky rock treatment; their individual locations are much less identifiable. The scene language is consistent enough to be one game, but currently repetitive rather than a sequence of authored discoveries. No UI is shown, so no interface acceptance is possible.

## The three largest gaps from the references

1. **Creature presentation and material unity — `09`, `05`, `34`.** Palworld gives creatures clean body masses, face focus and distinct action silhouettes. The Cloudreach board does the same with controlled white/blue/gold blocks. Here the companion's blotchy body conflicts with its sharply layered feathers, and the pale finale creatures merge. Repair creature material art and staging first; a whole new roster is not justified by this subset.

2. **A believable world built above clouds — `26`, `34`, `05`, with `09`/`12` exposing the shape problem.** The Sky Aviary board makes architecture, ledges, paths and cloud depth one composition. These views hide settlement height, bury the finale in a plain chute and replace expressive terrain with continuous walls or folded pale sheets. Scene composition can restore reveals and connections; appropriate cliff/cloud shape art is also needed.

3. **Authored ground and occupied places — `01`, `05`, `23`, `34`.** The key art and Palworld gameplay provide worn transitions, varied cover and props integrated into activity spaces. These frames expose broad tiled lawns, hard path edges, rectangular grass pads and isolated props. Much is fixable with scene shaping, verge/material blending and purposeful prop clusters; weak close ground, bridge and rock surfaces need local art work as well.

## Bar answers

**Bar A — No.** The green/stone/blue palette, readable trainer-companion pairing, gate, bridge, clouds and visible aviary dome point toward the intended world. They do not yet carry the chapter-specific identity across this subset: the settlement lacks a visible relationship to height, the distant cliff/cloud shapes do not form a coherent natural family, and the finale approach loses the sanctuary-above-clouds composition. Scene fixes can address staging, reveals, settlement connections, vegetation layout and light hierarchy. Cliff/cloud silhouette work and local creature/environment material art cannot be replaced by more scatter or global tint.

**Bar B — No.** These clearly attempt a third-person creature adventure, but beside the supplied Palworld gameplay they do not yet read as the same kind of polished creature-adventure presentation. The visible separation is the inconsistent creature finish, empty and sharply bounded ground surfaces, and terrain/settlement construction that remains too exposed. Scene work can fix occupation, density rhythm, body placement and route/destination composition. Better coherent cliff/cloud shapes and local surface art are still required. This answer concerns the inspected presentation only, not commercial parity, technical feasibility, unseen inventory, or the uninspected chapter.

Full C2 remains open. These eight stills cannot certify the missing chapter views or motion requirements.
