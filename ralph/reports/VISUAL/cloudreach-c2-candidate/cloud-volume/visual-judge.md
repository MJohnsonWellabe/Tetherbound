# Cloudreach cloud-volume-v2 — independent visual verdict

Scope: six supplied individual frames and `_sheet_frame_matrix.png` in `shots/cloudreach-c2-cloud-volume-v2`. References visually inspected: Cloudreach Sky Aviary stronghold board, Meadows key art, and Palworld 02 (path), 04 (plateau/landmark), and 05 (base). Rubric: visual-judge and ART_DIRECTION. This is a static-image subset review, not the full C2 matrix or whole-criterion completion. No source, git, or implementation files were inspected. Judgements below come from the images, not configuration or provenance claims.

The trainer and owl-like companion have readable silhouettes and convincing relative size. The companion's eye and face remain identifiable in frame 12, and its blue/cream blocks separate it from the terrain. They establish creature-adventure intent. However, frame 32 puts two nearly coincident luminous purple creatures across much of the left foreground, with tangled appendages and another cropped face at the edge. Their overlap makes them read as a doubled body. This is a conspicuous presentation defect, irrespective of the environment findings below. The feathers in frames 01/12/17/30 also have a busy, patchwork surface compared with the cleaner designed colour masses in the Palworld references.

## Ranked primary gaps

1. **Cliffs and cloud sea do not yet form a coherent layered landscape.** Frames 01 and 12 have clear drops and far spires, and frame 30 successfully reads as a path above a cloud sea. These are positive altitude cues. But the nearby tan cliffs have broad plain faces and shelf-like protrusions, while the far cliffs turn almost white in daylight and partly merge with the cloud banks. In 01/12 the clouds read as broad smooth white mounds with weak internal depth; frame 30 shows more separable lobes and blue shadow, but long smooth ribbons still flatten the sea. The board uses pale rock with shaded vertical breaks, overlapping cliff masses and cloud occlusion to explain distance continuously. Here the near tan rock and distant white rock can read as different materials instead of the same geology at different distances. Frame 25 exposes angular, sparsely dressed terrain without a strong cloud horizon, and frame 17 reads as an ordinary grassy shelf with almost no high-country depth. **Scene fixes:** compose intermediate cliff layers, retain blue-grey detail in distant rock, vary cloud-bank scale/overlap and shadow, and expose the drop or a distant upper/lower landmark in the high-roost and settlement views. **Art work beyond placement:** the nearby cliff forms need more convincing vertical fracture and strata; broad smooth cloud forms need richer shaped and shaded definition. Screenshots cannot establish whether suitable replacement art already exists in the asset catalogue.

2. **The settlement sits on a hilltop rather than reading as a place built into the heights.** Frame 25 provides a clear route, recognizable timber houses, slate roofs and a stone lookout. At small size its silhouette is still recognizable as a settlement. Yet four detached houses and a tower sit on a relatively bare ridge, with little visible terracing, supported edge construction, connected levels, sheltered thresholds or inhabited work areas. The foreground slopes end in coarse angular faces; a few fence sections do not visually organize the exposed edges. The Cloudreach board makes architecture and vertical landform one composition through supports, connected terraces and bridges. The Meadows board and Palworld 05 also make thresholds and everyday use visible. **Scene fixes:** dress house approaches and use areas, connect the buildings with believable terraces and paths, and establish edge protection and a clear arrival threshold. **Art work beyond placement:** height-specific supporting/connecting architecture is needed if the available modules cannot express those forms. This frame does not show the Aviary, so it cannot accept or reject that hero structure.

3. **Material scale and vegetation transitions break the shared finish.** In 01 and 12, the path is a smooth-edged applied strip over blurred green ground; sparse black blade clusters sit beside dense grass blocks. Frame 12 adds very broad pale foreground blades whose scale and flat shading disagree with the finer verge. Frame 17 has a dense, nearly uniform blade carpet and a sharply bounded dark clearing; grass hides the trainer's lower body and weakens route readability. In 25, straw-coloured tall grass, thin pale tufts, green ground and detailed roof/masonry textures feel assembled at different scales. The reference gameplay frames blend worn ground, fine cover, larger vegetation and rock with clearer material transitions. **Scene/material fixes:** soften and break path edges, vary verge depth through intentional clusters, match vegetation hue and blade scale, and give exposed ground appropriate stone/wear transitions. **Art work beyond placement:** the conspicuous flat oversized blades and incompatible texture scales require mesh/material treatment, not extra scatter.

## Day/night cohesion and other visible defects

- Frames 01 and 30 are recognizably the same place. Night preserves the trainer's boots, companion silhouette, path and far rock/cloud separation. That is a useful foundation. The distant cloud sea actually reads more dimensional at night than during the near-white day presentation.
- Night in 30/32 is dominated by blue-green fill. Pale grass is unusually prominent while the built destination remains subdued; the warm inhabited light hierarchy in the reference boards is missing from these particular views. Frame 32's luminous creatures dominate the scene more than the route or tower. The night settlement itself is not supplied, so its lighting cannot be judged here.
- Frame 32 contains conspicuous rectangular dark patches on the path beneath/near the flying creatures. Whatever their cause, the straight-edged patches look artificial rather than like convincing creature shadows.
- Frame 17 has small disconnected-looking tan fragments against the distant sky at far left. They need a closer visual check; this still does not establish their cause.
- Frame 25's companion lies across a sharp terrain fold, with uncertain foot contact; a motion witness is needed before calling it a clipping or grounding failure.
- No HUD is present, so interface acceptance is outside this evidence. Static frames cannot establish popping, traversal continuity, animation quality or performance.

## Bar answers

**A — No.** The blue/cream flying creature, open sky, natural greens, pale distant spires and recognizably rustic structures point toward the intended world. The subset does not yet sustain the Cloudreach board's layered vertical geology, architecture integrated into the heights, and coherent natural material treatment. The three ranked gaps explain the failure and distinguish scene work from mesh/material work.

**B — No.** Creature-adventure intent is unmistakable, but beside the supplied Palworld gameplay references these frames still read as a visibly unfinished assembly: abrupt ground-cover transitions, incompatible material scales, sparse settlement integration, and frame 32's overlapping creatures/rectangular shadow patches prevent a coherent polished-game register. This is not a demand for concept-art pixel fidelity or a claim about unseen game content.

C2 remains open. These six views cannot certify the full chapter, full day/night matrix, Aviary approach, or continuous route.
