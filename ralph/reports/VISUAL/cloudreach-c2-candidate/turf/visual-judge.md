# Blind paired review

Reviewer: cloudreach_ground_pair_judge. Fresh context, no source/history/reports/git. Fourteen individual frames, rubric, keyart and Palworld references inspected. X=current turf-linked candidate; Y=prior buttress native. Mapping was withheld until verdict.

**X is stronger for ground/foliage cohesion**, modestly by day and clearly at night. Its grass sits closer to the ground’s value and feels less independently illuminated. Y’s brighter yellow-green day grass and pale mint night grass draw attention to separate planted patches. This is an improvement in cohesion, not whole-frame acceptance.

The creatures still fall short of the reference finish. The griffin has a recognizable face, strong silhouette and credible large-companion scale, but its mottled surface fragments the body into noisy patches. In frame 32, the pale purple flyers, dark insect and griffin lack a convincing shared material style; overlapping bodies overwhelm the trainer and route.

Frame-specific findings, applying to both unless stated:

- **05 Galefoot day:** X’s foreground grass belongs more convincingly in the house shadow. Both retain broad bare green flooring, abrupt outlined path edges and buildings sitting on conspicuous dark base strips. The griffin crowds the right building.
- **12 Windscar route day:** Little meaningful foreground difference; X’s distant foliage is slightly calmer. Giant flat blade shapes at lower right, isolated oversized tufts, repetitive ground texture and severely pale distant masses remain. The route is clear.
- **13 Windscar reverse day:** X’s distant grass is less glaring. Both show abrupt sparse-to-dense vegetation transitions, flat pale leaf clumps and an enormous plain cliff wall. Y additionally contains a solid magenta distant creature silhouette; its cause cannot be established from the still.
- **25 Cliffhold day:** X reduces the yellow glare of the dense right verge. Both retain sharp planting boundaries, visible angular ground transitions, sparse settlement dressing and small houses that feel crowded by the companion.
- **30 lower-cliffs night:** X is materially better: grass no longer looks as brightly lit independently of its surroundings. Both still juxtapose nearly black ground with pale grass and exceptionally bright distant terrain; the gate silhouette and readable trainer are strengths.
- **31 broken-causeways night:** X lowers grass glare, but a featureless dark slope fills most of the frame, the route disappears into its edge, and the companion is severely cropped. Composition dominates the failure.
- **32 upper approach night:** X’s grass is calmer, but both have hard patch boundaries, bare ground and a severe creature traffic jam. The trainer overlaps the griffin’s feet/body; silhouettes merge. Different passing creatures prevent a clean whole-frame pair comparison.

The three largest reference gaps are:

1. **Creature presentation and cohesion — 12, 32:** Palworld gives creatures clean colour masses, expressive readable faces and space to perform. These need both staging repairs and creature surface/shape art work.
2. **Ground integration — 05, 13, 25, 30:** References blend paths, cover and terrain through varied edges and natural clusters. Scene dressing, material transitions and vegetation treatment can address this.
3. **Authored depth and landform — 12, 25, 31:** References layer inhabited destinations and varied terrain; these expose plain walls, angular platforms and empty surfaces. Composition can improve locally; stronger terrain forms require art work.

**Bar A: No.** Some palette and adventure identity connect, but landform, planting and inhabited warmth remain too weak. Cloudreach boards were also inspected.

**Bar B: No.** The creature-adventure intent is clear; the cohesive polished production register is not.

Scope: fourteen stills only; no source, reports or git inspected. No motion, combat, performance or chapter-wide acceptance inferred.
