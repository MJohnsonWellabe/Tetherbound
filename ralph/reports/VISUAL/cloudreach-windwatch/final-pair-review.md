## What happens to the earlier regressions

**Landmark height and separation: improved in 007–010; not visibly changed in 001–006.** In 007–008, C lifts the roof into clear sky and recovers a distinct tower above the cottage. In 009–010, the upper mass is higher and less entangled with the chimney. Those are observable fixes to the specific B-relative-to-A regression. The low, broad cap and banner crowding visible in 005–006 remain, so this is not evidence of a universal correction across every supplied view.

**Nighttime top readability: partly improved through silhouette, not resolved through light.** C/008 has a clearer outside contour because of the changed background relationship. C/010 is less compressed against the cottage. The upper opening and roof still form a dark band with limited internal value separation; C/006 is visually unchanged. No convincing inhabited light cue is added to the lookout.

**Ornament readability: unresolved.** It still reads as a thin upright spike in 003–004 and as a projecting angular vane in 007–010. Raising it makes it easier to notice in the latter views, but does not make its identity consistently clear. The especially isolated silhouette in 009–010 makes the angular shape more conspicuous.

## New regressions

I do **not** identify a substantial new whole-frame regression in C relative to B. The raised upper mass in 007–010 is a net improvement. It does expose more plain repeating masonry and give the ambiguous ornament greater prominence, but these are existing weaknesses made more visible rather than a reason to prefer B's compressed silhouette. The first six C frames do not earn additional credit merely because they belong to another set. They visually tie B, including the forecourt-night compromise.

## Remaining scene defects and rubric outcome

The three most important scene defects remain:

1. **Square ground breakup:** tan rectangles form a conspicuous grid over green ground in 005–006 and especially 009–010. C's stronger tower does not hide this large foreground artifact.
2. **Landscape and vegetation integration:** isolated giant blades over a cloud-like shape, white faceted distant masses, abruptly bounded oversized grass beside houses, and broad blunt cliffs still undermine scale and depth, particularly in 003–004 and 007–008.
3. **Night lighting hierarchy:** pale foreground grass and bright clothing compete with dark settlement architecture. Windows and bulbs do not produce a convincing network of localized warm light, and the tower's upper level still lacks a persuasive occupied-night appearance.

On the broader rubric, C improves authored composition and landmark readability only where its upper tower separates better. Palette, overall light/grounding, landscape depth, surface artifacts, inhabited character, and creature/trainer appeal show no meaningful visible change from B. The image sheets confirm that the new separation survives small size in 007–010, but the settlement still competes with large empty sky and ground areas. C is the preferable tower presentation in those four views; its complete frames still fail both acceptance bars.
