# Galecrest visual verdict — r1

Code-blind review of rendered evidence. Read only the visual-judge rubric, `docs/design/ART_DIRECTION.md`, and reference imagery; no implementation, prior verdict, or change history was inspected. The art-direction document contains historical status and technical budgets; neither was used as visual evidence or a reason for this verdict.

## Evidence

Inspected all four native 1920×1080 production frames: `world-r1/12_windscar_ravine_route_day.png`, `world-r1/13_windscar_ravine_reverse_day.png`, and their matching `_night.png` files in `world-r1-night/`. Also inspected both production contact sheets at small size. Inspected all 18 native studio samples in `clips-r1/`: `galecrest-{idle,walk,run,attack,hit,faint}-{25,50,75}.png`.

References: Meadows key art; Cloudreach Sky Aviary and Cloudreach creature-roster boards; all five `palworld-0*.jpg` gameplay comparisons. The Cloudreach roster board supplies a style target, not an exact Galecrest design specification. No matching named Galecrest panel is present on that board.

## Scoped Galecrest verdicts

**Bird anatomy and primary silhouette: PASS for the visible standing/locomotion construction.** This reads immediately as a large crested raptor, with two wings, a distinct hooked beak, a coherent chest, two scaled legs, grasping feet and a feathered tail. The wing fans have a deliberate hierarchy of long dark flight feathers and smaller pale coverts. `galecrest-idle-25.png` and `galecrest-walk-75.png` show a distinct head above the shoulders and leg attachments that make visual sense. Relative to the trainer, it is convincingly a large creature; it is not undersized. All four production frames retain that bird silhouette at contact-sheet size. This is a silhouette/anatomy pass, not a flight or full rig acceptance.

**Face and neck: studio PASS; production face readability NOT PASSED.** `galecrest-idle-25.png` and `galecrest-walk-25.png` show a clean amber eye, dark eye surround, readable upper/lower beak transition and a feathered neck continuous with the body. The face is not buried in the chest. However, in both production route frames the pale brow, crest and chest dominate while the eye is too small/dark to carry expression. The reverse frames turn the head sufficiently downward that the crest and brow obscure most of the face. At small size the creature reads as a handsome winged silhouette without a readable eye region. A gameplay-facing head pose and clearer separation of brow/eye/beak need a production recapture. Enlarging a studio crop would not close this finding.

**Feather/material finish: PASS for the principal feather design; final close finish remains OPEN.** The navy, cream and restrained gold palette fits the Cloudreach creature family. In `galecrest-idle-50.png`, individual primaries, overlapping coverts and chest feathers read as feathers rather than a smooth wing slab; feet and beak have distinct materials. The lower belly and thigh surfaces are noticeably softer and mottled compared with the crisp wing feathers. `galecrest-attack-25.png` exposes the change from sharply cut throat quills to the softer stretched-looking breast surface, and `galecrest-faint-75.png` shows crowded, jagged feather intersections around the wing root/back. Those close views do not yet have the consistently resolved surfaces and clean feature hierarchy of the creature references. Material/bake cleanup may address the soft transitions; the crowded intersections need geometry/deformation inspection rather than global tint.

**Day/night body readability: PASS within these four frames.** The pale torso and coverts separate from grass and cliffs in both times of day. Dark wings remain outlined against the night background, and the trainer's legs and route remain visible. The day and night palettes are distinctly different. The face limitation above persists at both times. The night route frame loses some outer feather detail against the dark cliff, but not the bird's identity. No numerical contrast ratio was measured.

**Animation deformation: FAIL for sampled faint ground contact; other sampled deformation is provisionally clean.** `galecrest-faint-50.png` visibly cuts the lower left-on-screen leg/thigh mass into the studio floor, hiding the foot at the contact point. In `galecrest-faint-75.png`, the lower wing and underside terminate along the floor plane while the opposite wing remains upright; the low wing reads embedded in the floor rather than resting on it. This needs a grounded collapse pose, with wing folding/contact resolved. In the idle, walk, run, attack and hit samples, the major body parts stay attached and no gross neck tearing or detached wing is visible. `galecrest-run-25.png` and `galecrest-run-75.png` show coherent alternate foot lifts and wing angles. Three phases per action cannot establish smooth timing, foot locking, transitions or the absence of defects between samples. The high wing tips are cropped in several extreme studio samples, limiting their inspection.

## Three largest gaps from the references, ranked

1. **The creature's visible performance is not fully resolved.** The faint floor penetration in `galecrest-faint-50.png` and `galecrest-faint-75.png`, together with the obscured production eye region in both reverse frames, prevents a finished creature presentation. The Cloudreach board makes face, eye and pose clear design anchors; the Palworld fight frames communicate creature action through readable bodies and faces. Fix the collapse/contact pose and gameplay head presentation. The good standing bird anatomy should be retained.

2. **Ground and vegetation use visibly inconsistent levels of finish.** In `12_windscar_ravine_route_day.png`, the foreground has a soft tiled green ground and enormous flat blade shapes beside much finer distant grass. In `13_windscar_ravine_reverse_day.png`, dense grass forms narrow ribbons along a sharply painted path while the surrounding slope remains broadly empty. Palworld's path/plateau frames use broken verges, varied clumps and coherent near-ground detail. Rework verge transitions and clustering in the scene; replace or reauthor the coarse foreground blade geometry/material where placement cannot hide its flat shape.

3. **Cliff composition and surface treatment flatten the intended high-altitude world.** The route-day frame's left wall is a large nearly uniform tan mass, while reverse-day has a broad straight grassy wall edge and abrupt repeated shelf forms. The pale distance and cloud layer establish altitude, but the low variation of near cliff values and sparse transition dressing weaken depth. The Sky Aviary board uses broken vertical masses, strong warm/cool planes and vegetation tied to ledges; the Palworld plateau frame has nested landforms and grounded architectural context. Scene lighting, composition and ledge planting can help; richer cliff silhouette/strata art is needed where the current broad faces and ruler-straight edges remain visible.

## Bar answers

**A — NO.** These views contain compatible ingredients—natural grass, blue distance, pale cliffs, clouds, a readable trainer and a well-coloured feathered creature—but do not yet read as a finished part of the key-art world and Cloudreach board. Abrupt terrain treatment, coarse foreground foliage and slab-like cliff masses dominate the frame. The shortfall is partly scene work (verges, clustering, value separation, creature facing) and partly art/animation work (foreground vegetation, cliff forms and faint contact). This verdict concerns the supplied ravine views, not unseen chapter locations.

**B — NO.** The creature-adventure genre is recognizable, and the standing Galecrest is a credible designed subject. Beside the five Palworld gameplay frames, however, the environmental finish and creature action/contact are too uneven to reach the intended polished-game register. This is not a demand for matching concept-art pixels or replacing the bird's successful main silhouette. The concrete blockers are the ranked gaps above.

## Limits

Studio samples support anatomy/material/deformation inspection but do not prove habitat integration. Only the four production frames support the day/night and gameplay-camera findings. All supplied evidence is still imagery, despite the `clips-r1` directory name. It cannot close continuous animation, gait sliding, wing motion, aliasing, popping, traversal, flight/mount poses, slope contact or transition behavior. There is no combat event or active UI in the production evidence, so fight staging and UI cannot be judged. No target-device performance or fine on-device lighting conclusion is made. No before/after improvement claim is made.
