## Coherence, clarity and regressions

The candidate's benefit is limited but real: blade visibility is more even, particularly at night. However, more visible grass is not automatically more readable ground. These images already establish where the grassy surface and enclosing cliffs lie. The added pale linework makes the repeated straight/bent strip shapes more conspicuous without adding convincing tuft mass, ecological clustering, worn access or a clearer route.

The principal regression is **visual noise and material integration**, strongest in First Shore 002/004 and Deep Watch 012. Pale blades contrast more sharply with the darker green ground and look less like one natural ground-cover layer. The candidate does not obscure the trainer through a new large shape, and it does not make the pictured passage impassable to read. Claims that trainer visibility collapses would overstate the evidence. No obvious new whole-frame color shift or changed cliff/prop silhouette is visible outside the grass.

Both versions retain important problems: uniform coverage over large areas, repeated angular blade silhouettes, blurred/mottled ground beneath them, very simple cliff forms and abrupt material joins. Brine Steps retains useful warm timber, cyan/navy water and a clear beach foreground, but its sparse dock composition and mechanical-looking shore outline remain separate scene deficits. Painted sky, distinct day/night mood and the clear trainer silhouette are strengths to preserve.

## Separate regional bars

**Bar A — identity: FAIL for the pictured environment sample in both versions.** Coastal colors and timber cues fit Tidewake, but uniform grass treatment, smooth exposed cliff bands and weakly integrated shore/place composition do not establish the authored natural environment in the approved chapter reference. The candidate does not move this judgment toward a pass. These three stands do not establish the Veilfall landmark or the full region.

**Bar B — genre/finish: FAIL for the pictured environment sample in both versions.** Ground cover still reads as repeated strips over a simplified surface rather than the layered, grounded vegetation and terrain in the supplied gameplay comparison. The candidate strengthens that conspicuous strip pattern, so it is not a useful finish improvement despite better visibility of individual blades.
