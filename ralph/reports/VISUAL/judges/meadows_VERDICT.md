# Meadows visual verdict (code-blind)

Inputs: ART_DIRECTION.md, RUBRIC.md, ref/ (Meadows key art, Meadows asset board, Palworld 01–05), frames/ (30 env + 23 place captures, both contact sheets). I studied both sheets at small size first. Then I opened 45 individual frames at full size: every env night frame (003, 006, 009, 012, 015, 018, 021, 024, 027, 030), every place frame, and the dusk frames 002, 011, 014 and 026. I cropped two of them for close inspection (004 env paws, 008 place deer).

**Evidence caveat:** `manifest_places.jsonl` is a byte-for-byte copy of `manifest_env.jsonl` (same md5). Place labels and the "off-road stand" flag were therefore unavailable. Place frames are identified by filename only. I could not tell which place frames were taken from an off-road stand, so every place frame is judged as the approach it claims to be.

Frame shorthand: `E###` = `###_meadows_env_*.png`, `P###` = `###_meadows_place_*.png`. Pixel coordinates refer to the 1280×720 frame.

## Silhouette test (contact sheets at ~30%)

- **Still readable:** the trainer in every frame. The white back panel and blue sleeves keep reading, including at night. The badger companion's white face mask also reads, even at night. Village roofs (E001–003, P001–002). The Burrow Warrens mound with its crown of gnarled trees (E010–012). The mill tower (E016–018). The line of pylons (E025–027). The griffins (P022–023).
- **Lost:** most place frames. At sheet size, P010, P011, P014, P015, P019, P020 and P021 are the same thumbnail: a dirt path into a green tree wall. P006 and P007 read as a green corridor, not a bridge. The three Hall frames (E028–030) are a brown box and nearly identical across day, dusk and night. At thumbnail size, rock and creature cannot be told apart in P014 (mossy heaps on the right), P019 (peach lump at bottom-left) or P018 (the pink armoured pair next to grey rocks).

## 2. The three largest gaps against the references (ranked)

1. **Places do not announce themselves.** In the key art's top-left panel, the village roofs, the tower, the road, the fence and a smoking chimney are all readable in one view. The "Starting Settlement" and "Meadows Hall" panels each have one dominant, unmistakable mass. Palworld 04 keeps a spire landmark and a stone building in view down the path. Here, P010, P011, P014, P015, P019 and P020 are interchangeable forest-trail frames, P006 is a trench, and the Hall is a speck (E025) or a brown room (E028). The good frames prove it can be done: P001 has the village, fence and rabbit herd, and E016 has the path, mill and lake. The rest of the chapter does not stage its places that way.
2. **Ground and grass register.** Palworld 02 and 03 cover the ground in a continuous, soft, value-varied grass mass. Their roads are broad and pale and feather into grass, so the frame never shows bare terrain paint. Here the ground is an even scatter of separate identical tufts and lollipop flowers on a flat yellow-green base, with blotchy round dirt pads and white pebble dots (P012, P018, P021, E010). This single register fills more screen area than anything else in the set, and it is what most reads as "procedural" and "unfinished" beside Palworld.
3. **Distance, landform and sky.** The key art layers rolling hills and groves into blue-hazed mountains with real form, and its dusk panel is a distinct warm-to-purple mood. Here every stand ends in the same flat sawtooth card. The card has no aerial gradient, glows brighter than the land at night (E003, E027, P023) and goes beige at dusk (E026). The night clouds become soot smears. The open-field frames (E022, P018, E025) therefore end in a flat line, with none of the §1 "distant mass" or horizon crossings.

Behind all three is creature quality. The badger companion is expressive and holds up. The griffins are the best creature art in the set. The deer (P008) are visibly broken, and the pink armoured pair (P018) are generic blobs. Beside Palworld 01/03, the wild creatures are not yet good enough to carry a creature game.

## 3. Bar answers

**Bar A: do these frames belong to the world of the Meadows key art? YES.**
What carries it:
- Palette and mood: warm grass, cool sky, painted clouds, timber-frame houses with fences, a rabbit herd in a paddock (P001, E001), a mill on the water (E016, P016), and warm windows at night (E003, P002).
- The Burrow Warrens mound (E010), which reads like the asset board's "creature environment" language.
- A chunky, friendly companion beside the trainer, which matches the key art's day/night vignettes.

It passes on identity, not on content. The key art's Team Tether Hall, its streams and its oak-grove light are absent or unrecognisable (defects 1, 3, 13).

**Bar B: beside the Palworld frames, do these read as the same kind of polished creature-adventure game? NO.**
The genre intent is visible: a third-person trainer with a large creature, herds in open fields, and a path to a landmark. Someone would recognise what it is trying to be. The finish sinks it:
- Carpet-scatter grass over bare terrain paint (defect 6).
- Blotchy pad paths (defect 5).
- A broken deer mesh (defect 2).
- Cyan and white placeholder rocks (defect 8).
- Stretched trench walls and untextured slabs (defect 9).
- Floating paws and cloned, interpenetrating creatures (defect 10).
- Frames with nothing to look at (defects 1, 11).

**Fixable by scene, material and lighting changes:**
- Defect 1: sightlines, staging and lures.
- Defect 4: mountain-card night and dusk values, and cloud night shading.
- Defect 5: paths.
- Defect 6: clustering and density.
- Defect 7: faction recolour and pylon emissive.
- Defect 8 (a) and (b): Nature Kit material.
- Defect 9: triplanar and slab texturing.
- Defects 10 and 11: creature staging and composition.
- Defects 13, 14 and 16: water dressing, dusk lighting and foliage alpha.

**Needs art that is not in the build:**
- The deer rig and mesh (defect 2).
- Meadows Hall exterior massing and occupation set (defect 3).
- Readable landmark silhouettes for the quarry, broken tower and Stonewater Reach (part of defect 1).
- A coherent Ironwood canopy (defect 12).
- Real sign boards to replace `Label3D` and planes (defect 8c).
- A better flower mesh and flower beds (part of defect 6).
- A Pond dock (defect 13).

## 4. Strengths to preserve

- **The badger companion.** It has a readable face, a strong white-mask silhouette at every time of day, correct scale dominance over the trainer (at least ~1.5× trainer height seated, E028), and a warm palette that belongs to the world. Do not shrink it.
- **Trainer legibility.** The white back panel, blue sleeves and orange pack read in every frame, including all ten night env frames. Lower-body and route readability survive at night (E018, E024, E027), which meets the §3.3 night requirement.
- **Painted daytime clouds and night mood.** The day skies are genuinely good (E016, P012, E025), and the night palette of deep blue, moon, warm windows and cool grass (E003, P002, E018) is a distinct look. Keep both; fix only the cloud night shading and the backdrop values.
- **Village read in band 1.** P001 and E001 are the best frames in the set: fenced paddock with rabbits, timber houses, a road in and a near rail.
- **Mill and river composition.** E016 has a near verge, the path, the companion, the mill and the lake with the backdrop. It is the one env frame that meets every §1 composition point.
- **The pylon line as a route spine.** E025/E026 march the pylons toward the finale and read as escalation. They need oxblood and night glow, not removal.
- **Forest interiors.** P010, P011 and P015 have good trunk variety, dappled light and clear trail framing. Their problem is the missing destination, not the woods.
- **Burrow Warrens mound silhouette** (E010–E012) and the **griffins** (P022), which are the strongest wild-creature art in the set.
