# Cloudreach night practicals: code-blind verdict (sheet `_sheet.jpg`, 23:00)

Judged from the pictures only. The rubric is visual-judge (lighting, colour/value, artefacts). The references are ART_DIRECTION §3.3 ("Golden hour and night are distinct looks, not exposure multipliers. Night must preserve … route readability") and §4 Cloudreach ("strong warm/cool separation rather than white washout"; "rustic stone domed aviary"; "height-aware settlements"). The Bar A reference is the NIGHT VIEW panel of the Cloudreach sky-aviary board. In that panel warm gold light fills every window, arch and dome rib, against a deep blue sky.

## Row 1: Windscar Beacon approach (baseline vs doorway)
1. **Difference:** small. The revision adds one white-hot point of light just above the top of the beacon arch. It is about 6 to 8 px on the sheet. Nothing else changed: the arch uprights, the path and the grass match the baseline.
2. **Lit destination?** **No.** At this distance the beacon is still a thin, dark, rust-coloured frame about 40 px tall on the horizon. The new point reads as a star or a distant lamp, not a lit beacon. It throws no warm light onto the arch, the stones or the ground around it. The warm/cool separation in §4 is absent.
3. **New defects:** the light seems to **float** above the arch with no visible holder, brazier or bowl. It is **near-white, not gold**, which clashes with the warm-gold language of the board. It is clipped, though so small that it is not a blown area.

## Row 2: Cliffhold settlement approach (baseline vs mounted)
1. **Difference:** the left face of the stone tower now has a warm peach wash, strongest near the red/dark figure on its face. The houses look essentially the same. One small warm window was already faintly visible on the leftmost house in the baseline and is no brighter. The other houses show no new lit windows.
2. **Lit, inhabited?** **No.** The settlement still reads as dark blue boxes on a dark blue plateau. The only warm element is the tower wash, and that looks like an uplight on a monument, not an occupied home. It gives no sense of hearths, doorways or lanterns.
3. **New defects:** the wash is **light without a visible source**: no lamp, sconce or fire is visible near the tower. The **red detail** on the tower face (a figure or banner) is now more lit and more noticeable. If that element is not Team Tether, it risks oxblood leaking onto a friendly settlement landmark. If it is Team Tether, it is correct but confusing next to the village.

## Row 3: Summit Eyrie stronghold approach (baseline vs doorway)
1. **Difference:** almost none. At this distance the dome ribs are possibly a shade warmer. The glass, the stone base and the ground are identical.
2. **Lit destination?** **No.** A glass dome under a night sky is the easiest possible shot for "lantern in the dark", and here it reads as an unlit greenhouse. Nothing inside glows and no windows or gate light are visible. It remains a silhouette.
3. **New defects:** none, because nothing changed.

## Row 4: Aviary entrance (baseline vs doorway)
1. **Difference:** visible. The dome ribs and the arch masonry turned copper-brown. A warm band now crosses the dome where the arch meets it. The stone of the facade is warmer and slightly brighter. The view through the gateway to the interior is still cool blue.
2. **Lit destination?** **Partly.** The building now separates from the sky better and hints at warmth. But the warmth reads as a **tint on the materials, not light coming out of the building**. The doorway, the space under the arch and the dome glass stay blue-black, and no lamps or braziers flank the gate. The board's night view is the opposite: the openings glow and the masonry is lit by them. The small warm flame to the right of the doorway in the baseline looks cooler and less noticeable in the revision, so one real practical seems to have been weakened.
3. **New defects:** the lit surfaces have **no source**: the arch masonry is lit, yet nothing in the frame is emitting light. The white fabric strip left of the arch is still the brightest element (it was already the brightest in the baseline). No blown highlights. The black-and-white banners are fine, with no red leakage.

## Row 5: Aviary interior (baseline vs doorway)
1. **Difference:** large. The whole interior shifts from cool blue-grey to warm tan and brown. The walls, the floor flagstones and the foreground chains are all warmer and brighter. The dome glass and the sky stay night blue.
2. **Lit, inhabited?** **Partly.** It no longer reads as a cold dungeon, and the warm-interior/cool-sky contrast is the right idea. But the effect is a **uniform global grade**. The light has no pool, falloff or direction: the wall bricks at the top of the frame are lit as evenly as the floor at the trainer's feet. No lantern, brazier, hanging lamp or lit window appears anywhere in frame. §3.3 says night is "not exposure multipliers". This is close to a warm exposure multiplier, and it makes the interior look like dusk rather than lamplit night.
3. **New defects:** the lighting has **no visible source**. The warm-lit stone under a pure blue sky seen through the glass is a mild colour clash: the dome does not pick up any of the interior warmth, so the dome and the walls look lit by two different worlds. No blown highlights and no red leakage. The trainer reads fine.

## Bar A (revision vs the board's Cloudreach NIGHT VIEW)
**No.** The board's night view is defined by gold light coming out of the windows, arches and dome of the aviary against deep blue. The revision frames only warm the surface colour of the stone and the ribs. They add no visible emitters, leave every opening dark, and keep the dome (row 3) and the settlement (row 2) unlit. At a glance they still read as a cool-blue unlit night.

Fixable by scene work (no new art needed): emissive window and door planes on the Cliffhold houses and the aviary openings. Visible lantern and brazier props with local, falloff-limited omni lights at the gate and inside the aviary, replacing the interior grade. A warm interior glow behind the dome glass. A real fire bowl or lamp mesh on the beacon with warm spill onto its uprights. Lowering the night albedo or emission of grass blades below the ground value.
