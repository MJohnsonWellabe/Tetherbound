# Stormwood environment: code-blind visual verdict

Material judged: 33 frames (E001–E033) at 1280×720, production camera, Compatibility renderer, plus the contact sheet. I opened every calm frame and a full calm/building/break triple for every stand. References: the Stormwood stronghold boards A and B, the Stormwood creature roster board, and the Palworld frames. Per the capture note, the lack of wild spawns is not counted as a defect. Hollow Crown stand 0 was skipped by the capture tool (detached rig), so I could not judge it.

A note on figures: in every frame the backpack character in blue and orange is centred and has the camera behind them, so I treat them as the 1.80 m trainer. Other humanoids appear in some frames: a green-hat adventurer, a white-haired figure in a green cloak, and an old bearded man.

## 2. Surge phases (§3.3): can calm/building/break be told apart per stand?

What changes in a still: rain streak density (sparse, then medium, then heavy), overall exposure (break is clearly darker), occasional sky lightning bolts in break (E006, E024 faint, E018 as a sky glow), and in the Dynamo break frames (E030, E033) the magenta ring. Things that do **not** change: the gold ground cracks (roughly the same brightness in all three), the Struck Sentinel's white bolt, moss or understory value, foliage pose (no wind response), copper flicker (there is no copper anywhere) and post-strike steam or afterglow (none).

Verdict: **Break is nameable from a still in every stand, but only as "darker and wetter"**, which is the screen-tint change §3.3 forbids as the sole signal. **Calm and Building cannot be reliably told apart from a still in any stand.** Rain streak count is the only cue, and it would not survive a small viewing size. The art half §3.3 names (copper flicker, wind response, moss/understory value, steam or afterglow) is absent from the stills. Motion and sound may carry more, but these frames give no evidence that they do.

## 3. Companion creature (terrapup)

- **Scale vs trainer:** Correct and consistent. Seated at equal or greater depth than the trainer (E004, E010, E025, E031) it is about 235 px against the trainer's about 155 px, so roughly 2.7–3 m. Walking (E001, E007, E013, E019, E028) it is a similar size. It is always well above the 80 px floor. It is clearly bigger than the trainer, which passes the "fight alongside" size check.
- **Occlusion and overlap:** It never overlaps or occludes the trainer in these frames. It flanks left in most frames and right in E016 and E025. In E016–E018 it sits forward of the trainer toward the camera, but laterally clear. The camera-to-trainer line is blocked in other frames by other bodies (defects 9 and 11), not by the companion. E013–E015 show a clipping issue: the walking companion's head intersects a small gabled roof or shrine behind it.
- **Readability:** The front-facing seated pose reads instantly: big eyes, a white badger blaze and brown fur. It is the most readable subject in every frame where it faces the camera. The walking back view reads as a terracotta pile of grey stone slabs; at 30% size E001, E007 and E019 show "a rock with ears".
- **Art quality:** The face is the best-looking asset in the capture. It is appealing, clean and close to Palworld's expressive cute register. The body lets it down. The rear and walking model has a clay-like, untextured-looking orange-brown surface, blotchy white patches that look like UV or texture errors, and stone plates that look glued on with no fur transition. The seated pose is identical in every calm and building frame, with the same expression, so it looks like a plush statue. It does not react to the storm: no flinch, no hunkering in Break. Its style (a smooth vinyl-toy look) also differs from the roster board's detailed fur with lightning, and from the flat blue non-companion creatures in E016. That makes three different creature styles in one chapter.

## 4. Strengths to keep

- **The terrapup's face and front silhouette.** It is designed, appealing and correctly scaled against the trainer. This is the one thing in the frames that already looks like the target game.
- **The trainer model.** It has clear blue and orange colour blocks and a readable backpack silhouette in calm and building. It stays centred and uncluttered by the companion.
- **The fixed purple storm sky** with painted, moving-looking cloud masses (E006, E012, E018). The owner's "fixed purple storm" is recognisable.
- **Glowing ground cracks as a route cue.** Gold on dark ground reads the main path instantly (E004, E013, E022). Keep the idea; change the hue and finish (see gaps).
- **Break has a real sky bolt in E006.** That single frame is the best phase read, and it shows what the other stands need.
- **Stand composition in E004 and E022** has a near rail, a path, a mid subject and a distant object; the structure is there.

## 5. Three biggest gaps vs references

1. **No Stormheart Tree, and no landmark at all (E031–E033, E028–E030).** Boards A and B build the chapter around a colossal split trunk with a blue-white lightning core visible from everywhere. These frames give a barn wall and a grey column. Palworld frames (palworld-04) likewise anchor distance with a big readable silhouette; here nothing on the horizon is worth walking to.
2. **It is Meadows with a purple sky, not an electric forest (all frames, e.g. E019, E010, E007).** The boards show deep, vertically layered old-growth, moss, crystal, wooden walkways, blue-white energy and warm lanterns. These frames show a flat open field with a thin ring of stock broadleaf trees, a uniform lilac tulip carpet, yellow floor cracks and a flat plain beyond. Palworld's field shots (palworld-02) have landform, cliffs, a cave mouth and varied ground; here the ground is one brown decal plus one flower.
3. **Creature art is inconsistent and below the roster board (E016–E018, E013, companion rear views).** The roster board sets a detailed-fur, blue-lightning, semi-realistic cute bar. The on-screen creatures are a flat-blue blob, two overlapping navy wolf recolours half the trainer's height, small foxes, and a companion whose back is clay. Palworld's creatures (palworld-01) are lit, shaded, textured and in poses; these do not hold that up.

## 6. Bar answers

**Bar A (belongs to the Stormwood art direction: stronghold boards A/B and the roster board): NO.**
- What sank it: the missing Stormheart Tree and Dynamo landmark (1, 3); the absent Stormwood palette and materials (moss, black pools, copper, glass, white-violet lightning) (2); Meadows cottages, a Meadows well and flowers standing in for the chapter's structure language (4, 12); non-companion creatures that look nothing like the roster board (9).
- What partly carried it: the purple storm sky and rain and the gold path glow give a mood, and the companion face is on-style for the franchise.

**Bar B (beside Palworld, same kind of game?): YES, weakly.** A cute, big-eyed companion larger than a chibi backpack trainer, on a stylised 3D path in a third-person camera, is recognisably the same genre. Someone would say "creature-collector adventure". It is a low-finish example, though. The decal seams, black-filled ring, sunk trainer, blue blob creature and empty plain would read as early-access or prototype next to Palworld, not as parity.

**Fixable in scene (existing assets and settings):**
- Close the forest: densify and cluster the existing trunks and canopy so the horizon plain and flat sky are hidden, cutting sky to under 20% (defect 3). Remove the grey horizon slabs, pink blob and blue castle block (14, 15, 12).
- Break up the tulip carpet into clusters, clearings and understory layers. Drop the flowers from the deep-forest stands (4).
- Recolour the ground cracks from yellow-gold to white-violet or cyan with a copper edge (2). Fix the decal projector bounds so they fade or clip cleanly (5).
- Replace the ring's black fill with a transparent or ground-tinted fill, and change its hue away from the combat wind-up magenta (6).
- Fix the trainer sinking in E027 and the green leg glow (7, 16). Move the extra humanoids off the camera-to-trainer axis and check their scale (11). Keep the blue creature out of the camera path, fix its lighting and material, stop the wolves interpenetrating and vary their pose (9 partly).
- Phase grammar: vary foliage sway, add flash rhythm (periodic whole-scene white-violet flashes), raise or lower understory and moss value per phase, and add post-strike steam or afterglow, beyond darkening (8, and section 2).
- Add rim or fill light in Break so the trainer's lower body survives (8).

**Needs new art (not in the build as seen):**
- The Stormheart Tree / Dynamo exterior and core: a colossal split trunk with lightning core, platforms, banners and walkways (1). This is the chapter's biggest missing asset.
- Stormwood materials and props: glowing moss, black reflective pools, copper vines, glass-fused scars, Stormglass arches, rod-line scaffolds, and giant old-growth trunks larger than the current broadleaf set (2, 3, 13).
- A Stormwood settlement or outpost kit to replace the Meadows cottages (12).
- Stormwood roster creatures at board quality: Voltarach, Tanglevolt and the rest are currently flat blue recolours or undersized (9, 10).
- A companion rear and walking texture and model pass so the terrapup's body matches its face (section 3).
