# Code-blind visual verdict: lightning shape / F10#3

**Scoped verdict: FAIL.** The strike footprint and actual impact are legible in both modes, but the warning does not clearly communicate a countdown to an imminent sky-to-ground strike, and the effect's shape finish remains below the owning art contract. This is a failure of the requested visual slice, not a finding about damage logic or the whole F10 feature.

## Evidence and boundary

Reviewed the two complete 19-frame 1920×1080 production-camera sequences under `shots/vis_f10_3_motion/art-shape-normal/` and `art-shape-reduced/`, their manifests, individual warning/impact/recovery frames, and chronological contact sheets generated only for inspection. `t000` through `t170` means 0.00 through 1.70 seconds; impact first appears at `t120` (1.20 seconds), not 120 seconds. Individually opened normal t000/t060/t110/t120/t125/t130/t140/t150/t170 and reduced t000/t040/t080/t110/t120/t125/t140/t170. All 38 samples were also inspected on the sheets.

Read the visual-judge rubric, `docs/design/ART_DIRECTION.md`, and ACCEPTANCE F10/S2. Compared both Stormwood Stormheart stronghold boards, the Stormwood creature-roster board, and all five `palworld-0*.jpg` references. No source code, git diff, implementation discussion, or earlier verdict was used. The manifests disclose a fixed camera, staged trainer-centred strike, pinned weather/time, and hidden HUD. Those are evidence limits, not reasons to disregard visible defects.

This package covers one place and one stationary strike in each mode. It cannot certify the chapter matrix, all four Surge phases, safe-radius behavior, audio, player avoidance, damage timing, co-op, performance, or creature art. There is no clear creature subject to judge against the roster. A sampled sequence can show ordering and visual changes but cannot establish continuous-motion comfort, aliasing, or flashing safety.

## Scoped findings

- **Warning footprint: PASS.** In normal and reduced t000/t060–t110, the magenta perimeter encloses the trainer and separates reasonably from the brown ground and green verge. The same centre receives the bolt. The perimeter survives thumbnail inspection. Exact 3 m radius cannot be measured reliably from these perspective frames.
- **Imminent sky-to-ground meaning and countdown: FAIL.** Across normal t000/t060/t110 and reduced t000/t040/t080/t110, the warning remains a full circle with broad radial zigzags on the floor. Its main evolution is brightness. There is no obvious advancing, closing, or completing shape that lets a viewer read how close the strike is. Before t120, this can equally read as an active electrical floor or spell area. Persistent gold zigzags along the path are brighter than much of the warning and use closely related shapes. A clearly monotonic progression and a distinct central impending-strike cue would separate the warning from the already energized ground; adding more global flash would not solve that ambiguity.
- **Impact identity and contact: PASS, with finish defects.** Normal and reduced t120/t125 show an unmistakable descending violet bolt terminating at the trainer's feet, white radial ground energy, and a white perimeter. The impact reads at small size and remains identifiable without the normal mode's stronger illumination. Body overlap obscures the exact central contact spot, but the convergence at foot level makes the location clear. This is a lightning hit visually; a still trainer cannot prove gameplay reaction.
- **Shape/light finish: FAIL.** In normal and reduced t120/t125/t130, the main bolt is a broad, almost uniform lavender strip with hard parallel outlines, a large crossed diamond-like branch near the top, and only one thin offshoot. It reads as luminous bent tubing more than a sharply tapered electrical discharge. The radial floor strips are similarly broad and flat. The unchanged bolt silhouette over these samples gives the impact a held graphic quality. Normal t120 does illuminate the trainer and nearby ground, which grounds the event, but lighting cannot repair the coarse shape. Refine the hierarchy of a narrow hot core, taper, asymmetric forks and concentrated contact; avoid replacing the problem with a larger washed-out flash.
- **Reduced-motion legibility: PASS for retained information; FAIL for the same countdown ambiguity.** Reduced t120/t125 retains the full bolt/white-footprint change without relying on broad scene brightening. Reduced t000–t110 keeps the footprint stable and readable, but brightness alone is an especially weak timer here. A restrained progression of shape would retain urgency while preserving the calmer presentation. Both modes have lost the bolt by t140; the white footprint is fading there and the warning has cleared by t150, making recovery understandable.

## Three largest reference gaps, ranked

1. **The landscape has almost no authored middle/distant structure.** In both t000 and t170, foreground trees and grass abruptly give way to a broad flat brown-purple horizon with a few isolated rocks. Open sky dominates the centre. The Stormwood boards organize old trees, landform and routes into layered destinations; the Palworld path/plateau references similarly use shaped terrain and readable destinations. This is scene/terrain/composition work. The boards' particular stronghold is not required in this one shot, but a convincing forest beyond the near verge is.
2. **Electrical shapes look like broad graphic strips instead of integrated energy.** Both t120/t125 show the tubular bolt and white floor spokes competing with very bright gold path stripes. The Stormwood references use tapering, irregular branching to locate electrical power within the setting. This gap is fixable through effect shape/material and scene-light hierarchy; it does not establish a need to buy a different environment pack.
3. **Ground and vegetation lose material hierarchy.** In both t000/t170, near grass is dense but thin and repetitive, while the path is blurry and the side canopies collapse into near-black masses. A hard diagonal ground tonal boundary is visible in the lower foreground, especially normal t120. Palworld's ground/foliage references distinguish worn earth, grass masses, rocks and lit/shaded surfaces more convincingly. Scene lighting, grass clustering and ground-material/seam work can address much of this. Better tree crown/root silhouettes may require authored geometry if the available assets cannot form the old-growth masses; installed asset availability was not inspected.

## Independent bar judgments

**Bar A — Stormwood identity: NO for these frames.** Purple storm light, large trunks, green understory and violet lightning carry recognisable ingredients. The open, largely empty horizon, black side foliage and broad luminous floor graphics do not yet read as the layered living electric forest of the Stormwood boards and art contract. Scene composition, canopy/middle-distance masses, material hierarchy and effect finish are the actionable gaps. Missing root/crown/landmark silhouettes may be an asset issue, but this package cannot establish what assets exist elsewhere.

**Bar B — Palworld genre/finish: NO for these frames.** The trainer has readable clothing blocks and a plausible adventure silhouette, but the empty distance, noisy thin grass, blurry ground and rigid effect shapes remain below the production register of the gameplay comparisons. No visible creature interaction supplies the creature-adventure centre that the references make unmistakable. This does not condemn unseen creature models; it means these shots cannot establish that part of the bar. Most demonstrated gaps are scene/material/VFX work. Creature silhouette, face, scale and animation acceptance needs separate current gameplay evidence and may require asset work; it cannot be inferred here.

Neither bar answer is a whole-chapter certification. The scoped impact/reduced-mode successes should be retained while the warning's temporal meaning and effect finish are repaired and recaptured.
