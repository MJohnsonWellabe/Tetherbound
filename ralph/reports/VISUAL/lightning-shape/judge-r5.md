## Intrinsic effect defects versus surrounding scene

**Intrinsic lightning art:** the weak main-channel-to-branch hierarchy, excessively sparse angular contour, fragile terminal visibility, and underdeveloped contact shape all appear in N/R t120, t125 and t130. These remain art defects even if rain and the gold ground treatment are subdued. Repair the discharge's width/branch hierarchy, luminous core and termination, and author a compact contact treatment. A blanket exposure increase would not repair the shape.

**Ground integration:** N/R t100–t130 layer a soft segmented circle, fine planar leaders and broad fuzzy gold streaks without a clear hierarchy. Keep a clear playable perimeter, but make the warning, strike and contact share a coherent edge/material treatment. The gold must leave value and shape room for the active strike. This requires both effect art and scene/material integration.

**Existing scene hierarchy:** the broad gold streaks are already present at N/R t000 and remain at t170. Their dominance is therefore not evidence that the airborne discharge itself has a thick silhouette. Rain streaks are often heavier than the bolt's branches at t120/t125/t130. The dark trees form near-black flank masses while the centre opens onto a very broad, almost empty flat horizon (especially N/R t000, t110 and t170). These are independent whole-frame defects, not reasons to excuse the lightning.

The trainer has a readable backpack, blue sleeves and human silhouette. The left NPC's dark outfit merges into the dark vegetation at overview size (N/R t000 and t170). No sufficiently readable creature subject is offered by this framing to judge creature appeal or roster fidelity; distant tiny shapes do not prove that bar.

## Three largest reference gaps, ranked

1. **Missing authored depth and destination.** N/R t000, t110 and t170 devote much of the centre to open purple sky over a thin, flat, sparsely populated horizon. The Stormheart boards use layered forest, a dominant nature-built destination and linked routes; Palworld 02/04 use landform and readable subjects to structure the middle distance. The captures' near trees do not substitute for that depth. Scene composition, terrain silhouette and placement can repair much of this; any absent final landmark silhouette needs suitable art.
2. **Electrical energy lacks a convincing primary shape and contact.** N/R t120, t125 and t130 show faint bent lines over disproportionately strong ground ribbons. Both Stormheart boards organize electricity as a strong irregular luminous channel with subsidiary branching and visible relationship to its host material. This comparison is about shape and energy hierarchy, not matching painted bloom. It needs authored lightning/contact art as well as scene value adjustment.
3. **Material and vegetation finish flatten the world.** N/R t000, t120 and t170 show a soft brown ground surface with broad gold paint-like streaks, repetitive blade accents and heavily darkened canopy masses. Meadows/Stormheart references preserve differentiated bark, moss, earth and foliage; Palworld 02/03/05 retain ground transitions and subject separation at gameplay scale. Lighting, material scale, ground transitions and foliage grouping are scene-fixable; more expressive silhouette/material assets may be needed where existing forms cannot support that treatment.

## Whole-frame bars

**Bar A — NO.** The purple storm, green understory and white-violet electrical hue point toward Stormwood, and the trainer belongs to a stylized adventure. The combination does not yet read as the richly layered natural world established by Meadows and extended by the Stormheart boards. The empty middle distance, flattened forest masses, oversized gold surface graphics and weak electrical contact sink it. This is not a demand to replace the fixed purple storm with the boards' daylight. Scene composition and value/material relationships are repairable in scene; missing landmark/forest silhouette quality and finished discharge/contact art need suitable art, not merely tint changes.

**Bar B — NO.** Beside the five Palworld gameplay images, these frames read as a less finished presentation rather than the same polished creature-adventure production register. There is a readable trainer, but no comparable creature-led subject hierarchy, no substantial authored middle-distance activity, and the lightning event is visually weaker than its persistent ground graphics. Scene staging, ground integration and lighting can address part of the gap. The discharge/contact finish requires art work; these captures cannot establish creature-art parity because they do not present a creature clearly enough.
