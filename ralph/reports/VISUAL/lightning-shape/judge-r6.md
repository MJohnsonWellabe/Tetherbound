# Lightning visual judgment — r6

Scoped verdict: **FAIL for final lightning material finish.** Warning footprint/progression, sky-to-ground identity, ground contact, recovery state and reduced-mode information retention pass the still-image check. The failure is narrow: the discharge still presents a soft, swollen main channel against very fine secondary filaments. Whole-frame **Bar A: NO. Bar B: NO**, independently of that lightning defect.

## Scoped lightning findings

### Warning footprint and progression — PASS

N000/R000 already show a broad magenta boundary with a center mark. Its perspective matches the road plane, remains readable through the rain, and encloses the intended contact location. It stays distinguishable at small size. The boundary is separated by hue from the persistent gold marks on the road.

N010–N110 and R010–R110 show successive lengths of thin angular magenta leaders reaching inward. N030/R030, N060/R060 and N100/R100 provide visibly different intermediate states; N110/R110 connect these lines at the trainer's feet. This supplies spatial progression without requiring a text label. At native resolution the thin leaders remain visible, although the gold road marks compete with them. The progression is less obvious on the small contact sheets than the boundary itself. That is a scene-integration weakness, not a missing warning.

### Sky-to-ground silhouette/readability — PASS; material finish — FAIL

N120, N125, N130 and R120, R125, R130 read immediately as a lightning strike. A main channel enters from above the frame and reaches the warning center, with an asymmetrical large right branch and smaller left filaments. The large shape remains identifiable at 20% size. It is not a straight beam, disconnected sky decoration or a solid column. Its scale is appropriate for a sky event beside the trainer.

The intrinsic defect is visible in **N125/R125**, also N120/N130/R120/R130: the upper fork around image x1000–1100, y110–150 swells into a broad, cloudy elbow, while the left-turning bend near x890, y200 reads as a rounded luminous tube. The main channel has a broad pale pink/lilac body with little distinction between a hot core and its soft surrounding light. Meanwhile, the thinner left and far-right filaments become hairlines that are readily confused with rain at small size. This abrupt change in material/readability makes the central channel look softer and more solid than the energized, tapering electrical lines in the Stormheart boards. Those boards are being used for shape and material language, not pixel fidelity.

Addressable repair: keep the successful irregular path and asymmetric forks, but give the primary channel a more deliberate narrow bright core, smoother thickness taper through junctions and a restrained softer envelope. Avoid visible swollen elbows. Keep a few secondary forks strong enough to read as lightning against this sky/rain, without uniformly thickening every twig. More branches are not the missing ingredient.

### Contact — PASS

N120/R120 place a small bright contact directly at the warning center under the trainer, with multiple short ground forks and a brighter boundary. N125/N130 and R125/R130 preserve this connection. There is no visible air gap between the principal channel and its impact. The trainer's silhouette remains identifiable through the effect; the contact does not become a screen-filling opaque ball. In normal mode the local pink light across the road and hair helps ground the event. Reduced mode has less surrounding illumination while keeping the contact shape.

### Recovery state — PASS within still-image limits

At N140/R140 the vertical discharge, short contact forks and hazard ring are absent; a restrained pink residual tint is visible around the center, clearer in normal mode. N150/R150 through N170/R170 return to the road and rain without a stranded bolt segment or ring. Thus the sampled recovery is visually distinct from the strike and warning. These images do not establish whether the transition feels abrupt or smooth, or whether the afterglow is perceptible during play.

The gold road lines are present before, during and after the event. They cannot be counted as strike recovery evidence.

### Reduced mode — PASS for retained information

R000–R110 retain the footprint and inward progression; R120/R125/R130 retain the same sky-to-ground branch hierarchy, center contact, short ground branches and bright boundary; R140–R170 retain the cleared end state. There is no visible loss of essential event-location information. The same discharge-material defect remains. Stills do not establish how much temporal flashing has been reduced.

## Whole-frame rubric — separate from intrinsic lightning

1. **Silhouette/readability:** The trainer has a readable backpack, blue sleeves, exposed arms and two separated legs in N000/R000. The left NPC is identifiable but its dark lower body merges with the verge. No clearly readable companion or creature encounter is presented at this camera; tiny forms behind the trainer cannot demonstrate creature appeal or scale. This does not establish that creatures are absent elsewhere. The creature-centered appeal of Palworld 01/03/04/05 is not evidenced here.
2. **Colour/value:** The violet sky and cool dark forest belong to a storm palette. Gold road marks occupy much of the brightest foreground in N000/R000 and remain brighter than most of the actual strike in N125/R125. They dilute the event hierarchy. Foliage masses on both sides collapse toward black, losing moss/root variation. No conspicuous oxblood misuse is visible.
3. **Intentionality:** Foreground side framing and an open road are clear. Beyond them, N000/R000 and N170/R170 expose a nearly bare, broad horizontal middle distance with scattered isolated rocks. There is little destination or optional lure. The boards compose electric woodland from layered old trunks, rooted structures and a readable landmark; this view ends in a flat sparse band. More uniform scatter would not solve that.
4. **Lighting:** Storm mood is readable and the trainer retains useful material separation. N120 shows a localized lighting response. Terrain and most trees otherwise lack enough directional/form separation to communicate wet roots and uneven forest ground. The broad dark vegetation masses are less finished than the reference gameplay's distinct grass, rock, trunk and shade shapes.
5. **Horizon/depth:** Large foreground trunks frame a flat horizon around mid-frame in N000/R000. Distance is present through reduced contrast, but the middle and far land lack layered silhouettes. The large amount of exposed sky and empty horizon conflicts with the enclosed giant-forest direction. This is a staging/landform issue, independent of the bolt.
6. **Interface:** No HUD, prompts or text interface are shown. Safe-area and interface hierarchy are unassessed. The world-space ring is legible, as described above.
7. **Artifacts/materials:** N070 and N120/N140/N170 show a conspicuous straight cross-road change near the lower foreground, especially where the gold tracks cross it. Similar surface discontinuity is visible in R140/R170. It reads like a patch/texture boundary, not organic wear. Foreground soil is blurry relative to the crisp grass strips and small rocks. The gold tracks resemble broad painted luminous ribbons rather than embedded glass/copper scars. Rain streaks are visually prominent throughout, competing with thin electrical branches. No temporal popping or z-fighting claim is possible from these stills.
8. **Scale agreement:** The trainer and NPC are plausibly human relative to the big trunks. Grass and flowers are tall but do not create an obvious contradictory metre scale. No suitably readable creature pair or companion is present to validate creature/trainer relative scale. The bolt's several-trainer-height visible reach is convincing.

## Three largest reference gaps, ranked

1. **Missing readable creature subject and relationship.** N000/R000 and N170/R170 offer a trainer and side NPC, with no readable creature personality, face or substantial companion silhouette. Palworld 03/04/05 make creature identity a principal part of the frame. Fix staging with a real, readable subject first; these frames cannot decide whether the necessary creature art exists or is adequate.
2. **The view opens onto an under-authored, nearly flat middle distance.** N000/R000/N170/R170 lack the layered forest/landmark language of both Stormheart boards and the composed terrain/route pulls in Palworld 02/04. This is primarily scene, camera, landform and placement work. If appropriate old-trunk/root/rod-line silhouettes do not exist, new art will also be needed; source availability was not inspected.
3. **Surface and effect hierarchy lacks finish.** N125/R125 combine the swollen lilac main bolt, fine rain-like branches, oversized luminous gold road ribbons and blurred foreground soil; N140/R170 reveal a straight cross-road material boundary. The references distinguish an energetic electrical core from natural material and use ground transitions that belong to the land. Bolt shaping/material work and road texture/lighting/staging repairs are addressable without assuming a renderer change. New creature art cannot be assessed here.

## Independent whole-frame bars

**Bar A — NO.** The storm palette, large trees and white-violet discharge connect to the chapter direction, but the open empty middle distance, broad luminous road ribbons and weak rooted/inhabited forest composition do not yet read as a convincing Stormwood extension of the key-art world. This is a Stormwood judgment informed by both stronghold boards, not a demand that the biome become a sunny Meadows copy. Scene composition, ground treatment and material hierarchy are fixable. Adequacy/availability of missing landmark or creature art is unproven from this view.

**Bar B — NO.** The third-person adventurer and recognisable event establish the general game category, but these complete frames do not yet share the polished creature-adventure register of the five gameplay references. There is no readable creature subject, the world beyond the verge is sparse and flat, and the foreground surface/effect finish is visibly uneven. The scope-specific lightning verdict does not cause or stand in for this broader failure. Staging and material repairs are clear work; creature/hero-asset adequacy requires evidence with those subjects actually visible.
