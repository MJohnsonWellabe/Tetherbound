Literal criterion disclosed after neutral observations

Mapped verdict: Cloudreach Medium — FAIL
The matrix does not meet the full visual bar. Several landmarks are identifiable without source knowledge: the bridge and bells at 03, the village at 02, and the dome at 11 give useful destination silhouettes. Camp windows and bulbs add some warm/cool night contrast. These strengths do not carry the full matrix. Broad empty horizons at 05/06 and elsewhere, smooth bare lawns, repeated thin grass shafts, and large brown cliff faces with stretched/repeated texture make the world read as sparsely assembled rather than a cohesive, appealing place. The supplied Palworld and meadow references have deliberate layers of foreground vegetation, midground rocks and foliage, distant landforms, and a more consistent material/light language. The Cloudreach set lacks that density and staging through many normal-distance views; this criticism does not require copying a meadow biome into Cloudreach.
Stand 04 is a particularly strong composition failure: a close rock surface fills nearly the entire frame in both day and night, so the advertised location cannot be judged as a readable environment from those samples. At 08/10, very large columns, saturated solid rings/rails/marks, and repeated stone surfaces look like exposed geometric components rather than an integrated atmospheric setting. At 09, angular green patches cut through the dirt surface; visible ground seams break material consistency. Dense grass at 07 hides the avatar's lower body without supplying a rich layered landscape beyond it. These problems are visible across multiple stands, not just one missing effect.
Day/night difference is visible, but atmosphere is largely a uniform blue shift with bright white avatar face/shirt and isolated lit props. The sky has cloud texture, yet expansive pale/blue voids, limited layered fog/depth, and little visible directional atmospheric light do not reach the requested Valheim-like atmosphere. I cannot prove dynamic weather or moving sun shafts from stills; their broader runtime behavior remains unproved. HUD text is legible, but the large right objective/hotbar/control panels cover substantial environment area and remain visually heavy relative to the small avatar and distant creatures. The same entry objective appears throughout these samples; its gameplay correctness is outside this art review.

Mapped verdict: Cloudreach High — FAIL
High adds useful distant cliff context at 05, 06, and 10. This improves the sense of elevation and surrounding landforms compared with Medium. It does not resolve the shared full-bar failures: repeated near materials, broad bare ground, primitive-looking saturated rings and rails, sparse or uniform planting, the near-total rock obstruction at 04, heavy HUD footprint, or night lighting that separates a very bright avatar from dark blue surroundings. High also shows conspicuous angular pale-green patches in the stone floor at 08. More far geometry alone does not make the matrix meet the reference quality. The dome and bridge retain clear silhouettes, but much of the world still reads as assembled parts with insufficient material and atmospheric cohesion.

Creature and proof limits
The avatar is readable, has an appealing stylized face, and has recognizable clothing. Nearby wildlife is not presented clearly enough throughout this matrix to establish the full Palworld/Animo creature appeal requirement: some shapes are tiny bright spots or distant glowing forms. Creature roster quality and animation are UNPROVED by these samples. This does not soften the two preset FAIL verdicts because the observed world, composition, material, and atmosphere deficiencies already fail the full bar.
