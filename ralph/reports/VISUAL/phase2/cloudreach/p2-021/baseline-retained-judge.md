## Completed triage

Blind retained-image triage only. I inspected the four sheets, Cloudreach board, Meadows key art, and all five Palworld references. Tile coordinates below use rows 1–6 and columns 1–4. These are **320×180 retained tiles**, not fresh captures or full-resolution proof; this is not before/after acceptance.

**Bar A — world identity: Yes, with substantial Cloudreach-specific gaps.** The natural green/stone/blue palette, timber settlements, ruins, clouds below exposed ledges, and warm sunset variants belong to the Meadows board’s fantasy world. Cloudreach is clearest at the bridges and exposed arches. Broad interiors often lose the distinctive stacked-cliff, high-altitude identity.

**Bar B — reference finish: No.** The visible scenes do not sustain the authored terrain composition, material integration, and layered landmark presentation shown by the Palworld references. This is an environment verdict: the trainer is too small here to assess character-art finish reliably, and there is no adequate creature or combat evidence.

The three largest gaps, ranked:

1. **Too many routes read as broad lawns with distant props.** Named 02, R1C1–4 and R2C1–2; Named 03, R5C3–4 and R6C1–4; Route/Terrain, R2C2–4 and R3C1–2. Foreground grass dominates, with isolated trees, a distant ruin, or a nearly continuous horizontal cliff strip. Palworld 02 and 04 use changing elevations, rock masses, foliage clusters, and paths to build distinct foreground, middle distance, and destination. These unobstructed views support a real composition gap; they do **not** prove the named shrine or gate is absent.

2. **Landmark presentation is uneven, especially around the aviary.** Route/Terrain R4C2–3 and R5C1–2 show a recognizable dome at gameplay distance. Its silhouette reads mainly as a thin cage above a low base; it lacks the board’s strong masonry base, flanking towers, and integration with vertically stacked cliffs. Three Bells Bridge is much stronger: Named 04 R4C1–4 and R5C1–2 clearly show suspended crossing, bells, stone supports, and cloud-filled drops. This is the best demonstrated Cloudreach identity in the set.

3. **Ground and architectural transitions look unfinished at even this scale.** Named 03 R2C3 and R3C1 show abrupt islands of tall grass against broad short-grass surfaces. Named 02 R4C1–4 exposes a very clean circular turf/platform edge. Named 04 R5C3 and R6C1 show a conspicuously plain yellow panel; R5C4 and R6C2/4 include stark white round objects. Their intended function is unknowable here, but they visually interrupt the natural palette. Palworld 05 demonstrates more convincing transitions among worn ground, vegetation, construction, and occupied space.

Additional frame-specific findings:

- **Broken Skyroad Arch — Named 02 R4 and R5C1–2:** The opening, grassy ledge, distant cliff bands, and clouds establish elevation and depth. The huge smooth arch faces and regular platform edge weaken weathered-stone character. Grass appears on horizontal ground; I cannot establish a grass-placement defect on vertical faces.
- **Lower Cliffs Waycamp — Named 03 R2C3–4 and R3:** Houses, tower, route, and backdrop are readable. This is one of the more coherent inhabited spaces. The background cliffs remain relatively slab-like and continuous, with less layered silhouette variation than the references.
- **Cliffhold Settlement — Named 02 R5C3–4 and R6:** Buildings and central ground are legible, but large exposed surfaces and sparse connecting detail reduce the inhabited atmosphere. The diagonal foreground pole is camera obstruction, not evidence of a broken landmark.
- **High Roost Perches — Named 03 R1 and R2C1–2:** Stone columns, banners, and a circular feature are visible. Alternate views are heavily blocked by a close brown support. These cannot establish the whole landmark’s distant readability.
- **Old Wind Observatory — Named 03 R4 and R5C1–2:** First viewpoints show enough rings, supports, and platform to suggest a distinct structure. Second viewpoints face a close wall. Treat those as misdirected cameras, not missing-observatory evidence.
- **Summit Eyrie — Named 04 R2C3 and R3C1/3:** Close geometry blocks much of the frame. R2C4 and R3C2/4 reveal masonry openings and an internal route, but none supplies a fair exterior silhouette test.
- **Route/Terrain R6C4:** Severe foreground occlusion; unusable for terrain acceptance.

Most identified gaps are addressable through scene composition, terrain variation, vegetation transitions, landmark sightlines, and material treatment. Matching the aviary board’s architectural character may require additional or revised art, but these images cannot establish what assets already exist in the build.

Day, sunset, and night remain recognizably related. Night preserves the trainer and major structures reasonably well, while suppressing smaller terrain distinctions. No conclusion here covers motion, popping, traversal, full-resolution materials, or performance.
