# Cloudreach regional baseline visual judgment — p2-022

**Regional visual acceptance: no.** The submitted pictures show a legible creature-adventure region, but the terrain, landmark construction, creature staging, and several unusable landmark views do not meet the supplied visual references.

This is a code-blind image review. I read the visual-judge skill, the supplied manifests/stand receipt, the five contact sheets, all 37 individual 1920 × 1080 chapter frames, and all four native Summit Eyrie day/night images. References inspected: the Meadows key-art board; the five locally supplied `palworld-01-boss-fight-forest.jpg` through `palworld-05-base-building.jpg` images; the Sky Aviary stronghold board; and the Cloud Cliffs creature-roster board. No source, diff, earlier review narrative, or implementation claim was used to decide appearance. Frame names below omit only their file extension. Summit abbreviations refer to `cloudreach__summit_final_stronghold__11__summit_eyrie__{day|night}__{approach|close}`.

## Creatures and trainer first

The creatures have recognizable designs. Skyrill's orange-blue sails and Ribbonray's purple trailing fins correspond visibly to the roster board. The white-and-blue feathered companion has a strong outline and is plainly larger than the trainer. These are not anonymous capsules. Nevertheless, the rendered cast does not hold together at Palworld's presentation quality.

In `06_broken_causeways_approach_day`, Skyrill's broad pale body surfaces look blotchy and bleached beside the much more finely shaded feathered companion. In `21_upper_cloudreach_approach_day` and `32_upper_cloudreach_approach_night`, the paired Ribbonrays are an overlapping mass of white faces and saturated purple ribbons; their near placement consumes the route reveal. The companion's dense dark feather marks and extended wings dominate almost every small sheet thumbnail, while the trainer remains a small brown-and-blue figure. The Summit Eyrie front views reveal a readable youthful trainer, but the night face and shirt are conspicuously bright against the dim setting. The cast feels placed in front of the scenery rather than consistently lit and composed within it.

The companion's repeated raised-wing stance gives the still set a posed display quality. These stills do **not** establish that its animation is absent or defective. Its placement is a visible problem independently of motion: it hides the trainer almost entirely in `09_broken_causeways_detail-ropebridge_day` and covers the trainer, entrance, and central dome support in `29_summit_final_stronghold_detail-aviary_day`.

## Scene-fixable work versus art-dependent limitations

**Scene-fixable:** correct the obstructed camera/stand views and companion placement; open sightlines to the shrine, beacon, bridge, and distant dome; break the repeated grass islands into varied clusters and graduated margins; add smaller-scale ground clutter at tree/rock/building contacts; reduce bare near-ground expanses; soften conspicuous road/terrain joins; vary tree scale/orientation/grouping; establish atmospheric layers between cliffs; and give the night aviary a deliberate warm entry/interior light hierarchy. Recompose summit arrival so the base, entrance, and dome can read together. Stage a visible aftermath in the matched postfinale views. These can improve pictures with scene, material, light, placement, and framing changes.

**Art-dependent:** the visible aviary kit lacks the board's articulated arch bays, buttresses, secondary towers, tiered terraces, meaningful rib joints, blue banner program, and sculptural accents. A gold sphere framework over a plain drum will not gain that architectural language from more grass. Large cliff modules need convincing authored strata/erosion and transitions at shelf edges; the broad flat leaf shapes and visibly repetitive turf need a more consistent foliage/material treatment. Creature surface art needs a common shading/detail language, especially the bleached Skyrill and Ribbonray surfaces against the densely marked companion. The trainer needs better integration of face/clothing values with scene light. These are asset/material authoring requirements, not just scatter adjustments. This review cannot inventory unseen assets, so it does not claim the necessary art is absent from every file in the build; it is absent from the submitted visual result.

Relative scale is not the principal failure here: the companion is visibly taller and wider than the 1.80 m trainer, houses broadly read as human buildings, and the gate/dome read as large structures. The pictures do not establish the intended size hierarchy of all roster roles, so no species-role size error is inferred. The repeated overlap and oversized foreground presence are composition failures regardless of intended species size.

## Three largest reference gaps, ranked

1. **Authored landscape structure and natural ground transitions.** Palworld's field/path and plateau shots combine varied rock forms, continuous grass coverage, irregular clearings, and convincing near/mid/far layers. The Meadows board uses those same compositional principles. `02_gate_lower_cliffs_route_day`, `07_broken_causeways_route_day`, `22_upper_cloudreach_route_day`, and `34_summit_final_stronghold_finale100_day` instead repeat a dirt strip on plain turf between rectangular grass masses and huge nearly uniform brown banks. This is the dominant difference across the full sheet. Scatter, placement, terrain joins, and depth lighting are scene-fixable; the rock/foliage material language also needs art work.

2. **The landmark does not deliver the Sky Aviary's silhouette, setting, or night mood.** The board makes its dome one part of a tiered, towered sanctuary rooted in cliffs, with arches, bridges, greenery, and warm inhabited light. `26_summit_final_stronghold_approach_day` barely reveals a destination; `29_summit_final_stronghold_detail-aviary_day` hides its entry; the four Summit Eyrie views show a plain cage-and-drum building on sparse terrain, with little warm nighttime presence. Scene changes can reveal and light the structure; the missing architectural hierarchy requires art.

3. **The creature cast is insufficiently integrated into readable scenes.** Palworld's creatures and trainer have distinct, clean major shapes, coordinated surface treatment, and readable relationships to the action/terrain. The roster board differentiates faces, silhouettes, and color accents within one family of rendering. `06_broken_causeways_approach_day` exposes pale, patchy creature surfaces beside densely detailed feathers; `21_upper_cloudreach_approach_day` and `32_upper_cloudreach_approach_night` fuse paired Ribbonrays into a foreground mass; `09_broken_causeways_detail-ropebridge_day` and `29_summit_final_stronghold_detail-aviary_day` sacrifice the trainer and objective to companion overlap. Placement/framing are scene-fixable. A common creature material/value treatment is art-dependent. No motion claim is made from repeated poses.

## The two bar questions

**Bar A — Do these frames read as belonging to the world of the Meadows key-art board? No.** Green, stone, timber, blue sky, and friendly creatures supply shared ingredients, but the repeated bare corridors, abrupt landforms, weak landmark integration, and cold uninhabited summit night mood dominate the presented region. The scene-fixable work and art requirements above are both necessary; palette similarity alone does not carry this bar.

**Bar B — Shown beside the supplied Palworld images, would someone say these are trying to be the same kind of game? No, judged as the skill's visible-art comparison rather than genre recognition alone.** The trainer and fantasy companions establish the intended genre, but the comparison fails on the dimensions the skill specifies: ground coverage is a sequence of hard-edged grass blocks with broad empty turf (`02`, `07`, `22`); beige cliff walls and near-blank horizons consume much of the scenery (`26` and the Summit Eyrie day views); creature saturation/detail levels disagree and overlapping bodies reduce readability (`06`, `21`, `32`); and settlement/summit staging lacks the layered lived-in density of Palworld's plateau and base-building shots (`25`, `29`, Summit Eyrie views). The ranked scene and art work above is required to close those gaps. No combat/event-quality acceptance can be drawn from this noncombat still set. Capture completion is not visual acceptance, and the obstructed/inadequate rows cannot be counted as evidence that their intended subjects pass.
