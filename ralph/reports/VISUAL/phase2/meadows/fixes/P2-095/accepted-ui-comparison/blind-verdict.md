## Eight visual rubric areas

1. **Silhouette/readability.** Both variants have recognizable broad tree silhouettes in Meadows and an arch/rock silhouette in `cloudreach-1080__A.jpg` and `cloudreach-1080__B.jpg`. The partially exposed Meadows human at lower right reads as a traveler, but brown clothing and dark equipment merge into one block; facial and clothing distinction falls away at small size. Cloudreach's central trainer and Stormwood's central subject are mostly hidden by the panel. No supplied frame gives a clear creature face, full silhouette and ground contact comparable with the creature boards or Palworld examples. That is an evidence limitation, not proof that such creatures do not exist elsewhere.
2. **Colour/value.** Both Meadows variants retain warm soil, green foliage and cooler distant rock, but the world is dim and muddy compared with the airy yellow-green/blue reference balance. Both Cloudreach frames have sky, pale cloud and grass separation, yet dull olive ground and brown stone dominate. Both Stormwood frames establish rain and a violet sky, but little visible blue-green moss or copper/glass material identity. The interfaces themselves use the requested blue-gray surface, pale edge and cyan focus. A's orange/green values are clearer than B's smaller text; the numeric text also communicates status without relying only on colour.
3. **Intentional composition.** Meadows has a useful large left trunk, path opening at right and far mass. Its visible approach lacks the reference's varied banks, outcrops and destination cues. Cloudreach's arch is a potential destination, but the broad grass wedge and largely bare approach feel minimally dressed. Stormwood has repeated trunks around a fairly flat clearing without a compelling visible middle-distance destination. Both panels are orderly; A's stronger hierarchy costs more world coverage. B exposes more scenery but its additional list rows carry incomplete information.
4. **Lighting.** Meadows' trunk shadows and Cloudreach's ground shadow establish direction and some contact. Across both variants, large dark zones lose material form. Stormwood's violet weather is distinct, but the white strokes on the right tree do not visibly light adjacent bark or ground in the way the Stormheart boards' energy does. Menu dimming may contribute to these values; only this dimmed presentation is judged.
5. **Horizon/depth.** Meadows has a cool far ridge but little visible intermediate layering. Cloudreach's cloud sea and distant peaks communicate height; its nearby grassy edge and smooth pale background shapes lack the richly stacked cliff separation of the aviary board. Stormwood's near-black ground meeting a violet horizon is the weakest depth treatment; the reference's layered forest masses are not apparent. All observations apply to each named realm's A/B pair.
6. **Interface.** A passes the scoped content and hint checks above with strong focus borders, full requirements, safe margins and no visible overlapping text. B fails through ellipsis and weak action hierarchy, rather than panel-edge clipping. Neither still set establishes navigation, scrolling, crafting success or refusal behavior. Icons are clear category marks, but upgrade recipes such as Ironwood Haft and Reinforce Axe have a mostly empty central presentation compared with the stronger saddle/berry symbols.
7. **Artifacts/material finish.** In both Cloudreach frames the foreground grass appears smooth and repetitious, with a sharp, sparsely dressed island edge; the arch masonry and large rock mass have visibly different surface scales. In both Stormwood frames, several thick straight white segments beside/on the right trunk look pasted across the scene rather than integrated glass scars or emissive lightning. Both Meadows versions show conspicuous thin grass blades over relatively uniform soil. No definite character interpenetration, floating object or broken UI geometry can be proved in these obstructed stills.
8. **Scale agreement.** The 1.80m trainer cannot serve as a reliable metric ruler here: bodies/feet or their comparison targets are cropped, dimmed or covered. The exposed Meadows person is broadly human-proportioned relative to the path and trunk, but no absolute size or species-above-trainer requirement can be certified. Cloudreach's arch is monumental in appearance, but perspective and occlusion prevent a defensible height measurement. Both variants have the same limitation.

## Three ranked reference gaps

1. **Terrain, material transitions and authored approaches.** Most acute in `cloudreach-1080__A.jpg` and `cloudreach-1080__B.jpg`, with secondary examples in `meadows-1080__A.jpg`, `meadows-1080__B.jpg` and all Meadows 720p frames. The Cloudreach board builds readable cliff strata, planted ledges and an intentional route into its destination. The Meadows boards and `palworld-02-open-field-path.jpg`, `palworld-04-plateau-landmark.jpg` and `palworld-05-base-building.jpg` vary ground cover, thresholds, landmarks and inhabited clearings. The supplied frames expose smooth ground spans, repetitive thin cover and weakly dressed transitions. Terrain/material blending and placement are scene-fixable; more varied finished rock, verge and approach pieces may also be needed, but an asset inventory cannot be inferred here.
2. **Chapter-specific lighting and Stormwood material identity.** `stormwood-1080__A.jpg` and `stormwood-1080__B.jpg` show dark ordinary trunks, a violet backdrop and conspicuous straight white marks. Both Stormheart boards give the energy a designed trunk structure, light spill, readable traversable spaces and warm/cool material separation; the roster board adds vivid but coherent blue-violet accents and moss. The visible frames need stronger forest depth, grounded wet surfaces and integrated scar/energy treatment. Lighting/composition can be repaired at scene level; the visible white marks would require better authored geometry/material treatment to achieve the depicted identity. These pictures cannot establish which required assets already exist out of view.
3. **Readable creature/trainer subjects and life in the scene.** `meadows-1080__A.jpg`, `meadows-1080__B.jpg`, the four Meadows 720p pairs and both Cloudreach/Stormwood pairs do not provide a clear creature subject. The exposed Meadows human's earth-coloured blocks have limited separation. The chapter creature boards make face, eye, silhouette and habitat colour separation deliberate. All five Palworld references show immediately legible subjects and purposeful activity or occupancy; the two boss images additionally show large creature scale and an eventful scene. The submitted UI-covered frames cannot demonstrate this bar. A useful follow-up view would expose a full trainer/creature pairing and grounded environment; claiming missing creature production or judging combat quality from these menus would exceed the evidence.
