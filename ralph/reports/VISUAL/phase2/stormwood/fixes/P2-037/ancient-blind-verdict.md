# Ancient Stormheart: blind A/B visual verdict

Reviewed the two paired sheets at reduced size and every full, uncropped 1920×1080 frame in A and B: 14 pairs, 28 native images. References were the Meadows key art, all five supplied Palworld screenshots, both Stormheart stronghold boards and the Stormwood creature roster. The current aspirational definitions in ART_DIRECTION.md govern the bar answers. No candidate source, manifest, Git history or implementation report was used to identify either alternative.

**Overall preference: B.** Its timber surfaces, visible supports and blue/gold banners make the approach and ascent easier to understand as constructed places. A's dark, broadly smeared wood loses too many of those distinctions. This is a useful architectural presentation improvement, not a completed landmark repair. B also exposes very regular planking, overly broad empty floors and stretched trunk surfaces more clearly. The distant crown is essentially the same unresolved silhouette in both sets.

The creature defect remains shared and deserves explicit attention: in both `core_arena_calm` and `core_arena_break`, the giant cobra has an immediately recognizable hood, open mouth and imposing size, but its eyes are difficult to locate and its dark mottled hood lacks the roster board's readable gold-edged scale pattern and luminous storm channels. It reads more like a large natural snake than the designed ancient storm legendary. Its pink mouth does most of the expressive work. B's improved surrounding wood does not repair this. The cyan core intersects the creature's silhouette and crosses its lower body in the image, while a dark horizontal rail cuts across the coil/trainer-head area. These overlaps damage the hero presentation even before judging the creature asset itself.

## Landmark requirement

**Set A: no. Set B: no. Neither satisfies the complete giant living tree / branching crown / visible core landmark read.**

Both establish colossal scale and the idea of an electrically split tree. At 400 m, however, the central mass is two tall, mostly straight slabs with a narrow slot, a few blunt side limbs and separated pale foliage tufts. It reads as a split tower or trunk shell with foliage attached. It lacks a connected hierarchy of major boughs, secondary branches and broad living crown. The ordinary foreground trees have more convincing organic trunk transitions than the hero landmark. B slightly improves the taper/splayed edge read, but does not resolve that hierarchy.

The cyan vertical feature remains visible at distance in both sets. Visibility alone is not enough: closer views reveal disconnected, uniformly filled polygon blocks rather than a coherent power source or lightning-filled wound. The repeated rings across the slot read as an exposed circulation shaft. At 100 m, B makes this shaft recognizably wooden, but neither supplies the rooted threshold, branching architecture and integrated energy that make the reference tree an authored destination.

The crown chamber is particularly incomplete. Its frame offers an empty ring floor, tall bark walls and enormous flat cyan shapes. There is no visible spreading crown, foliage enclosure, distinctive focal apparatus or convincing arrival composition. This remains true in Calm and Break for both alternatives.

## Shared and regressed presentation issues

At sheet size, B's warm route is the clearest improvement: the viewer can follow leftward ascent and distinguish built wood from the enclosing walls. The cobra remains identifiable in both, whereas the crown chamber becomes little more than a dark floor and cyan bars. At native size, B reveals useful planks and braces, but also a very repetitive orange wood treatment over huge surfaces. Warm/cool separation is welcome; a uniformly bright orange floor is a weaker substitute for the references' localized warm lamps, sheltered landings, variable deck edges and organic interruptions.

The tree's surface scale is inconsistent with the trainer and ordinary nearby trees. The hero shell carries long unbroken grain and stretched fans over vast smooth faces; this advertises enlarged surface mapping rather than age, bark plates, root folds or branching growth. Both versions need more than tint adjustment. The exterior scene also leaves the destination standing beyond an evenly grassed foreground with little middle-distance forest or approach structure. The key art and Palworld landmark frame use landform, clustered vegetation and intermediate structures to connect the traveller to a destination.

There is no visible companion relationship in these fixtures. A small peripheral animal-like shape near the far left of some 100 m frames is too small and peripheral to establish the intended roster presentation. That is a limit of this supplied composition, not evidence that the game lacks companions elsewhere. The rear-view trainer is reasonably readable outdoors and in B's ascent; the dark crown Break frame in A is the clearest lower-body readability failure. Neither still set establishes relative roster scale beyond the visibly imposing cobra.

## Evidence limits

The supplied frames are production-camera, HUD-hidden environment fixtures. They support judgments about visible silhouettes, placement, material relationships, overlaps and composition. They do not demonstrate traversal, collision, combat behavior, animation, continuous ground contact, weather rhythm, sound, popping, frame pacing or hardware performance. Different flash/rain instants are not controlled comparisons of storm behavior. Aftermath retains purple with lighter visible rain and no captured lightning as intended; no clock differentiation is requested. UI is outside this evidence.

## Ranked three visible gaps

1. **Complete the organic landmark and make the core a coherent focal feature.** Frames: `stormheart400_calm`, `stormheart400_aftermath_calm`, `stormheart100_calm`, `entry_deck_calm`, `middle_ascent_calm`, in both sets. The Stormheart boards join a rooted trunk to branching boughs and a broad living canopy around a continuous charged split; these frames have tall wall slabs, blunt appendages, separate foliage tufts and flat cyan pieces. **Asset/geometry need:** a convincing primary/secondary branch hierarchy, connected crown mass, organic trunk/root transitions and finished core form/material. **Scene-fixable portion:** canopy clustering, reveal composition, intermediate forest/route staging, core/background contrast and restrained supporting illumination. These scene adjustments cannot conceal the missing large-scale form.

2. **Turn the ascent and crown into a finished architectural destination.** Frames: `entry_deck_break`, `middle_ascent_calm`, `middle_ascent_break`, `crown_chamber_calm`, `crown_chamber_break`, with A's crown Break the most severe darkness case. The boards provide readable supported landings, warm route lights, irregular growth and an unmistakable culminating space; both sets offer a regular shaft and a nearly empty ring room. **Scene-fixable portion:** retain B's construction readability, reduce uniform orange coverage, introduce localized warm lighting, cluster existing dressing at meaningful landings, strengthen route-edge continuity and frame a distinct crown focus. **Asset/geometry need:** repair stretched shell surfaces and disconnected-looking rail segments, give platform edges/supports credible construction, and provide visible crown architecture if suitable pieces are absent. A brighter empty floor is insufficient.

3. **Stage and finish the legendary creature without opaque geometry competing for attention.** Frames: `core_arena_calm` and `core_arena_break`, both sets. The roster board offers a readable eye/face, designed gold-edged armour and a deliberate storm silhouette; the Palworld encounter references keep creature bodies and expressions central and grounded. Here the cobra's eyes are buried, its hood is mottled and dark, and cyan blocks, a rail, a mint disc and sometimes a lavender wedge interrupt the encounter image. **Scene-fixable portion:** remove the core/face and rail/coil tangencies, preserve floor/contact cues through effects, compose the arena around the creature and use light/value separation that reveals its eyes and hood. **Asset/material need:** restore the legendary's designed face and gold storm-scale language where those features are absent or unreadable; replace the flat block/disc presentation with finished effects. Do not shrink the creature to solve the staging.
