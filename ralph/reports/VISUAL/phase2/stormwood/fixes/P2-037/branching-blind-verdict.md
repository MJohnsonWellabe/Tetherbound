# Stormwood paired blind visual verdict — sets A and B

This is a picture-only review. I read the visual-judge rubric and viewed the approved images; I did not read source, configurations, STATE, earlier reviews, change notes, or project conversation. A and B remain neutral identifiers. Neither chronology nor implementation intent is inferred.

I inspected all 14 individual JPGs in each set at their native 1920×1080 resolution, and both `pairs_01.jpg` and `pairs_02.jpg` with individual views reduced to approximately one quarter to one third of native width. References inspected: Meadows keyart; Stormheart stronghold boards A and B; all five Palworld screenshots; and the Stormwood creature roster board. The creature board supports a limited presentation comparison, not species identification from these small subjects.

Exterior positions and phase were supplied as matching, with legacy camera basis unrecorded. Interior positions and basis were supplied as within tolerances and phase matching. Consequently, small framing differences are not treated as geometry defects. Rain, lightning and creature animation are unsynchronized; their momentary differences are not credited as improvements or regressions. These are staged stills. This review makes no claims about traversal, motion, performance, or whether unseen assets exist elsewhere.

## Overall observation

The visible trainer reads as a coherent stylized adventurer, with a useful blue shirt, light collar and brown backpack silhouette. The creatures do not receive anything close to the clear, expressive presentation in Palworld or the roster board: one small creature is peripheral in the 100-distance exterior frames, a green subject/effect ahead of the trainer merges with the trainer in the tree close views, and other distant figures are too small to characterize. Neither set establishes a convincing creature cast or a fight that reads as an event. That is a failure of the offered presentation; the pictures do not establish that creature models are missing or intrinsically inadequate.

**B has the stronger distant tree silhouette.** Its substantial outward branches and wider leaf distribution interrupt the narrow tower shape seen in A. This improvement is visible at small size in all three 400-distance pairs. It does not complete the landmark: both still look like extremely large, smooth-sided wooden shells with a cyan slot and dark repeated rings. B also makes the primitive branch construction and pale, fragmented canopy more conspicuous. The interior views show no clear corresponding improvement or new structural regression.

## Small-size, scale and material findings

At contact-sheet size, the trainer, ordinary trees, landmark slot and grass plane are identifiable in the exterior frames. B's branches are the only substantial improvement that survives this test. Individual stronghold fixtures collapse to specks; the rings remain heavy dark bands. The tree approach interiors read as a grassy field under a low black structure, not the boards' soaring inhabited hollow. Both core approach pairs become almost black rectangles.

The trainer supplies a useful size cue: the stronghold is convincingly enormous. The problem is the lack of intermediate scale cues. Long plain wall stretches and oversized bark grain provide little human-sized construction to connect the 1.80 m trainer to the giant tree. The small entrance frame and sparse poles cannot carry that job alone. Normal foreground trees have more developed bark form than the landmark itself. Creature-role scale comparisons are not defensible from these distant, differently placed subjects.

Both sets use green groundcover, purple flowers, brown wood and cyan energy, so the raw palette ingredients have some relationship to the references. The exterior repeatedly settles into green/brown below and purple-grey above; interiors settle into near-black brown bands above mottled ground. There is little warm/cool architectural hierarchy. Neither set shows obvious oxblood leaking onto friendly elements. No interface is offered, so interface hierarchy and safe area cannot be assessed.

The repeated, similarly sized grass blades and flower stalks create fine noise across large ground areas. Trees and rocks are sparse against a mostly empty horizon. This is materially less authored than the Meadows board's layered terrain, groves and routes or Palworld 02/04's terrain-led clearings and edges. Adding more identical grass alone would not close that gap.

## Full landmark identity

**Set A — full Stormheart landmark identity: NO.** A conveys a giant split wooden tower with a cyan vertical feature. It does not combine the broad living crown, organically articulated ancient trunk, inhabited tiered architecture, warm entry, forest setting and compelling exposed power source shown in the two stronghold boards.

**Set B — full Stormheart landmark identity: NO.** Its tree silhouette is stronger and clearly closer to the boards. The full identity still fails because the canopy, trunk finish, stronghold architecture and interior/core presentation remain incomplete in the offered views. Partial recognition of “huge electric tree” is not full recognition of the reference's living stronghold.

## Three largest remaining reference gaps, ranked

1. **The landmark lacks the integrated living-tree and inhabited-stronghold design.** In both `stormheart400_aftermath_calm.jpg` and `stormheart100_calm.jpg`, the boards' branching ancient bark, substantial canopy, attached balconies, braces, railings, banners and inviting framed entrance become broad simple trunk surfaces and bare dark rings. B improves the outline but leaves this gap. Scene/material work can improve foliage color, branch/trunk integration, texture scale and hierarchy. Detailed branching/canopy art and the board's architectural kit are not evidenced at useful fidelity in these views; they require authored art or already-existing art brought into this presentation. I cannot identify which from screenshots.

2. **The interior fails to present its core and vertical architectural drama.** Both `dynamo_core` approach pairs are obstructed; all four `the_stormheart_tree` interior views feature heavy dark strips and an open grassy floor, and both core close views face walls and a small opening. Board A shows an inhabited ascending hollow with clear warm-lit bridges; board B adds a strong blue energy column and a designed suspended core. Camera/occlusion repair, lighting, material values and composition are direct scene repairs. Warm fixtures, railings, layered platforms and a distinctive core assembly are absent or unreadable in the offered presentation; lighting alone cannot demonstrate that art.

3. **The world and cast are not presented as a rich creature adventure.** In `stormheart400_calm.jpg`/`stormheart400_aftermath_calm.jpg`, the horizon is empty and groundcover is repetitive. In the tree close pairs the possible companion is small and overlaps the trainer. Palworld 01/03 clearly stage expressive creatures and encounters, 02/04 combine readable actors with terrain-led depth, and 05 makes even a working space feel occupied. Meadows does the same through varied groves, hills, paths and settlements. The roster board offers distinct facial and silhouette character that these frames do not show. Terrain composition, clustering, subject placement, contrast and encounter staging are scene repairs. Bespoke creature appeal and character expression are not demonstrated here; better subject views are required before concluding whether new creature art is needed. No visible evidence supports a claim that the roster is fulfilled.

## Explicit acceptance bars

| Set | Bar A — belongs to the Meadows keyart world? | Bar B — beside the five Palworld images, reads as trying to be the same kind of game? |
|---|---|---|
| A | **NO.** Compatible color ingredients and an adventurer are present, but the empty setting, flat/simple landmark surfaces and oppressive, almost undecorated interiors do not carry the board's natural, inviting and inhabited world. | **NO.** A third-person fantasy prototype is evident, but these offered frames do not establish the creature-led, expressive, populated adventure conveyed by the Palworld references. Repetitive ground and minimal encounter/creature presentation are decisive. |
| B | **NO.** The wider branching silhouette moves closer to the stronghold boards, but does not repair material coherence, landscape depth, inhabited architecture or interior mood. | **NO.** The tree improvement is real but the same limitations in creature presentation, environmental authorship and finished landmark detail remain. |

The scene/material repair side is concrete: repair the obstructed core views, establish readable warm/cool light and day/night differences, correct oversized/repetitive material scaling and the path discontinuity, compose terrain and foliage in varied clusters, and separate creatures from the trainer and ground. The art-evidence side is also concrete: the pictures do not deliver the boards' finished ancient trunk/canopy, architectural detail, hero core or expressive creature cast. Those elements must become visibly present and convincing; whether that means making art, replacing art, or staging existing art cannot be settled by this blind review.
