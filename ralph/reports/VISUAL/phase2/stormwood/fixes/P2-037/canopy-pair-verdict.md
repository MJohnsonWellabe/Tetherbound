# Canopy comparison: limited improvement, not accepted as a fix

Independent fresh code-blind reviewer: `stormwood_exterior_pair_r2_judge`.
The reviewer inspected all twelve native images, a paired sheet at approximately
30% size, Meadows key art, both Stormheart stronghold boards and all five
Palworld references. No source, configs, diffs, earlier verdicts or change intent
were supplied. Neutral set A maps to `after-exterior-r1` (grounded baseline);
set B maps to `after-canopy-r1`. Original hashes and camera/phase checks are in
the retained manifests and `canopy-comparison-coverage.json`.

**Verdict: a small visible improvement in distant crown contrast, with a cutout
tradeoff. P2-037 remains open. Both candidate flags remain off. Bars A/B: NO/NO
for both sets.**

## Paired observations

- In B's `stormheart400_calm.jpg`, `stormheart400_break.jpg` and
  `stormheart400_aftermath_calm.jpg`, the pale foliage clusters separate more
  clearly from the sky. This survives reduction, especially in Calm and aftermath.
  The clusters also appear flatter and more conspicuously attached to narrow
  prongs. A integrates more softly at distance but is less identifiable.
- The three `stormheart100_*` pairs show no substantial near-view improvement:
  the same large forms, approach, spiral bands, materials and entrance framing
  remain. Different rain positions and a small creature's position do not prove
  an art improvement. These views exclude the crown.
- The split trunk, cyan central feature, surrounding trees, vegetation, trainer
  and broad weather/aftermath appearance persist. No obvious major silhouette
  loss was found. This is static preservation evidence only.

## Remaining defects

1. **Tree identity and construction.** Both sets' `stormheart400_calm.jpg` and
   `stormheart400_aftermath_calm.jpg` read as a split wooden tower with a few
   small crowns. The boards establish branching structural limbs, a spreading
   canopy, supported platforms and inhabited routes. The supplied landmark lacks
   that hierarchy. In `stormheart100_calm.jpg` and
   `stormheart100_aftermath_calm.jpg`, enormous uninterrupted bark surfaces and
   dark spiral ribbons make the scale feel like enlarged geometry. Human-scale
   plank divisions, supports, railings and occupied platforms are difficult to
   read; bark streaks look stretched on broad angled surfaces.
2. **Environment layering.** `stormheart400_aftermath_calm.jpg` has repetitive
   grass/flower scatter over broad soil with little middle-distance forest or
   terrain layering. Three prominent trees do not establish reference-level
   woodland richness. More scatter alone would not resolve it.
3. **Focal light and material readability.** In `stormheart400_break.jpg` and
   `stormheart100_break.jpg`, the white-purple sky takes the strongest emphasis
   while the landmark and trainer darken and the cyan centre stays comparatively
   weak. Aftermath improves visibility, but the natural value variety is limited.

The trainer's backpack/silhouette remains readable, particularly after the
storm, but rear views cannot establish expressive character quality. The small
creature is too distant and inconsistently exposed for species-scale or appeal
judgement. Thin railing silhouettes and stretched material appearance remain;
the review does not establish z-fighting, chunk seams or temporal artifacts.

**Bar A: NO.** Natural greens and wood connect partially to the Meadows board,
but sparse world structure, purple-dominated values and tower-like landmark
prevent the match. **Bar B: NO.** The views lack the Palworld references'
authored environmental depth, readable construction and visible creature presence.

Clustering, middle-distance composition, atmosphere and focal lighting are scene
work. The landmark needs substantial geometry/material and construction-detail
work. These frames do not prove that sufficient art exists elsewhere. Static
images cannot establish motion, performance, traversal or progression.
