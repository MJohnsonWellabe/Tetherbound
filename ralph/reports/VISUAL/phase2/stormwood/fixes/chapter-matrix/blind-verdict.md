# Stormwood chapter matrix — blind visual verdict

Reviewed 2026-09-28. Code-blind picture review under `.claude/skills/visual-judge/SKILL.md`. No source, STATE, fixes notes, or earlier verdict was inspected. All 30 supplied native 1920 × 1080 images were opened individually at original detail; all three supplied compact sheets were inspected with the frames at small size. This is a still-image judgement, without claims about performance, animation, progression, traversal, hidden encounters, or offscreen expressions.

## Character and creature art first

The visible creatures do not yet carry the presentation as strongly as the Palworld references. In `ashfoot_aftermath_calm`, the large bird has a readable beak, legs, layered feathers, and eye, but virtually its entire body is one saturated cobalt-blue material. Head, belly, wings, and feet lack the clear value and material separation that makes the roster board's Stormraven distinctive. At sheet size the birds in `ashfoot_calm`, `arrival_calm`, and `arrival_aftermath_calm` reduce to blue lumps that can initially be read as props. In `ashfoot_break`, the nearer bird's eye and head become clearer, but its dark body still merges into the tree and field. The problem is visible material hierarchy and presentation; these pictures do not establish the asset's provenance.

The trainer's blue sleeves, brown backpack, and pale collar make a coherent small adventurer silhouette. Their boots and lower legs disappear against dark soil in `forest_break` and `giant_break`; foliage hides much of the body in the giant and arrival compositions. The trainer has a markedly more textured finish than the flat flower petals and simple grass blades around them. These back views do not support conclusions about facial expression or facial quality.

The partially visible bear at the far left of `forest_aftermath_calm` and the golden, leaf-backed small quadruped at right in `pocket_verge_ash_hollow_a_approach` provide some species identity. Neither supplies enough clean, unobstructed subject coverage to establish the appeal of the whole roster. I do not infer that unpictured species are absent.

## Small-size read and frame-specific defects

The compact sheets consistently read as a purple-sky biome with green ground cover. The storm images have a clearly stronger weather silhouette than the calm images; the aftermath images are visibly brighter and have less conspicuous road energy. Those are observable picture differences, not verification of a runtime transition. Roads, gold beacons, and cyan accents survive reduction. In contrast, forest depth, creature detail, and the stronghold's organic identity do not.

## Three ranked reference gaps

1. **Creature-led visual appeal and material hierarchy.** Palworld 01, 03, and 04 give creatures bold body shapes, differentiated faces and limbs, and clear separation from the world. The roster's Stormraven has dark feather masses, deliberate electric accents, and a distinct head/eye focus. In `ashfoot_aftermath_calm`, `ashfoot_break`, and `arrival_aftermath_calm`, the birds are substantially one blue material and lose that hierarchy, especially on the compact sheets. The trainer and creatures also have a much denser surface treatment than nearby flat plant geometry. Material remapping, light, clearings, and staging can address a substantial portion. Any missing intentional feather/body masks or distinctive electric detail needs authored material/texture art, not more scatter. These images do not prove the whole creature mesh must be replaced.

2. **An authored landscape with depth and readable light.** The Meadows board and Palworld 02, 04, and 05 use terrain masses, vegetation clusters, clearings, shadow/light separation, and inhabited prop groupings to build places. `glass_aftermath_calm`, `rod_line_aftermath_calm`, `reverse_route_aftermath_calm`, and `arrival_aftermath_calm` expose large plain distant strips behind a repeated foreground plant layer. `forest_break` and `giant_break` go to the opposite extreme: dark overlapping trunks erase the spatial hierarchy. These are primarily scene, terrain, material, planting, and lighting changes. More individual grass blades alone would not address the bare distance or black trunk wall.

3. **The Stormheart's organic, inhabited landmark language.** Both Stormheart boards show a huge branching tree with crafted structures and readable energy woven into it. `stormheart400_aftermath_calm` instead reads as a split cylinder, while `stormheart100_aftermath_calm` exposes smooth walls, stretched bark, dark ribbon floors, and disconnected cyan shapes. Composition and contrast can improve discovery, and material/effect work can improve bark and the core, but the silhouette, roots, crown, entrance, and visible structural detailing require authored geometry and surface art absent from the pictured result. Re-lighting this shape will not reproduce the board's landmark language.

## Separate bar decisions

**Bar A — Do these frames read as belonging to the world of `tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`? No.** There are shared ingredients: the backpacked adventurer, green vegetation, warm lanterns, a natural dirt route, and fantasy energy. The persistent purple weather itself is not the failure. The deciding gap is the loss of the board's organic grouping, readable natural value structure, and inhabited landmark composition: the black trunk wall in `forest_break`, exposed plain distance in `arrival_aftermath_calm`, and geometric stronghold in both Stormheart distances do not yet form a convincing regional extension of that world.

**Bar B — Shown beside the five Palworld screenshots, would someone say these are trying to be the same kind of game? No, as a complete visual presentation at the specified bar.** The third-person trainer and large fantastical animals make the intended genre understandable. What the submitted pictures primarily communicate, however, is a sparsely assembled exploration scene with repeated foliage and a few isolated creature props. They do not yet communicate the creature-led, visually authored world visible across the five Palworld references. `ashfoot_aftermath_calm` provides the best counterexample because its bird is large and identifiable; its flat blue material and the almost empty backdrop still prevent it from carrying the comparison. This judgement does not assert that the game lacks creature mechanics or combat outside these images.

**Scene/material/composition work:** raise useful separation inside the forest without flattening all lighting; open view corridors; reduce player occlusion; cluster vegetation and props; compose clearings around creatures and entrances; add intermediate/background landform and tree masses; integrate the road surface and repair the rectangular energy joins; frame Stormheart without the overlapping foreground tree; vary and simplify the pocket's repeated branch cage. The gold pocket focal axis and road readability are worth preserving.

**Art required beyond placement or exposure:** a convincing organic Stormheart silhouette and visible crafted architectural kit; improved bark/wood treatment across that large surface; irregular, materially credible glass-scar shards; deliberately authored creature material masks/accents where the current blue bird surface cannot provide them. The frames only establish that this finished art is missing from the visible build presentation. A blind image review cannot audit whether unused suitable assets already exist elsewhere in the repository. No recommendation here assumes they do.
