# Colony 06 — blind visual verdict

Reviewed the contact sheet, all six supplied 1920×1080 frames with `view_image` at original resolution, the Meadows key art, both Water Realm boards, and all five supplied Palworld references. A means baseline; B means candidate. This verdict concerns these three daytime close views only. No source, configuration, implementation description, or prior review was consulted. No scores.

**Result: no convincing visible improvement from A to B.** Small differences in grass silhouettes and local tuft presence do not change the environment's organization, material reading, or small-size composition. No convincing broad regression is demonstrated either. The three views cannot support chapterwide acceptance.

The trainer is the clearest successful element: the blue shirt, brown pack, dark trousers, hair, and grounded shadow stay distinguishable against the sand in every pair. The visible back view belongs broadly to the stylized adventure vocabulary of the references. It does not demonstrate facial appeal or animation quality. Creatures are too distant, partly obscured, or low-contrast to carry the creature-focused promise of the roster board or Palworld frames. That is a visible presentation failure; these images cannot establish whether their underlying models are good or bad.

## Pair findings

**Gull Rest — no convincing improvement.** B appears to thin some grass along the middle-right slope, but retains the same large empty foreground, isolated repeated blade clusters, and distant dense strip. It does not create a stronger dune-grass colony or a clearer destination. At contact-sheet size both versions read as a trainer facing an enormous dark wall with pale sand below. The warm sand and blue sky are compatible coastal colors, but the wall's continuous mottled material and steep rounded profile dominate the scene. Its sawtooth sand boundary at the bottom looks mechanically stepped. The blue banner helps locate a distant route; the pale and colored shapes near it and on the right slope do not resolve into convincing creature subjects. Trainer contrast, materials, lighting, and overall environment reading are effectively unchanged.

**Sluice Isle — no convincing improvement.** Both versions retain a denser grass patch on the left, a mostly empty sand foreground, scattered isolated low plants on the right, and a thin grassy crest. This is more organized than uniform all-over scatter, but the narrow repeated upright tufts do not build a substantial dune-grass mass. A huge gray slope occupies the central destination area. Its nearly continuous fine texture offers little structural scale, while a horizontal dark band across its left half makes the surface look striped. The three trees on the left repeat a similar canopy/trunk vocabulary. The pale creature-like form on the crest merges with the pale sand; the darker form low in the left gap has weak separation from its surroundings. Minor silhouette differences do not make either subject legible at sheet size. The distant waterfall supplies a depth cue, but the foreground wall masks most of the regional vista. No readable twin-pump identity is proven by this view, regardless of the filename.

**First Shore — no convincing improvement.** A and B have essentially the same useful composition: the ridge leads toward a blue-bannered wooden arch, the sea opens the left side, and grass borders the right side of the trainer's clear space. This is the strongest of the three views for route and landmark readability. The grass patch nevertheless remains a belt of similar thin upright tufts, with little low basal body or varied clump silhouette. Large gray ribbons on successive slopes and sharply faceted green-bottomed boulders compete with the smoother sand and more detailed trainer. The water reads as a broad pale cyan field with little visible surface character. The blue creature near the left shoreline becomes a small cyan mark in the contact sheet. B does not convincingly improve these relationships.

## Shared reading and limits

The images communicate a sunny sandy archipelago consistently. Strong light/dark contrast keeps the trainer legible, but much of that contrast comes from enormous gray slopes rather than layered natural features. Lake Michigan dune direction is suggested by pale rippled sand and sparse grasses; repeated cliff-like gray faces overpower the sand-dune identity. Improving that direction does not require turning beaches into lush meadows or adding fortress props.

There is no visible HUD to assess. No conclusion about motion, performance, popping, or whole-island coverage follows from these stills. The trainer provides a plausible human scale for nearby grass and boulders, but different creature distances and partial occlusion prevent a reliable relative creature-size judgment. There is no basis here to declare a species incorrectly scaled.

## Three largest reference gaps, ranked

1. **Creature presence and readability — all three pairs, especially Sluice Isle and First Shore.** Palworld's field and base views present recognizable creatures with readable bodies, faces, and separation from terrain; the Water Realm roster gives each creature a distinct silhouette. These frames leave creatures as distant patches. Scene-fixable work: stage at least one clear creature along the visible route, separated from the pale crest or cyan shoreline, at a useful apparent size. The stills do not prove that replacement creature or trainer art is needed; a closer view is necessary to judge that.
2. **Terrain form and material hierarchy — Gull Rest and Sluice Isle, also First Shore's gray ribbons.** The Meadows and Veilfall boards use distinct landform masses, ledges, rock shapes, and vegetation transitions; Palworld's plateau view resolves cliff structure at a glance. Here, smooth steep masses carry almost continuous mottled gray texture, with conspicuous material boundaries. Scene-fixable work: reduce the wall's dominance and shape gentler sand slopes, selective exposed-rock areas, and clearer layered silhouettes. Art/material work: convincing rock structure and less homogeneous surface treatment require revised material or rock assets if suitable art is unavailable. This blind review cannot inventory the build.
3. **Vegetation mass and authored ground transitions — all three pairs.** Palworld's path and base views distinguish traversable openings from substantial irregular foliage masses; the Meadows key art carries similar clustered edge composition. These dunes mostly use isolated narrow tufts or uniform belts. Scene-fixable work: organize irregular colonies around dune shoulders and hollows, vary patch size and density, and retain intentional sand clearings. Art work: fuller multi-stem dune-grass clumps with varied silhouettes and credible bases would address the thin-stroke appearance; whether existing assets can supply them is unproven.

**Limited Bar A — No.** These three views share the project's natural palette and readable adventurer, but their dominant uniform slopes, weak vegetation masses, and barely legible creatures do not yet read convincingly as the richly composed world of the Meadows art direction. Scene composition and terrain/vegetation arrangement can address much of this; the material and grass-shape gaps may require revised art.

**Limited Bar B — No.** Beside the supplied Palworld frames, this pilot does not yet convincingly present the same creature-centered, inhabited adventure experience. The trainer alone carries that association; creatures and environment interactions are too visually remote. Better staging and ground organization are scene changes. Whether the creatures themselves need new art remains unproven by these views. This is a verdict on the supplied pilot images, not chapterwide acceptance or rejection.
