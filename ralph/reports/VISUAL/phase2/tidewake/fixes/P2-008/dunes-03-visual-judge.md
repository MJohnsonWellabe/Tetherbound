## Dune ecology and coastal surface judgment

**Direction visibly improved; finished item acceptance: no.** All seven candidate views now share pale sand, open dune ground, and straw/green grass rather than the baseline's meadow carpet and gray rock bands. FirstShorehorizonroute gives the strongest Great Lakes dune impression: open water, sand islands, beach grass, and sparse deciduous trees. FirstShorewelcome also establishes a useful bare crest beside a grass pocket. The ecological direction is evident even in the compact sheets.

The result still resembles a sand coating over broad cut banks more than a convincing dune landscape in several required views. Sand acreage by itself is not the missing ingredient. The remaining work is to make dune profiles, rooted grass colonies, exposed sheltered ground, and shoreline transitions agree.

- **FirstShorewelcome:** The right-hand grass colony has a ruler-like lower edge and nearly uniform height. Its pale tips disappear into the sand at sheet size. Break this into interlocking clumps with sparse outliers and local olive/straw value variation. The foreground left bank has an abrupt dark slit and a steep, smooth face; its shape reads as a cut rather than accumulated sand. The wooden gate is readable, but its lower structure disappears behind the crest, leaving little visual connection to a traveled approach.
- **Shellwatchjetty:** The dune and grass are recognizable, but the entire frame is an enormous smooth sandy slope with two broad grass bands. There is no visible jetty or coastal destination. The almost white blades merge with the hill into low-contrast fuzz at sheet size. Add irregular rooted colonies and a visually useful break in the slope or destination sightline; merely filling the blank with more identical grass will not solve it.
- **Sluicepumps and Sluicepumpsroute:** The central near-vertical sand wall occupies most of the scene. Its flat-topped profile, clean upper edge, and uninterrupted face read like a sheer terrain block covered in sand. A pale repeating curved pattern is visible on its left face. Grass follows thin contour ribbons along the base and crest, reinforcing the manufactured outline. No twin pumps are identifiable. Reshape the bank into plausible dune slopes or reveal a distinct exposed substrate where such steepness is required, and connect vegetation to sheltered pockets rather than perimeter strips.
- **Gullbeach:** The large left bank has triangular scallops along its upper shadow edge and a sawtooth boundary at its foot. A vertical surface break near the upper center-left is also visible. These are visible terrain/shading artifacts, not appealing sand erosion. The dark face is otherwise almost featureless. The right grass is an improvement in biome language, but the isolated bright broadleaf plant near the blue banner and the smooth sand all around it lack a coherent planted pocket. A tiny upright figure and green object appear on the steep upper-right slope; the picture offers no readable footing for them.
- **FirstShorehorizonroute:** This is the best overall coastal composition, but nearby grass blades are straight, flat, pale spikes with dark green bases and little sense of individual rooted tussocks. The Sleeping Bear reference supplies long bending leaves, green/straw variation, and irregular colonies separated by exposed sand; those are useful ecological cues without requiring photorealism. Across the water, angular dark cuts and terrace-like shores remain conspicuous. Water reads as an almost uniform cyan field with a thin foam outline rather than a convincing shallow-to-deep coastal transition.
- **SaltCrownwalk01:** Foreground grass is a dense, bright picket-like mass that obscures the trainer's legs; nearly all blades share the same light tip. The opposite bank is a blank dark brown wall with a thin bright grass trim. Grass can plausibly be knee or thigh high beside a 1.80 m trainer, so height alone is not the defect: uniform blade form, color, and density are. The central tree is a strong silhouette, but its dense dark crown and the blank bank crowd the modest creature focal point. No shrine is legible in this view.

## Matched baseline changes and regressions

The broad biome change is a real improvement toward the owner's Lake Michigan direction. The baseline's continuous green grass, mossy ground, and gray rock cliffs did not read as a dune coast. The candidate also gives the trainer more separation and offers clearings rather than grass everywhere.

There are visual regressions alongside that improvement:

- **Shellwatchjetty, FirstShorewelcome, and both Sluice views:** The candidate loses much of the baseline's dark vegetation structure and surface/value differentiation. Sand and grass tips now occupy almost the same light value across broad areas, making the scene emptier and flatter. Restoring a meadow carpet would undo the direction; stronger local grass values, dune relief, and a few placed vegetation groups can recover structure within the dune palette.
- **Gullbeach:** Baseline stone texture partly disguised the jagged bank shading. Candidate smooth sand exposes the scalloped and sawtooth boundaries much more strongly. The cliff geometry itself was already severe in the baseline; its increased visual prominence is the regression.
- **FirstShorehorizonroute:** The far shore loses its baseline distinction between green plateau, dark rock, and warm beach. Candidate terrain is more ecologically aligned but becomes one pale surface from summit to water. Keep the dune identity while introducing slope shading, sheltered vegetation, and readable beach/water transitions.
- **SaltCrownwalk01:** Foreground blades are more erect and visually harsher than the baseline's bent grass. The large central tree occupies more of the view and screens part of the distant island. This is a visible compositional difference between the matched files; the images alone do not establish its cause.

The magenta ridge patch occurs in the matched baseline Sluicepumps picture as well as the candidate Sluicepumpsroute picture. It should not be claimed as a newly introduced asset defect from this comparison. The lack of visible pumps and the imposing terrain banks also predate the candidate.

Across the sheets, the locations now agree about biome and daylight. Player silhouettes survive reduction. Grass species, fine props, and most creature identities do not. Shadows anchor the trainer, but large banks alternate between very pale and nearly uniform dull brown without enough intermediate form. No UI is present to judge. No obvious friendly use of oxblood dominates these frames.

## Three ranked reference gaps

1. **Authored terrain, coastal depth, and destination composition — both Sluice views, Gullbeach, FirstShorehorizonroute.** Palworld 04 organizes cliffs, a path, architecture, and a distant landmark into distinct readable layers. The Meadows and Water boards use landforms and paths to point toward destinations. These frames give large smooth walls, angular distant cuts, and a simple gray rounded waterfall mountain with white streaks. Scene work can fix dune profiles, shoreline layering, destination visibility, and foreground/midground balance. A distinctive Veilfall landmark and coherent architectural detail may require dedicated environment art; the supplied pictures do not demonstrate that art at the reference's design level.
2. **Convincing vegetation colonies and material/value hierarchy — Shellwatchjetty, FirstShorewelcome, SaltCrownwalk01.** Palworld 02 and 05 use readable vegetation masses, darker rooted ground, varied heights, and clearings. The key art similarly separates warm paths, olive vegetation, stone, and shadows. Here near-white rigid grass forms strips on near-white sand, with large flat banks between them. Scene density, placement, palette, and lighting can improve this substantially. If the shown straight blade shapes are the available foliage art, more varied bending dune-grass forms and rooted tussocks need to be made or obtained; scatter alone cannot supply those shapes.
3. **Creature appeal and world activity — FirstShorehorizonroute, SaltCrownwalk01, both Sluice views.** Palworld 01/03/05 gives creatures clear poses, large readable faces/forms, and visible relationships with the trainer or surroundings. Candidate creatures are distant lumps, speckled silhouettes, or color accents with little readable action. Framing, encounter placement, and presentation can improve the evidence. Bespoke creature art or material revision may be needed after close review, but this survey cannot honestly establish that requirement from these small subjects. No fighting frame was supplied, so combat spectacle is untested.

## Separate limited bar answers

**Bar A — No.** These seven frames do not yet convincingly belong to the world presented by the Meadows key art. The trainer, natural sand/green/blue palette, clear daylight, and hints of stone markers support the intended family. Broad blank terrain, weak planted edges, and rudimentary distant landmark treatment sink the result. This is not a demand for meadow coverage on a dune coast or for painted fidelity. Scene work can address profiles, vegetation grouping, value structure, and visual routes; distinctive landmark/environment art and more natural grass forms are the art-side gaps visible here.

**Bar B — No.** Beside the five Palworld screenshots, these limited views read as an early sparse environment study rather than a comparably resolved creature-adventure world. The trainer gives the correct broad genre cue, but the environment and creatures do not jointly carry it. Scene composition, ground variation, grass clustering, shoreline depth, and encounter presentation are fixable in the scene. The landmark and grass-form gaps need better art if suitable assets are unavailable; creature asset adequacy remains unproven and requires closer evidence.

Neither answer accepts or rejects unseen chapter views. The dune/ecology improvement is distinct from both chapter bars, and the missing BrineStepswalk03 candidate independently prevents complete P2-008 item acceptance.
