# P2-008 — dunes-05 limited pilot visual judgment

**Item verdict: FAIL (limited three-view pilot). Limited Bar A: No. Limited Bar B: No.**

B does not resolve the catalog defect in these views. Broad, rounded landforms and steep smooth skirts remain; a much more conspicuous gray face treatment strengthens the impression of stone retaining walls rather than Lake Michigan dunes. Grass coverage changes locally, but the shore still lacks convincing layered transitions. This is an image-based judgment, not a claim about implementation.

This covers **three views only**, not all eight P2-008 sightings and not the chapter. No conclusions about performance, animation, popping, traversal, combat, or unseen views can be drawn. ## Exact pair findings

### Gull Rest beach — A versus B

- **What improves:** B extends scattered grass farther into the foreground and makes individual dune-grass tufts easy to identify at full size. The trainer and route opening remain legible.
- **What regresses:** The dominant left face changes from muted gray-brown to very dark blue-gray, strongly textured across nearly its entire height. The right face also becomes gray. At small size the left wall becomes a single heavy dark mass, consuming the composition and making this sandy passage read as a rock trench. The thin pale cap does not restore a dune reading.
- **What remains:** The left face is still an enormous continuous rounded skirt, with a jagged sawtooth boundary near its foot and angular peaks at the sand-cap edge. Texture variation does not supply ledges, slumps, lower dunes, or a believable slope-to-beach sequence. The visible grass remains isolated tufts on smooth sand plus a narrow denser strip farther up the passage. The many-trainer-height walls and almost empty foreground overwhelm the human scale.
- **Actionable scene/material gap:** Reduce the continuous dark coverage, break the long steep face with landform variation and sand runouts, and group grass into irregular mats with sparse edges and exposed sand between them. A few more evenly separated blades are insufficient.

### Sluice Isle twin pumps — A versus B

- **What improves:** The face is easier to distinguish from the sandy crest and floor. Grass on the left remains a recognizable dune element.
- **What regresses:** B puts a broad gray blanket over the dominant right landform, making the rounded, near-vertical wall more conspicuous. Its narrow pale top and long uninterrupted gray surface resemble a sand-capped block. The fairly dense grass strip at the lower right in A is largely absent in B, leaving tiny scattered plants against a larger barren foot slope. This is a visible reduction in ecological layering at that edge.
- **What remains:** The landform outline is essentially the same: a long flattened rounded crest with steep sides, paired with another rounded steep mound on the left. Sand/gray boundaries trace those forms without creating a middle scale of dunes or shelves. The foreground still offers large smooth bright fields with little local variation. The distant central mountain silhouette is readable, but the foreground mass obscures most destination information.
- **Actionable scene/material gap:** Change the slope profile and crest rhythm rather than relying on face color; make grass-bearing shoulders and toe slopes visible; restore irregular grouped vegetation at the right toe. Material breakup should follow plausible sand accumulation and exposure instead of producing a uniform gray wall.

### First Shore horizon stones — A versus B

- **What improves:** B's darker slopes make some distant cuts easier to distinguish. Foreground grass, standing stones, turquoise water, and the far landmark still provide a clear layered composition. This remains the strongest of the three views.
- **What regresses:** The central island's gray skirt becomes a more continuous belt, with a conspicuous serrated pale/gray boundary. It draws attention to the uniform island rim. Similar gray interruptions across the rear dunes make the terrain feel more mechanically segmented. The visible silhouette does not demonstrate a corresponding landform improvement.
- **What remains:** The central island is still a broad raised platter with a steep skirt, and large rounded mounds repeat behind it. The distant shores consist chiefly of pale tops, gray faces, and a narrow foam outline; lower beach shelves and vegetation-to-shore transitions are weak. Foreground grass is fuller than in the close views, but many tufts share the same simple upright shape and the long grass band crossing the frame reads as a narrow stripe. The standing stones and trainer remain readable and their relative scale is coherent.
- **Actionable scene/material gap:** Give individual islands different shore profiles, interrupt the continuous skirt with lower sand tongues and sloped coves, and vary grass patches across elevation and exposure. Preserve the open water and clear horizon composition.

## Scene/material work versus art needs

**Scene/material-fixable from visible evidence:**

- Reshape the repeated high rounded skirts and flattened crests, especially Sluice Isle and the central island in First Shore. Make more than one slope scale visible.
- Remove the blanket gray-cliff reading in Gull Rest and Sluice Isle; soften and vary transitions in ways that support dunes. Fix the visible sawtooth boundaries rather than accentuating them with high contrast.
- Recompose grass as irregular patches and graduated edges. Sluice Isle's right toe needs its lost vegetation transition restored; Gull Rest needs meaningful clusters, not merely foreground singles.
- Add differentiated dry sand, sheltered vegetated sand, and shoreline transition zones where the frame exposes them. First Shore needs more than the same narrow foam rim around steep islands.
- Improve terrain value structure through form and material placement. More texture contrast alone has made the current forms harsher.

**Art needs / limits:** The visible grass vocabulary is narrow: repeated thin upright blade clumps and a few tiny low plants. Fuller, differently shaped dune-grass clumps, low coastal groundcover, and restrained shoreline debris/stone groupings would support the missing middle scale. If that variety is absent from the asset library, it needs to be made or acquired; this code-blind review cannot verify the inventory. New assets alone will not fix the dominant terrain shapes or the continuous gray face treatment. No specific character replacement is justified by these rear-view frames.

## Three largest gaps from the references, ranked

1. **Landform and material transitions lack authored structure.** B Sluice Isle's enormous gray wall and B First Shore's continuous island belt dominate without internal silhouette variety. Palworld's open-field and plateau references use broken rock masses, grass-bearing slopes, and clear ground transitions; the key art separates riverbank, path, and hill into distinct readable forms. The dune version needs its own equivalent structure, not those exact biome assets.
2. **Ecology has one main size and distribution pattern.** B Gull Rest's isolated blades and B First Shore's narrow grass stripe do not build a convincing progression from bare sand to established vegetation. Palworld's field/base frames mix dense ground cover, patch edges, exposed traveled ground, and larger vegetation. The key art uses these layers to make open terrain feel inhabited. Dunes can remain sparse while still showing that organization.
3. **The environment is less coherent and inviting at small size.** B Gull Rest reduces to pale floor plus a near-black wall; B Sluice Isle becomes pale floor plus a gray block. The references retain connected natural color masses, readable routes, and varied intermediate forms around their focal characters. First Shore carries a more promising blue/cream/green palette and strong landmarks, but its repeated rim and mound language still looks unfinished beside them.

## Reference gates

**Limited Bar A — Do these frames belong to the world of the Tetherbound key art? No.** The trainer palette, blue water, sand, green grass, and landmark visibility are compatible ingredients, particularly in First Shore. Across this pilot, the dominant gray walls, simplified terrain forms, and weak ecological transitions do not yet sustain the key art's natural, welcoming, intentionally composed world. This is a palette/composition/landmark/mood judgment, not a painted-detail comparison.

**Limited Bar B — Beside the Palworld screenshots, would these read as trying to be the same kind of game? No.** The third-person trainer and scattered creatures establish the broad genre intention. The rendered world in these three views still reads as an early terrain pass: enormous unsupported surfaces, repeated profiles, thin vegetation organization, and distant creatures with weak small-size identity. Palworld's references present much more coherent ground-to-foliage layering and readable creature/environment relationships. The scene/material work above is the immediate bottleneck; a broader vegetation vocabulary may require additional art. This is a limited pilot gate, not a chapter-wide rejection or assessment of unseen combat.
