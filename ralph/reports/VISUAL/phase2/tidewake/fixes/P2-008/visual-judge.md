# P2-008 independent visual judgment

**Specific item: PARTIAL. Chapter visual acceptance: FAIL.**

B improves the grass-to-sand transition and reduces the harsh yellow sand and noisy gray rock of A. It does not establish convincing layered shore ecology. The dominant landforms remain broad, smooth, largely uninterrupted walls with a grass cap. Some material transitions now reveal conspicuous geometric patterns. The improvement is real, but insufficient to close the item.

The trainer remains identifiable and reasonably coherent with the illustrated adventure direction. Creature and NPC art is less cohesive: the densely outlined, mottled turtle, the smoother blue aquatic creature, and the heavily caricatured merchant have noticeably different surface treatments. The turtle has appeal and a readable face, but these assets do not yet achieve the consistent character finish of the Palworld references. This is most directly visible in Salt Crown `walk_02_day`, Brine Steps `route_day`, and Salt Crown `walk_03_day`. The images cannot establish animation quality or intended creature roles; no intended scale hierarchy is assumed.

## Gains, regressions, and retained problems

The most convincing gain is the intermediate sparse-grass-on-sand zone at Gull Rest and Salt Crown `walk_03_day`. Grass and sand remain distinguishable while their junction is less abrupt. Brine Steps now has a readable dry-to-wet color change. The calmer sand also makes people and their shadows easier to see. Across the sheets, the palette is more coherent and less dominated by bright yellow beaches and bright gray cliffs.

The strongest regressions are geometric material boundaries in B: rectangular green patches on Gull Rest's upper-right wall, repeated pointed pale wedges under its left cliff and across Brine Steps' opposing island, and the regular tiled/ripple appearance of Brine Steps `walk_01_day` foreground. These are visible surface defects, not inferred implementation causes. Existing coarse geometry may underlie some patterns, but B makes them more conspicuous. Sluice Isle `walk_03_day` trades distinct rock for an overly smooth green surface, reducing material readability.

Layered ecology remains weak. The survey mostly presents tall trees, a continuous grass carpet, and smooth rock walls. Shrubs, groundcover clusters, exposed roots, broken stone feet, ledge growth, and irregular bare patches are not legible as a connected hierarchy. Greater grass quantity is not the missing ingredient. Shellwatch location, First Shore `walk_01_day`, and Sluice Isle `walk_02_day` need breakup of large shapes and vegetation distribution.

At small size the trainer remains readable, and water/land separation is clear. Dusk and day are distinguishable. The blue sea gives the chapter an inviting coastal identity. However, dense pale grass has a wiry, nearly white surface that competes with the trainer in several grass views. Distant islands expose repeated smooth terraces and sparse prop scattering rather than distinctive coastal silhouettes. No UI is present to evaluate, and these images do not show a combat event.

## Three largest gaps against the references

1. **Landform structure and ecological integration.** Palworld's open-field path and plateau images contain broken rock masses, uneven ledges, varied groundcover, and vegetation integrated around terrain. The Veilfall board carries growth in rock pockets and around stepped silhouettes. Shellwatch location, First Shore `walk_01_day`, and Sluice Isle `walk_02_day` instead show smooth walls with grass above and below. B alters their color without resolving that underlying visual structure.
2. **Cohesive creature, character, and prop art.** Palworld's trainer and creatures share a controlled stylized finish with clear facial and body shapes. Salt Crown `walk_02_day` juxtaposes a heavily mottled turtle with a smoother blue creature; Brine Steps `route_day` juxtaposes a comparatively restrained trainer with an exaggerated, densely textured merchant. The camp arch and separated triangular paving in Salt Crown `walk_03_day` also lack the integrated architectural language of either board.
3. **Authored composition and readable surface hierarchy.** The key art and Palworld field/base views vary occupied ground, clearings, paths, foliage heights, shadow masses, and landmarks. Brine Steps `route_day` has a broad empty beach, a simple prop cluster, and a sparse island behind it; Salt Crown `vista_dusk` spends much of the frame on a wall; Sluice Isle `walk_03_day` alternates very noisy grass with an overly smooth green patch. B reduces noise but does not create that hierarchy.

## Reference bars and work split

**Bar A — Do these frames read as belonging to the world in the Meadows key art? No.** The inviting greens, blue water, backpacked trainer, and day/dusk warmth support the direction, but the broad smooth terrain, nearly uniform grass, inconsistent asset finish, and weak integration of settlements/landmarks prevent the overall world from reading as the board's authored, inhabited landscape. The Veilfall board reinforces the missing coastal rock-and-growth structure; its monumental stronghold is not present in this survey and is not judged here.

**Bar B — Beside the Palworld screenshots, would someone say these are trying to be the same kind of game? Yes.** The third-person trainer, appealing fantasy creatures, saturated natural palette, explorable terrain, and small settlement props establish the same broad creature-adventure intent. This is a yes to the stated genre/readability question, not a claim of equivalent finish. The three gaps above still prevent chapter acceptance.

**Scene/material work supported by the images:** remove visible rectangular/triangular material boundaries; preserve wet/dry sand variation without a uniform grid-like foreground; retain rock identity where green blending currently erases it; interrupt long wall faces with broken silhouettes and ledges; place existing rock and vegetation in connected clusters; vary grass height/density and add intentional clearings; integrate prop groups and paths with their surrounding ground. These are actionable without assuming any performance budget.

**Art not demonstrated in the supplied frames:** a cohesive final creature/NPC surface style, a stronger family of coastal rock formations, varied shore understory, and integrated coastal architectural pieces. The screenshots cannot prove what is present elsewhere in the asset library. If suitable assets already exist, scene work can use them; if not, these gaps require art to be made or acquired. Palette or scatter changes alone will not solve them.

**Final gate: P2-008 PARTIAL; chapter FAIL; Bar A NO; Bar B YES.** Preserve the better sand and grass transition, fix the exposed geometric blending artifacts, and establish actual intermediate landform/vegetation layers before closing the shoreline item.
