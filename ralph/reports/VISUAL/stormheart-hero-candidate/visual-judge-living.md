# Stormheart hero tree: independent visual judgment

Scoped hero-tree verdict: **FAIL**. Full-scene Bar A: **NO**. Full-scene Bar B: **NO**.

Evidence: all six PNGs in `living-corrected1080`, inspected individually and together as a contact sheet with each frame reduced to 30% of its original dimensions. References inspected: both Stormwood/Stormheart stronghold boards, the Stormwood creature board, Meadows key art and all five Palworld gameplay images. The visual-judge rubric and ART_DIRECTION were read. No implementation, capture manifest, previous verdict or other agent report was consulted. Filenames identify views; their distance labels are not independently verified measurements. This is a still-image judgment, not an assessment of motion or performance.

## Scoped hero asset

The broad idea works at distance. `stormheart_road_320m.png` and both Glass Field views show an unmistakably enormous, split, living tree. Its bifurcated silhouette survives the small contact-sheet view. The opening, roots and spreading branches give it a stronger identity than an oversized ordinary tree. It agrees with the boards' basic landmark language and looks monumental beside the trainer. Those successes do not resolve the following visible defects:

1. **Branch and crown surfaces read as torn blades, not convincing wood or foliage.** In `stormheart_road_220m.png`, the upper-left crown contains a mass of flat, pointed brown fragments. The upper-right and rightmost branch ends show the same sharply cut, hanging strips. `stormheart_road_320m.png` preserves that ragged silhouette even at reduced size. A lightning-split trunk can be jagged; repeated sheet-like shards across ordinary outer branches make the asset look damaged as geometry rather than deliberately scarred as a tree. These surfaces need revised geometry and/or foliage construction, not more scene scatter.
2. **The near bark fails as a continuous material.** In `stormheart_road_120m_verge.png`, the enormous horizontal root/arch across the upper middle has long vertical brown streaks and abrupt directional changes; the right trunk combines broad blurry patches with sharp triangular facets. The exposed bark above and to the right of the foreground pylon in `stormheart_road_120m.png` repeats this problem. The boards use a readable hierarchy of thick structural roots, branching grain and smaller fissures. Here the grain stretches across the form and resembles draped, smeared material. Rework the affected surface mapping/material and malformed transitions; distant-only approval would hide a failure visible on approach.
3. **Living crown mass is inconsistent with the surrounding forest.** In `stormheart_road_320m.png`, the hero's pale sage foliage forms thin, patchy plates while the near trees have dense dark-green crowns. In `stormheart_road_220m.png`, the sparse pale patches emphasize exposed pointed branch fragments. Distancing can lighten foliage, but this difference reads as a different construction/material family rather than a coherent ancient member of the forest. The boards show substantial irregular crown masses that reinforce the main branches. Improve canopy volume and integrate its values with the scene without erasing the split silhouette.

The dark rings and pale cyan segmented vertical feature within the cleft are judged separately from the tree's wood/leaf mesh. They are prominent in `stormheart_road_220m.png` and `stormheart_road_320m.png`: large almost-black elliptical slabs and a dashed cyan spine compete with the living form. They do not read as the boards' articulated bridges, railings and energetic core. Their presentation weakens the complete stronghold, but the scoped tree FAIL already follows from the tree surfaces themselves.

## Frame-specific scene findings

- `stormheart_glass_field_look.png`: the left foreground trees create useful framing and the road reaches the hero. The hero is much paler than those trees against a large almost featureless violet sky, so its bark and foliage lose material richness. The road occupies a large part of the image as a blurred brown plane with a yellow line; its actual verge and wear do less navigational work than that line. The trainer is readable, but the dark purple shape beside the trainer does not expose enough face or anatomy to establish a creature identity.
- `stormheart_road_120m_verge.png`: the root arch conveys monumental scale, but bark stretching becomes the dominant detail. The giant near-camera flat flower/stem at the left slices across the view, with another oversized flower clipped by the top edge. Grass partly obscures the trainer's feet. Relative flower scale cannot be inferred from perspective alone, but the camera intrusion is plainly visible. The interior rings remain dark, simple bands through the opening.
- `stormheart_road_120m.png`: the pylon occupies the central foreground, hides almost all of the trainer and blocks the tree's entrance/cleft. This is a failed approach composition even if the tree were otherwise accepted. The right exposed trunk is useful material evidence; this frame cannot establish a readable entrance or close stronghold arrival.
- `stormheart_road_220m.png`: the best evidence of the crown-fragment problem, plus an exposed black platform underside that reads as an unfinished disc. Pale mushrooms obscure the trainer's lower body while dark bushes obscure the base. The scene is dense, but the layers obstruct the subject and entrance rather than staging them. The cropped orange roof at lower left does not yet establish a settlement or inhabited approach.
- `stormheart_road_320m.png`: the strongest complete landmark view and clearest road/trainer composition. The tree's split identity survives at small size. However, bark, near foliage, grass and cyan core have distinct material treatments without a convincing shared lighting response. The tan animal at the right has a visible head silhouette but the small, partly concealed body does not provide a strong creature presentation comparable with the reference gameplay subjects. Its actual size relative to the trainer cannot be settled from this perspective.
- `stormwood__dynamo__11__the_glass_field__day.png`: repeats the useful framed distant-tree composition, but the bright yellow foreground road streak becomes a stronger accent than the hero's energy core. The purple sky, pale tree and very dark forest flatten into a few disconnected value groups. The flat angular foreground flowers and low-detail road remain conspicuous at reduced size. This is not evidence of a readable combat event or expressive creature encounter.

Across the set, the trainer has readable blue, skin and brown clothing blocks when unobstructed. The available views are mainly side/back views; they do not justify a face-quality verdict. Creature presentation is insufficiently readable to carry the creature-adventure identity: no clear, expressive creature subject anchors these images as it does in the Palworld shots. This is a full-scene failure, not grounds to invent anatomical defects for bodies hidden in foliage.

## Three largest reference gaps, ranked

1. **Hero form and surface finish — `stormheart_road_220m.png`, `stormheart_road_120m_verge.png`.** The Stormheart boards communicate thick branching wood, coherent foliage masses and a deliberately broken central trunk. The candidate has a recognizable overall idea but pointed sheet fragments, thin crowns and stretched bark that fail at the reachable close view. Geometry, surface mapping and canopy art need repair.
2. **A living stronghold, not just a monumental silhouette — `stormheart_road_320m.png`, `stormheart_road_120m.png`.** The boards stage an entrance, human-scale platforms and bridges, and a compelling central energy feature. These frames offer dark rings, a dashed cyan spine and an approach obstructed by a pylon. Scene placement and value hierarchy can improve arrival; the visible platform/core construction also needs authored visual detail. No amount of extra grass supplies that architecture.
3. **Coherent, inhabited creature-adventure presentation — both Glass Field frames and `stormheart_road_220m.png`.** Meadows key art uses layered land, integrated verges and readable natural materials; Palworld combines grounded grass/terrain with immediately identifiable creature subjects. These frames place blurred road, flat angular flowers, near-black shrubs, pale hero foliage and mostly hidden creatures into one view. Camera clearance, clustering, lighting and subject staging are scene work. The tree's faulty surfaces and primitive flower/stronghold forms require asset/material work beyond rearrangement.

## Acceptance bars

**A — Do these frames belong to the project's art-direction world? NO.** The split ancient tree, rain, purple storm palette and copper rod line establish relevant chapter ingredients. The enormous tree is a meaningful identity success. The complete view still lacks the boards' coherent living canopy, authored stronghold detail and natural material integration. The purple sky itself is not a defect: it is the chapter's fixed direction. Its broad empty presentation, weak depth layering and mismatched surrounding values are the scene issues. Fixable through scene work: pylon/camera obstruction, vegetation masking, foreground clutter, palette/value integration and approach staging. Requiring asset work: torn-looking branch/crown surfaces, bark mapping, canopy volume and convincing visible stronghold/platform/core treatment.

**B — Beside the Palworld gameplay references, do these frames read as the same kind of polished creature-adventure game? NO.** The trainer and explorable road communicate a third-person adventure, but the current ground/foliage material inconsistency, conspicuous hero-surface defects and absent clear creature focal subject keep these images below that production register. This is not a demand for the concept boards' per-leaf detail. The comparison-game images already show sufficient cohesive materials, grounded environment layers and expressive visible creatures at gameplay distance. Fixable through scene work: expose and stage creature subjects, improve route/ground integration and lighting separation, and clear camera obstructions. Asset-dependent gaps: hero geometry/material defects and low-detail botanical/architectural forms. The six frames cannot establish whether suitable expressive creature art exists elsewhere in the build, so new creature acquisition is not inferred from this evidence.

The scoped hero asset is not accepted. Full-scene Bars A and B remain independently open even if the tree mesh is later repaired.
