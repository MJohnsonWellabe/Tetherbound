# Cloudreach vegetation source audit

Read-only investigation at worktree HEAD `f8b07a0814308b1bf08f8b9f88a19b6f46014209`, after the integration described by the coordinator. No Godot launch or production edit. Inspected reference frame `shots/cloudreach-wear-ordered/region/cloudreach/006_cloudreach_place_cliffhold_court_candidate_day.png`. The frame contains a disconnected blade group left of the tower and a tall-looking continuous blade layer at right; source alone cannot assign those pixels to a node/instance.

## Most actionable findings

1. **The original cover pass still scatters onto guessed flat heights.** `cloudreach_ground_cover.gd::_sample_patch` (~260-295) sets ellipse points to `centre.y+0.08` and segments to interpolated route height plus a constant offset. No raycast, visible-triangle sample, or support rejection occurs. `cloudreach_look.gd:799-811` itself describes this as producing floating/buried cover, but its subsequent finish pass only adds another layer; it does not relocate/remove the original `ProceduralGroundCover` instances. Isolate `ProceduralGroundCover` first.

2. **Specific settlement patches extend onto descending terrain while remaining flat.** The settlement's flat inner visible crown reaches an axis-aligned square of half-width24.1 at local y=.11 (`cloudreach_world.gd:3619-3650`). Outer triangles descend by10..17m. Upper-only patches at local `(20+5*i,.14,4+6*i)` have half extents `(4.5,5)` and height multiplier.68 (`cloudreach_world.gd:3936-3940`). The i=2 patch is centered x=30, entirely beyond the inner square even at its sampled negative-x extreme `30-4.5*.88=26.04`; all its roots nevertheless stay y=.22. Earlier perimeter patches near local z=-26 also cross the flat boundary. These are concrete unsupported-placement candidates at Cliffhold independent of shader behavior.

3. **Regional elevation can make a floating layer look roof-high without large blades.** Upper Cloudreach's configured crown is y850 at `(-100,4200)`; Cliffhold is y830 at `(-340,3970)` (`cloudreach_world.json:127-133,256-260`). Region cover inherits crown top and a broad .34*size ellipse (`cloudreach_world.gd:1199-1207`). The ordinary regional `CliffMass` is eroded away from its inner core, whereas the first cover pass retains flat y850. Settlement clearance only recognizes points within12m vertically and36m horizontally (`_inside_settlement_clearance`, ~2397), so a y850 layer is not excluded just for overlapping a y830 village. Whether this particular patch reaches the offending pixels requires runtime instance positions/support measurements.

4. **Geometry-based fill does not actually restrict itself to visible geometry.** `_collect_mesh_instances` / `_collect_turf_triangles` (`cloudreach_look.gd:1100-1161`) traverse hidden meshes too, and accept both triangle orientations with `abs(normal.y)`. Thus hidden walkable crowns/terraces remain candidates despite comments saying this reads visible turf. `_fill_tuft_at` raycasts and rejects hits above the source point+.25, but does not require the hit height to match closely below, nor require a turf hit/normal. Its ray ends1m below the point. This can accept a hidden support or small unsupported gap; unlike flat ellipses it cannot normally create tens of metres of root separation. Isolate `CoverFillMain/Dry/Far` separately if original cover is not responsible.

## Scale, shader and extraction checks

- Procedural `surface_tuft_mesh(7,2)` is a seven-blade mesh with baked maxY below.98m (`grass_field.gd:1535-1607`). `cloudreach_grass_roles.gd` and current `cloudreach_visual.json:253-264` cap ordinary role height at1.10 before local patch multipliers, so ordinary visible height is at most about1.078m, or .733m for .68 settlement patches. The older `grass_scale_min/max` and finish `far_scale_m` are remapped through role jitter; they do not multiply these heights again in current code.
- `cloudreach_ground_cover.gdshader` only bends xz for wind/curve. It never adds y; camera clearance multiplies VERTEX by a factor between.015 and1, so it only shrinks. Roots at UV.y=0 do not wind-bend. A root elevated many metres is not explained by this shader. Wind/curve can move tip silhouettes and requires expanded runtime bounds if judging exact support near edges.
- Original cover transforms correctly subtract the patch origin before upload, while the patch node holds that origin. Finish transforms are world positions under identity-created look roots. Runtime mounts `CloudreachLook` and procedural cover without scale changes. No obvious double world translation or parent scale multiplication was found in these construction paths; runtime global transforms should confirm that assumption.
- Alpine rim uses `_first_mesh` (`cloudreach_look.gd:537-545,1369`) and discards scene hierarchy transforms. This is a general extraction weakness, but **not supported as this asset's cause**: all six current Alpine source glTFs have one mesh node with no translation/rotation/scale, and Grass_Wispy_Tall import settings have root_scale1/apply_root_scale=true. Its position accessor bounds are minY=-.0360518,maxY=1.6357505. Alpine scale.7..1.3 gives top height at most2.1265m and total height2.1734m; its baked base extends only a few cm below placement. These numbers do not support an8m blade hypothesis. The alpine material is the installed mesh material, not the procedural wind shader. Isolate `LookAlpineRimMix/AlpineMixAlpineGrass` if blades look like the installed wispy asset.
- Route verges preserve imported hierarchy transforms in `_prepared_mesh`/`_commit_batch` (`cloudreach_route_verges.gd:184-242`). Grass_Wide_Tall is maxY1.5222324,minY-.0871767; Wispy as above. Current height scales.85..1.25 give roughly2.05m maximum top height for wispy. Their `_supported_ground` calls `_route_detail_ground`, which can fall back to constant-height segment patches (`cloudreach_world.gd:3000-3019`), so support remains more suspect than scale. Isolate `RouteEcologyVerges/RouteVergeGrassWide` and `RouteVergeGrassWispy` separately.
- Finish/alpine raycasts span top_hint+140 to top_hint-320; accepted hits must be a `StaticBody3D` named exactly `Collision`, with parent name not containing deck/floor. They do not check slope/material, and allow a ground difference up to60m for finish/alpine. This can choose another stacked level or non-turf colliders matching the naming heuristic, although accepted roots are placed on that collision hit rather than left at the old hint.

## Cloud relationship and targeted evidence

CloudSea and CloudDecks are visual geometry with cloud materials; the reviewed builders create no collision bodies for them (`cloudreach_world.gd:808-1084`). They neither register cover patches nor match the upland material identity used by turf fill. Consequently, the apparent left clump on a cloud is not explained by intentional cloud planting or these clouds catching the look raycast. It may be a perspective overlap between an unsupported tuft and a separate cloud. MultiMesh cloud lobes have large visual bounds but share neither the tuft mesh nor the tuft material.

The useful runtime receipt is the offending instance's node path/index, mesh local AABB, full node*instance transform, transformed root/top, patch kind/center if applicable, nearest visible turf triangle height, and collision hit height. Compare root to **visible** crown as well as collider: hidden support boxes can validate a physics hit while still leaving a visual gap. Do not fix apparent size before confirming whether the offending root is20m above the village. Layer-isolation frames should establish node ownership first; this audit makes no claim that one specific layer owns the supplied pixels.

## Smallest coherent grounding remedy after ownership isolation

Recommended seam: optional support resolver on `ProceduralGroundCover.build`, default absent for existing callers/tests. Call it after `_sample_patch` and before exclusion/cluster evaluation, passing the sample **and patch context**. A Vector3-only callback loses the intended stacked layer. Return a supported root or NAN; preserve the original behavior only when no resolver was supplied, never as a fallback after the resolver fails.

Use a read-only spatial index of the world's already-built **visible turf triangles**, not a broad physics ray plus collider-name heuristic. Reuse the conceptual material-identity walk from the look pass while fixing its hidden-mesh omission. Resolve effective material override/surface material, require identity with actual upland/upland_dry, ignore hidden ancestry, transform vertices once, reject near-vertical triangles and downward-facing undersides, and bin projected triangle bounds in XZ. Use geometric slope for eligibility and transformed source normals to distinguish undersides, accounting for the engine's clockwise winding. Match the selected slope ceiling to the configured turf transition; the existing fill's normal_y.55 allows surfaces already shading mostly rock.

At each candidate, test XZ barycentrically against nearby triangles and interpolate real Y. Prefer triangles belonging to the patch's generating crown/shoulder. An optional source-node/surface reference in patch metadata is the least ambiguous layer identifier; it is runtime placement metadata, not gameplay/save state. Without that reference, choose the nearest supported Y to the hint within a declared bounded range and reject ambiguous separated layers. A fixed ±1m range safely prunes unsupported small pockets but cannot seat eroded region crowns that descend tens of metres. Conversely a blanket60m range can jump to an unrelated lower terrace. Keep those cases distinct; do not silently claim nearest-Y alone guarantees the intended layer.

For settlement pocket patches, a narrow drop/slope allowance and rejection beyond the visible crown is sufficient and conservative: it removes hovering roots rather than hanging them halfway down a cliff. For regional eroded crowns, preserve ownership of the region's actual crown triangles and seat roots there with only a small fixed embed/lift. Re-run exclusions on the **resolved** point, so a relocated root entering a building/route footprint is rejected. Existing footprint exclusions remain necessary: a turf-only index cannot itself detect a roof, paving overlay or other occluder above turf. If coverage outside those exclusions needs validation, add a bounded overhead obstruction test against real colliders or visible opaque geometry; reject any blocking hit independent of collider name. Never accept a roof as support just because its body is called Collision. Do not introduce `ground_height_at` or segment-flat fallback after failure.

Determinism/performance: build the index once before scatter with the existing `_shell_build.breathe` budget; retain stable traversal/triangle ordering and deterministic tie breaking. The resolver must consume no RNG and never depend on frame/time. Existing placement rejection will naturally change later accepted samples within the deterministic seed stream; preserve that repeatability without promising identical instance positions after correcting support. Keep existing bounded attempts and sliced upload behavior. Avoid one global scan per tuft or an unbounded all-realm physics ray per attempt. Dense configured scatter makes indexed local queries the better long-term cost.

Tradeoff: a physics-only callback is fewer lines and can be a useful diagnostic, but hidden terrace colliders differ from the visible descending skirt and some visible shelves intentionally have no collider. It would keep accepting visually unsupported turf or drop legitimate visible cover. A triangle resolver is the smallest approach that directly meets the stated visible-ground/stacked-layer requirement; adding a second decorative pass without repairing/rejecting the original roots does not.
