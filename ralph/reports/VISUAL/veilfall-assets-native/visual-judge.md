# Veilfall native-frame blind visual verdict

Reviewed 2026-09-27. Evidence is the nine PNGs in `after-refined`: `intake_gallery.png`, `pump_hall.png`, `sluice_crossing.png`, `heart_chamber_entry.png`, `heart_chamber_crystal.png`, `heart_chamber_banner.png`, and `motion_0.png` through `motion_2.png`. Every frame was inspected. References: the Veilfall stronghold board, Meadows key art, Water Realm creature roster board, and all five `palworld-0*.jpg` frames. Rubric: `.claude/skills/visual-judge/SKILL.md` and `docs/design/ART_DIRECTION.md`. No renderer/source files, history, implementation reports or capture logs were read. This is a visual verdict on the supplied frames, not a whole-chapter acceptance or a performance finding.

## Characters and creatures first

Ripplet has a readable, appealing face and a deliberate cyan/cream silhouette in `intake_gallery.png`. It is visibly larger than the trainer, and the trainer's blue jacket, pale collar and brown backpack remain identifiable. These are positive creature-adventure cues. However, Ripplet's broad nearly flat cyan regions, dark mottled surface marks and hard colour transitions look less integrated with the detailed stone and trainer materials than the consistently modeled, lit subjects in the Palworld references. This is especially visible on its torso and feet in `intake_gallery.png`.

In `sluice_crossing.png`, Ripplet's lower body disappears into a featureless cyan surface without a readable waterline, wake or contact response. The cyan body also merges with that water. A still cannot establish whether this is intended swimming or an intersection error; it does establish a poor body/water read. The remote humanoid in `heart_chamber_entry.png` and `sluice_crossing.png` is a small dark figure on a large empty floor; its role cannot be read from its silhouette. No combat is shown, so these frames cannot establish creature animation, expressive fighting or encounter spectacle.

## Scoped asset-read verdicts

**Heart Chamber crystal: PASS for recognizable facets and internal-light appearance in the supplied views.** `heart_chamber_crystal.png` and `motion_0.png`–`motion_2.png` show pointed clustered prisms, distinct blue facet planes, lighter edges and pale luminous areas apparently within the body. It reads immediately as an illuminated crystal. That identity survives in `heart_chamber_entry.png` and is still recognizable as the endpoint in `pump_hall.png` and `sluice_crossing.png`. At the distant `intake_gallery.png` view it is primarily a blue focal silhouette against the white fall; individual facets should not be claimed readable there. The narrow scope passes without requiring a match to painted reference fidelity.

The asset's setting remains unfinished: the central support and two flanking objects are blank blue-grey cubes in `heart_chamber_crystal.png` and `heart_chamber_entry.png`, with no readable designed mounting or ritual mechanism. The white waterfall directly behind the crystal also reduces edge separation compared with a darker architectural recess. These are integration defects, not grounds to deny that the crystal itself reads as faceted and internally lit.

**Interior waterfall: FAIL for the complete requested lip/plunge/splash/mist read; PASS only for recognizable falling strands/sheets.** In `heart_chamber_crystal.png` and all three `motion_*.png` frames, the pale vertical ribbons, longitudinal streaks and irregular narrow gaps communicate falling water. There is a small frothy/jagged splash fringe at the foot. Those features also read from `heart_chamber_entry.png`.

The top is a straight clipped edge against the flat ceiling, with no readable rock or stone lip, curved turn-over or visible source. The ribbons remain almost parallel and similarly wide over their height, which makes them resemble a hanging curtain. At the bottom, the splash is a thin line ending on what looks like the same continuous stone floor; a receiving plunge pool or convincing falling-water-to-basin transition is not legible. Mist is not a distinct visible cloud or depth layer. These failures are clearest in `heart_chamber_crystal.png` and persist in `motion_0.png`, `motion_1.png` and `motion_2.png`. The more distant views compress the fall into a bright rectangular panel and do not resolve the missing source/receiver relationship. A tiny bright fringe is insufficient to certify the entire requested waterfall read. Three sampled stills cannot certify flow rhythm or animated splash behaviour.

Neither scoped result closes the Heart Chamber, Veilfall, Tidewake or either full visual bar.

## Frame-specific scene defects

- **A chamber made from uninterrupted boxes:** `intake_gallery.png`, `pump_hall.png` and `heart_chamber_entry.png` present long flat walls, a flat low-looking ceiling and expansive flat floors carrying substantially the same small-stone pattern. The Veilfall board communicates a cavern with vertical water, arched stone openings, stacked galleries and a bridge over depth. These frames communicate an enclosed rectangular corridor with a prop at its end. This is a silhouette and spatial-design gap, not a demand for painted detail.
- **Weak material hierarchy:** the large close floor in `heart_chamber_banner.png` and `heart_chamber_crystal.png` shows stretched-looking stone courses and conspicuous repeated highlights. Wall, ceiling and floor stay in a narrow dark grey range, with little separation by structure or material role. Small amber lantern pools provide warmth but do not give the room a convincing main light direction or reveal major architectural form.
- **Unfinished hero surroundings:** `heart_chamber_crystal.png` exposes three blank cubes. `heart_chamber_banner.png` exposes a flat blue rectangle with a plain white square instead of a readable authored insignia, fabric form or finished banner edge. These shapes are immediately visible at reduced size and read as stand-ins.
- **Unresolved water surface:** the pools in `pump_hall.png` and particularly `sluice_crossing.png` are uniform cyan planes with abrupt straight perimeter lines. There is no clear shallow/deep variation, current, local foam or physical relationship to the bridge and creature. Non-reflective stylization can work; these planes still need visible water behaviour and contact.
- **Repetition and empty staging:** lanterns repeat at regular height/spacing along the otherwise undressed walls in `intake_gallery.png`, `pump_hall.png` and `heart_chamber_banner.png`. The forward route and blue endpoint are clear, but there are few structural landmarks or secondary lures to make the spaces feel inhabited or individually authored. Increasing uniform prop scatter would not solve this.
- **HUD obscures supporting reads:** the team list covers part of Ripplet and the left pool in `sluice_crossing.png`; the large lower action panels cover much of the near route, and the upper quest panel obscures the right wall/banner in `heart_chamber_entry.png`. Text is legible, but visual hierarchy spends a substantial part of the frame on persistent panels. This is judged directly, not against Palworld UI.

At small presentation size, the trainer, Ripplet, crystal, waterfall rectangle and bridge route remain identifiable. The room's texture detail collapses into a grey field, the cubes remain visibly cubes, and the waterfall reads more like a luminous screen. Depth exists through perspective and repetition but lacks the reference's intervening structural silhouettes and layered value masses. The supplied stills do not establish popping, traversal, dynamic grounding or target-device behaviour.

## Three largest reference gaps, ranked

1. **Architectural identity and depth.** The Veilfall interior reference makes water and multi-level stone architecture occupy one deep cavern. `heart_chamber_entry.png`, `pump_hall.png` and `intake_gallery.png` instead show a flat-ceilinged stone box. Recompose the space with actual openings, depth beneath crossings, substantial arches/supports and a credible waterfall source/receiver. This requires modeled spatial structure; colour changes alone cannot supply it.
2. **Finished material and object language.** Palworld's structures, ground and characters sustain a consistent material register; the project boards use readable stone, metal, cloth and water roles. `heart_chamber_banner.png`, `heart_chamber_crystal.png` and `sluice_crossing.png` mix tiled masonry, blank cubes, a square-mark banner and flat cyan water. Finished support/occupation objects and banner art are missing from the visible scene, while water contact and masonry scale need material/staging work.
3. **Authored life and subject integration.** The Palworld frames stage expressive creatures in grounded, layered environments; even open areas have terrain form and meaningful activity. `intake_gallery.png` has a readable companion but harsh surface integration; `sluice_crossing.png` loses its lower body in colour-matched water; `heart_chamber_entry.png` leaves the small humanoid isolated on a vast floor. Improve creature material cohesion and water contact, then stage human-scale activity and meaningful architectural landmarks. This is not a request to fill an indoor stronghold with outdoor foliage.

## Full bars

**Bar A — No.** These frames do not yet read as belonging to the fully realized world of the Meadows key art and its Tidewake/Veilfall extension. Cool water/crystal against warm lamps is directionally appropriate, and the trainer and companion carry some of the identity. The defining cavern, cascading water, monumental stone structure and inhabited spatial layering are absent from these views. The floor/wall repetition and stand-in furnishings dominate the scene.

Scene/material work can improve waterfall placement and source/receiver continuity, pool/contact treatment, light hierarchy, texture scale, staging and silhouette separation. The cavern/bridge/gallery forms, finished crystal support and other cube replacements, and an authored banner require actual geometry or object art that is not visible in the presented scene. The screenshots cannot determine whether suitable unused assets exist elsewhere in the build.

**Bar B — No.** A third-person trainer and appealing companion communicate the general creature-adventure category, but beside the Palworld gameplay references this set does not sustain the same intended polished production register. Sparse box rooms, blank objects, flat pool surfaces and inconsistent creature/environment material integration make the visual presentation read as an unfinished test space.

Scene/material changes can improve grounding, value hierarchy, authored activity, water integration and camera/HUD composition. Finishing the architecture and replacing the visibly blank support/banner art requires modeled or authored asset work, not merely more illumination or denser scatter. The crystal's scoped pass does not outweigh those whole-frame defects, and the complete waterfall-read gate remains open.
