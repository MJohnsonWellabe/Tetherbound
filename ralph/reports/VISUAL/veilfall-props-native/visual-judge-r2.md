# Veilfall props: independent visual verdict

Evidence reviewed: all eight PNGs in `asset-validation/shots/veilfall-new-props-r2/after/` and both PNGs in `after-open/`, opened individually. References inspected: the Veilfall stronghold board, Meadows key art, and all five `palworld-0*.jpg` images. Standards: the visual-judge rubric and the visual standards in ART_DIRECTION.md. No source, previous reports, or before captures were consulted. This verdict makes no improvement claim. No contact sheet was supplied; small-size readability was considered from the displayed frames as well as their full-size detail.

The trainer has readable blue, cream and brown clothing blocks and a clear human silhouette in these views. There are no visible companion creatures, so this set provides no evidence for creature design quality, companion scale, or creature/environment coherence. The armored figure in `after/heart_entry.png` reads as a person, but the distant, largely frontal presentation does not establish expressive character quality comparable with the Palworld subjects. These limitations prevent a broader character-art acceptance claim.

## Scoped assets

### Pump machinery: PASS for machinery readability and internal material coherence; scene integration remains unresolved

In `after/pump_intake.png` and `after/pump_return.png`, the twin vertical cylinders, flywheel, connecting linkage and bent pipe mouths identify an industrial pump without a label. The wheel remains the key shape at small size. Dark worn metal, restrained warm edges and a timber support base form a coherent old mechanical object. `after/gate_intake.png` offers a larger oblique view and supports that reading. The scale is substantial beside the trainer but believable as fixed machinery.

Specific remaining issues:

- `after/pump_intake.png`, `after/pump_return.png`, and `after/gate_intake.png`: the inlet/outlet mouths visibly terminate in air, and the base sits on ordinary paving. No visible pipe route ties the machine to the water, floor channel, wall, or gate mechanism. It reads as a placed machine rather than functioning infrastructure. This is scene assembly work: connect and seat the existing pump using pipe runs, mounts and an appropriate foundation. The screenshots do not justify replacing the pump body.
- `after/pump_intake.png` and `after/pump_return.png`: black cylinders, lower linkage and base merge in the shaded middle of the object. The wheel and elbow carry readability, while the mechanism within them loses hierarchy. Local lighting/material-value tuning can preserve worn iron while separating cylinder shoulders, linkage and foundation. Do not globally brighten the chamber to solve this.
- `after/intake_gallery.png`: the pump becomes a dark intricate patch beside a very flat, clean gate. The pump's internal materials work, but the two objects have markedly different surface/detail treatments. Bring the gate into the same authored material family rather than degrading the readable pump silhouette.

### Timber/metal sluice gates: FAIL for finished asset presentation; closed/open state readability passes

`after/gate_intake.png` and `after/gate_return.png` unmistakably show a barrier. Brown lower panels, blue-black vertical bars, pale fasteners, diagonal braces and overhead handwheel distinguish its parts. `after-open/gate_intake.png` and `after-open/gate_return.png` unmistakably show an unobstructed route. Those state silhouettes work at small size.

Specific defects:

- `after/gate_intake.png` and `after/gate_return.png`: the lower timber field is an almost uninterrupted brown slab. Fine vertical marks do not establish substantial individual boards; thin diagonal strips and uniform pale dots read as diagrammatic decoration. The metal bars and fasteners have much less edge/material definition than the pump immediately beside them. This needs authored gate material and construction detail: meaningful plank separation/end structure, fastener seating, and restrained edge/wear treatment consistent with the pump and lanterns. It is asset finishing work, not a scatter or global tint fix; it does not require discarding the basic gate silhouette.
- `after/gate_return.png`: the very broad gate spans the room with little visible side-guide or structural support articulation. Together with the regular bars and slab panel this reads as an oversized repeating assembly rather than weighty water-control architecture. Add readable guides/supports and a convincing relationship to the water channel. Support placement is scene work; absent brackets, channels or connection pieces require local modeling.
- `after-open/gate_intake.png` and `after-open/gate_return.png`: the moving panel is completely absent beneath an apparently continuous masonry ceiling. Short hanging vertical rods and the overhead assembly remain, but there is no legible pocket, slot or visible stowed gate explaining where the large panel went. These stills do not prove actual mesh interpenetration, but the visible result reads as disappearance into the ceiling. Resolve the raised-state housing/clearance in the scene and show the stored gate or a credible receiving structure. A pair of stills cannot certify the animation or collision.

### Blue diamond-crest hanging banners: PASS for the requested scoped asset, visible fit and clipping checks

`after/banner_east.png` and `after/banner_west.png` show an identifiable hanging cloth object with a blue field, pale crest, gold edge/fringe, shallow folds, hanging loops and a horizontal support. The crest remains clear at small size. The pointed hem and the warm border against stone match the Veilfall board's banner language. Each banner fits between the pale vertical architectural strips and finishes above the floor. The visible cloth does not obviously pierce the wall, lanterns or foliage. No obvious banner clipping is visible in either dedicated view or in `after/heart_entry.png`.

This is a scoped pass, not proof of all-angle or animated clearance. The scene's repeated wall bays and foliage remain separate defects; they do not invalidate these banners. No new banner art is required by this evidence.

## Whole-scene defects and repair split

- `after/heart_entry.png` and both `after-open/` frames: a low, unbroken rectangular ceiling and huge uniform floor dominate the room. The reference chamber has enclosing rock, vertical changes, bridges and a water shaft that organize the space around the crystal. Here the crystal is an isolated display at the end of a broad masonry box. Scene restructuring can establish height, route layers, framing and a less empty approach; believable cavern/rock integration and water architecture need actual shaped geometry if the current pieces cannot provide it. More small props along the same flat perimeter would not solve this.
- `after/banner_east.png`, `after/banner_west.png`, and `after/heart_entry.png`: the identical lantern/ivy/arch rhythm reads as repeated wall dressing. The ivy is flat bright green and separates poorly into lit/shaded leaf masses; ornate dark upper rails become dense noisy strips. Recompose clusters and gaps in scene, then give foliage readable mass and shading. The latter is material/asset art, not placement alone.
- `after/pump_intake.png`, `after/intake_gallery.png`, and `after/heart_entry.png`: nearly every enclosure surface carries the same high-frequency cobble language. Floor, walls and ceiling compete equally, and the large field of floor texture becomes the primary visual information. Differentiate paving, structural masonry, ceiling and wet edges with authored materials and geometry, then tune lighting to emphasize the route and subjects. Light placement alone cannot create the absent construction hierarchy.
- `after/gate_return.png` and `after-open/gate_return.png`: the side water areas read as flat blue empty planes with hard pale borders, without readable contact/wet-edge treatment. Separate fence modules have large repeated gaps and no continuous handrail, producing a row of ornamental fence fragments beside the bridge. Water-edge treatment and rail assembly are scene/material work; proper bridge support and rail connection pieces may require local modeling. No reflective-renderer requirement is implied.
- `after/heart_entry.png` and `after-open/gate_return.png`: the waterfall is a soft rectangular white-gray curtain with a hard architectural termination. It supplies a bright backdrop but not the reference's sense of falling water occupying a deep shaft. A better-shaped cascade, readable flow bands and a landing/spray/contact treatment require waterfall art/material work as well as placement.
- `after/heart_entry.png`: the blue crystal successfully creates a distinct focal silhouette and warm lanterns provide some cool/warm contrast. However, the lighting field across the floor and ceiling stays nearly uniform, so there is little depth hierarchy between entry, chamber and background. Scene lighting/value tuning can improve this without changing the crystal.
- `after/gate_intake.png` and `after/heart_entry.png`: the large story panel covers parts of the pump/chamber and a right-side banner. `after/intake_gallery.png` stacks broad control bars across much of the lower view. The text is legible and contained within the image, but the occupied screen area weakens subject inspection and environmental composition. This is UI/layout tuning, judged independently of the Palworld UI.

No obvious banner seam, gross trainer/floor penetration, or surface z-fighting is established by these stills. Repeated intricate rails look noisy, but a still cannot establish temporal shimmer or popping. Performance and traversal are not judged from these images.

## The three largest gaps from the references, ranked

1. **The space does not yet carry Veilfall's landmark identity.** `after/heart_entry.png` and `after-open/gate_return.png` are dominated by a rectangular tiled room. The Veilfall board makes natural rock, vertical circulation and falling water the room's actual structure. The candidate has the right blue crystal/banner accents but lacks that spatial language. Scene restructuring plus rock/water/structural art is required.
2. **Material families and construction detail disagree.** `after/gate_intake.png`, `after/banner_east.png`, and `after/intake_gallery.png` mix a convincingly weathered pump with clean elementary gate parts, noisy ornament, flat leaf silhouettes and uniformly tiled enclosure surfaces. The gameplay references maintain a more consistent stylized treatment across subjects and environment. This needs targeted material/model finishing, with scene lighting used afterward to establish hierarchy.
3. **The world feels sparsely staged and detached from creature adventure.** `after-open/gate_intake.png` and `after/heart_entry.png` devote much of the frame to empty paving with isolated props/one distant figure. Palworld's base and landmark frames use inhabited working spaces and readable creature groups to make the player's activity apparent. Authored route/use clusters and stronger foreground/middle/background staging are scene work. This set cannot establish whether suitable creature art is available, because no creature is shown; a creature-bearing gameplay witness is necessary before judging that part of the bar.

## Bar A — NO

These full-scene frames do not yet read as a realized place in the key-art world when evaluated with the chapter-specific Veilfall board. Blue banners, warm lamps, stone and the crystal are useful identity cues, but the broad rectangular cobble enclosure and weak water/rock integration outweigh them. Scene fixes: room/route composition, vertical layering, light/value hierarchy, mechanical connections, water contacts and less regular wall dressing. Art fixes: stronger rock/water architecture, differentiated enclosure materials, gate construction/material finishing and better-shaped/shaded foliage where the visible assets cannot supply the needed forms. The banners and recognizable pump body do not need replacement to address this failure.

## Bar B — NO

Beside the supplied Palworld gameplay frames, this set reads as a fantasy environment in assembly rather than the same polished creature-adventure production register. The largest visible causes are empty/repetitive staging, inconsistent object finish, and the visibly simplified water/enclosure construction. The trainer's readable silhouette and the pump/banner shapes are strengths, but they do not carry the whole frame. Scene tuning can address staging, grounding, connections, lighting and UI occlusion. Gate/environment/water material and geometry deficiencies need art work. No judgment of unseen companion quality, animation or performance is inferred.
