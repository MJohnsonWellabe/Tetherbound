# Independent visual verdict: Veilfall sluice gates

## Evidence and scope

Personally opened all six native PNGs in `D:/tetherbound/asset-validation/shots/veilfall-sluice-r3-fixed/after/` and `after-open/`: `gate_intake.png`, `gate_return.png`, and `intake_gallery.png` in each folder. Also inspected a six-frame contact sheet at 30% of native image dimensions (`sluice-final-contact.png`). Compared directly with the Veilfall stronghold board, Meadows key art, and all five `palworld-0*.jpg` references in `visual-acceptance/docs/reference/`. Applied the visual-judge rubric and ART_DIRECTION visual standards. No implementation, previous verdict, or earlier candidate images were inspected. This verdict assesses the supplied static views, not movement, collision, internal mechanical operation, or other chapter locations.

## Scoped sluice asset verdict: PASS

This is a pass for the gate assemblies' visible construction, housing placement, and state silhouettes in these six views. It is not acceptance of the surrounding chamber or of a complete hydraulic installation.

- **Construction/material coherence — PASS.** In `after/gate_intake.png` and `after/gate_return.png`, warm brown lower panels, dark vertical bars, horizontal bands, diagonal braces and visible round fasteners form a consistent timber-and-metal assembly. Connections terminate at bands or fastener plates rather than visibly floating. The solid lower portion and open grille above create two readable masses. The structure is clearly larger than the trainer and appropriate to a large controlled opening. At contact-sheet size, it remains a constructed gate, rather than an indistinct dark rectangle. No obvious torn geometry, exposed backfaces, or disconnected gate pieces appear in these views.
- **Mechanical housing/placement readability — PASS.** `after/intake_gallery.png` gives the clearest evidence: a distinct overhead casing, centred wheel, lower beam and brackets sit immediately above the gate and span between the stone sides. The same casing remains in `after-open/intake_gallery.png`, making the opening look like a controlled threshold. Its wheel and bounded housing silhouette remain legible at reduced size. This establishes the visual reading of a lifting gate assembly; the stills do not establish how the rigid leaf is stored or how the wheel drives it.
- **Closed/open-state silhouette — PASS.** Every paired view has an unmistakable change from a wall-to-wall barrier to clear passage beneath the retained overhead assembly. `gate_return` is particularly clear: closed bars cross the crystal and the bridge destination, while the open state exposes a continuous route toward the crystal. The distinction survives contact-sheet size without a label. No gate remnant visibly obstructs the open route.

Remaining asset finish limitation: the gate's broad brown panels and housing are comparatively flat and clean, especially in `after/gate_intake.png`, while the nearby pump has dense mottled wear and the masonry has pronounced texture. More coherent surface detail and wear would bring them closer together. This does not erase the gate's readable material divisions or construction, so it is not a scoped rejection here.

## Scene integration gaps, separate from gate defects

1. **Hydraulic purpose is weakly communicated.** Both `gate_intake` frames place the gate above uninterrupted dry-looking cobblestone. The adjacent pump ends in conspicuous open pipe mouths with no visible connection to a channel, gate actuator or water route. Both `gate_return` frames have water beside the bridge, but the gate crosses the broad walkway and reads first as a portcullis. These are missing relationships in the scene. The gate itself remains readable, but the pictures do not clearly explain what water it controls. Channel edges, intake/outfall placement and an authored relationship between pump, flow and threshold are needed.
2. **The return bridge has separated fence panels rather than a continuous constructed railing.** In both `gate_return` frames, evenly spaced dark fence sections leave regular large gaps, with their bases disappearing into the flat blue surface along the bridge. That creates a repeated-prop appearance. Continuous mounting and deliberate endpoint treatment would improve the scene without replacing the gate.
3. **The housing loses presentation in the close cameras.** The nearest casing and wheel are cropped by the top edge in both `gate_return` frames, and largely outside the view in both `gate_intake` frames. `intake_gallery` supplies the missing evidence, so this is a composition limitation rather than a missing asset feature.

## Full-scene Bars

**Bar A — NO.** There are relevant identity anchors: brown and dark-metal hardware, warm lanterns, blue banners, stone, a central blue crystal, and white falling water. The trainer's blue clothing and brown pack also belong to the broad adventure palette. Nevertheless, the Veilfall board's interior is defined by tall water-carved volume, layered stone architecture, upper crossings, powerful cool water light, and warm pockets around inhabited edges. These supplied rooms are low rectangular boxes dominated by the same cobblestone pattern on floor, walls and ceiling. They do not yet carry the chapter's architectural silhouette or the key art's authored depth and atmosphere.

**Bar B — NO.** The third-person trainer and interactable props identify an adventure-game intent, but these frames do not yet read as the same kind of polished creature-adventure presentation as the Palworld references. The references give subjects strong separation, fill space with meaningful habitat or occupation, and make creatures prominent. Here, broad repeated floors, uniform walls and sparse isolated props dominate. No companion creature is visible in any of the six images, so creature appeal and relative trainer/creature scale cannot be judged; the scene also supplies no visual evidence of the core creature relationship. The trainer has readable blue/brown colour blocks and plausible contact, but its dark legs and hair blend with the surrounding values at reduced size. These are scene presentation conclusions, not a finding that an unseen creature asset is defective.

## Three largest reference gaps, ranked

1. **Architectural volume and authored layering.** Most evident in both `intake_gallery` frames and `after-open/gate_return.png`: a long flat ceiling, parallel walls and broadly uninterrupted tiled floor produce a uniform corridor/chamber. The Veilfall interior reference uses arches, broken natural rock, vertical falls and crossings at multiple heights to establish a monumental place. Scene layout, height, framing and intermediate structures can address much of this; distinctive rock/arch/water-support silhouettes will require suitable geometry if the available scene pieces cannot provide them. Texture changes alone cannot supply that shape.
2. **Water and material hierarchy.** Most evident in both `gate_return` frames: the blue pools are nearly featureless planes with bright pale boundary strips, while the far fall reads as a rectangular white patch behind the crystal. The board gives water a legible path, scale and contact with stone. Across all six frames, similarly detailed cobblestone competes everywhere and the lighting keeps much of the architecture in one mid-dark range. Water edge/flow/contact treatment, intentional wet zones and larger warm/cool light separation are scene/material work. The rectangular room and fall silhouette may need geometry changes; higher texture detail by itself is insufficient.
3. **Subject presence and meaningful occupation.** Most evident in `after-open/gate_intake.png` and both `intake_gallery` frames: the trainer faces a large amount of empty floor with a few isolated pumps, chests or barrels. Palworld's base frame groups work, props and creatures into activities, and its travel frames maintain readable creatures within designed habitat. Authored prop groupings, work areas, companion staging and stronger subject separation are scene fixes. No creature is visible here, so these images cannot establish whether new creature art is needed. They establish a presentation gap, not evidence for replacing an unseen asset.

No comparative improvement claim is made. The gate assembly passes its stated visual scope; both full-scene reference bars remain open.
