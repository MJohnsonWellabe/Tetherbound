# Blind visual verdict — Meadows tree-line survey (4 stands)

Frames judged: `place2-the-rise.png`, `place5-bridge-approach.png`, `comp7-pond-reveal.png`,
`comp8-bridge-rim.png`, plus the contact sheet. References read: the Meadows key-art board and
`palworld-01..05`.

Instrument caveats honoured: no judgement of aliasing, AA, post, shadow softness or frame rate.
Everything below is composition, silhouette, scale, geometry, colour relationships or value
structure.

---

## 0. Two things that break the brief, stated up front

**The player is only in two of the four frames.** `comp7-pond-reveal` and `comp8-bridge-rim`
contain the trainer. **`place2-the-rise` and `place5-bridge-approach` contain no human figure at
all** — I checked at 6x zoom across both frames. So two of the four stands have no ruler in shot.

**The four stands do not share a camera.** In `comp7` the trainer measures 124 px tall standing on
ground at y=408 with the horizon at y≈210, which puts the camera at **2.86 m**. In `comp8` the
trainer measures 118 px at y=428 with horizon y≈250 — camera **2.71 m**. Consistent third-person
rig. But in `place2` the waymarker signpost at x≈862 measures 83 px of visible post on ground at
y=235 with the horizon at y≈197; for that post to be a normal ~2.2 m waymarker the camera must be
at **≈1.05 m**. `place5` is framed identically low. So `place2` and `place5` were shot from a
knee-height camera, not the game's camera. That is why their bottom half is a wall of blurred dirt.

Consequence: absolute heights in `place2`/`place5` below are given as ranges derived from in-frame
props (signpost, fence rail), not from the player, and I say so each time. Ratios within a frame
are calibration-free and are the numbers to trust.

## 1. TREE-LINE SILHOUETTE, per stand

Method: for any object standing on the camera's ground plane, real height =
`camera_height x (pixel height) / (base_y - horizon_y)`. Numbers rounded; where a trunk base is
occluded the figure is a floor, not a value.

### comp7-pond-reveal — **the only stand with a real tree-line, and it is good**

This is the one frame where the answer is yes. Zoomed on the right-hand run (x 960–1280) there are
four distinguishable individuals: one leaning right with a broad asymmetric crown, one upright with
a taller narrower crown, a smaller dark one tucked behind, and a low one at the hill edge. Canopy
tops sit at y≈145, 158, 172 and 190 on bases within 15 px of each other — a genuine ragged outline
with peaks and shoulders. The left grove (x 260–620) is a separate, much older-reading mass:
canopy tops at y≈35–60, a layered interior with visible sky holes between crowns, and secondary
branch structure inside the canopy rather than a solid blob.

- Tallest: the left grove, ~215 px on Δ=40 → **≈15.5 m, about 8.6 player heights** (bases partly
  hidden behind the rise, so this is a floor).
- Right-hand line: ~127 px on Δ=62 → **≈5.9 m, about 3.3 player heights**.
- Small lollipop at x 195–260, canopy a single flat disc on a bare Y-fork: **≈2 player heights**.

Verdict for this stand: **varied**, roughly a 4:1 height spread with three legible age classes.
This is the standard the other three stands are failing to meet.

### place2-the-rise — **the worst offender: a repeated row**

The mid-ground run between x≈310 and x≈565 is six trunks that are the same tree. Straight,
near-parallel, no lean variation, trunk widths within about 20% of each other (18–25 px), canopy
undersides all beginning at y≈185–195, canopy tops all landing between y≈97 and y≈113 — a 16 px
spread across six trees — and bases sitting on a near-straight line at roughly even spacing. Read
the contact sheet at 30% and that band is a picket fence with green on top. It is the single most
generator-looking element in the whole set.

Against that row there are exactly two other classes and nothing between them: the leaning
left-hand tree at x 195–330 (one small dark disc on a bare forked trunk, **≈2.0–2.4 m, about 1.2
player heights**), and the giant cropped trunks. So the "range" here is bimodal, not graded.

- Tallest with a visible crown: the mid-row, ~115 px on Δ=23 → **≈5–6 m, about 3 player heights**.
- The central grey giant at x 500–597 has **no visible crown at all** — it exits the top of the
  frame. Visible portion alone is ≥5 m; true height unknown and unjudgeable.

Verdict: **near-uniform**, with the variation that does exist coming from two outlier assets rather
than from an age range.

### place5-bridge-approach — **no tree-line is presented**

The frame is walled left and right by two cropped giants and two near-camera canopy masses. The
only fully visible trees are the distant plateau row at x 780–940, which sits on ground above the
camera's eye level so it cannot be measured against the horizon — but read as a row of near-equal
lollipops about 30 px tall each, tops within 10 px of one another, evenly spaced along the bank
crest. Same picket-fence read as `place2`.

- Tallest with a visible crown: the plateau row, **roughly 3–4 player heights** by comparison with
  the 1.1 m rail fence directly below it.
- The central trunk is cropped; measured against that fence its base is **≈3.0 m across**, and
  again there is no crown in frame.

Verdict: **not varied, and mostly not present.** You cannot read a tree-line from this stand.

### comp8-bridge-rim — **the camera is inside the grove; there is no silhouette to read**

Every one of the seven large trunks in this frame is cut by the top edge. The upper third of the
image is a continuous near-camera leaf ceiling that closes off the entire skyline. The only trees
whose full shape you can see are three background ones: the round-crowned tree at x 700–770 (72 px
on Δ=50 → **≈3.9 m, 2.2 player heights**) and two in the sky gap at x 880–1000 (**≈2.7–3.0 m, ~1.6
player heights**).

- Tallest measurable: **2.2 player heights.** The tall ones are unmeasurable because they have no
  visible top.

Verdict: **unjudgeable by design, and that is itself the defect.** A stand named for a landmark
should not be shot from inside a canopy.

## 2. SCALE AGREEMENT

**What is right.** The trainer reads correctly at 1.80 m everywhere he appears. The rail fence in
`place5` (x 620–783) measures ≈1.1 m to the top rail — correct. The stone ruin at `place5` x
460–490 reads ≈3.9 m — plausible. Rocks and pebbles are plausible. The road width in `comp8`
(≈4.5 m at the player's depth, measured against his 1.80 m) is a believable cart track.

**What is wrong, in order of loudness:**

**a) Trunks are roughly one-third the width of their own canopy.** This is the consistent
proportion error and it appears in every frame.
- `comp7`, right-hand tree line, the leaning tree at x 1150–1250: trunk 30 px, canopy 93 px —
  **1:3.1**. The neighbouring upright at x 1080–1160: 22 px trunk, 80 px canopy — **1:3.6**.
- `place2`, mid-row: ~20 px trunk under a ~70 px canopy — **1:3.5**.
- `comp8`, the round tree at x 700–770: 14 px trunk, 70 px canopy — **1:5**, the closest to right.

A broadleaf with a 9 m crown carries roughly a 1 m trunk, about 1:9. These read at 1:3–1:3.6, so
the trunks are **two to three times too thick for the crowns they carry.** The result is that even
the good `comp7` tree line reads as a row of stumpy mushrooms rather than as oaks.

**b) The giant trunks carry no crown and no consistent diameter.** Measured three ways:
- `comp8`, the hero trunk the player stands against — measured directly against him (his shoulders
  are ~37 px, his height 118 px), the trunk is **≈1.2–1.4 m across**.
- `place5`, the central trunk, against the 1.1 m fence — **≈3.0 m across**.
- `place2`, the central grey giant, against the signpost — **≈1.8–2.1 m across**.

A 1.3 m trunk implies a 25–30 m tree. A 3 m trunk implies a redwood. None of them has a crown
inside the frame, and the trees that *do* have crowns in the same frames are 3–6 m tall. So the
world contains a 5 m tree population and a 25 m+ trunk population with nothing in between, and the
big trunks do not agree with each other about how thick a tree of that species is.

**c) The cottage in `comp7` is roughly half size.** The thatched building at x 623–648 measures 28
px tall on a base at y≈252 with the horizon at y≈210 — **≈1.9 m to the roof ridge**, about one
player height. Even allowing that its footing is partly screened by mid-ground shrubs, it cannot be
more than ~2.5 m. A pitched-thatch cottage should be 5–6 m to the ridge. Beside it, trees at a
comparable depth measure 5.2 m — the trees are nearly three times the height of the house they
stand next to, which is the opposite of the key-art board's settlement panels where the roofs come
to about half tree height. This is the clearest single scale disagreement in the set.

**d) `comp8`'s gate landmark is 50 x 25 px.** The stone gate with its hanging banner — the thing
the shot is named for — occupies about 0.14% of the frame and sits between two trunks with a leaf
mass hanging over its top edge. Whether or not its modelled size is right, at this framing it is
not a landmark; it is a smudge.

**e) The `place2` waymarker signboard.** At the ~1.05 m camera implied by its own post, the board
measures **≈1.3 m wide and 0.5 m tall** — an oversized board on a slender post. Minor, but it is
the only signage in the set and it reads wrong.

## 3. DEFECTS

**Grounding — every trunk in the set meets the ground as a cone cut by a plane.** Best seen at 6x
on `comp8` behind the player: the trunk has no root flare, no widening at the base, no contact
darkening, no debris or undergrowth skirt, and terminates in a hard, sharp, perfectly horizontal
edge against the grass. The same on `comp7`'s leaning tree at x 1180 (it simply stops at y≈275) and
on `place5`'s central trunk. Nothing is floating and nothing is sunk — but nothing is *planted*
either. This is one fix applied once to the tree asset, not four fixes.

**No contact shadow under the player in `comp8`.** The frame carries a large cast canopy shadow
across the road, so shadowing is clearly working — but the trainer has no darkening beneath his
boots, which reads as a decal standing on the ground rather than in it.

**`place5`: the trunk is planted in the path.** The central trunk's flared base sits on bare dirt
with path surface continuing to its left and to its right. Whether or not it blocks navigation, it
reads as a scatter instance dropped without a road mask.

**`place5`: the bridge crosses nothing.** At 4x, the plank deck's right edge meets the terrain along
a straight diagonal seam with the ground shorn flat against it — an unresolved mesh intersection,
no abutment, no bank geometry. There is **no water, no gap and no drop** under the span; grass runs
continuously beneath. A "bridge approach" over solid ground. The left end of the rail fence also
appears to run out over the road cut with no post reaching ground.

**`place5`: a hard-shaded terrain shear at x 400–470.** The left hill's right flank is a single
flat-shaded polygon face with a straight silhouette edge from y≈150 to y≈230 — a terrain cut with
no rock geometry to justify it. Beside it at x 500–553, y 208–230 there is a pale grey-green flat
wedge beside the road that reads as an untextured or LOD-swapped terrain patch.

**`place2`: the world ends in a straight grey line.** The distant terrain terminates at y≈200 in a
hard horizontal pale band with a crisp bottom edge, with nothing beyond it — no hills, no
mountains, no distant landmark. `comp7` and `place5` both have distant terrain; `place2` does not,
so the four stands disagree about whether the Meadows has a horizon.

**Repeated scatter, evenly spread — the loudest procedural tell.** In `place2` the same
white-flowered V-shaped bush appears at least fifteen times across the rise at near-identical scale
(no size jitter I can detect), upright, at broadly even spacing, with no clustering and no clearings.
In `comp7` the same asset repeats down the left slope. Palworld's fields cluster their bushes and
leave bare patches; these do not.

**Ground cover is loose quads over painted dirt, not a mat.** In `place2` at 3x the grass is
individual isolated blades with visible dirt between nearly all of them; the illusion of a meadow
is carried by the ground texture, not by geometry. `comp8`'s roadside and `place5`'s bank are the
same.

**Two bark materials that are not the same species.** In `place2`, the central giant samples
`#4A3A2E` (cool desaturated grey-brown) while every other trunk in the same frame samples `#833B1F`
/ `#864024` (warm rust). Two trees ten metres apart with unrelated bark. In `comp7` and `comp8`
everything is the warm one.

**Style outliers: bare twig-trees.** `place5` at x 617–650, y 150–175 and `place2` at x 1165–1215,
y 240–300 are leafless trees with thin, near-realistic branch geometry standing among flat blobby
stylised canopies. They read as imported from a different asset family.

**The composed subject is swallowed in two of four stands.** In `comp8` the trainer is placed
directly in front of the largest, darkest trunk in the frame — brown hair and a tan backpack on
`#49180A` bark. Drop the contact sheet to 30% and he vanishes; two metres to his left there is open
sunlit road he would read against instantly. In `comp8` the gate is likewise half-veiled by a
hanging leaf mass. `place5`'s left canopy mass and `place2`'s cropped central trunk each eat about a
fifth of their frame for no compositional return.

**`comp7-pond-reveal` contains no pond.** There is no water anywhere in it, at any zoom. Whatever
the reveal is, it is not in this frame.

**Value structure is crushed, and the frames do not agree about the time of day.** Measured
luminance medians: `place5` **39**, `comp8` **61**, `place2` **78**, `comp7` **89** — a 2.3x swing
across four stands on one walk. `place5`'s lower 60% has a median of 32 and samples `#161000` — an
effectively black foreground occupying about half the image. And the top end is missing everywhere:
the 98th-percentile luminance is 150–179 across all four, where `palworld-02/03/04` reach 221, 231
and 239. **Nothing in any of these four frames is sunlit-bright.** That is not a post-processing
judgement; it is the histogram.

**The Team Tether oxblood has leaked onto the forest.** Trunk bark samples `#833B1F`–`#864024`,
which is the same rust-oxblood the art board reserves for Team Tether banners and the stronghold.
In `comp8` the entire frame is walled with it. The colour that is supposed to mean danger is
currently the ambient colour of a friendly wood.

## 4. THE THREE BIGGEST REMAINING GAPS vs the Palworld bar, ranked

### 1. There is nothing alive in any of these frames. — **needs no new art; needs population**

Every Palworld reference has creatures in it: `palworld-01` a boss filling a third of frame,
`palworld-03` four pals mid-fight, `palworld-04` a mounted pal plus three more, `palworld-02` a herd
on the path. The key-art board puts wildlife in most of its panels and a companion beside the
trainer in both the day and night strips. These four frames contain **zero creatures, zero NPCs,
zero birds, zero livestock** — a walked route through the first region of a creature-training game
with no creatures on it. This is by a wide margin the largest gap, and it is the one that decides
bar question B.

Fixable with what is in the build **if creature assets exist elsewhere in the project** — this is
placement and spawn work, not modelling. If the survey route genuinely has no spawns along it, that
is a content gap, not an art gap.

### 2. The frame is empty and dark where Palworld's is dense and bright. — **mostly tuning; partly new art**

`place5` gives 55% of its area to a single blurred near-black dirt plane; `place2` gives ~55% to a
featureless blurred rise; `comp8` ~45%; `comp7` ~40%. In `palworld-02` and `palworld-03` the
equivalent foreground band carries a continuous grass mat, clustered shrubs, rocks, a readable path
edge and characters. Combined with the histogram finding above — median luminance 39 to 89 against
Palworld's 102 to 130, and no frame reaching past L=179 against their 221–239 — the set reads
underlit and underfilled at the same time.

Fixable by scene work for most of it: raise the two low cameras in `place2`/`place5` back to the
2.7 m rig so the foreground is meadow and not mud; increase grass instance density and add size
jitter; cluster the scatter and cut clearings instead of spreading it evenly; lift the exposure/key
so the sunlit slopes actually reach a highlight. The part that is **not** fixable this way is the
grass and shrub asset variety itself — one V-bush and one grass blade repeated cannot be made to
look like Palworld's undergrowth no matter how it is scattered. That needs two or three more
ground-cover meshes.

### 3. The trees are one shape at two sizes, and the crowns never enter the frame. — **half tuning, half new art**

Ranked third only because `comp7` proves the asset can look right. But: `place2`'s six-trunk picket
row, `place5`'s equal-lollipop plateau row, and `comp8`'s complete absence of any visible crown are
three quarters of the survey. Add the 1:3 trunk-to-canopy proportion and the 1.3 m / 2 m / 3 m trunk
diameters that don't agree with each other, and the Meadows currently has no legible oak-grove
identity — which is the first noun in the key-art board's own description ("rolling hills, oak
groves, clear streams").

Fixable by scene work: per-instance scale jitter (±40% instead of the current ~±10%), rotation and
lean variation, framing the two "inside the grove" stands back far enough that crowns are in shot,
and reserving the giant-trunk asset for a handful of authored landmark trees instead of using it as
roadside filler. **Not** fixable by scene work: the trunk-to-canopy proportion is baked into the
mesh, and there is no mid-age tree — nothing between a 5 m lollipop and a 25 m column. That is one
new tree LOD family, or a re-proportioned version of the existing one.

## The two bar questions

### A. Do these frames read as belonging to the world of `tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`?

**No.**

What carries: `comp7`'s composition genuinely does. Rolling hill, a road cutting across it, a
layered grove at left, a settlement roof in the middle distance, hills beyond — that is the board's
top-left panel, in engine. The greens (`#5E912D`, `#799B29`), the road ochre and the sky blue are
recognisably from the board's swatch strip.

What sinks it: three things the board insists on and these frames do not have. **Water** — the board
names "clear streams" and puts a pond, a river and a jetty in three of its panels; there is not a
single pixel of water in four frames, including two named for a pond and a bridge. **Skyline
landmarks** — the board's panels all carry a distant silhouette (a peak, a windmill, a tower, a
standing stone); `place2` ends the world in a flat grey line and `comp8` closes the sky off
entirely. **Bright, cheerful daylight** — the board's panels are high-key with real highlights;
`place5` is a near-black frame at a median luminance of 39 and reads closer to a horror wood than to
"peaceful by day". Add the oxblood bark and the doll's-house cottage and the family resemblance
breaks.

### B. Shown beside `palworld-0*.jpg`, would someone say these are trying to be the same kind of game?

**No.**

They would say these are trying to be the same kind of *world* — the palette, the stylised-realism
register, the third-person over-shoulder framing in `comp7` and `comp8`, and the road-through-a-
meadow subject all land in the same neighbourhood as `palworld-02`. `comp8`'s value structure, dark
canopy foreground opening onto a bright sunlit slope at the gate, is the closest thing in this set
to `palworld-01`'s forest depth and is a genuinely good composition idea.

But nobody looking at these four frames would say they are the same kind of *game*, because there
is nothing in them that indicates a game at all: no creature, no character in two of the four, no
encounter, no event. Every Palworld reference answers "what do you do here" in the first half
second. These four answer "you walk". That is the split, and it is not a rendering problem.

**Fixable by scene work:** creature and NPC placement along the route; camera height on `place2` and
`place5`; subject placement in `comp8` (move the trainer off the trunk onto the lit road); framing
back from the canopy so the gate and the crowns are in shot; scatter clustering, size jitter and
clearings; grass density; exposure/key lift; a water body under at least one of the two bridges;
reserving the giant-trunk asset for authored landmarks; reconciling the two bark materials; removing
the bare twig-trees or committing to them as a deliberate dead-tree species.

**Needs art that is not in this build:** a mid-age tree with a correct trunk-to-canopy proportion
(the 1:3 ratio is in the mesh); two or three more ground-cover meshes so the undergrowth is not one
bush repeated; root-flare or a base-blend treatment on the tree asset so trunks stop meeting the
ground as cut cones; a distant skyline landmark for `place2`'s empty horizon; and a village building
at a habitable scale — the `comp7` cottage is roughly half the size a person could live in.
