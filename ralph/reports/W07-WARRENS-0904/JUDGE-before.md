# Blind visual judgement — Burrow Warrens, `before` pass (W07-WARRENS-0904)

Judged: `shots/warrens_0904/_sheet_before.png`, the five frames in
`shots/warrens_0904/before/`, against `docs/reference/tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`
and `docs/reference/palworld-0*.jpg`.

Compatibility / software GL, so composition, silhouette, value structure, colour
relationships, scale and geometry are judged; fine shadow and post quality are not.
Measurements are taken against the 1.80 m trainer standing 3 m ahead of the camera.

---

## 1. Per-frame defects, ranked

### 01 — entry mouth (approach from outside)

1. **The cave mouth is not readable as a cave mouth, and at thumbnail size it is not
   visible at all.** It is a small grey rectangle with a flat grey frame set into a
   brown slab — a doorway on a wall, not a hole in the ground. No lip, no threshold,
   no spoil, no soil spilling out, no darkness gradient: the interior behind the
   opening sits at roughly the same value as the shadow on the rock beside it, so the
   eye reads a panel, not a void. On the contact sheet this frame reads as "boulders in
   a field"; nothing says the required dungeon of the Meadows is here.
2. **The trainer stands dead centre directly in front of the mouth and occludes most of
   it.** The one thing the stand exists to show is behind the character.
3. **Floating geometry.** The large overhanging boulder mass across the top of the
   frame is unsupported: at the left, open sky and the distant meadow horizon are
   visible *underneath* it. A rock cluster that size hanging over nothing is the
   loudest artefact in the set.
4. **Two unrelated rock materials in one shot.** The near boulders are untextured
   flat-shaded facets in flat chocolate brown; behind them is a pale grey speckled
   rock. They do not read as the same formation or the same world.
5. **Sharp-edged green paint on the rock tops.** Mid-green sits on the upper faces of
   the brown boulders with a razor line and no blend, no moss silhouette, no
   scattered growth — it reads as terrain colour bleeding onto the rock rather than
   vegetation.
6. **Ground colour is off-palette.** A saturated sulphur-yellow/ochre blotch fills the
   foreground next to acid-green grass. The key art's meadow is fresh green with warm
   earth paths; this yellow is not in that palette and it is the loudest colour in the
   frame.
7. **Grass is countable.** Single blade cards, near-identical height, all vertical,
   evenly scattered with bare ground showing between every one. Two fern clumps and a
   couple of flower sprigs are the entire dressing.
8. **The distance is empty.** The visible horizon is a flat green-brown plate with no
   trees, no landmark, no silhouette of anything.

### 02 — first chamber, looking deeper

1. **The corridor is a row of extruded boxes.** Every wall, ceiling, lintel and opening
   is an axis-aligned rectangular prism with hard 90° corners and dead-flat faces. The
   view down the hall is a plain rectangle nested inside a plain rectangle nested
   inside a plain rectangle. Nothing curves, narrows, slumps or is irregular.
2. **The wall surface is one tiled polished-granite bitmap.** The same mottled
   grey/white speckle runs across walls, ceiling, protruding beams and the floor edges
   at the same texel scale, near and far, with no darkening in corners, no wear on
   edges, no strata, no tool marks, no roots. This is a mineral countertop, not dug
   earth.
3. **No light and no light source.** 95% of the frame sits below roughly 40% grey and
   highlights are effectively absent; the only bright thing in the shot is the doorway
   to the next room. Walls are lit identically from floor to ceiling, so they have no
   form. Nothing in the frame emits, and nothing tells the eye where to go except the
   hole at the end.
4. **The frame is near-monochrome** (mean saturation ~0.16). Slate grey wall, slate
   grey floor, brown trainer, cream boar. There is no colour identity for this place.
5. **Nothing has a contact shadow.** The trainer's boots and the boar's trotters both
   meet the floor with no darkening whatsoever; both float on a flat plane. The two
   loose rocks on the floor do the same.
6. **The proportions read as a basement, not a burrow.** Measured off the trainer, the
   hall is roughly 6–7 m wall to wall and under 3 m to the ceiling — about two and a
   half times wider than tall. A dug tunnel should be at least as tall as it is wide,
   or round.
7. **The boar is pasted on.** It is markedly brighter and higher in contrast than the
   floor and wall immediately around it, as though it carries its own light or is not
   receiving the cave's. Its scale is fine (roughly chest-height to the trainer,
   ~1.5–1.7 m long) but its lighting divorces it from the room.
8. **The far doorway shows a warm tan room with a bright green tree in it.** At the end
   of a cold grey tube the eye is offered what looks like the outdoors. It reads as
   the cave exit, not as a deeper chamber.
9. **The floor tile repeats visibly** — the same dark green-grey moss blobs recur at
   regular spacing across an otherwise featureless plane. There is no rubble, no
   depression, no debris where wall meets floor; that junction is a clean straight
   line the full width of the frame.

### 03 — far end of the hall, looking toward the den

1. **A creature is jammed into the right quarter of the frame, cropped by two edges,
   and reads as nothing.** At full size it is a smear of black, teal and blown white;
   at thumbnail it is an unidentifiable dark blob. Its value sits inside the value of
   the wall behind it, so it has no silhouette separation at all. Whatever this stand
   was meant to show, a third of the frame is spent on an unreadable mass.
2. **That creature's material is a quality tier below the trainer's.** Close in, the
   fur is smeared posterised paint with hard black scribbles and blown near-white
   blobs; the shell plates are glossy plastic pebbles. No fur, no fine detail, no
   surface at all. Beside the trainer's clean sculpted clothing it reads as a
   different game.
3. **Wall blocks protrude at right angles with pure-black end faces.** Six or seven
   dark rectangles across the walls and ceiling are the unlit side faces of box beams,
   reading as flat black cut-outs stuck on the wall rather than as geometry in shadow.
4. **Same tiled granite everywhere, same lack of light, same monochrome** as 02 (mean
   saturation ~0.18, 95th-percentile luma ~81). The hall has no bright anchor.
5. **The view through the passage disagrees with the hall it is seen from.** Warm gold
   sandstone, dark timber-looking cross-beams, a bright green tree, sunlit sandy floor.
   Two different buildings, not two rooms of one cave.
6. **The far badger in the den is small, dim and lost** against the tan wall — the room
   the whole frame is aiming at gives the eye nothing to fix on.
7. **Nothing occupies the mid-ground.** Between the trainer and the passage there is
   bare floor and bare wall.

### 04 — inside the den, looking at the guardian

1. **The guardian is not staged as a guardian.** The trainer stands directly in front of
   it on the same axis and cuts its chest in half; the tree canopy covers its right
   flank; its head sits at the trainer's head height. Measured, its shell top is
   roughly 1.3–1.5× the trainer's height, which is correct — but *on screen* it reads
   as person-sized because of the crouched pose and the occlusion. Nothing frames it,
   nothing lights it, no arena, no floor marking, no effect, no scale cue. Compare
   `palworld-01`, where the boss is large, centred, unoccluded, lit and sparking.
2. **The room reads as an outdoor adobe courtyard, not a den.** Warm gold sandstone
   walls, a full-size tree with a brown trunk and dark green leaf cards growing inside
   the cave, a bright orange wooden crate, an evenly lit sandy floor. There is no
   bedding, no bones, no nest, no hoard, no spoil, no scratch marks — nothing that says
   an animal dug this and lives in it.
3. **A hard material seam at the left.** A grey granite box wall abuts the gold
   sandstone wall along a razor-straight vertical edge with no blend and no transition
   piece. The grey slab reads as a separate object pushed into the wall.
4. **The frame's palette does not belong to the other four.** Mean saturation ~0.49 and
   a warm gold cast, against the near-monochrome slate of 02/03/05.
5. **The alcove/doorway in the left wall is a painted rectangle.** A dark brown lintel
   over a flat mid-grey face with another darker rectangle inside it and no depth.
6. **The tree is a flat card cluster in near-black green** with a smooth plastic
   trunk — the darkest, most saturated thing in the shot, and it is occluding the boss.
7. **The crate is off-palette and cut by the frame edge** — pure saturated orange planks
   with black gaps, unlit, sitting at a hue nothing else in the cave shares.
8. **Wall and ceiling beams carry the same sandstone texture at different stretch
   ratios**, so the "timber" beams read as stretched stone.
9. **No contact shadow under the guardian's paws** — a broad soft ambient darkening
   only, nothing that pins 2+ tonnes of animal to the floor.

### 05 — back in the first chamber, looking out to daylight

1. **A flesh-coloured blob fills the bottom-right corner.** Cream and tan blotches,
   unreadable, clipped by the near plane — a piece of a creature standing inside the
   camera. Straightforward bug, and it is the first thing the eye finds in the
   thumbnail.
2. **The exit reads as a built structure, not a burrow.** A colonnade of rectangular
   posts and lintels, three frames deep, dolmen- or mine-shaft-like, plus a plank ramp
   leading out. Post-and-lintel construction says someone built this; nothing says an
   animal dug it.
3. **Every element is the same granite tile again**, on the posts, the lintels, the
   walls, the ceiling and the loose boulders, so the frame has one surface and no
   material variety at all. Mean saturation ~0.13 — the least colourful frame in the
   set.
4. **The daylight beyond does not match frame 01's daylight.** Seen through the mouth
   the meadow is dull, hazy and desaturated with a washed pale sky; frame 01's exterior
   is vivid green and yellow under a blue sky with clouds. The same time of day
   photographed from two sides gives two different worlds.
5. **The black slab on the right** is a near-pure-black untextured face with a
   stone-coloured strip on top — reads as missing material rather than shadow.
6. **The wall meets the floor along a perfectly clean straight line** the width of the
   frame, with two or three loose rocks and one ivy sprig as the only dressing across a
   very large area of empty floor.
7. **The ivy sprigs are single flat cards** with visible hard alpha edges, stuck to the
   wall base in one small patch.
8. **The trainer holds the identical arms-out rest pose here as in all four other
   frames** — arms straight from the sides, fingers splayed. Across five frames the
   character never once looks like he is doing anything.

## 2. The eight biggest defects of the room as a designed place, ranked

**R1 — There is no light structure, and therefore no value structure.** In 02, 03 and
05 the 25th-to-95th-percentile band of the whole image spans roughly luma 35–95 out of
255, with highlights (>200) at 0.1–0.3% of pixels. The references sit at p50 ≈ 105–133
and p95 ≈ 195–235. Nothing in the cave emits: no torch, brazier, ember, fungus glow,
crack of daylight, shaft or bounce. Walls are lit the same at the ceiling as at the
floor, so no surface has form, and the only bright thing anywhere is the doorway to
the next room. A cave being dark is correct; a cave being *uniformly dim with no bright
anchor* is not the same thing, and that is what these are.

**R2 — The cave is boxes.** Every wall, ceiling, pier, lintel, beam and opening is an
axis-aligned rectangular prism with razor 90° corners and flat faces (02, 03, 05
throughout; the alcove in 04). Openings are rectangles nested inside rectangles.
Nothing curves, narrows, slumps, tapers, or is irregular. This is a grey-box level with
a texture on it, and the texture does not disguise it.

**R3 — The walls do not read as dug earth; they read as one tiled granite bitmap.**
Identical mottled grey/white speckle on walls, ceiling, floor, protruding blocks and
door jambs, at the same texel scale near and far, with no corner darkening, no edge
wear, no dirt at the wall/floor junction, no strata, no root, no tool mark, no
variation between one chamber and the next. Frame 04 swaps it for a gold sandstone
tile with the same problems and an unblended seam between the two.

**R4 — There is no evidence a creature dug or lives here.** No spoil heaps, bedding,
nest, bones, hoard, scratch marks, droppings, tracks, side burrows, narrowing throats,
root ceilings or hanging growth in any of the five frames. The guardian's den contains
a tree and a shipping crate. The exit is a post-and-lintel colonnade. Taken together the
Warrens read as a stone mine or a temple corridor that the game has told me is a burrow.

**R5 — Four palettes across five frames; it does not read as one place.** 01 is acid
yellow-green under blue sky (saturation ~0.58); 02, 03 and 05 are near-monochrome slate
(0.13–0.18); 04 is warm gold sandstone (0.49). Worse, the seams are the compositions
themselves: 02 and 03 both look down a cold grey tube at a warm sunlit tan room with a
green tree in it, which reads as looking *out* of the cave rather than deeper into it.

**R6 — Nothing is anchored to the ground.** No contact shadow under the trainer's boots
(all five frames), the boar's trotters (02), the guardian's paws (04), the loose rocks,
the crates or the tree. Lighting is a flat ambient with no cast shadow, so every object
sits *in front of* the floor rather than *on* it.

**R7 — No layering, and the foreground is accidental.** Foreground, mid-ground and far
ground are the same bare surface; in 02/03/05 the only depth cue is corridor
perspective plus one bright rectangle at the end. Where something *is* in the near
field it is a mistake — the cropped badger owning the right quarter of 03, the creature
clipping the camera in 05. Nothing has been placed to occlude, frame or lead. Compare
`palworld-01` and `palworld-04`, which put a character shoulder, foliage or a rock into
the near field deliberately in every shot.

**R8 — Artefacts.** In rough order of loudness: the unsupported boulder mass in 01 with
the horizon visible under it; the creature clipped into the camera at the bottom-right
of 05; the pure-black unlit end faces on protruding wall blocks throughout 02/03/05
and the black slab in 05; the razor material seam between granite and sandstone in 04;
the visibly repeating floor tile in 02; the identical arms-out rest pose on the trainer
in all five frames.

*(Scale, checked and mostly clearing: measured against the 1.80 m trainer, the boar in
02 is roughly chest height and ~1.5–1.7 m long — plausible; the guardian in 04 measures
roughly 1.3–1.5× the trainer's height at the top of its shell, i.e. correctly taller
than him. The defect is not the numbers, it is that the staging in 04 destroys the read
— crouched pose, head at his head height, body split by his body and covered by a tree
— so the boss looks person-sized. The one genuine proportion complaint is the hall in
02: about 6–7 m wide and under 3 m high, two and a half times wider than tall, which is
a basement, not a tunnel.)*

## 3. The two bar questions

Before answering, the three things that most separate these frames from the references:

- **Value and colour range.** The Palworld shots run p5 ≈ 15–50, p50 ≈ 105–133,
  p95 ≈ 195–235 at 0.41–0.47 mean saturation, and the key art the same; the Warrens
  interiors (02, 03, 05) run p50 ≈ 47–60, p95 ≈ 73–97 at 0.13–0.18. Half the top of the
  value range and two thirds of the colour are missing, and — allowing fully that a cave
  should be darker — the references always give the eye one bright, coloured anchor and
  these give none.
- **Density.** `palworld-01` and `palworld-04` fill the mid-ground with grass, flowers,
  rocks, ruins, trees, creatures and effects; every square metre is dressed. Frames 02,
  03 and 05 spend 40% of the image on empty tiled floor holding three or four loose
  rocks, and frame 01's meadow has countable grass blades with bare ground between them.
- **The boss as an event.** `palworld-01` stages Mammorest large, centred, unoccluded,
  lit, sparking, with a name over it. Frame 04 puts the trainer's torso across the
  guardian's chest, a tree over its flank, no light on it, no arena, no effect.

> **A. Do these frames read as belonging to the world of
> `tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`?**

**No.** Frame 01 gets closest and still misses: the key art's meadow is fresh green with
warm earth paths, dense wildflower banks, big oaks and a landmark on the horizon;
frame 01 has a sulphur-yellow ground blotch, countable grass, untextured flat-brown
boulders and an empty horizon. The four interior frames share nothing with the board at
all — the board's palette strip is greens, golds, warm creams, slate blues and a
reserved oxblood, and the interiors are a single desaturated slate with a gold room
bolted on. The one thing that does carry is the trainer himself, who reads as the
board's stylised-realism character in every frame.

> **B. Shown beside `palworld-0*.jpg`, would someone say these are trying to be the
> same kind of game?**

**No.** Character-wise, maybe — the trainer would pass in a Palworld frame. Everything
else would not. The Palworld shots are dense, brightly keyed, fully dressed spaces where
creatures read instantly by silhouette and colour; these are empty, flat-lit grey boxes
where a creature can occupy a quarter of the frame and remain unidentifiable (03). The
two creatures shown here also sit a visible tier below the reference's creature art:
close in, both the boar and the badger-guardian are smeared, posterised painted texture
with hard black scribbles and blown highlights, no fur, no fine surface, glossy plastic
shell plates. They are not mismatched with *each other* — the two agree — but they are
mismatched with the trainer beside them and with the bar. The badger's black-and-white
face mask is the single strongest piece of design in the set and the only creature
element that survives the thumbnail; that is the thing worth building on.

### Fixable by changing the scene (no new art required)

- Put light *in* the cave and let it fall off: a key per chamber, embers/fungus/torch
  glow, a shaft at the mouth and exit, ambient graded darker at the ceiling. This alone
  addresses R1 and most of the "flat" complaints.
- Contact shadows / AO so boots, paws, crates and rocks sit on the floor (R6).
- Unify the palette: pull the interiors off pure slate toward earth, warm the hall or
  cool the den so 04 belongs to 02/03/05, and lose the yellow ground blotch in 01 (R5).
- Re-stage frame 04: move the guardian off the trainer's axis, get the tree off its
  flank, raise it or lower the camera, give it floor and light of its own (R-per-frame
  04.1). Move the near badger out of frame 03's foreground and the clipped creature out
  of frame 05's corner (R8). Move the camera or trainer in 01 so the mouth is visible.
- Break the corridor proportions: narrow the 6–7 m hall, vary ceiling height, offset the
  openings so the view is not three concentric rectangles (R2 partially, 02.6).
- Dress the floor and the wall/floor junction: clustered rubble, spoil, fallen blocks,
  root bundles, mushroom clumps, tracks — clustered, varied in scale, not scattered
  evenly (R4, R7, and the density gap against Palworld).
- Add deliberate foreground occluders at each stand (R7).
- Fix the floating boulder mass in 01 and the black unlit block faces (R8).
- Give the trainer an idle instead of the arms-out rest pose (05.8).

### Needs art that is not in the build

- **A dug-earth material set.** Soil, clay, packed-mud, root and strata textures with
  corner/crevice darkening, replacing the single granite tile. Without this, R3 cannot
  be fixed by lighting or dressing — the surface will remain wallpaper on boxes.
- **Irregular cave modules.** Curved and non-orthogonal wall/ceiling/throat pieces, a
  real cave mouth with a lip and an overhang, so R2 stops being true at the geometry
  level rather than being hidden.
- **Burrow dressing props.** Bedding nest, bone/shell midden, spoil heaps, hanging
  roots, cave flora, claw-marked wall decals — the vocabulary that makes R4 answerable.
  The current den is furnished with a shipping crate and an oak tree.
- **A creature-art pass.** The boar and the badger-guardian need real surface work
  (fur, sculpted detail, non-blown highlights) to hold up beside the trainer at the
  distances these stands use. This is the item that most clearly reads as "buy or make",
  not "tune the scene".
- **A rock material for the exterior boulders in 01**, which are currently untextured
  flat-shaded facets sitting beside a differently-authored grey rock.
