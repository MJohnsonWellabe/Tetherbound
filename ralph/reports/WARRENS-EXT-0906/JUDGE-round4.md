# Burrow Warrens — Visual Judge, Round 4

Blind read of `shots/warrens_63/` (eight frames plus `_sheet.png`) against
`docs/reference/tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png` and `docs/reference/palworld-0*.jpg`.
Software GL: composition, silhouette, colour relationships, scale and geometry only.
No source, no history, no earlier verdicts consulted. Nothing is scored.

Frame-of-reference facts I was given and used as rulers: trainer 1.80 m (does not
appear in any frame), Team Tether lamp post at the threshold 2.6 m, guardian 3.2 m at the
shoulder, doorway 3.4 m.

---

## The sheet at 30 %

Downscaled to 575 px wide, what survives:

- **Row 1 (00–02) reads as one outdoor place**: bright sky, pale ground, a dark mound.
  In 00 the mound-with-two-trees is a recognisable landmark shape. The mouth is not
  recognisable — it is a slightly darker fleck at the mound's foot, and at 30 % you only
  find it because you know to look.
- **03 reads as a brown arch with a black hole in it.** Good: the entrance is the
  frame's subject. Bad: the grey pole and diagonal bar at bottom-left survive the
  downscale as a stray line across the picture.
- **04 and 07 read as brown-grey noise.** Nothing in either frame is identifiable at
  30 % — no doorway, no focal object, no creature.
- **05 is a green blob (mushrooms) and a tiny dark blob (guardian).** The mushrooms win
  the frame; the boss loses it.
- **06 is the one interior frame where a creature silhouette holds**: dark bear shape on
  a warm wall. This works and should be the template.

The overall impression at thumbnail size is **outdoor frames from one game, indoor frames
from another** — exterior is brown clay and grey-green meadow; interior is grey granite
and sand.

---

## Exterior — judged first and hardest

### 00-approach-60m

**Does the bank read as a landmark?** Partly. The main mound is a smooth, symmetrical
cone crowned by two trees plus a third leaning tree to its right; the silhouette is
legible against the sky and is the tallest thing on the horizon. That is landmark
language and it is the strongest thing in the exterior set. Defects:

- **The mound is geometrically a cone.** No shoulders, no slump, no erosion notch, no
  irregularity. From 60 m it reads as a slag heap or a tent, not an earth bank that
  animals have dug into. The key art's landmarks (standing stone, Meadows Hall, the
  distant mountain) are all asymmetric with a "front".
- **There is a second, smaller cone immediately left of the main one** (approx x 540,
  y 350). It has the same profile at half the size and reads as a second hut. It
  competes with the main mound for the "which one is the place?" question.
- **The mouth is a black notch a few pixels tall at the base of the cone.** There is no
  warm light at it, no lighter earth apron in front of it, no path leading to it. From
  60 m the player sees a hill, not an entrance. The brief says "a warm-lit entrance the
  player walks toward across open ground" — the light is not in this frame.
- **No approach road.** The frame is titled "60 m along the approach road" but there is
  no road, track, desire line or worn strip anywhere between camera and mound. Compare
  `palworld-02-open-field-path.jpg`: a bare dirt path runs from the player's feet
  straight into the cave mouth in the cliff, and the eye follows it without being told.
- **Focus stealers**: the saturated red-brown trunk of the foreground tree (left third,
  the most saturated object in the frame), the orange/red tussock grass across the whole
  bottom-right quarter, and the magenta bush at x 300, y 420. All three are louder than
  the entrance. The orange grass in particular reads as autumn and disagrees with the
  green summer meadow everywhere else in the frame.
- **Ground colour**: the grass plane is pale grey-green, near white where it catches
  light. Against the key art's saturated yellow-greens it reads as frost or a
  washed-out photo. This is true of 00, 01 and 02 alike.
- **Displaced earth**: none visible from here. The rocks flanking the mound base are
  flat-topped brown blocks with green caps — they read as stacked boulders or crates,
  not spoil thrown out of a burrow.
- **Scale**: legible only by inference from the trees (mound is roughly one tree-height
  tall, ~10 m). There is no trainer and no person-scale object in frame, so the 1.80 m
  ruler is absent from the whole survey.

### 01-knoll-from-outside

- **The mound reads as felt, not earth.** A uniform fine-noise texture on a smooth
  dome with a single light gradient. There is no stratification, no grass line, no
  rubble skirt — the only relief is a rock outcrop set into the right-hand dome.
- **The rocks are a stack of boxes.** Centre-left cluster (x 300–700, y 250–400):
  three or four dark-brown slabs at right-angles to each other, flat green tops, hard
  facets, one slab (approx x 600, y 330) sitting on top of the others with a visible
  gap under it — it floats. This is the closest the exterior gets to "displaced earth"
  and it reads as placed props, not dug spoil.
- **A tree trunk emerges from the very apex of the dome** (x 665, y 170–260) with a
  bare grey trunk and no root flare. It looks planted on, not grown from.
- **Unidentified orange object** on the ground at x 410, y 410 — a bright orange blob
  with no readable form. It is the most saturated thing in the frame.
- **Grass tufts along the rock tops** are single identical clumps at similar spacing.
  Procedural, not authored.
- No entrance is visible from this angle. If this is the knoll's side, the frame is
  fine as a side; if it is meant to sell the landmark, nothing in it says "burrow".

### 02-knoll-from-outside

- **The bank here reads as a long, flat-topped ridge — a dyke or a wall.** It is a
  different silhouette from the cone in 00 and 01; the three exterior frames do not
  agree on what shape the landmark is.
- **Floating slab at the left frame edge** (x 0–80, y 150–200): a rock slab cantilevers
  out from the top of the ridge with sky under it. Artefact.
- **Dead tree plus live tree on top** is the best silhouette idea in the exterior set —
  two contrasting canopies against the sky. Keep that.
- **Rocks at the ridge foot** are the same brown block family as 01, again with hard
  right-angle facets and green caps.
- **Regularly spaced grass tufts along the ridge top** (x 60–300, y 220–260): five
  identical clumps at near-equal spacing. Reads as generated.
- **Ground**: same near-white grass plane as 00 and 01; the pale patch at x 1050, y 440
  is close to pure white.

### 03-mouth

The subject is unambiguous, which is right. The problems are material and detail:

- **The arch is a smooth, faceted, single-tone brown.** It reads as clay, chocolate or
  plasticine — no grain, no clods, no roots protruding from the cut face, no strata, no
  grass overhanging the lip. Nothing about it says "dug". A creature-dug mouth should
  show what a claw does to soil: torn fibrous edges, roots dangling, loose material at
  the sill.
- **The material changes to grey quarried stone one step inside the arch.** Through the
  opening the walls, pilasters and lintels are a granite/marble noise texture with
  rectangular sections. Earth outside, masonry inside, with no transition. This is the
  single loudest "not dug into earth" tell in the survey, and it is visible in one frame.
- **The mouth is not warm-lit.** The opening is black; light appears only far down the
  corridor. The 2.6 m lamp post at bottom-left is unlit (a white sphere at x 5, y 180
  and a grey pole). The "warm-lit entrance" the brief describes is absent at the
  threshold as well as from 60 m.
- **Lamp post artefact**: the pole is cropped by the frame edge and a second grey bar
  runs diagonally from x 60, y 540 to x 270, y 530 across the ground. Whether it is a
  fallen post or a leaning one, it reads as a bug. The bulb sphere is separated from the
  pole.
- **Ground seam**: a hard straight edge between the pale grass texture (right) and the
  dark brown ground (left), and a black, visibly banded strip (the ramp) between them
  (x 570–720, y 480–720). Straight-edged textures meeting at a line read as terrain
  chunks, not a worn threshold.
- **Oversized fern fronds** top right (x 1000–1280, y 0–260) and top left (x 180–280,
  y 160–220): individual fronds ~1.5 m long. Jungle, not meadow, and they crowd the arch.
- **Two dark vertical protrusions above the arch** (x 520 and x 760, y 0–50) poke up
  from the mound behind. Unreadable; they look like poles clipping through the bank.
- **Hanging root cluster** inside the opening (x 540–700, y 240–300) reads as a black
  scribble. It is the one hint of "roots" in the whole entrance and it is too dark to
  read as roots.
- **The small creature at the sill** (x 560–620, y 350–400) is a low-resolution brown
  quadruped with a green patch on its back; at this size it is blurry and pixel-edged
  and reads noticeably softer than the arch around it.

### Exterior summary against the five questions

| Question | Answer from the frames |
|---|---|
| Landmark from 60 m? | Yes as a shape (cone with trees), but it reads as a hill, not a burrow — and the three exterior frames disagree on the shape (cone in 00/01, flat ridge in 02). |
| Entrance reads as dug into earth? | No. Smooth faceted brown arch, immediate change to grey masonry inside, no roots, no torn edges. |

## Interior — briefly

### 04-hall-dressing
- **Left 45 % of the frame is one flat granite slab** at grazing angle. It has no
  edge detail and dominates a frame that is supposed to show dressing.
- **The hall is a rectilinear box**: flat walls, flat ceiling, flat floor, right-angle
  corners. Ceiling "beams" are wide slabs in the same granite texture as the walls,
  so they read as stone lintels, not as roots or timbers holding up a dug roof. Nothing
  here is burrow-shaped.
- **Dressing** is crate, barrel, three pebbles, one blue-grey boulder, one dead root
  sprig on the ceiling (x 1000–1100, y 100–190, reading as a black scribble), and a
  rectangular darker patch floating on the back wall (x 605–670, y 160–245) that looks
  like a stray decal. Generic dungeon starter kit; nothing says animals live here.
- **Floor** is a pale sand texture with green blotches. It reads as beach sand, not
  packed earth.

### 05-hall-from-the-doorway
- **Mushrooms are the frame.** Pale, self-lit, flat-shaded green, no cap gills or
  stem thickness variation, caps ~0.8–1 m across at the near cluster. They are the
  only saturated object and they upstage the guardian, who is a small dark shape at the
  far doorway.
- **Corridor architecture**: rectangular pilasters at regular intervals, flat ceiling
  with parallel slab beams, a rectangular doorway with a hard black frame. This is a
  built hall, not a tunnel.
- **Guardian vs doorway** (both at roughly the same depth here): the guardian's shoulder
  sits at about 70 % of the doorway's height, and it is sitting on its haunches. At
  3.2 m vs 3.4 m the shoulder line should nearly reach the lintel; the pose is what is
  costing it. A boss glimpsed from the hall should fill the door.
- **Bright vertical seam** on the right-hand pilaster (x 1060–1080, y 0–420).
- A second pale-and-dark creature is visible inside the far doorway at x 780, y 350 —
  readable only as a smudge.

### 06-den-and-guardian
- **This is the best interior frame.** Warm wall, dark creature silhouette, readable at
  30 %. It also shows the problems most clearly:
- **Scale.** The doorway on the back-left wall spans roughly y 235–410 (~175 px). The
  guardian, standing *closer* to the camera than that doorway, spans y 245–405 (~160 px)
  overall and its shoulder sits at about y 270 — roughly three-quarters of the doorway's
  height. A 3.2 m shoulder in a 3.4 m doorway should be near-equal at equal depth and
  *taller* when nearer. In this frame the guardian is reading well under its stated
  size. Grow the creature; do not shrink the door.
- **Posture.** Four-square idle, head turned toward the wall, presenting its flank to
  the entrance. Nothing about it says "guardian". `palworld-01` and `palworld-03` put
  the boss facing the player, mouth open, mid-action. This one is waiting for a
  screenshot.
- **Style mismatch.** The guardian's surface is a cel/ink texture: hard black painted
  strokes for fur, flat plates of moss-green on the back, a white face with black
  outline. Everything around it — walls, floor, boulder — is photographic noise texture.
  The creature looks pasted onto the room. The small burrowback in 03 has the same ink
  style at lower resolution. Either the world moves toward the creature's stylisation or
  the creature moves toward the world's; right now they are two games.
- **The den is empty.** One yellow sack, four pebbles, a boulder at right, a crate
  cropped at the right edge. No nest, no bedding, no bones, no gnawed roots, no scrape
  marks. A 3 m animal lives here and has left no trace.
- **Lighting artefacts**: a warm light pool on the floor (x 900–1280, y 380–470) with a
  hard edge and no visible source; a lighter horizontal band across the back wall at
  y ≈ 300 that reads as a texture seam rather than a light effect.

### 07-den-dressing
- **The horizontal stone band running through the pillars** (y 160–200, x 540–1280)
  reads as a misaligned timber frame rendered in rock. It contradicts "dug".
- **Left wall** (x 0–380) is a flat dark red-brown plane with almost no texture.
- **Dressing** is again crate, sack, rock cone, a broken plank, pebbles, mushrooms,
  one dead root sprig on the ceiling. Nothing distinguishes this den from the hall in
  04 except the sack colour.
- **Warm floor light** from bottom-left has no source in frame.

## Rubric items not covered above

- **Colour and value.** Exterior has a real value range (bright sky, pale ground, dark
  mound) but the ground is desaturated to near-white and the accents (red trunk, orange
  grass) are misplaced. Interior is a narrow brown-grey mid-range lifted only by the
  mushrooms and the sack. The eight frames do **not** read as one place: exterior brown
  clay, interior grey granite.
- **Oxblood.** No Team Tether red anywhere. The red foreground trunk in 00 is a
  saturated rust, not oxblood, but it is the reddest thing in the survey and it is on a
  friendly tree; worth checking it is not drifting toward the reserved hue.
- **Intentionality.** Exterior rocks are one prop family at one scale, right-angled to
  each other; grass tufts on rock tops at regular spacing; interior pilasters at regular
  intervals. Reads generated in both halves.
- **Horizon and depth.** 00 has a horizon and distant tree line; depth reads. 01 and 02
  have no fog gradient and the far tree line at x 1000–1280 (02) is a flat green band.
  Interior has no depth cue beyond perspective.
- **Interface.** None in frame. Not judged.

## The three things that most separate these frames from the references

1. **The entrance does not read as a burrow dug into earth, and it is not lit.**
   (03, and the notch in 00.) `palworld-02` shows a cave mouth as a dark opening in
   strata with grass overhanging the lip and a dirt path worn straight into it; the eye
   goes there unprompted. Here the arch is smooth single-tone clay that becomes grey
   masonry one step inside, there is no light at the mouth, no roots, no torn edge, no
   path. Fixable by the scene: material and edge detail on the arch, root/overhang
   props at the lip, a warm light at the threshold, an earth-textured first bay so the
   transition to stone happens out of sight, and fix the lamp post.

2. **The approach has no approach.** (00, 01, 02.) No road, no spoil fan, no lighter
   trodden ground, no props that point at the mouth; the ground plane is near-white
   against the key art's saturated meadow; and the frame's saturation budget is spent on
   a red trunk, orange grass and a magenta bush instead of on the entrance. The key art
   and `palworld-02` both use a path and a colour hierarchy to make the destination
   obvious. Fixable by the scene: a worn track from the road to the mouth, a spoil
   skirt of loose earth and torn turf in front of the arch, desaturate or move the
   orange grass and red-trunk tree, raise ground saturation toward the palette, and
   give the mound an asymmetric profile so 00, 01 and 02 agree on one landmark shape.

3. **The interior is a rectilinear stone dungeon with a creature from a different
   art style, rendered under size.** (04–07.) `palworld-01` and `-03` make a boss an
   event: it fills the frame, faces the player, and shares the world's material
   language. Here the guardian stands flank-on in an empty box room, its shoulder at
   about three-quarters of a doorway it should nearly fill, wearing an ink-outlined
   cel texture against photographic stone. **Split:** scale, posture, den dressing (a
   nest, bedding, scrapes) and moving the doorway/creature framing are scene fixes;
   the box-room geometry becomes a burrow only if there are curved tunnel and
   root-crossed ceiling pieces to use, and the creature/world style mismatch needs
   art — either a re-texture of the creature toward the world or a stylisation pass on
   the environment materials. Neither of those two is a scatter or lighting change.

## The two bar questions

**A. Do these frames read as belonging to the world in
`tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`?** — **No**, narrowly, and 00 is the frame nearest to
passing. What carries 00: sky, tree silhouettes, a crowned mound on the horizon, the
"landmark visible from distance" note taken literally. What sinks it: the ground is
grey-white rather than the board's saturated greens and yellows; the accent colours are
autumn oranges and a red trunk the board does not have; and nothing in the entrance or
interior delivers "cozy and inviting with hints of mystery" — the mouth is a black hole
and the inside is a bare stone box. All of the exterior gaps are scene-fixable
(palette, path, spoil, focus, light). The interior's failure against the board is
mostly geometry and material family, which is only fixable if burrow-shaped pieces exist.

**B. Shown beside `palworld-0*.jpg`, would someone say these are trying to be the same
kind of game?** — **No.** The exterior is trying: a landmark hill, a cave-mouth
destination, foreground grass, big sky — the same shot grammar as `palworld-02` and
`-04`. What sinks it is density and finish: the ground is one texture with sparse
identical tufts where Palworld's is layered cover; the rocks are right-angled blocks;
the entrance is a hole with nothing at it. The interior would not be recognised as the
same kind of game at all — a corridor with pilasters and a flat-shaded mushroom cluster
is not the language of those five shots — and the guardian does not read as a creature
those games would ship: wrong style against its room, wrong size against its door,
wrong pose for a boss. Scene-fixable: ground density and saturation, rock placement,
path, threshold light, guardian scale and pose, den dressing. Needs art not in the
build: burrow-shaped tunnel geometry, a creature surface that matches the environment
(or the reverse), and a higher-resolution small burrowback than the one at the sill in
03.
