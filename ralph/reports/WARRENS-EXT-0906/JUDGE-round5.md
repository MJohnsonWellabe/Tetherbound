# Burrow Warrens — Visual Judge, Round 5

Blind pass over `shots/warrens_63/` (sheet + eight frames), judged against
`docs/reference/tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png` and `docs/reference/palworld-0*.jpg`.
Software GL frames: composition, silhouette, colour relationships, scale and geometry
only. No score. Rulers available in frame: the 2.6 m Team Tether lamp post (03) and the
3.4 m den doorway (05, 06). **The 1.80 m trainer is not in any of the eight frames**, so
every scale claim below is made against the post or the doorway.

---

## Exterior (00–03) — judged first and hardest

### Does the bank read as a landmark from 60 m? (00)

Partly. At full size the dome is the tallest dark mass in the frame, sits on the
horizon between two tree groups, and the eye finds it. At 30% on the sheet it survives
as "dark hump under trees", which is enough to steer toward. Two things weaken it:

- **00 — the foreground steals the first look.** At 30% the eye lands on the
  orange/yellow grass tufts in the bottom-right quarter and the two brick-red trunks
  on the left before it reaches the mound. Both are more saturated and higher contrast
  than the landmark. The landmark is the third thing you see in a frame that exists to
  show it.
- **00 — the "warm light" is a spot on the hill, not light from the hole.** Above the
  arch there is a large yellow-green oval on the dome face, and the arch beneath it is
  dark. From 60 m this reads as a searchlight aimed at the mound, or a light leak, not
  as glow spilling out of an entrance. The thing that should be the beacon (the mouth)
  is the darkest part of the landmark.

### Does the entrance read as something dug into earth? (03, 00)

No. This is the largest exterior defect and it is the same defect from every angle.

- **03 — the mouth is five unrelated material families stacked on one another:** a
  near-black, high-frequency noise dome; smooth flat-shaded brown "sausage" tubes
  forming the arch; faceted low-poly brown boulders with flat, hard-edged lime-green
  caps painted on their top faces; saturated tropical ferns and aloe/agave clumps
  sitting on top of all of it; and, visible through the arch, a **grey stone doorway
  with dead-straight jambs and a flat lintel**. Palworld-02's cave mouth is one
  material — the cliff — with grass draping over its lip and a worn path running into
  it. Here nothing is continuous with anything else, so nothing reads as the same
  earth that was dug.
- **03 — the inner doorway is built, not dug.** Through the organic arch the player
  sees a rectangular cut with vertical grey jambs, a flat lintel and a lit rectangular
  corridor beyond. This is the first interior thing the player sees and it says
  "cellar" before they have stepped in.
- **03 — two tall dark spikes rise above the arch** (roughly x 550 and x 725, from
  the arch top to above the dome's shoulder). They read as giant grass blades or
  thorns 6–8 m tall, taller than the entrance, and carry the same yellow-green light
  as the oval on the dome. They are the most vertical thing in the frame and are
  unexplained by anything else in the scene.
- **03, 00 — pancake slabs.** Flat brown discs sit on stems/boulders at the left of
  the arch (03, x≈230 y≈300) and top-left (03, x≈300 y≈290; 00 crop shows the same
  shelf on the left shoulder). They read as mushroom caps or tables, not as any kind of
  earth or stone.
- **00, 03 — trees pinned into the dome.** Two trunks emerge from the dome's upper
  slope with no root flare, no soil disturbance and no ground plane under them; the
  trunk simply intersects the dome surface. They read as stuck in, not grown.

### Does the earth read as displaced? (01, 02, 03)

No. A warren dug by large burrowers should show spoil: fans of loose earth below the
holes, a broken lip, clods, more than one hole.

- **01, 02 — the mound is a single smooth dome/ridge with one flat tone.** No strata,
  no exposed roots on its face, no spoil fan, no slumping, no secondary burrow holes
  anywhere on its ~10–12 m height (02 puts the crest about five lamp-post heights above
  the grass). It reads as a slag heap or a burial tumulus, not a warren. The word
  "warrens" implies many holes; there is exactly one authored arch.
- **02 — the mound meets the grass along a crisp line** with no blend, no darkening,
  no scattered earth at the foot. The bank sits on the meadow like a placed object.
- **03 — the foreground "apron" is a flat brown plane with a razor-straight diagonal
  edge** against the pale grass (from about x 560 y 470 to x 1280 y 640). It is level,
  with no mounding, no lip and no falloff, and reads as a dirt car park or a decal, not
  as earth thrown out of a hole. A single band of rake-mark texture at the bottom right
  is the only gesture at spoil.
- **01 — boulders sit on the grass with no ground contact**: no grass occlusion, no
  contact shadow, no embedding. The cluster at x 250–650 y 280–400 floats.

### Does anything steal focus from the entrance? (00–03)

- **00** — orange grass tufts and red trunks (above).
- **03** — the neon ferns/aloe on top of the arch and the two spikes are brighter and
  more saturated than anything at ground level; the actual hole is the dimmest region
  of the arch. The lamp post, the one object that could mark the threshold, is an
  **unlit black pole with a white sphere** — no glow, no colour, no Team Tether
  identity. It reads as a street lamp that has been switched off.
- **01** — a small saturated orange object at the mound's foot (x≈415 y≈410) is the
  only warm accent in the frame and pulls the eye away from the bank.

### Is scale legible? (02, 03)

Mostly yes, which is the exterior's one strength.

- **03** — the lamp post (2.6 m) stands about 170 px tall; the arch opening behind it
  is about 130 px tall at greater depth, which puts the mouth in the 3–4 m range and
  agrees with a 3.4 m doorway. Arch tubes are ~1 m thick. Boulders read 2–4 m. This is
  plausible for a burrow dug by something 3 m at the shoulder.
- **02** — the post visible at the right (x≈850 y≈340) beside 3–4 m boulders under a
  ~11 m ridge: consistent.
- Defects: the **ferns on the arch top are ~1.5–2 m across** and the **aloe clumps
  ~1.4 m tall** — house-plant assets scaled to boulder size; the two spikes exceed the
  arch height; and (01, 03) **every boulder wears the same flat green cap at the same
  thickness regardless of boulder size**, which is the clearest procedural tell in the
  exterior.

### Ground, sky and palette (00–02)

- **00, 01, 02 — the meadow ground is the lightest value in the frame.** It is a pale,
  desaturated grey-green with a bluish sheen and sparse single blades; in 02 it is
  lighter than the sky. It reads as frost, ash or overexposed lawn. The key art's
  meadows are a warm mid-green with wildflowers and ochre paths; Palworld-02/03 ground
  is a saturated yellow-green dense enough that no base texture shows. This inverts the
  reference value structure (sky lightest, ground mid, shadow darkest) and is the
  single biggest reason the exterior does not look like the board.
- **00–03 — the mound is near black.** Nothing in the key art palette is that dark;
  earth on the board is ochre-brown. Dark hill on pale ground is why the landmark reads
  as a silhouette cut-out rather than a lit form.
- **00 — one shrub asset repeated at regular spacing** (three-lobed bush with white
  dots) across the whole field at uniform scale. Procedural read.
- **00 — no approach road.** The frame is described as taken from the approach road,
  but no path, rut or wear line leads to the mouth; the key art and Palworld-02 both
  put a worn path into the landmark.
- No oxblood anywhere in these frames — the reserved colour has not leaked, but it is
  also entirely absent from a Team Tether-lit threshold.

## Interior (04–07) — briefly

### Guardian scale and posture (06, 05)

- **06 — scale passes.** The guardian's back rises to the same screen height as the
  lintel of the side doorway at the left (both ≈ y 235, feet and threshold ≈ y 400),
  with the creature slightly nearer the camera. That makes its shoulder a touch under
  the 3.4 m doorway, i.e. the stated 3.2 m. It is unambiguously bigger than a person
  and would have to duck for its own door. Good.
- **06 — posture fails.** The creature is shown three-quarter from behind with its
  head hidden; from the den entrance the player sees a rump and a rock-pile back.
  Nothing about it says "guardian": no facing, no stance, no eye contact, no
  displacement of the floor around it. Palworld-01's boss fills the frame with an open
  mouth and a lunge; this reads as a boulder that happens to have legs.
- **05 — the badger steals the scene from the boss.** At the right of the far
  doorway a black-and-white badger sits upright, front paws forward, looking at the
  camera with a flat, painted cartoon face. It is at roughly the same screen height as
  the doorway, so it reads as boss-sized too. It is drawn in a different style from the
  guardian (flat illustrated fur with dark outline shading vs. the guardian's
  sculpted, cel-shaded stone), and it is brighter and better lit than the guardian,
  which is camouflaged against the far room. Two creatures in one frame, two art
  styles, and the sidekick is the one you notice.

### Room shape (04–07)

- **04, 05, 06, 07 — every room is a rectangular box with a flat ceiling, rectangular
  ceiling beams and rectangular slab pillars; 07's back wall carries a grid of vertical
  and horizontal stone bars like half-timbering.** Corners are 90°. This is a mine or a
  cellar; nothing about it says a creature dug it. The only burrow cues are the dead
  root clusters on the ceiling (04) and walls (06, 07), which are sparse and small.
- **04, 06, 07 — two wall materials at wildly different texture frequencies.** The
  pillars and ceiling slabs are a very busy granite noise; the plain walls are a
  near-flat dark brown with a few dots. Side by side the brown walls read as
  "material not assigned".
- **04 — half the frame is a wall.** The survey camera has a granite corner ~45% of
  the image in its face, hiding the hall. Whatever the hall dressing is, this frame
  does not show it.

### Interior dressing (04–07)

- **04, 06, 07 — generic loot-room props**: crate, barrel, gold sack, small rocks,
  pale cone rock. Human-scale civilisation items with no story of who carried them
  in, and no faction mark on any of them.
- **04 — at least four unrelated rock materials in one space**: busy granite
  pillars, a smooth navy boulder (x≈1050 y≈340), a pale pink-grey cone (07), and
  small brown pebbles. They do not read as the same geology.
- **05 — the mushrooms are pale mint, self-coloured, unlit, with visibly faceted
  low-poly caps and no gills or underside.** They read as paper cut-outs at ~2.5 m
  tall. At 30% they are the only thing legible in the frame.
- **06 — the same neon aloe/fern assets from the exterior are beside the guardian**,
  saturated well past anything else in the room; at 30% the plant reads before the
  boss does.
- **03 → interior — a bright white/blue rectangle is visible at the far end of the
  corridor through the mouth.** It looks like daylight through the back of the dungeon.
  If that is a skylight it needs to read as one; as shown it reads as a leak.

### What works inside

Value range is real: warm-lit ceiling, dark walls, pale floor, and the guardian is
lit and cast-shadowed onto the sand in 06. The guardian's own material — pale stone
plates, dark cracks, moss — is the best-looking surface in the set and matches the
"large burrower" brief. 06 is the one frame in eight that begins to look like an
event.

## The three things that most separate these frames from the references

1. **The entrance is not dug earth (03, 00).** Palworld-02 shows a cave mouth that is
   the same cliff material as the hill it is in, grass draping its lip, a worn path
   running into it, and darkness inside. Frame 03 stacks a black noise dome, brown
   clay tubes, faceted boulders with painted green tops, tropical house plants and a
   square grey stone doorway; nothing continues into anything else, there is no spoil,
   no path, no second hole, and the light that should come out of the mouth is
   instead painted on the hill above it.
2. **The meadow ground is the wrong value and the wrong density (00, 01, 02).** The
   key art and Palworld-02/03 both put the sky as the brightest plane and a saturated,
   dense green ground below it. Here the ground is the lightest thing in frame, pale
   grey-green with a bluish sheen and sparse single blades, with one shrub repeated
   at even spacing. It reads as frost on a lawn, and it makes the near-black mound
   look like a cut-out.
3. **The interior is a stone cellar with a boss that shows its back (04–07, 06,
   05).** Palworld-01's boss faces the player, fills the frame, and sits in a space
   that belongs to the world. Here the rooms are right-angled boxes with beams and a
   half-timber grid, the guardian is seen rump-first with its head hidden, and a
   cartoon badger in a different art style is brighter and more central than the boss.

## Bar questions

**A. Do these frames read as belonging to the world in `tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`?
— No.** The sky, the cloud shapes and the round stylised tree canopies carry the
"yes" half; they are close to the board. The ground sinks it: the board's meadows are
warm mid-green with wildflowers and ochre paths, and these are pale grey-green fields
under a near-black mound with lime tropical foliage. None of those three colours exists
on the board's palette strip.

**B. Beside `palworld-0*.jpg`, would someone say these are trying to be the same kind of
game? — No.** Ground density and saturation are far below Palworld-02/03; the landmark
is a smooth dark dome rather than a lit, material-continuous cave in a cliff; and the
boss encounter (06) is a passive back view in a box room where Palworld-01 is a
face-on lunge. Scale agreement is the one place these frames would survive the
comparison.

### What is fixable in the scene

- Ground: warm the grass palette, raise saturation, lower value below the sky,
  densify blades so the base texture stops showing, break the shrub repeat with
  clustering and scale variety, add a worn path from the 60 m point to the mouth.
- Mound: retint from near-black to the board's earth ochre-brown; add a horizon band or
  strata; add two or three secondary burrow holes with spoil fans and a broken lip;
  blend the foot into the grass; shape the apron into a mounded spoil fan instead of a
  flat plane with a straight edge.
- Mouth: remove or replace the tropical ferns/aloe and the pancake slabs; delete or
  shrink the two spikes; make the lamp glow warm and put the warm light *inside* the
  hole rather than on the dome face; give the lamp its faction identity.
- Boulders: vary or remove the uniform flat green caps; embed them and give them
  contact shadows.
- Guardian: rotate it to face the den entrance; set a stance; move the badger out of
  the boss sightline or make it clearly smaller than the door; kill the neon plant
  beside it.
- Interior: assign a real material to the plain brown walls; reduce the rock material
  families to one; hide or explain the white patch at the corridor end.

### What needs art that is not in the build

- An organic dug-tunnel kit (curved walls, rounded ceilings, root-bound openings) to
  replace the rectangular rooms, beams and half-timber grid; scene tweaks will not turn
  a box into a burrow.
- A single-material burrow-mouth set (earth lip, spoil, root overhang) that is
  continuous with the mound, replacing the clay tubes and the grey stone jambs.
- A retexture of the badger to the guardian's sculpted, cel-shaded style, or vice
  versa; the two cannot share a frame as drawn.
- A guardian head and face that read at doorway distance; from the angles shown it has
  no expressive front, and a boss needs one.
