## D2. Ground variation looks natural and authored; a genuine second grass species

**Test:** `tests/test_veg_corridor.gd` (assertions on `drygrass`'s band
gradient) — **9 tests, 0 failed, run this session** — plus visual inspection
of AFTER frames.

**Verdict: PASS.** A genuine second grass species exists, is visible, and
reads as a distinct plant rather than a retint.

**Evidence:** T1-GROUND-3 shipped a second grass species (`drygrass` —
Grass_Wispy_Short/Tall + Grass_Wheat, straw retint, previously suppressed and
invisible) with a per-band density gradient (0.45 lower meadows → 1.6
stronghold approach) and re-baked scatter. I opened
`ground-04-band4-ironwood-day-AFTER.png` myself: the right third of the frame
shows clearly distinct amber/rust upright seed-head stalks standing taller
than and structurally different from the green blade-card carpet around
them — an unmistakably different silhouette and colour, not a hue-swap of the
same mesh. This is the exact defect class B1 flags for the creature Aspect
variants ("one shared decal mask hue-swapped") and `drygrass` does not
repeat it. `ground-05-band5-approach-day-AFTER.png` (the driest, highest
band_scale stand) reads scrubbier and sparser than the lower bands, matching
the intended "gets drier as the chapter gets harder" gradient.

Test suite confirms the mechanism is wired correctly:
`test_drygrass_carries_a_band_gradient_over_the_real_band_table` and
`test_layer_band_scale_is_keyed_by_band_id_and_may_exceed_one` both pass
against the real `vegetation.json`/band tables, not a fixture — so a
dropped/typo'd band id (which silently flattens the gradient to 1.0) would
fail this suite.

**One caveat, not fully closed:** T1-GROUND-3 itself flagged that no blind
visual-judge round has specifically evaluated whether the species reads as
"a second grass" versus "clutter" — my own crop-level read (above) is not a
substitute for that. T1-WORLD's later whole-board pass judged general scatter
placement (D3, below) but did not single out `drygrass` for a variety
verdict.

**Cost to close further:** one blind-judge round targeted at
`drygrass` specifically, ~10 minutes, no new render needed (frames already
exist and are sanity-checked).

## D3. Open fields attractive without clutter — interest from shape/silhouette, not asset spam

**Verdict: FAIL (confirmed open defect).**

**Test:** T1-WORLD's blind visual-judge pass, §3.

**Evidence:** `ralph/reports/T1-WORLD/VERDICT-2026-08-30.md` §3, on frames
already sanity-checked above (`capture_check.gd` ran on every shutter, 74/75
passed). Direct quote from the blind critic: *"the broadleaf rosette repeats
across the entire right-hand meadow at a near-constant ~1m interval. There is
no clustering, no bare patch, no density gradient, no drift."* Path pebbles
lie along the centreline at even intervals, reading as a dotted line.

**Root cause, precisely diagnosed and NOT fixed** (this is the single most
load-bearing finding in this section, so it is quoted rather than
summarised): the placement rule itself is the problem, not its parameters —
"a keep-roll that is an independent Bernoulli trial per site, over a linearly
remapped noise mask, is a near-Poisson point process. Poisson has a
characteristic spacing and no clustering, by construction." Five rounds of
config tuning on the wrong system (`grass_field.json`'s cover tiers, which do
not reach the `bushes`/`groundmat` layers the critic was describing) measured
no change (CV 3.88→3.65, 1.03→1.06, 1.96→1.77 — within noise) and were
reverted. A shader lever (`drift_gate` in `cover_tier.gdshader`) exists for
the *right* system but ships at a no-op default; the baked-scatter
equivalent (in `scatter_rules.gd`) was never built.

Separately, the critic counts total meadow-scatter variety at **~4 meshes**
(broadleaf rosette, orange sedge, purple flower blob, grey pebble) across all
seven stands — not fixable by placement tuning, needs more meshes (governed
by `CLAUDE.md`'s no-Meshy-without-reference-art rule for environment props).

**Cost to close:** two separable pieces of work. (a) The placement-rule fix
for the baked `bushes`/`groundmat` layers in `scatter_rules.gd` — the
mechanism is understood, the fix is un-designed; cost is a design-and-code
pass plus a re-bake (owner-flagged as coordinator-owned per
`GATE_D_LANE_CONTRACT` §4), free in draw calls per
`docs/PERFORMANCE_BUDGET.md` §3.3. (b) The mesh-variety gap needs 6–8 more
meadow meshes with distinct scale/desaturated colour plus a second textured
rock family — an owner-supplied-reference-art decision, not code.

## D6. No visible seams (dashed terrain seam lines)

**Verdict: FAIL (open), with the clipmap hypothesis now RULED OUT — new finding this session.**

**Test:** `tools/_audit_d_seam_probe.gd` — the predecessor lane's own instrument,
run this session. Three frames from one fixed camera at the band-1 opening
stand (the artefact's original report location), differing only in
Terrain3D's `mesh_size`/`mesh_lods`: shipped (48/7), `mesh_size` halved to
24, and `mesh_lods` reduced to 4. This is exactly the test
`ralph/reports/handover-T1-GROUND-3-2026-08-30.md` §2 named as the deciding
next step and that no lane had yet run.

**Evidence:** `ralph/reports/audit/D6-seam-probe/seam-probe-{default,meshsize,lods}.png`
(the raw frames) and `...-crop-dashcorner.png` (the decisive close-up crop
per config, committed with this report). `capture_check.gd` passed on all
three shutters (grass field bound and drawing). All three frames are
compositionally identical (same boulder, same treeline, same grass —
confirming the camera never moved), so any pixel difference is attributable
to the clipmap change and/or run-to-run render noise, not to a different
shot.

**The decisive measurement.** A bulk pixel diff over the full frame shows
~30% of pixels differing between configurations — but per
`ralph/reports/handover-T1-GROUND-3-2026-08-30.md`'s own prior finding,
`_capture_ground_and_sky.gd` frames at identical settings already differ by
~39% run-to-run purely from cloud/sky/far-tier non-determinism, so a bulk
diff cannot isolate the seam. Instead I extracted per-row luminance profiles
across the sand path where the dashed line is visible (rows 440/470/500/530,
x=100–340 in the crop coordinate frame) and located every "dark dip" pixel
(a pixel more than 6 luminance units below the average of its ±3px
neighbours — this catches both the terrain-dither noise and the seam dashes
together). **The set of dip x-positions is identical, pixel-for-pixel, across
all three clipmap configurations at every row tested** (see the four
per-row comparisons committed in this session's tool output; e.g. row 500:
`default` and `meshsize` and `lods` all report dips at x=145,147,153–156,
161–163,173,174,186,187,214,215,223,224,232,233,275–277,296–298,313–315 —
no shift). A visual crop of the same corner
(`ralph/reports/audit/D6-seam-probe/seam-probe-{default,meshsize,lods}-crop-dashcorner.png`)
confirms the same dashed diagonal line, same position, same spacing, in all
three.

**Conclusion:** if the artefact were geometry belonging to Terrain3D's
clipmap, changing `mesh_size` (vertex density) or `mesh_lods` (LOD ring
count) would restructure the mesh under the same terrain and shift or
resample the line, even slightly. It did not move by even one pixel. **The
clipmap hypothesis, which T1-GROUND-3 left as "most likely" and the last
standing candidate, is now ruled out**, joining the sin-hash and the control
map (both ruled out previously) and the far-cover sheet (confirmed as a
*different*, already-fixed contributor — see below). What remains
unexplained is genuinely open: T1-GROUND-3's own fallback candidate — "the
terrain material's own mip/filter behaviour at grazing angles" — is now the
only named hypothesis left standing, and it was not tested this session
(would need a mip-bias/anisotropy change on the terrain material, a
different kind of probe than this one).

**The far-cover sheet contribution (a separate, already-fixed mechanism)
should not be confused with this residual.** T1-GROUND-3 separately found and
fixed real breaches in `grass_field`'s far-cover sheet (a 6m-cell mesh that
floats over the terrain and can gap over convex ground) via a `dilate_lift`
uniform — that fix is landed and un-related to the *dashed*, sub-pixel-wide
hairlines this probe targets, which are far too thin (~1px) and too close
(well inside the far-cover's 40m fade-in) to be that mechanism. Both are real
and both are already characterised; only the hairline is unattributed.

**Cost to close:** the mip/filter hypothesis needs one more probe (not
built): render the same fixed stand with the terrain material's texture
filter/anisotropy or mip bias changed, the same before/after-position
comparison technique used here. If the line moves, it's texture-space
(fixable via import/material settings); if it doesn't move either, every
named hypothesis is exhausted and the next escalation is an
upstream Terrain3D issue report. Either probe is minutes of headless probe
work, no render-heavy capture needed once the harness exists — this
session's probe cost ~13 minutes of wall clock for 3 frames on this box.

## D7. Aerial perspective gives distance real depth (terrain-material gradient, not fog)

**Verdict: PASS (closed and measured), with one flagged known limit.**

**Test:** direct config read (`terrain_playground.json`) plus T1-WORLD's
measured before/after on the AFTER frames.

**Evidence:** `aerial_fade_range_m` 160 → 900 (`aerial_fade_start_m` 45
unchanged) — the gradient now stays unsaturated out to ~945m instead of
fully saturating at 205m. Measured on the mid-ground strip of the elevated
band-2/4 frames: RGB (132,143,97) → (123,133,73), a 24-point blue-channel
drop — the mid-ground stops borrowing the sky's hue/value. Near-ground
pixels are unchanged ((124,135,57) vs (125,136,58)), confirming the effect
is isolated to the intended distance band. This is a terrain-shader
uniform, not fog — deliberately, since fog was tried and rejected twice at
R9.4 ("fog is eating the world").

**Known limit, not part of this item's own pass/fail:** the far horizon
strip (top ~25px of every elevated frame) is a blown white band, peaking at
(208,213,203), byte-identical before and after the aerial-range fix — it is
fog-meets-sky, not terrain, so this fix structurally cannot reach it.
Flagged for an owner call per `art.json`'s own record of the fog axis being
rejected twice already; changing it unilaterally would reopen a settled
decision.

**Cost to close the horizon-band limit:** an owner decision on the fog axis
(`fog_aerial_perspective`/density), not a config tune — the prior two
attempts (0.0022 → 0.0011 → 0.00055) were both rejected on the same grounds
("fog is eating the world").
