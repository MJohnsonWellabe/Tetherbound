## Method (fixed up front, before any verdict)

- Engine: CI's pinned Godot 4.7-stable (`.github/workflows/ci.yml` `GODOT_VERSION:
  4.7-stable`), installed into `~/godot-bin/godot`. `godot --headless --path .
  --import` run twice to build a clean import cache before any test or capture.
- No game code was changed. This lane wrote only `ralph/reports/audit/` (this
  file and its frames). No file under `scripts/`, `scenes/`, `data/`, `assets/`,
  or `shaders/` was touched. Defects found are written down, not fixed.
- Evidence rule: no frame counts as evidence until sanity-checked for real
  grass geometry, real lighting, real world content. The project's own
  `tools/capture_check.gd` runs at every shutter in the capture tools used here
  (`_judge_capture_hall.gd`, `_capture_locations.gd`, `capture_warrens_63.gd`)
  and prints `[capture_check] ok` or a named failure per stand — that per-shot
  console line is this lane's sanity check, cited per frame set below, rather
  than a separate manual look. `capture_check` verifies: the capture camera is
  actually the one drawing, the GrassField is bound to it and holds instances,
  Terrain3D is streaming around it (not the last gameplay position), the camera
  is not below the terrain height at its own XZ or embedded in a solid body,
  and the weather is the pinned/frozen one the tool asked for (not a drifting
  preset). It does not judge how anything looks — only that the frame is
  actually showing the shipping game.
- Visual verdicts come from the `visual-judge` skill, invoked fresh against the
  frames captured in this session — the judge gets the contact sheet and
  `docs/reference/` only (`tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`,
  `palworld-0*.jpg`), no design context, no handovers, no prior judge report,
  no knowledge of what changed underneath it — and answers the two bar
  questions, never a score. This lane captured the frames but did not write
  the judge's verdict; the verdict below is the skill's own output, reproduced
  or summarized with the skill's wording preserved for anything load-bearing.

### The two bar questions

**A. Do these frames read as belonging to the keyart's world? NO.**
The pastoral pieces (grass, trees, fences, wildflowers) match the keyart's
Meadows palette. The stronghold itself — the subject of all 11 frames — misses
its own keyart stronghold panel on three axes at once: no mountain backdrop
depth, no legible industrial/tech intrusion on the ruin, a softened rather
than dangerous antagonist red.

**B. Beside the Palworld screenshots, the same kind of game? NO**, on balance.
Ground/foliage density is competitive. But no frame contains a creature, a
character in motion, or lived-in base clutter to compare against, and the
architecture on screen (flat pale castle wall, uniform ivy "wallpaper",
unweathered furniture) reads a tier below Palworld's weathered cliffs and
lived-in base.

### The headline disagreement this lane had to resolve

`T1-HALL-4`'s handover claims the silhouette-contrast defect JUDGE-6 measured
(fortress at L=137.6 against a hill at L=162.5, a −4.9 point *inversion*) is
fixed, citing a re-measurement of **+33.1** separation against the keyart's own
+32.0. That claim was never re-verified by an independent pass — T1-HALL-ART,
the lane that shipped after it, explicitly declined to re-check it and called
a fresh judge pass "the top priority for the next lane." That is this lane's
job, and the fresh pass says the headline claim does not hold up in practice:

- The blind judge's **#1-ranked defect**, unprompted: *"the hall does not read
  as a landmark from distance — it disappears... In H-01, the supposed
  landmark is a faint grey smudge... indistinguishable from the flat grey
  horizon band behind it, and easy to miss entirely on a first look."*
- This lane opened `H-01-approach-400.png` directly (the 400 m approach stand
  — the exact "does it announce itself from a distance" question E2 asks) and
  confirms this by eye: the Hall is a barely-visible pale nub between two
  trees at roughly x 605–640, y 370–390, in a frame otherwise dominated by a
  dense, well-composed foreground grove. It is essentially the same read
  JUDGE-6 described in its own H-01 crop before T1-HALL-4's fix ("a 25×35 px
  pale nub").
- **Both measurements can be true at once, which is itself the finding.**
  T1-HALL-4 measured its separation at the `H-02b-sigil-gate-raised` stand (a
  26 m-elevated eye, above canopy) — and at that specific vantage, opened
  directly by this lane, the Hall genuinely does read as a darker mass than
  the ground beside it; the fix is real at that camera height. But E2's actual
  question — "does it announce itself from the distance a player first sees
  it" — is answered at ground-eye stands (H-01, H-02), where the same building
  is swallowed by a flat, blurred, hard-edged dark band across the horizon
  that the judge and this lane both read as fog/backdrop rather than terrain
  (see below), and the fortress has nowhere near enough value separation from
  *that* to register. **A silhouette fix measured from an elevated survey
  camera is not evidence the landmark reads from the eye height a player
  actually approaches at**, and T1-HALL-4's own report does not appear to have
  drawn that distinction.

### New defect this lane's own read agrees with the blind judge on

**The horizon has no mountain form — it is a flat, heavily blurred dark band**
across every outdoor stand (confirmed by this lane directly in H-01 and
H-02b: a hard-edged, uniformly dark, soft-blurred strip with zero ridgeline or
value gradation, sitting directly behind the Hall in every approach frame).
This is not a JUDGE-6-era defect — JUDGE-6 called it "bald ground," not "no
mountains" — so either it is a genuinely new observation this pass is the
first to make with the right vantage, or T1-HALL-4/T1-HALL-ART's fog/haze
changes (T1-HALL-4 explicitly "lifted and warmed fog/sky/terrain-aerial-fade
behind" the Hall to fix the silhouette number) produced this flat band as a
side effect. Either way it is open now and it is a large part of why the
ground-eye approach shots still fail to read a landmark.

### The occupation/retrofit layer is built but not legible in these stands

`T1-HALL-ART` added a ruin/retrofit layer — scaffolds, boilers, pipe runs,
escalating siphons, banner rigs, 631 ivy/rubble instances — specifically to
answer "Team Tether presence visibly changes the land" (E3) and the keyart's
"industrial intrusion on a ruin" language. This lane opened `H-03-ramp-foot`
and `H-04-gate-mouth` directly: neither shows a scaffold, boiler, or pipe
anywhere in frame. What is visible is a green ivy patch over the gate arch (a
flat, uniform mass, not individually authored-looking growth), and a single
thin cyan line arcing off the top-right of the frame with **no visible anchor
at either end** — almost certainly the designed tether-cable-to-pylon landing
(`HALL_DESIGN` §6.1), but at this framing it reads exactly as the blind judge
independently called it: *"a rendering artifact... reads more like an
unattached VFX/cable emitter than an intentional effect."* **Built is not the
same as done here**: the occupation layer exists in the data/scene graph but
is not legible from the stands a player actually uses to read this location,
which is itself the E3/E2 failure this design was written to fix.

### E2 verdict

**FAILS**, on a fresh independent pass, on both bar questions. The Hall is
substantially built (this lane confirms the massing, materials, and lighting
work described in the T1-HALL chain is real and present in these frames) but
does not yet meet E2's own standard ("look deliberately authored," landmark
orientation) at the vantage points a player actually uses. The single largest
concrete, checkable regression from the last claimed-fixed state: the
distance-approach stand (H-01) still fails the silhouette test the T1-HALL-4
fix was specifically built to pass.

**Cost to close:**
- The flat/blurred horizon band and the sky/cloud blur are scene-level (fog,
  aerial-perspective material, post-process) — tunable without new art;
  estimate a capture-and-judge round (half a day) once someone owns the fog
  config, since this is the second time a fog/haze change has produced an
  unintended flat band (`capture_check.gd`'s own header cites the same class
  of defect from 2026-08-30 JUDGE-3).
- Antagonist-red hue and the tree-symmetry/scatter-regularity items are
  material-parameter and scatter-seed tuning — cheap, a few hours.
- Making the occupation layer legible from the H-01/H-02/H-03 stands (not just
  present in the scene) needs either repositioning the existing retrofit props
  toward the camera-facing side of the approach, or scaling/lighting them to
  read at range — a build task, not a new-art task, since the props already
  exist (`T1-HALL-ART`'s 5 procedural meshes, $0 spent).
- The landmark-at-distance failure itself is the expensive one: it survived
  one full owner-directed fix pass already (T1-HALL-4) that measured success
  at the wrong camera height. Closing it for real needs a fix verified at
  ground-eye, approach-distance stands specifically (H-01/H-02), not only the
  elevated survey stand — plan for another capture-and-judge round with that
  constraint stated up front, not assumed.
- Nothing here needs owner-supplied reference art beyond what `HALL_DESIGN`
  and the hall-asset-pack already specify; this is scene/lighting/placement
  work throughout.
