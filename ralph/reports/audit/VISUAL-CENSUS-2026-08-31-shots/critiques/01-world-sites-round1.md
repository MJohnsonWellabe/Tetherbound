# Visual judgement — `shots/census/01-world-sites/`

## First, plainly: the cast

The rubric asks me to lead with the characters and creatures if they don't hold up. They are the strongest and the weakest thing in this set at the same time, because they are not one thing.

**The trainer holds up.** In `03-quarry-standing-day.png` and `02-mill-pond-standing-day.png` he is clean, appealing, correctly proportioned, cel-shaded with flat skin and a readable eye — he belongs in the keyart's DAY/NIGHT panels and he would not embarrass himself next to the archer in `palworld-02-open-field-path.jpg`. **The badger in `04-warrens-den-day.png` also holds up**: a real silhouette, character in the face, a hard shell-plate read against soft fur, and — critically — it is *big*, which is a design statement a still frame can carry.

**The rest of the cast does not, and the problem is that they are four different games.** In one contact sheet I count four incompatible shading idioms:

- `02-mill-pond-standing-day.png` — the bearded NPC at the lower left is painterly-realistic: muddy olive hand-painted grime, heavy dark linework baked into the diffuse, a texture density three or four times the trainer's. He is standing eighteen inches from a cel-shaded anime boy and they do not look like they are in the same render, let alone the same world.
- `04-warrens-approach-day.png` — the armoured figure at bottom-centre is a third idiom again: desaturated grey-black hard-surface armour with fine mechanical detail and silver hair, essentially zero colour, reading like a character from a different, darker game who walked into a Meadows screenshot.
- `04-warrens-standing-day.png` — the boar at the doorway is a fourth: soft, toy-like, warm tan speckle, head roughly 45% of body length. Cute, but it is a *chibi* creature standing in the same site as the semi-realistic armoured badger of `04-warrens-den-day.png`. Those two cannot both be right.

Palworld's entire cast — the archer in `palworld-02`, the swordsman in `palworld-03`, Mammorest and Grintale — is one shading language with one specular response and one level of texture frequency. That coherence is most of why it looks shipped. This build has the anchor characters right and everyone else sourced from somewhere else, and it shows at thumbnail size on the sheet.

## 1. Silhouette and readability at small size

Judged from `_sheet.png` at thumbnail scale, **two of ten frames have a readable subject.**

- **Reads:** the mill in `02-mill-pond-standing-day.png` and `02-mill-pond-wheel-day.png`. The tall gable, the white plaster against the dark timber, the terracotta roof — that is a landmark, and it is the best thing in the survey. The badger in `04-warrens-den-day.png` also reads as a distinct shape at thumbnail.
- **Does not read:** `04-warrens-approach-day.png` reduces at thumbnail to *one grey lump*. It has no notch, no arch, no vertical accent, no colour break — the only thing separating it from the sky is that it is grey and the sky is blue. This is supposed to be the announcement frame for a place, and there is nothing in the silhouette to announce.
- **Does not read at all:** `05-relay-camp-approach-day.png` is, at thumbnail, a green rectangle with a tree line. There is a single ~40-pixel red banner in the entire frame and it disappears completely at small size. Nothing above the ridge, no smoke, no mast, no roof. A player at this stand does not know there is anything there.
- `03-quarry-standing-day.png` and `03-quarry-conduit-head-day.png` reduce to a speckle of dark dots on green-and-sand. The pylons are the only vertical and they are narrow, dark, and lost against the tree line behind them.
- `03-quarry-approach-day.png` reduces to a black rectangle (see criterion 7).

Compare `palworld-04-plateau-landmark.jpg`: at thumbnail you still read *a tall spire, a stone arch, a ruined cottage, and three creatures* as four separate shapes. Compare the keyart's sunset panel: one standing stone against a bright sky, unmistakable at any size. Compare even `palworld-02`, the plainest reference — the cliff arch and the black cave mouth read at 30%.

**The player figure is readable** in `02-mill-pond-approach-day.png`, `04-warrens-approach-day.png` and `05-relay-camp-approach-day.png` — his blue-and-white torso separates from grass, and that is a genuine win. He is *not* readable in `03-quarry-conduit-head-day.png`, where his head and face wash out to near-white against the pale sand and cloud and the whole figure loses its edge.

## 2. Colour and value structure

**Value range is the structural failure.** Excluding the broken frame and the two interiors, every outdoor frame lives in a narrow mid-to-light band. `05-relay-camp-approach-day.png` has no dark anywhere: the darkest pixels are the ridge-line tree canopies at maybe 25% value, everything else is between 45% and 85%. `04-warrens-approach-day.png` is the same problem in grey — the rock massif is a single mid-grey from base to crown with only texture noise separating one 10-metre boulder from the next.

`palworld-01-boss-fight-forest.jpg` has near-black tree silhouettes at both frame edges, a mid-value creature, and blown-out spark highlights — three clear value tiers in one image. The keyart's oak grove panel does the same with deep canopy shadow against sunlit path. Neither of those structures exists here.

**Do the frames read as one place? Yes — arguably too much so.** The same sky gradient, the same cloud texture, the same grass card, the same white five-petal flower and the same cabbage-leaved plant appear in `02-mill-pond-approach-day.png`, `03-quarry-standing-day.png`, `04-warrens-approach-day.png` and `05-relay-camp-approach-day.png`. Continuity is not in question. Regional identity is: the quarry's only distinguishing signal is a sand material and some bare-branch trees, and the warrens' is grey rock. Nothing in the vegetation, ground clutter or palette says "you are somewhere new."

**Oxblood discipline is intact.** The only true oxblood in the survey is the small banner in `05-relay-camp-approach-day.png`. The terracotta roof tiles in `02-mill-pond-*` are orange-red and clearly separate from it. Nothing friendly wears the danger colour.

But the inverse problem exists: **nothing in these ten frames signals danger at all.** The tether apparatus in `03-quarry-standing-day.png` and `03-quarry-conduit-head-day.png` is teal-and-cyan on gunmetal, which reads magical and inviting. If this is hostile hardware, the palette is telling the player the opposite.

## 3. Intentionality — authored or generated?

**`05-relay-camp-approach-day.png` reads as generator output.** The foreground is the same cabbage-leaved plant repeated at near-uniform ~1.5 m spacing across the entire lower half of the frame, all at the same scale, all at the same rotation family, with no clustering and no clearings. There is one thin dirt trail and no other break. This is the definition of "evenly scattered density."

**`03-quarry-standing-day.png` is a clearing with objects in it, not a place.** Named a quarry, it contains: a signpost, a brown mound, a wicker basket, two grey boulders, a pylon, a dark boulder, and dead trees — arranged along the sand at roughly even intervals with no relationship to each other. There is no quarry *face*, no benched cut, no spoil heap with cart ruts, no cut blocks, no tool left at a workface, no wear path where stone was dragged. Nothing here was quarried. `palworld-05-base-building.jpg` sells a worked site with a *dozen* small authored decisions — a crate at the wall, a harvest basket at the pal's feet, a workbench, a fence line that follows the terrain — none of that reasoning is present at this site.

**`03-quarry-conduit-head-day.png` shows one deliberate arrangement and undermines it.** The wicker planter boxes and the barrel-and-crate group read as placed by hand — good. But the *same* brown mound asset appears twice in the same frame at the same scale (left of the signpost, and again at the far left), which reads as scatter, not staging.

**`04-warrens-approach-day.png` is the most authored composition in the set** — the massif is deliberately arranged with a foreground boulder leading the eye and a doorway on axis. Credit where due. The trail worn into the grass toward the right-hand door is exactly the right instinct.

**Scale variety is absent in the trees.** Every tree in the survey is the same lollipop canopy silhouette at a different scale — the ~2.5 m tree at the lower-left of `04-warrens-approach-day.png` is the same shape as the ~12 m trees on the ridge behind it. There is no mid-storey, no shrub layer, no fallen trunk, no leaning tree, no two-canopy species mix. `palworld-01` has canopy, mid-branch and ground fern in a single frame.

## 4. Lighting

**Time of day reads** — a clear mid-to-late morning, warm key, cool sky bounce, consistent across all outdoor frames. Sun direction is consistent too (tree shadow falls camera-left in `02-mill-pond-approach-day.png`; pylon shadow falls left in `03-quarry-standing-day.png`; sun sits behind and above the mill in `02-mill-pond-standing-day.png`). That is real coherence and it is worth keeping.

**But shadows stop at short range, and it guts the frames that need them most.**

- `04-warrens-approach-day.png` — a rock formation roughly 10 m tall and 60 m wide casts **no readable shadow onto the meadow in front of it.** The grass at its base is the same brightness as the grass twenty metres away. The massif is not sitting on the ground; it is pasted onto it.
- `05-relay-camp-approach-day.png` — the entire ridge tree line casts nothing. The hill has no form; it reads as a flat green plane with tree decals along the top edge.
- `03-quarry-standing-day.png` — the dead trees at mid-distance cast nothing, while the pylon in the near ground does. The shadow cutoff distance is visible in-frame.

The build clearly *can* cast shadows — the foreground tree in `02-mill-pond-approach-day.png` lays a large, correct, soft-edged shadow across the meadow, and it is the single most convincing lighting moment in the survey. The problem is range, not capability.

**No contact darkening anywhere.** The grey boulders in `03-quarry-conduit-head-day.png` meet the sand at a hard geometric seam with no occlusion; the wicker planters do the same; the large dark boulder at the right of that frame reads as hovering. Terrain has almost no form outside the near field.

**The sun disc in `02-mill-pond-standing-day.png` is a hard-edged white circle with no bloom, no glow falloff and no atmospheric scatter** — it reads as a sticker on the sky. The cloud layer under it is a low-resolution cream-brown smear with visible blur at that scale.

**Interior lighting in `04-warrens-den-day.png` is the weakest in the set:** the badger receives one flat frontal-ambient term with no rim, no bounce off the sand floor, and no key from the doorway. A large creature in a dark room is a gift for lighting and this frame takes none of it.

## 5. Horizon and depth

**There is essentially no aerial perspective anywhere in the survey, and it is the second-largest gap versus the references.**

- `05-relay-camp-approach-day.png` — the ridge tree line ~150 m out is as saturated, as contrasty and as dark as the plants two metres from camera. There is no haze, no desaturation, no value lift. The result is a flat postcard.
- `03-quarry-standing-day.png` — the wall of trees along the horizon has the same green and the same contrast as the trees at 20 m.
- Compare `palworld-04-plateau-landmark.jpg`, where the distant spire is lifted almost to sky value and the mid-ground cliff sits at an intermediate step; and `palworld-05-base-building.jpg`, where the mountains are nearly white. That single tonal device is doing most of the depth work in both references and it is absent here.

**Visible world edges:**

- `03-quarry-standing-day.png` — at the left horizon (roughly x 0–260) the terrain ends in a straight, flat pale band against the sky with no distant landform behind it. The world visibly runs out.
- `02-mill-pond-approach-day.png` — the left horizon is a straight, flat green line for a third of the frame width.
- `05-relay-camp-approach-day.png` — at the right edge the terrain terminates on a hard plateau cut against the sky.

**Visible LOD band:** in `05-relay-camp-approach-day.png` the ridge-line trees are a distinctly darker, bluer and blockier variant than the near trees, with an abrupt transition rather than a blend. In `02-mill-pond-approach-day.png` the mismatch is worse and in the opposite direction: the foreground trunk is bright terracotta-orange while the same tree species at 30 m has an almost black trunk. Those are not the same tree.

**No chunk seams observed** in the terrain itself — that part is clean.

## 6. Interface

**There is no HUD in any of the ten frames.** No health, no party, no compass, no prompt, no reticle. I cannot judge safe area, hierarchy or glance legibility, and this survey does not answer that question. It is worth noting that every one of the five Palworld references carries a full HUD and part of what makes them read as a shipped game is that furniture; a survey with the HUD suppressed is measuring a different thing.

## 7. Artefacts

Ranked roughly by severity.

1. **`03-quarry-approach-day.png` is a broken frame.** The camera is inside foliage or terrain. Roughly 95% of the image is near-black dark green, with three small ragged apertures showing fragments of grass, a tree trunk and one triangle of sky. There is nothing to judge. Whatever this stand was meant to show, it shows nothing.
2. **`04-warrens-den-day.png` — the trainer's torso and backpack interpenetrate the badger's left foreleg and chest.** Geometry through geometry, front and centre of the frame.
3. **`04-warrens-den-day.png` — the ceiling beams carry the same rock texture as the walls.** They read as stone beams spanning a stone ceiling, which looks like a material assignment error rather than a choice. The leftmost beam also enters the wall with no bracket, corbel or join.
4. **`04-warrens-den-day.png` — the vertical cream-white slab on the left wall (approx. x 145–165)** has no geometry, no falloff and no visible source. It reads as an untextured emissive quad, not a light shaft.
5. **`04-warrens-standing-day.png` — a thin white hairline runs diagonally across the rock face at the right** (approx. x 1050–1230, y 500–590). Reads as a z-fighting sliver or a stray edge.
6. **`04-warrens-standing-day.png` — the ground under the trainer is a smeared olive-yellow blur** at a visibly lower texel density than the stone around it, and it does not match the sand floor visible through the doorway a few metres away.
7. **`04-warrens-standing-day.png` — the bush at the lower right is a flat card cluster** with hard, stair-stepped alpha edges on every leaf, lit far brighter than the rock it sits against. It reads as pasted on.
8. **`02-mill-pond-wheel-day.png` contains no water wheel.** The frame is named for one; the mill's water-facing elevation is blank stone.
9. **`02-mill-pond-wheel-day.png` — the outbuilding at the left has its roof eaves at ground level.** Its walls are buried in the terrain; only the roof is above ground. The same building appears in `02-mill-pond-standing-day.png` at the right with its base cut off by the grass line.
10. **`02-mill-pond-wheel-day.png` and `02-mill-pond-standing-day.png` — severe wide-FOV keystoning.** The mill's verticals splay and the building appears to lean backwards. Both stands also give 55–65% of the frame to empty sky.
11. **`02-mill-pond-approach-day.png` — the foreground tree's leaf cards show hard black alpha edges** and thin dark slivers between overlapping cards, visible along the whole right side of the canopy.
12. **`03-quarry-conduit-head-day.png` — the stone slab path begins and ends in open sand** with a squared-off termination at the lower left. It connects nothing to nothing.
13. **`03-quarry-conduit-head-day.png` — a bare black branch floats in the top-right corner** with no trunk in frame and nothing behind it.
14. **`03-quarry-conduit-head-day.png` / `03-quarry-standing-day.png` — the teal tether ribbon between pylons is a flat, constant-width, unlit band with visible polygon kinks.** It does not glow, does not sag, does not receive or emit light, and does not read as energy.
15. **`03-quarry-standing-day.png` — the signpost decal text is illegible** at any scale; it is a sub-pixel dark smear on a light plank. A second illegible sign appears in `03-quarry-conduit-head-day.png`.
16. **`03-quarry-standing-day.png` — the sand/grass material boundary is a hard straight edge** with no transition zone, scatter blend or dirt fringe, running across the mid-ground.
17. **`04-warrens-approach-day.png` — the entire massif uses one grey noise texture at one UV scale** across both 10 m and 1 m rocks. No moss, no waterline, no dirt at the base, no colour break between blocks. It reads as untextured blockout geometry with a noise material on it.
18. **`04-warrens-standing-day.png` — the same complaint, worse:** floor, walls and overhangs all carry the identical grey-white speckle at the identical scale, with boxy right-angle intersections. This frame does not look like art; it looks like a greybox someone applied one material to.

## 8. Scale agreement

Using the trainer at 1.80 m.

**What agrees:**
- The mill in `02-mill-pond-standing-day.png`: stone base ≈ 1× trainer height, total ≈ 5× → roughly 9 m for a three-storey mill. Correct.
- **Creature scale variety is real and reads correctly.** The badger in `04-warrens-den-day.png` stands roughly 1.6–1.8 m at the shoulder — genuinely large, clearly a creature you fight *alongside*. The boar in `04-warrens-standing-day.png` reads roughly 0.8–1.0 m at the shoulder, and the small orange creature in the left doorway of `04-warrens-approach-day.png` is smaller again. That is three tiers and it is the right instinct. This criterion passes on creatures.
- The rock massif in `04-warrens-approach-day.png` at ≈ 8–10 m is plausible, if unimpressive for a landmark.

**What does not agree:**
- **`02-mill-pond-standing-day.png` — the bearded NPC is measurably shorter than the trainer despite standing closer to camera.** His head crown sits below the trainer's while his position in frame puts him nearer the lens; the true height difference is therefore larger than it looks. If both are meant to be adult humans, one of them is mis-scaled.
- **`03-quarry-standing-day.png` — the wicker basket beside the signpost reads at roughly 1.2 m tall.** That is a bathtub, not a basket. The same asset in `03-quarry-conduit-head-day.png` sits at a plausible ~0.6 m, so the two instances disagree with each other.
- **`03-quarry-conduit-head-day.png` — the large dark boulder in the right foreground (≈ 4 m across) carries the same texel density as the 1 m rocks beside it.** It reads as a small rock scaled up 4×, which is exactly what it looks like.
- **`04-warrens-approach-day.png` — the tree at the lower left is ≈ 2.5 m tall with a full mature canopy**, standing in the same frame as ≈ 12 m trees of identical silhouette. One tree asset scaled across a 5:1 range is doing the work of an entire forest, and the small instances read as bonsai.
- **`05-relay-camp-approach-day.png` — the cabbage-leaved foreground plants are ≈ 0.8 m across, at uniform scale across the whole frame.** No size jitter at all; the same plant is the same size at 3 m and at 25 m from camera, which flattens the depth further.
- **`04-warrens-den-day.png` — the room's ceiling is roughly 2× trainer height (~3.5 m)** while the doorway at left is barely over head height, and the beams span the full width with no supports. The room does not agree with its own openings.

# VERDICT

## The three things that most separate these frames from the references

**1. Nothing announces a place from the approach stand. The references never fail this and this survey fails it three times out of four.**

`05-relay-camp-approach-day.png` is the clearest case: a player standing here sees grass, a tree line, and a 40-pixel red banner. There is no vertical, no smoke column, no roof breaking the ridge, no mast, no silhouette of any kind — nothing that would make a player walk toward it. `04-warrens-approach-day.png` has a landmark but it is a featureless grey lump with no arch, notch, spire or colour break, indistinguishable at thumbnail from a hill. `03-quarry-approach-day.png` is a black frame.

What the references do instead: `palworld-04-plateau-landmark.jpg` puts a tall thin spire against pale haze plus a stone arch plus a ruin, three distinct shapes at three depths, all readable at 30%. `palworld-02-open-field-path.jpg` — the plainest reference in the folder — still gives you a cliff arch and a black cave mouth as destinations. The keyart's sunset panel does it with a single standing stone, and the settlement panel with a windmill and a tower. The build has exactly one object that clears this bar, the mill in `02-mill-pond-standing-day.png`, and it clears it easily — which proves the problem is that the other three sites have no such object, not that the engine can't render one.

**2. The world has no depth, because there is no aerial perspective and shadows stop before the mid-ground.**

In `05-relay-camp-approach-day.png` the trees on the ridge are as dark, as saturated and as contrasty as the plant two metres from the lens, and the ridge casts no shadow, so the hill has no form. `03-quarry-standing-day.png` has the same flat horizon tree wall plus a visible terrain edge at the left. `04-warrens-approach-day.png` puts a 10 m rock formation on a meadow and lays down no shadow at all, so the landmark floats.

`palworld-05-base-building.jpg` lifts its mountains almost to sky value; `palworld-04` steps spire, cliff and near-ground into three distinct tonal bands. That one device is most of what separates a reference frame from these. The build already proves it can cast a convincing shadow — the tree in `02-mill-pond-approach-day.png` does it well — so this is range and atmosphere settings, not capability.

**3. The humanoid cast is three unrelated art styles standing in the same world, and the creatures are two.**

`02-mill-pond-standing-day.png` puts a painterly, grimy, high-frequency bearded NPC eighteen inches from a clean cel-shaded trainer. `04-warrens-approach-day.png` adds a third: a desaturated hard-surface armoured figure with near-zero colour. `04-warrens-standing-day.png`'s toy-proportioned boar and `04-warrens-den-day.png`'s semi-realistic armoured badger are a fourth and fifth reading. Every character in all five Palworld references shares one shading language, one specular response and one texture frequency, and that uniformity is a large share of why they look shipped rather than assembled.

*Narrowly missing the top three, and worth naming:* the ground scatter in `05-relay-camp-approach-day.png` and `03-quarry-standing-day.png` is dense but uniform — one plant, one size, one spacing, no clusters and no clearings — which reads procedural next to `palworld-03-field-boss-meadow.jpg`'s clumped grass, bare dirt patches and scattered rock; and the "quarry" in `03-quarry-standing-day.png` contains nothing that has been quarried.

## The two bar questions

### A. Do these frames read as belonging to the world in `tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`?

**YES.**

What carried it: the palette is genuinely on the board. The grass green, the terracotta roof, the pale teal water and the warm cream cloud in `02-mill-pond-approach-day.png` could be sampled from the keyart's settlement and stream panels. The half-timbered mill in `02-mill-pond-standing-day.png` is the keyart's village building language executed correctly. The trainer is recognisably the boy from the DAY/NIGHT panels. The wildflower meadow, the oak-ish canopies and the pond are all board items, present and correct. The oxblood is reserved. This is not palette drift; the world in these frames came from that board.

Two exceptions I will name rather than hide: `04-warrens-approach-day.png` and `05-relay-camp-approach-day.png` do not belong to it, for opposite reasons — the first is a grey, colourless, formless rock mass that appears nowhere on the board, and the second is an empty field with none of the board's layered canopy, no landmark, and none of its "hints of mystery." The board's mood is cozy-with-depth; those two frames are neither.

### B. Shown these frames beside `palworld-0*.jpg`, would someone say these are trying to be the same kind of game?

**NO.**

They would say the genre is the same and the execution is not, and four specific things would drive that:

1. **`04-warrens-standing-day.png` and `04-warrens-den-day.png` do not look like art.** One grey speckle texture at one UV scale across floor, walls and overhangs; right-angle boxes; beams textured as rock; an emissive rectangle with no source. Placed beside `palworld-05-base-building.jpg` these read as an untextured blockout that someone screenshotted, and no viewer would mistake them for shipped content.
2. **`05-relay-camp-approach-day.png` has nothing in it.** Palworld's emptiest reference still contains a road, a cliff, a cave, three creatures, a combat and a quest log. This frame has grass.
3. **`03-quarry-approach-day.png` is a black frame.** A survey that ships a broken camera stand answers the question by itself.
4. **The cast does not cohere** (see gap 3), and Palworld's does, absolutely and without exception.

The counter-evidence is real and I want it on record: `02-mill-pond-approach-day.png` and `02-mill-pond-standing-day.png` would survive that comparison. Warm light, a real cast shadow, a landmark building, a readable trainer, a pond. If the whole survey looked like the mill pond, B would be yes.

## The split: fixable by changing the scene vs. needs art that is not in the build

### Fixable by changing the scene — this is work, not procurement

- **Move the `03-quarry-approach-day.png` camera stand out of the foliage.** Nothing else about that frame can be judged until this is done.
- **Raise the shadow draw distance** so the massif in `04-warrens-approach-day.png` and the ridge in `05-relay-camp-approach-day.png` sit on the ground.
- **Add aerial perspective / distance fog with a value lift**, tuned against `palworld-04`. This single change addresses most of gap 2 across `05-relay-camp-approach-day.png`, `03-quarry-standing-day.png` and `03-quarry-conduit-head-day.png`.
- **Reduce the camera FOV** on the `02-mill-pond-standing-day.png` and `02-mill-pond-wheel-day.png` stands, and tilt down — the mill stops leaning and the frames stop being 60% sky.
- **Cluster the scatter and jitter its scale** in `05-relay-camp-approach-day.png` and `03-quarry-standing-day.png`: clumps, bare patches, trampled ground, and a 0.5×–1.6× size range instead of one uniform plant.
- **Blend the sand/grass boundary** in `03-quarry-standing-day.png` with a transition scatter fringe.
- **Terminate the slab path** in `03-quarry-conduit-head-day.png` at something, or delete it.
- **Raise the sunken outbuilding** in `02-mill-pond-wheel-day.png` out of the terrain.
- **Fix material assignment on the ceiling beams** in `04-warrens-den-day.png` (wood, not rock) and delete or replace the emissive slab on the left wall.
- **Reposition the trainer** in `04-warrens-den-day.png` so he stops intersecting the badger.
- **Re-stage the quarry props** in `03-quarry-standing-day.png` into a worked site: tools at a face, a spoil heap with cart ruts, blocks in a stack, wear paths — with the assets already visible in `03-quarry-conduit-head-day.png`.
