# Visual critique — `shots/census/04-combat/`

## The thing to say first

The rubric asks me to say plainly if the creatures and characters don't hold up. Before that, though, there is a more basic problem, and it is the whole reason this set fails as combat frames:

**The fight has no visible opponent.**

In `02-arena-opens.png` and `03-closing-in.png` a large opaque slab across the top-centre of the screen announces `LEVEL 2 / Bramblebun / GROUND` with a full boss-style health bar — and I cannot find Bramblebun anywhere in either frame. I searched both at full size. There is a grey ground chevron floating at roughly (355, 310) in `02-arena-opens.png` with nothing beneath it but grass. There is a small grey puff at the far left of `03-closing-in.png`, which may be a dust particle. That is all. The named antagonist of the encounter is not in the picture.

In `04-enemy-winds-up-offaxis.png` I can finally identify it: the pale straw-coloured tuft at approximately (590, 430), directly under the floating chevron and just past the Terrapup's muzzle. Measured against the villager figure and the trainer elsewhere in the set, it is **roughly 0.30–0.35 m tall** — knee-high, and the same cream-and-tan value as the dry grass tufts scattered through the same frame. In `05-quick-attack-lands-offaxis.png` the equivalent blob sits near (680, 300) beside the trainer at about 15 px in a 720 px frame. At combat distance it is not distinguishable from ground scatter.

Compare `palworld-01-boss-fight-forest.jpg`: the boss occupies the right two-thirds of the frame, mouth open, and would still read at thumbnail size. `palworld-03-field-boss-meadow.jpg`: the magenta boss fills the right half, mid-lunge, at a colour found nowhere else in the frame. Both references answer "does this look like an event?" in the first quarter-second. This set does not answer it at all, in any frame.

## 1. Silhouette and readability at small size

Viewing `_sheet.png` as a thumbnail, what survives is: green field, a brown lump in the lower-left corner of each frame, the fence arc, the mint arena band, and the HUD slabs. In `04-enemy-winds-up-offaxis.png` the orange roofs also survive.

What does **not** survive:

- **The trainer.** In `02-arena-opens.png`, `03-closing-in.png` and `05-quick-attack-lands-offaxis.png` he collapses at sheet size to a 5–6 px dark tick, visually identical to the dark-green bush rosettes scattered across the same field. There is nothing about his value, colour or silhouette that separates the protagonist from a shrub.
- **The enemy.** Absent, as above.
- **The bare tree** in `02-arena-opens.png` / `03-closing-in.png` / `05-quick-attack-lands-offaxis.png` reads at thumbnail as the same dark blob as the bush clumps. Tree/bush/rock are not separable at sheet size anywhere except the boulder at left of `01-approach.png`.
- **Any difference between the five moments.** At sheet scale all five frames are the same picture of a lawn with a brown lump in the lower-left. `01-approach.png` is separable only because its sky is a different colour, and `04-enemy-winds-up-offaxis.png` only because of the houses. Nothing in the pixels distinguishes "arena opens" from "closing in" from "quick attack lands".

The Terrapup is the only element that passes this criterion, and it passes because it is enormous and close, not because it is well-staged.

## 2. Colour and value structure

- **The sky is dead in four of five frames.** `02-arena-opens.png`, `03-closing-in.png`, `04-enemy-winds-up-offaxis.png` and `05-quick-attack-lands-offaxis.png` all have a flat, blown-out grey-white sky with no blue, no cloud form, and a visible hard band where it meets the distant hills. That is 10–20% of each frame contributing nothing. The key art board is saturated blue with structured white cumulus in every single daylight panel.
- **`01-approach.png` does not match the other four.** It has a warm dusk sky with tan streaked cloud, a warm rim on the hill, and visible distance haze on the far ridge. The other four are flat overcast with no haze. This is one continuous fight in one location — the fence and the hill are the same — shot under two different lighting setups. Seeing this on `_sheet.png` is exactly what the contact sheet is for, and it is a straightforward inconsistency defect.
- **The value range in `03-closing-in.png` is almost nil.** The darkest thing in the frame is the bare tree and the HUD panel; the lightest is the flat sky. Between them the entire field sits in one mid-value yellow-green. There is no deep shadow anywhere and no highlight anywhere. The key art meadow panels hold near-black shaded canopy against near-white sunlit grass in the same image.
- **Hue variety is one note.** Roughly 70% of every frame is the same yellow-green. The only other colours in the whole set are the Terrapup's brown, the fence's red-brown, one small purple flower clump in `02-arena-opens.png`/`03-closing-in.png`, scattered white flower dots, and the orange roofs in `04-enemy-winds-up-offaxis.png`. Those roofs are the single strongest colour accent across five combat frames — and they are in the background of a fight, not in the fight.
- **Team Tether oxblood:** correctly absent. It has not leaked onto friendly elements. But the consequence is that **there is no danger colour anywhere in a combat scene**. The hostile creature's health bar is the same green as your own creature's health bar (`02-arena-opens.png`, `03-closing-in.png`); nothing in the frame is colour-coded as a threat.

## 3. Intentionality

This scatter reads as generated, not placed:

- **The bush rosettes** in `03-closing-in.png` — the same small dark-green rosette repeated roughly forty times across the field at near-identical scale and near-even spacing, with no clustering, no clearings, no bare ground. Same repetition in `02-arena-opens.png` and `04-enemy-winds-up-offaxis.png`.
- **The boulders on the right hillside of `01-approach.png`** — a dozen dark-brown rocks at almost identical size, evenly distributed down the slope at roughly regular intervals, at arbitrary rotations, none touching or overlapping, none large enough to be a landmark. This is the textbook procedural-scatter read.
- **The ridge rocks in `05-quick-attack-lands-offaxis.png`**, top-right, sit in a near-line at uniform ~0.7 m size.
- **The fence** in `02` through `05` is a single unbroken post-and-rail run of perfectly uniform spacing arcing from one frame edge to the other, with no gate, no gap, no lean, no broken rail, no variation in post height. In `03-closing-in.png` it crosses the entire frame as one mechanical curve. In `01-approach.png` the left-hand fence run simply **terminates in mid-air** with no end post. Fences in the key art's STARTING SETTLEMENT panel are short, leaning, broken runs that frame a path and a well — they are placed to compose, not to enclose.
- **The village-to-field transition in `04-enemy-winds-up-offaxis.png`** is a hard cut. Two half-timber houses stand directly in unbroken grass; there is no path, no dirt apron, no garden, no crate, no woodpile, no fence spur, nothing to say people live here. A lone yellow figure stands at approximately (285, 140) in open grass with no context.

Contrast `palworld-05-base-building.jpg` and `palworld-02-open-field-path.jpg`, where props cluster around function, the ground worn by a path is a different material, and rocks vary from pebble to cliff.

## 4. Lighting

- **There are essentially no cast shadows in the set, and this is the most damaging single defect after the missing enemy.** In `02-arena-opens.png` and `05-quick-attack-lands-offaxis.png` the trainer stands on open grass with **no contact shadow whatsoever** — he reads as a cut-out pasted onto the field, not a person standing in it. The Terrapup in `04-enemy-winds-up-offaxis.png` has a vague darkening beneath it but no defined shadow shape. Every character and pal in `palworld-02-open-field-path.jpg` and `palworld-04-plateau-landmark.jpg` has a crisp grounded contact shadow; that is what makes them present rather than composited.
- **Terrain has no form** in `02`, `03`, `04`, `05`. The flat overcast means the rolling hills are described only by their silhouette against the sky and by grass density, not by light. In `03-closing-in.png` the entire midground hill is one value; you cannot tell a rise from a hollow.
- `01-approach.png` is the exception and demonstrates what the others are missing: directional warm light gives the hill a lit face and a shaded face, and the grass has a warm-to-cool gradient. That frame proves the build can do it; the other four do not.
- **Time of day does not read** in `02`–`05`. It is not morning, noon, or evening — it is "no lighting decision made".

## 5. Horizon and depth

- **No aerial perspective in `02`–`05`.** In `04-enemy-winds-up-offaxis.png` the hills at the horizon are the same saturation and value as the grass three metres in front of the camera. The only depth cue in the frame is object size. The result is a world that looks 60 m deep instead of kilometres deep. `palworld-04-plateau-landmark.jpg` puts strong pale haze on the plateau and the distant tower and gets enormous depth from it; `palworld-01-boss-fight-forest.jpg` haze-separates the background trees from the boss.
- This is the opposite of "fog eating the world" — there is no fog at all, and the world is flat because nothing recedes. `01-approach.png` does have a little ridge haze, which again marks it as shot under different settings.
- **Grass render-distance ring.** In `01-approach.png` there is a hard horizontal line at roughly y=300 where dense grass cards stop and the ground becomes flat green texture. The same ring appears beyond the fence in `05-quick-attack-lands-offaxis.png`, and most obviously in `04-enemy-winds-up-offaxis.png`, where the ground around the houses is bare flat green with no grass cards at all while the foreground is fully dense. That seam is visible in every wide frame in the set.
- **Terrain/material seam** in the top-left corner of `02-arena-opens.png`: a lighter-green wedge of ground that does not match the surrounding field.

## 6. Interface

Judging only safe area, hierarchy and legibility:

- **The boss panel is the loudest object in the frame and it is in the worst possible place.** In `02-arena-opens.png` and `03-closing-in.png` the `Bramblebun` slab is a large opaque dark rectangle dead-centre at the top, occupying the compositional sweet spot and occluding exactly the band of the frame where the arena's far edge and the terrain behind the fight would establish the encounter. Both Palworld references put the boss nameplate in a thin, lightweight top-centre strip that takes maybe a fifth of the vertical space this one takes.
- **The health bar never moves.** It is 100% full and green in all five frames, including `05-quick-attack-lands-offaxis.png`, which is captioned as an attack landing. Nothing in the HUD communicates that combat is happening.
- **The enemy bar is the same green as the ally bars** (`Terrapup` health, `Terrapup` energy). No hostile/friendly colour hierarchy.
- **Keyboard glyphs in a controller-first project.** `01-approach.png` prompts `E  Engage Bramblebun`; `02`–`05` show `F`, `C` and mouse-button icons on the ability tray.
- **Prompt text replaces telegraph.** `it's open — hit it` (`03-closing-in.png`) and `! incoming — move` (`04-enemy-winds-up-offaxis.png`, `05-quick-attack-lands-offaxis.png`) are legible, but a telegraph delivered as a sentence in a box is what you write when the animation and the ground decal don't exist. Neither reference uses a single word of prose to communicate a wind-up.
- Safe area on the corner panels is tight — roughly 40 px on the left of the `Terrapup` panel and ~45 px right / ~55 px bottom on the `Orbs 15` tray in `02-arena-opens.png` — survivable but with no margin for a 16:10 handheld.
- In `01-approach.png` the `Engage Bramblebun` prompt is drawn directly on top of the Terrapup's face.

## 7. Artefacts

1. **Grass render-distance ring** — `01-approach.png` (y≈300), `04-enemy-winds-up-offaxis.png` (grass absent entirely around the houses), `05-quick-attack-lands-offaxis.png` (beyond the fence).
2. **Tiling rock texture.** The cliff face at top-right of `03-closing-in.png` shows a clearly repeating grey rock texture with a visible grid at roughly 1 m. Same on the right-hand hill of `01-approach.png` and the top-left cliff of `05-quick-attack-lands-offaxis.png`.
3. **Hard unblended rock/ground transition.** In `01-approach.png` the large grey boulder at left sits *on* the grass with a hard cut and no moss, dirt or feathering; the hillside rocks do the same. Same failure where the house's stone wall meets grass in `04-enemy-winds-up-offaxis.png` — a hard line, no plinth, no dirt.
4. **The arena decal reads as UI, not world.** The pale mint band in `02`–`05` is a flat unlit gradient stripe with hard edges. In `03-closing-in.png` it passes over and through the fence posts without conforming to them; in `05-quick-attack-lands-offaxis.png` it crosses the entire frame and clips off at a hard diagonal at the right edge. In `02` and `03` its near edge is off-frame, so you cannot read the arena's shape at all. Its hue is close enough to the grass that it is simultaneously intrusive and uninformative.
5. **Floating target chevron with nothing under it.** `02-arena-opens.png` (≈355, 310), `04-enemy-winds-up-offaxis.png` (≈580, 315, hovering well above the tuft it marks), and worst in `05-quick-attack-lands-offaxis.png`, where the chevron sits at the far left (≈130, 360) over empty grass while the action is centre-frame.
6. **Unidentifiable geometry.** In `05-quick-attack-lands-offaxis.png` there is a dark grey box-like object at approximately (730, 240) beyond the fence, and a small pale dome at (620, 240). Neither has a readable identity; the box in particular reads as untextured placeholder geometry sitting in the open world.
7. **The skybox in `01-approach.png`** is a painted streaky cloud texture with visible brush-stroke character that does not match the shading language of anything else in the frame.

## 8. Scale agreement

Using the trainer at 1.80 m as the ruler.

- **`01-approach.png` — the trees are roughly a third the height they should be.** Trainer ≈110 px at ~10 m. The leafy trees in the midfield at ~35–40 m measure ~90 px, which works out to **≈6 m tall**. A 6 m tree with a canopy that narrow is a sapling. The key art board's oak-grove panels show oaks of 15–20 m whose canopies dwarf a two-storey building. As rendered, this is scrubland with shrubs, not the "oak groves" the board names. Additionally, the hillside boulders are all ≈1.5–2 m and all the same, so the hill has no scale anchor at all — it could be 20 m or 200 m high and the frame doesn't say.
- **The bare tree in `02-arena-opens.png` / `03-closing-in.png` is ≈2.6 m** — measured against the trainer at comparable depth (≈180 px vs his 125 px) — with a trunk about as thick as a broom handle. It reads as a twig scaled up, not as a dead tree. It is also the largest object in the arena, which tells you how small the arena's furniture is.
- **The Terrapup is enormous.** In `02-arena-opens.png`, working from the trainer (125 px at ~12 m), the Terrapup at ~3.5 m measures **≈1.25 m at the shoulder and ≈2.5 m long** — larger at the shoulder than a black bear, bison-scale in length. I am not judging whether this species *should* be large; I am reporting the measurement and its consequence.
- **The consequence is the staging failure.** The friendly starter you fight *alongside* measures ≈1.25 m at the shoulder. The named opponent you fight measures ≈0.30 m. The linear ratio between the two combatants is on the order of 4–6×, in favour of the ally. In `palworld-01-boss-fight-forest.jpg` and `palworld-03-field-boss-meadow.jpg` the boss is the largest thing in frame and the player is the small one — that size relationship is what makes those images read as a confrontation. Here the largest thing in frame is your own pet's hindquarters and the smallest is the thing you are fighting. No camera angle recovers a duel from that arrangement.
- **Passing:** the houses in `04-enemy-winds-up-offaxis.png` check out — the villager at (285, 140) at ≈35 px against a house eave of ≈110 px gives an eave at ≈5.6 m and a ridge at ≈8.5 m, correct for a two-storey cottage. The fence in `05-quick-attack-lands-offaxis.png` reads at ≈1.1 m. Rocks along the ridge in `05` are all ≈0.7 m, which is not wrong but is monotonous — there is no large landmark boulder anywhere in the set.

## The camera, which is under judgement here

- **It frames the wrong subject in all five frames.** In `01-approach.png` the camera sits at the Terrapup's head height directly behind it, so the creature's skull occupies the bottom-centre of the frame and is cropped by the edge. In `02`, `03`, `04` the Terrapup's back and rump fill the lower-left quadrant. In `05-quick-attack-lands-offaxis.png` the Terrapup is cropped hard into the bottom-left corner — only shell and one leg — while the actual action sits small in the middle distance.
- **It never gets both combatants in one readable composition.** The two frames whose filenames say `offaxis` — `04-enemy-winds-up-offaxis.png` and `05-quick-attack-lands-offaxis.png` — are the two where the framing is worst: in `05` the target chevron is at the far left edge, the trainer is centre, and the ally is cropped out of the corner. A combat camera exists to prevent exactly this.
- Both references solve it the same way: foreground player silhouette on one third, opponent filling the other two thirds, both in focus, both in frame.

## The actors

- **Nobody in these frames is exerting force.** The trainer is in a neutral standing idle in `02-arena-opens.png`, `03-closing-in.png` and `05-quick-attack-lands-offaxis.png` — arms at sides, weight even, no weapon in hand. In `03-closing-in.png` he is looking off to the right while the HUD says `it's open — hit it`. In `05-quick-attack-lands-offaxis.png`, the frame whose own filename says an attack lands, he is stiff and neutral. The Terrapup has no attack pose either; in `04-enemy-winds-up-offaxis.png` it is in a static crouch. In `palworld-03-field-boss-meadow.jpg` the player is mid-lunge with the weapon extended and the boss is body-slamming with its legs off the ground.
- **There is not one combat VFX in the set.** No hit spark, no impact ring, no dust puff, no damage number, no flinch, no motion trail, no ground telegraph decal. `palworld-01-boss-fight-forest.jpg` alone carries muzzle sparks, orange impact flashes, green wind ribbons swirling around the boss, and floating numbers. `palworld-03-field-boss-meadow.jpg` has beam trails, ground dust and a red-orange burst. A frame captioned "quick attack lands" containing zero evidence that anything landed is the clearest possible statement of the gap.

## The creature and character art, judged as the game's look

**Terrapup** is the strongest asset in the set and it is genuinely not bad: hand-painted texture with visible brushwork on the fur, a clean rounded stylisation, and a silhouette that survives at thumbnail. Real problems, though:

- **It disappears into the field.** The mint-green shell scutes are within a hair of the grass in both hue and value. In `02-arena-opens.png` and `03-closing-in.png` the back half of the creature merges with the meadow behind it. Every Pal in `palworld-04-plateau-landmark.jpg` is a colour that does not exist in the terrain — yellow, teal, magenta. Creature-versus-ground separation is being solved there by design and is not being solved here at all.
- **Three animal grammars, no unifying logic.** Bear-cub head, tortoise shell, dog paws and tail. Mammorest in `palworld-01-boss-fight-forest.jpg` is also a chimera, but every part of it shares one shape language — chunky rounded masses, consistent outline weight, one silhouette read. Terrapup's shell plates are a hard-edged, comparatively realistic tiled material bolted onto a soft cartoon body; the two do not belong to the same design.
- **The face has no appeal.** No eye is visible at all from behind in `01`, `02`, `03`. In `04-enemy-winds-up-offaxis.png`, the one frame with a clear read on the face, the eye is a flat dark oval with no specular highlight and no lid shape. Every Palworld creature in these references has large, highlighted, readable eyes at 20 m; that is a large part of why they have personality and this does not.

**Bramblebun** — as delivered in `04-enemy-winds-up-offaxis.png` and `05-quick-attack-lands-offaxis.png` it is a straw-coloured tuft at ≈0.3 m, in the exact colour of the dry grass clumps around it. It has no readable silhouette, no face, no colour separation and no presence. It is not good enough to be the thing the game is named after fighting.

**The trainer** is not a designed protagonist. At full size in `02-arena-opens.png` and `03-closing-in.png`: hair is a solid brown blob with no shape or parting, the face has no discernible features, the costume is a plain mid-blue tunic over brown trousers with no accent colour, no silhouette-breaking prop, no visible weapon or tool. In `01-approach.png` at distance he is an anonymous brown-and-blue smudge that would be indistinguishable from the villager in `04-enemy-winds-up-offaxis.png`. Compare the character in `palworld-01-boss-fight-forest.jpg`: distinct red hair with a braid, layered leather harness, a large backpack breaking the silhouette, a weapon in hand — she reads as *the player* from any angle. Nothing here does.

# VERDICT

## Part 1 — The three things that most separate these frames from the references, ranked

**1. There is no opponent in frame, and the camera is pointed at the wrong thing, so nothing reads as an event.**
In `02-arena-opens.png` and `03-closing-in.png` a boss health bar is displayed for a creature that is not visible anywhere in the picture. In `04-enemy-winds-up-offaxis.png` and `05-quick-attack-lands-offaxis.png` the opponent is a 0.3 m straw tuft in the same colour as the ground scatter, while the camera devotes the lower-left quadrant to the ally's rump — cropped into the corner in `05`. What both references do that this does not: `palworld-01-boss-fight-forest.jpg` and `palworld-03-field-boss-meadow.jpg` each give the opponent between a half and two-thirds of the frame, at a colour and a size that make it the unambiguous subject, with the player staged as the smaller foreground read. A viewer shown `02-arena-opens.png` cold could not tell you a fight was occurring.

**2. There is no impact language of any kind — no VFX, no reaction, no damage.**
`05-quick-attack-lands-offaxis.png`, by its own filename an attack connecting, contains no spark, no flash, no dust, no ring, no damage number, no flinch, and a target health bar still at 100%. The trainer is in a neutral standing idle. `palworld-01-boss-fight-forest.jpg` carries muzzle sparks, orange impact flashes, green wind ribbons and floating numbers in one still; `palworld-03-field-boss-meadow.jpg` carries beam trails, ground dust and a burst under the boss. This is the difference between a fight and two models standing in a field.

**3. Flat overcast lighting with no cast shadows, no aerial perspective and a collapsed value range.**
`02`, `03`, `04`, `05` all have a blown-out featureless grey-white sky, no directional light, no terrain form, and — critically — no contact shadows: the trainer in `02-arena-opens.png` and `05-quick-attack-lands-offaxis.png` has no shadow at all and reads as pasted onto the grass. Distant hills in `04-enemy-winds-up-offaxis.png` carry the same saturation as foreground grass, so the world has no depth. Every Palworld reference has crisp grounded shadows and strong atmospheric separation; the key art board has near-black shaded canopy against sunlit meadow in the same panel. And `01-approach.png` — warm directional dusk light, visible haze, a lit and shaded hill face — proves the build can produce it, which makes the other four a settings problem as much as a capability one.

## Part 2 — The bar questions

### A. Do these frames read as belonging to the world in `tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`?

**No.**

What carries: the grass density is genuinely good — denser than `palworld-03-field-boss-meadow.jpg`'s field and closer to the board's meadow than I expected. The half-timber houses with orange roofs in `04-enemy-winds-up-offaxis.png` are drawn from the board's STARTING SETTLEMENT panel and read correctly. The rolling-hill landform and the rocky landmark hill in `01-approach.png` are the right shapes.

What sinks it: the board is a **sunlit** world — saturated blue sky, structured cumulus, deep canopy shadow, warm dirt paths, drifts of purple and white wildflowers, clear water. Four of these five frames have a dead white sky, no shadow, no haze, no water, and roughly six individual flowers. The board's headline feature is **oak groves**; the trees in `01-approach.png` measure ≈6 m against the 1.80 m ruler, which is scrub, and there is exactly one leafy species plus one twig-like bare species in the entire set. The board's value range is wide; `03-closing-in.png` has essentially none. `01-approach.png` alone gets within reach of the board — and it does so under lighting the other four frames don't share, which means the set doesn't even agree with itself about which world it is.

### B. Shown these frames beside `palworld-0*.jpg`, would someone say these are trying to be the same kind of game?

**Yes.**

The genre grammar is unmistakable and it is doing its job: over-the-shoulder third person, a large creature companion at the player's side, a top-centre opponent nameplate with level and elemental type, a four-slot ability tray, a capture-orb counter (`Orbs 15`), a fenced pastoral meadow with a village in the middle distance. Anyone who has played `palworld-02-open-field-path.jpg` would recognise `02-arena-opens.png` as the same kind of game at a glance. They would also, immediately, say it is a much earlier and much thinner build — but the intent reads, and B is a question about intent.

### The split: fixable by changing the scene vs. needs art that is not in the build

**Fixable by changing the scene — this is the work list:**

- Combat camera: raise it, pull it back, and target the midpoint between combatants rather than sitting behind the ally's shoulder; stop cropping the ally into the corner (`05-quick-attack-lands-offaxis.png`) and stop putting its skull in the bottom-centre (`01-approach.png`). Guarantee both combatants in frame — that is the camera's job and it is currently failing it.
- Enemy placement/framing: whatever is causing Bramblebun to be off-screen or unlocatable in `02-arena-opens.png` and `03-closing-in.png`. A boss bar with no boss in frame is a shipping-blocker.
- Lighting: apply `01-approach.png`'s time-of-day and sky to the whole survey; enable cast shadows; the trainer's missing contact shadow in `02`/`05` is the highest-value single fix in the set.
- Add distance haze / aerial perspective so the horizon in `04-enemy-winds-up-offaxis.png` recedes.
- Widen value range: deeper grass shadow, darker canopy, a real sky.
- Grass render-distance ring (`01`, `04`, `05`): extend the distance or feather the falloff.
- Rock texture tiling on the cliffs (`01`, `03`, `05`): triplanar or detail-break, plus ground blending where rocks and walls meet grass.
- Scatter authoring: cluster the bush rosettes and vary their scale (`03-closing-in.png`), vary the boulder sizes on the hill (`01-approach.png`), break the fence with a gate and gaps and a lean (`03-closing-in.png`), fix the fence that ends in mid-air (`01-approach.png`), and add a transition zone — path, dirt, props — between the houses and the field (`04-enemy-winds-up-offaxis.png`).
- Tree scale: scale the existing tree asset up 2–3× so a meadow oak measures 15–20 m against the ruler; add one clearing so the field has composition.

**Needs art that is not in the build:**

- **A combat VFX library.** Hit sparks, impact rings, ground dust, cast/charge trails, wind-up telegraph decals, damage numbers, hit-flash on the target. None of it exists in any frame. This is the largest missing art package and it is what separates `05-quick-attack-lands-offaxis.png` from `palworld-01-boss-fight-forest.jpg` more than anything else.
- **Combat animation.** Wind-up, attack, flinch and recoil for both creatures; a combat stance and a reaction for the trainer. Everyone in every frame is in a neutral idle. No camera or lighting change fixes this.
- **A readable Bramblebun.** At 0.3 m in ground-scatter colours it cannot read at combat distance under any framing. This needs either a larger silhouette or a high-contrast colourway that does not exist in the terrain — a design decision plus new art, not a scene tweak.
- **Terrapup's face.** The eye needs a lid shape and a specular highlight, and the shell needs a colourway that separates it from grass. The shell/body material mismatch is a design pass, not a setting.
- **Tree variety.** At least one more leafy species, a genuine wide-canopy oak, and a bark-textured dead tree to replace the black wire scribble in `02`/`03`/`05`.
- **A landmark boulder and rock size variety.** Every rock in the set is 0.7–2 m.
- **A designed protagonist.** Hair with shape, a face, an accent colour, a silhouette-breaking prop or weapon. As shipped, the trainer in `03-closing-in.png` is indistinguishable from the villager in `04-enemy-winds-up-offaxis.png`, and both are indistinguishable from a bush at sheet size.
- **A hostile visual language.** Wild creatures currently look exactly like allies. Some combination of colourway, aura VFX and nameplate treatment has to make a threat read as a threat before the player parses the HUD text.
