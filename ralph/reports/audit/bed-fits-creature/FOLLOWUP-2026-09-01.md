# BACKLOG-VISUAL-BED-FITS-CREATURE — investigation, 2026-09-01

**The literal defect (spatial overflow) does not reproduce as measured.**
What does reproduce, worse, is a different bug in the same area: the current
production `play_rest()` pose flips terrapup (and at least two other
species) onto its back with paws in the air rather than settling it onto its
side. Fixed for the one species the census actually screenshotted; the
broader pose bug is flagged, not fixed, below.

## What defect 113 claimed

`ralph/reports/audit/VISUAL-CENSUS-2026-08-31.md` #113, sourced from
`06-creature-resting.png`:

> The creature does not fit its own bed. The rope ring is ~1.15x the badger's
> body length, with hindquarters and rear paws outside it and front paws on
> the rim. This is `BACKLOG-BED-SCALE-POSE`'s own subject, and it is still
> failing after that lane's change.

The "badger" is terrapup (`data/creatures/species.json`'s brown-and-white
starter, styled off the Plumberry Plains bear character, not the turtle its
name suggests) — confirmed against the critique text, which explicitly
matches it to Palworld's creature-design bar.

## First finding: the census frame is stale, not current `main`

`06-creature-resting.png` / `07-creature-resting-far-side.png` are produced
by `tools/_capture_creature_bed_rest.gd`. That tool predates
OWNER-0901-CREATURE-BED-POSE's `play_rest()` roll feature and was never
updated — it called `play_faint()` directly (the standing/crouching faint
pose), not `play_rest()` (what `creature_bed.gd::_sync_rest_body()` actually
plays for every real occupant). The census critic judged a pose no real
player has ever seen a creature take in its bed.

Re-rendering that exact tool, unmodified, on unmodified `main` reproduces
the census's own frame pixel-for-pixel in composition
(`shots/before-06-creature-resting-stale-faint.png`,
`shots/before-07-creature-resting-far-side-stale-faint.png`) — so the stale
call is confirmed, not assumed.

## Second finding: it does not spatially overflow, in either pose

`tools/_capture_bed_topdown.gd` (new, throwaway per the project's `_`
convention) renders straight down the Y axis, where perspective
foreshortening cannot manufacture or hide an overflow the way the stale
tool's low 3/4 camera can. Measured against the *current* rim
(`creature_bed.gd`'s `RIM_RADIUS_X`=1.55, `RIM_RADIUS_Z`=1.35 — already
enlarged once by `BACKLOG-BED-SCALE-POSE`):

- Faint pose (what the census saw): fully inside the rim, nose at the front
  edge, no part outside.
- Rest pose (what `_sync_rest_body()` actually plays): also fully inside the
  rim — `tools/_diag_rest_roll_math.gd terrapup` measures the rolled body's
  own AABB at position=(-0.81, -0.18, -1.07), size=(1.62, 1.35, 2.15) in the
  body's local space, centred on the anchor on both the roll axis and the
  untouched nose-tail axis (2.15m against a 2.7-3.1m rim diameter).

Neither pose reproduces "hindquarters and rear paws outside the ring." The
perspective camera in the stale tool's far-side shot
(`shots/before-07-creature-resting-far-side-stale-faint.png`) makes a
standing creature's planted hind legs *look* like they cross the rim line
because the camera sits low and close to it — real 3D containment says
otherwise.

## Third finding: what's actually broken is uglier, and load-bearing

Rendering the *current, real* `play_rest()` pose (not the stale tool's
`play_faint()`) turned up a genuine, currently-shipping defect: for at least
terrapup and trailpup, `DEFAULT_REST_ROLL_DEG`'s 90-degree roll flips the
creature onto its BACK, all four paws in the air
(`shots/broken-terrapup-play-rest-default90-front.png`,
`shots/broken-terrapup-play-rest-default90-side.png`). bramblebun is worse
still — an unreadable tangle
(`shots/followup-bramblebun-broken-front.png`). This is spatially contained
(see above), so it is a pose-quality defect, not a fit defect, but it is a
worse "does this creature look right resting in its own bed" failure than
what the census caught, and it directly matches
`ralph/OWNER_PLAYTEST_2026-09-01.md` item 9 ("creatures don't lie down in
bed except galecrest... stand/pose wrong").

Root cause, as far as this investigation traced it: `play_rest()`'s roll
assumes a prone, standing quadruped whose paws sit near the ground-contact
centreline, so rotating 90 degrees around the model's own nose-tail (Z) axis
swings the standing-height axis down into a side-lying pose. Terrapup's
(and apparently trailpup's) idle pose already holds its front paws lifted
and drawn toward the chest rather than planted — those paws sit far from
centre on the axis the roll does NOT touch, so the same rotation swings them
outward into the air instead of settling them. Burrowback and mudsnout,
whose idle poses keep all four paws down and close to centre, roll cleanly
(`shots/comparison-burrowback-ok-front.png`). This is a body-plan/pose
mismatch in the shared roll math, not a per-model geometry bug.

## What this pass changed

1. **`data/creatures/species.json`** — terrapup gets `rest_roll_deg: 0.0`
   (the same escape hatch galecrest's entry already uses for a different
   reason), reverting it to the `play_faint()` crouch. That pose is
   confirmed contained in the current rim (above) and is no longer the
   broken back-flip; it reads as sitting rather than lying flat, which is a
   real but smaller gap than a creature flipped onto its back.
2. **`tools/_capture_creature_bed_rest.gd`** — now calls `play_rest()`
   instead of `play_faint()`, matching `_sync_rest_body()`'s real call path,
   so this tool's future evidence reflects what ships.
3. **`tools/_capture_bed_topdown.gd`** (new) — top-down bed-fit verification
   tool, kept for reuse by any future bed-fit lane.

No bed/rim/pad dimension changed. Per `ralph/OWNER_DIRECTIVES_2026-09-01.md`
(grow the bed rather than shrink the creature), that preference was checked
first and found moot: the creature already fits: the defect was pose, not
size, and the fix already available (`rest_roll_deg`) does not touch scale
in either direction.

## What this pass did NOT fix

trailpup and bramblebun (at least) still show the same back-flip / tangle in
real gameplay. Fixing the shared roll math for every body plan is a
multi-species animation-pose investigation, not a bite-sized, single-defect
fix, and is out of scope for census defect 113 (which named only the one
species it screenshotted). Left as a follow-up rather than silently folded
in or silently dropped:

**`BACKLOG-VISUAL-BED-REST-ROLL-FLIP`** — `play_rest()`'s 90-degree roll
flips at least trailpup and bramblebun onto their backs/into tangles instead
of settling them on their side, because their idle poses hold paws away
from the roll axis's centreline (same root cause as terrapup, above,
unfixed for these two). Needs either a per-species `rest_roll_deg` survey
across the full roster (mirroring OWNER-0901-CREATURE-BED-POSE's original
species-by-species tuning pass) or a smarter roll that grounds itself
against the model's actual bind-pose paw positions rather than a single
scalar constant. Not bite-sized as a whole roster pass; a same-shaped
single-species fix (like this one) is bite-sized per species.
