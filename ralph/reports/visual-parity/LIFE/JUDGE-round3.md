# VP9 "World Life" — Round 3 Judgment

Blind review. Compared round3 frames against round2 frames and against
`docs/reference/tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`, `docs/reference/palworld-0*.jpg`,
and `site/img/page-board.jpg`. All counts are strict, native-size, no zoom: a
creature only counts if a body, head, and legs are actually legible in the
frame as delivered.

Round 2 verdict (given to me as the starting point for comparison, not as
something to agree with): pairing frame had no starter at all; groups only
legible when zoomed 3-4x; night functionally empty; mill-pond creature was a
shapeless glowing blob; legible pairs shared identical poses.

---

## Per-frame

### 01 — village-edge-day
**Changed:** A new, severe problem: the camera is now placed against/inside
a creature's head and ear geometry. Two huge out-of-focus brown ear/head
shapes fill roughly 70% of the frame (left and right foreground), and
between them, small and mostly cropped, is one creature's face (rounded
ears, pale snout) peeking through the gap.
**Better/worse:** Worse than round2. Round2's version of this frame was a
clean, readable meadow-and-hill shot with one small unclear white blob;
round3 replaces that with unreadable near-camera geometry that occludes the
mountain, the grove, and almost the whole ground plane.
**Native-size legible animals:** 0. A face is visible but no body or legs —
does not meet the bar.

### 01 — village-edge-night
Same occlusion bug, at night. The two foreground shapes read as pure black
silhouettes; a pale muzzle is barely visible between them. Sky/mountain
silhouette is otherwise unchanged from round2.
**Legible animals:** 0.

### 02 — mill-pond-banks-day
**Changed:** Two new turtle-type creatures now sit at the shoreline —
domed shell, head, and stubby legs are all legible without zooming, and they
read immediately as animals. The blue "blob" creature from round2 is still
present, directly beside them, unchanged: a spiky/glowing cluster with no
discernible head, body outline, or legs. The same camera-occlusion problem
as frame 01 is present here too — a large brown creature shape (ear/rock)
blocks roughly the right third of the frame.
**Better/worse:** Genuinely better. This is the one frame where round3's
"add more creatures" work actually produced a legible result.
**Legible animals:** 2 (the turtles). The blob does not count — see (D)
below, it is not fixed.

### 02 — mill-pond-banks-night
Turtles remain faintly identifiable at night (shell silhouette + head
against the moonlit water) — degraded but still just legible. The blob is
present as a dim shapeless mass. The same right-side occlusion silhouette
blocks part of the frame, now solid black. Moon reflection on the water is
a nice touch and matches the keyart's day/night mood note.
**Legible animals:** ~2, marginal (readable only because I already know
their day-frame shape).

### 03 — band1-open-meadow-day
**Changed:** The camera-occlusion bug is now catastrophic here: an extreme
close-up of a snout/nose fills over half the frame in blurred, illegible
detail (looks like a leather or rock texture at this range, not
recognizable as a creature part unless you already expect one). The
meadow visible past it is completely empty — no creatures anywhere in the
clear portion of the frame.
**Better/worse:** Worse than round2, which at least showed a full, if
sparse and creature-free, meadow. This is the frame the "open meadow" band
exists to sell, and it currently shows nothing but an unreadable brown mass.
**Legible animals:** 0.

### 04 — relay-camp-day
**Changed:** This is the strongest frame in the set and the clearest sign
of real progress. In the clearing between the trees: one wolf-type creature
standing center frame (body, legs, tail, pointed ears all read at a glance)
and two small antlered deer-type creatures in front of it (body, legs,
antlers legible), plus a fourth pale creature partially occluded by a tree
trunk on the far left (body visible, less certain on legs).
**Better/worse:** Clearly better than round2, which had only two small
hedgehog-shapes in the same spot.
**Legible animals:** 3 confidently (wolf + 2 deer-types), a 4th partial.
This is the frame that comes closest to satisfying "a living group legible
at native size."

### 04 — relay-camp-night
Almost entirely black canopy silhouette. A small pale/blue glowing cluster
is visible low-center-left — plausibly the same creatures as the day
version, but at this size and darkness it reads as an indistinct glow, not
as an animal with a body, head, and legs. No meaningful change from round2's
equivalent frame (also a small glow cluster in roughly the same spot).
**Legible animals:** 0.

### 05 — ridge-camp-day
**Changed:** The worst occlusion failure in the set. The frame is entirely
filled, edge to edge, with blurred brown/black creature-surface noise —
no sky, no ground, no silhouette of anything recognizable as an animal,
just texture at point-blank camera range.
**Better/worse:** A total regression. Round2's version of this frame was
arguably the best frame in that round — two clearly legible badger-type
creatures sitting in a full, well-lit meadow with hills and trees. Round3
has replaced a working shot with an unusable one.
**Legible animals:** 0.

### 06 — starter-beside-trainer-day (the pairing frame)
**Changed:** A starter creature is now present — round2 had none. It is a
fox/red-panda-type creature, fully modeled and legible (ears, snout, paws,
tail, a visible harness/pack). It occupies the center-right of the frame,
large and close to camera. The trainer is visible only as a small,
partially cropped figure in the bottom-left corner.
**Better/worse:** Better in that a starter finally exists in this shot at
all — round2 failed this frame completely. But the composition does not
answer what the frame is supposed to prove: the creature and trainer are
not side by side, are not staged at comparable framing distance from the
camera, and are not both looking out at a shared vista the way the keyart
and page-board hero shot do. The trainer is barely "clearly in frame."
**Legible animals:** 1 (the starter). Trainer present but not usable as
the other half of a paired shot.

## Answers to the four required checks

**(A) Does the pairing frame now show trainer + starter side by side,
comparable scale, both clearly in frame?**
No. A starter is present for the first time, which is real progress over
round2's total absence — but it is not staged beside the trainer. The
creature dominates the frame at close range; the trainer is a small,
partially-cropped figure in the corner, not comparably scaled or framed.
Compare `site/img/page-board.jpg`'s hero shot or the keyart's day/night
pairing panels: trainer and creature stand shoulder-to-flank, both fully in
frame, both facing the same vista, at the same depth. This frame does not
reproduce that blocking.

**(B) Do any stands show a living group legible at native size?**
Yes, one: **04-relay-camp-day**. Three creatures (a wolf-type and two
antlered deer-types) are all identifiable as animals — body, head, legs —
without zooming, standing together in a clearing. This is a real pass on a
criterion round2 failed outright ("only legible when zoomed 3-4x"). No
other day frame reaches this bar; 02-mill-pond-banks-day comes close with
two turtles but reads as a pair, not a group scene.

**(C) Does night show creatures?**
Barely, and inconsistently. 02-mill-pond-banks-night shows the turtles as
faint but just-identifiable silhouettes — the one genuine night improvement.
01-village-edge-night shows only a sliver of muzzle. 04-relay-camp-night
shows an indistinct glow that cannot be read as an animal shape on its own.
Night is still functionally empty for 2 of the 3 night frames, and only
marginally populated in the third.

**(D) Is the mill-pond creature now a readable silhouette?**
No. The same blue spiky/glowing mass from round2 is still present in both
the day and night mill-pond frames, in the same spot, with no discernible
head/body/leg silhouette. It sits directly next to two new, well-modeled
turtle creatures, which makes the contrast worse, not better: the fix this
round added new legible creatures beside the broken one instead of fixing
the broken one.

## Three biggest remaining gaps, ranked

1. **A new camera/creature-occlusion bug now breaks 4 of 9 frames
   outright** (01-day, 01-night, 03-day, 05-day). The evidence-stand camera
   is being placed against or inside a creature's head/ear/snout geometry,
   filling most or all of the frame with blurred, out-of-focus surface
   texture and hiding the scene the stand exists to show. This is worse
   than round2's sparseness: round2's 03 and 05 frames were readable, empty
   meadows; round3's are unreadable regardless of what's in them. This is
   the single largest regression in the round and it costs the survey
   nearly half its frames.

2. **Night is still overwhelmingly empty**, exactly the round2 finding,
   only narrowly improved. Of three night frames, only mill-pond-night
   shows creatures at all, and only as faint, borderline-legible
   silhouettes. Village-edge-night and relay-camp-night show essentially
   nothing recognizable as an animal. The keyart's own night panel
   (bottom-right of `tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`) shows a lit
   campfire/village glow and full silhouette clarity on the trainer and
   creature at night; these frames do not reach that even where the scene
   itself (moon, water) is handled well.

3. **The pairing frame still doesn't deliver the shot it's named for.** A
   starter now exists, which fixes round2's total failure — but the
   creature and trainer are not composed together. The trainer is a small,
   cropped figure far from camera while the creature fills the frame at
   close range; there's no shared vista, no comparable scale check
   possible, and no side-by-side blocking. This is the frame every other
   reference image in this program treats as central (keyart's two pairing
   panels, page-board's hero image), so getting the population right
   without getting the staging right doesn't close it.

(Runner-up, not in the top three because it affects one frame rather than
several: the mill-pond blob creature is unchanged and still shapeless — see
(D).)

## The two bar questions

**A. Do these frames read as belonging to the world in the keyart?**
**No**, on balance. Where the frames are actually readable — 02-day's
pond/shoreline, 04-day's grove clearing, the sky and hills in 06 — the
palette, foliage density, and lighting genuinely match the keyart's warm,
vibrant, natural-palette mood, and that part of the work should be credited.
But four of nine frames are not "the keyart's world rendered worse," they
are camera-occlusion noise with no world visible at all, and a keyart panel
never fails to show its scene. A survey where nearly half the stands show
blurred creature-surface texture instead of the Meadows cannot be said to
read as that world.

**B. Shown these frames beside the Palworld screenshots, would someone say
these are trying to be the same kind of game?**
**No.** The Palworld references show multiple pals in nearly every frame,
in combat, riding, or working, always legible, always central to the
composition, with UI confirming their role. In this round, creatures appear
clearly in only two of nine stands (02, 04) and the pairing shot that is
supposed to make the trainer-creature bond legible fails its own
composition. Someone looking at both sets side by side would see a game
that is mostly empty grassland with occasional wildlife, not a
creature-companion game — even though frame 04 shows, for the first time,
what that game is supposed to look like at a glance.

## Fixable by scene vs. needs new art

**Fixable by scene / camera / spawn logic (this is where the next round
should spend its effort):**
- The camera-occlusion bug in 01, 03, 05 — this reads as a spawn-point or
  camera-rig placement bug (creature spawning on top of / at the literal
  camera position) rather than an art problem, since the same creature
  types render cleanly and legibly in 02 and 04. Moving spawn points back
  from the stand cameras, or excluding a minimum radius around each
  evidence-stand camera from creature spawn placement, should fix this
  outright.
- The pairing frame's composition — the asset exists and looks good; it
  needs to be staged beside the trainer at comparable camera distance,
  facing the same vista, the way the keyart and page-board hero shots do.
  This is a camera/placement fix, not a new asset.
- Night visibility in village-edge and relay-camp — likely a lighting/fill
  or moonlit rim-light gap rather than missing content, since mill-pond's
  night frame shows the same creature type can read at night when it's near
  the water's reflected light. Worth checking whether creatures at those
  two stands get any night-time rim/fill light at all.
- Frame 03's total absence of creatures in its unoccluded portion — once
  the occlusion bug is fixed, verify this stand actually has a populated
  scatter; round2 also showed nothing meaningful here, so this may be an
  underlying density gap independent of the camera bug.

**Needs new art / cannot be fixed by scene alone:**
- The mill-pond "blob" creature is not a modeled creature with a
  silhouette — it appears to be a particle/glow effect with no legible
  head, body, or leg shapes at any distance across two rounds now. Scene
  and lighting changes will not fix this; it needs an actual creature body
  (even reusing an installed mesh per the project's asset rules) rather
  than a glow shader standing in for one.
