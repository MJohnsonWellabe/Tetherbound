# Visual Parity Judge — LIFE round 5

Blind review. No code, diffs, or reports were read. Only: `.claude/skills/visual-judge/SKILL.md`, `docs/reference/tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`, `docs/reference/palworld-0*.jpg`, `site/img/page-board.jpg`, and the round 5 frames (native size, individually and via `_sheet.png`). Round 4 frames were also opened, only to describe what changed — not to accept round 5 on the strength of the delta.

Round 4's starting verdict, for reference: 5 of 9 frames had a creature body pressed against the lens; ridge camp was unreadable; the pairing frame had no trainer in it; relay camp day was the one strong group; the mill-pond blob was fixed.

## Per-frame

### 00-village-life-day.png (new this round)
Five half-timbered houses with tile roofs, well-lit, good silhouette variety, foreground dead tree for depth. But this is meant to be the **village-life** frame and at native size it reads as an empty film set. There is exactly **one** villager in the whole frame: a small brown figure standing near a woodpile/well at roughly screen-center-left. At native size, glanced rather than hunted for, it does not parse as a person — it is a same-toned blob against a same-toned dirt patch, no separation in silhouette or color from the woodpile beside it. Only on a 3x crop does it resolve into a standing NPC with a cap. Zero other figures anywhere in the frame — the doorway/window details I initially flagged as a second figure are just window shutters. **Living bodies legible at native size: 0.** The houses are good architecture; the village they compose reads as abandoned.

### 01-village-edge-day.png
Trainer + one NPC (cap, tan/olive outfit, backpack) standing together in a meadow with a rock and a mossy hill behind. Both fully legible at native size — heads, torsos, limbs all read, good silhouette separation against the grass and rock. No creatures in this frame. This is the strongest **people** group in the set. Two living bodies, clean.

### 01-village-edge-night.png
Same composition at night. Both figures keep enough rim/fill light to stay legible as bodies (head, torso, limbs), though detail drops to silhouette. Readable, not strong. Two living bodies at reduced fidelity.

### 02-mill-pond-banks-day.png
Trainer at a fence rail overlooking a pond, with one turtle-shaped creature standing in the open beside the fence — fully visible, head/shell/legs all clear, no occlusion, good scale relationship (turtle roughly knee-to-thigh height on the trainer, plausible). This is a clean two-body scene: 1 person + 1 creature, both fully legible. The earlier "blob" problem stays fixed. It is one animal, not a group, but it is honestly composed.

### 02-mill-pond-banks-night.png
Same shot at night. The trainer is now a near-silhouette and the turtle is a dark rounded mass that could be a rock if you didn't already know it was a turtle from the day shot. Body/legs are not distinguishable on the turtle at native size. Weakest of the three night frames for creature legibility.

### 03-band1-open-meadow-day.png
Trainer alone in a flower meadow. No creatures, no people. Not a life/group frame — a traversal shot. Fine as a landscape beat, contributes nothing to VP9.

### 04-relay-camp-day.png
Biggest change from round 4 (where the trainer was absent from view and only a wolf + a pale creature were visible in deep woods). Now: trainer present at center, a small pale sheep-like creature next to them (legible at native size, plausible companion scale), a wolf standing clearly in the open at left-midground (legible: head, body, legs, tail), a second wolf directly behind it whose body is cut by a large tree trunk — only its head clears the trunk — and a fourth, indistinct grey shape far in the background that does not resolve into anything at native size. So: 1 person + roughly 2.5 legible creatures + 1 ambiguous smudge. Better than round 4, but this is a forest with wildlife wandering through it, not a "camp" — no tents, packs, fire, or any built camp element anywhere in frame. If the stand's job is "relay camp," nothing here says camp.

### 04-relay-camp-night.png
Near-total black. The trainer is a rim-lit silhouette with a faint backpack highlight; nothing else in the frame resolves — no creatures, no trees except black cutouts against a barely-lighter sky. This is the least legible frame in the set. Whatever creatures were in the day version are not visible at night here.

### 05-ridge-camp-day.png
The clearest win in the set. Round 4's version of this frame was an unreadable close-range texture smear (camera inside/against geometry). Round 5: trainer + two bird/wyvern-type creatures standing beside them in an open hillside clearing, full sky and terrain visible, no lens-occlusion. All three bodies read cleanly at native size — heads, wings, legs, tails all distinguishable, and the two creatures are consistent with each other in scale and design. There is also a flat black wedge-shaped prop in the background (presumably a tent or shed) that has no texture or lighting and reads as an unfinished placeholder rather than a camp structure, but it doesn't hurt legibility of the living bodies. This is the one frame in round 5 that fully delivers "legible living group in an actual scene."

### 06-starter-beside-trainer-day.png (pairing frame)
Round 4's version had no trainer at all — just a creature's fur filling the lens. Round 5 fixes presence: the trainer is now in frame. But the composition fails the actual ask (trainer + starter side by side, comparable scale, both facing the vista, per the keyart/page-board hero pairing). Instead: a heroic low-angle shot with the creature in the near-foreground, roughly 2.5–3x the trainer's apparent screen height, positioned above and ahead of the trainer on a slope, both angled away from each other rather than standing shoulder-to-shoulder facing a vista. The trainer is pushed to the bottom-left, small, feet near the frame edge. Both bodies are individually legible (this is not an occlusion problem), but this is a "creature dwarfs trainer" hero shot, not the pairing shot the reference composition calls for.

## (A) Pairing frame — does it deliver trainer + starter side by side, comparable scale, both fully in frame?

**No.** Presence is fixed (round 4 had no trainer), but scale and composition are wrong in a way that is just as disqualifying for the intended reference match. In the keyart and in page-board's hero image, trainer and companion stand together at conversational distance, roughly matched in visual weight (the companion is chest/hip height on the trainer, not several times taller), both oriented outward toward the landscape. 06-starter-beside-trainer-day instead stages the creature as a looming hero subject and the trainer as a small foreground afterthought on a different part of the slope. This reads as a creature-reveal shot, not the pairing shot.

## (B) Which stands show a legible living group in an actual scene, no lens-occlusion?

- **05-ridge-camp-day** — yes, cleanly. Trainer + 2 creatures, full scene visible, no occlusion. The best frame in the set.
- **01-village-edge-day** — yes, for people (trainer + NPC), no creatures present.
- **02-mill-pond-banks-day** — yes, but it is one creature, not a group.
- **04-relay-camp-day** — partial. More bodies present than round 4, but one creature is cut by a tree trunk and one distant shape doesn't resolve, so "group" is generous; "legible" is only true for 2 of the ~4 shapes in frame.
- **00-village-life-day**, **03-band1-open-meadow-day**, **06-starter-beside-trainer-day** — no living group reads here (village-life has one illegible NPC; open-meadow has nobody but the trainer; the pairing frame is two bodies but not a "group" composition and fails its own brief).

## (C) Night

Three night frames, and none of them clear the bar the day frames set:
- **01-village-edge-night** is the best of the three — both bodies keep enough light to stay legible as people.
- **02-mill-pond-banks-night** — trainer is a silhouette, turtle degrades to an unreadable dark mass.
- **04-relay-camp-night** — effectively a black frame with one rim-lit human silhouette; nothing else in it can be judged at all.

Night in this build is currently "the world goes dark and mostly disappears," not "the world has a different, still-readable mood," which the keyart's own night pairing panel (trainer + fox creature, both clearly lit and legible under a full moon and firelight in the distance) explicitly shows is achievable within this same art direction.

## (D) Village-life frame — villagers legible, village reads inhabited?

**No.** One NPC in the entire frame, and at native/glance size it does not read as a person — same value and hue as the dirt/woodpile behind it, no silhouette separation, no other villagers doing anything to suggest activity. The architecture is good (roofline variety, materials, believable footprint) but a village of five houses and one static, illegible figure reads as staged and unoccupied, not lived-in.

## Ranked: the three biggest remaining gaps

1. **The pairing frame stages a creature reveal, not a pairing.** 05-ridge-camp-day proves the engine can put trainer and multiple creatures in one legible frame together at sane relative scale. 06-starter-beside-trainer-day, the one frame explicitly asked to match the keyart/page-board hero composition, instead triples the creature's apparent size over the trainer and separates them into foreground/background rather than side-by-side. This is a scene-composition and camera-placement problem, fully fixable without new assets — reposition both actors at comparable ground distance from camera, similar apparent height, facing the same direction toward open terrain.

2. **The village has architecture but no population.** 00-village-life-day is buildings only; one villager exists in the frame and is not legible at native size. Compare to `palworld-05-base-building.jpg` or the keyart's starting-settlement panel, both of which read as occupied by virtue of multiple humanoid figures with clear color contrast against their surroundings. This needs more NPCs placed with deliberate silhouette contrast (not camouflaged against dirt/wood), likely 3+ in frame, and ideally doing visibly different things. Scene-fixable — no new art required if the single NPC asset already used here can simply be placed multiple times with contrasting outfit tones.

3. **Night collapses legibility across the board.** All three night frames lose bodies to near-total blackness, worst in 04-relay-camp-night where nothing but a trainer rim-light survives. The keyart's own night reference panel proves the intended mood keeps both figures clearly lit under moon/firelight — this is a lighting-setup gap (fill light on the player-adjacent volume, or brighter ambient/moon term at night), scene-fixable, not an asset gap.

Runner-up, worth naming because it undoes real round-4 progress on a different axis: 04-relay-camp-day still has one creature body significantly cut by foreground tree geometry (the second wolf, reduced to a head over a trunk). Not as bad as round 4's literal lens-pressed fur, but the same underlying issue — a creature placed where world geometry occludes most of its body — has recurred in a new location.

## Two bar questions

**A. Do these frames read as belonging to the world in `tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`?**
**Partial — call it no as a set, yes for isolated frames.** 05-ridge-camp-day, 01-village-edge-day/night, and 02-mill-pond-banks-day individually hold the palette, the rolling-hills/oak/wildflower silhouette language, and a plausible sense of place — those could sit next to the keyart's grove or meadow panels without embarrassment. But the set as a whole does not, because two of the frames explicitly built to match named keyart panels — the village panel and the hero pairing panel — are the two that miss it: the village has the architecture but none of the keyart's implied population, and the pairing frame abandons the keyart's calm side-by-side companionship for a mismatched-scale hero shot the keyart doesn't do anywhere.

**B. Shown beside `palworld-0*.jpg`, would someone say this is trying to be the same kind of game?**
**No.** Two separate reasons, one fixable, one not from this seat:
- *Fixable:* Palworld's screenshots are almost never without multiple creatures and/or people in frame doing something (fighting, based-building, traveling together in a pack). Half of this set (00, 03, 04-night, 06) has zero or one legible living thing in it. Density of life-in-frame is a scene/placement problem.
- *Not scene-fixable, needs art:* even in the frames where creatures are present and legible (the turtle, the wolves, the birds, the big furry starter), the character and creature rendering itself sits well below Palworld's shipping bar the rubric names as the target — flatter shading, visibly faceted/low-poly silhouette edges on the trainer (hair, sleeve, boot edges all show hard polygon breaks at native size), and a mix of creature styles that don't yet cohere into one bespoke creature-art language the way Palworld's roster does. That gap is asset quality, not composition, and no amount of re-blocking these scenes closes it.

## Fixable vs. needs-new-art summary

**Fixable by changing the scene (composition, placement, lighting, density) — no new assets:**
- Pairing frame scale/positioning (put trainer and starter at matched screen height, comparable distance, same facing).
- Village-life population density and NPC placement/contrast (more bodies, better color separation from environment).
- Night ambient/fill lighting so bodies stay legible after dark (all three night frames).
- Relay-camp tree occlusion on the second wolf (move creature or thin the foreground trunk line).
- Naming/set-dressing mismatch: "relay camp" and "ridge camp" currently contain no camp geodesy (tents, fire, packs) — if that's meant to be visible in these stands, it's missing regardless of the creatures being fine.

**Needs new art (not achievable by re-blocking this build):**
- Character/creature rendering fidelity gap versus Palworld's shipping quality (shading model, silhouette cleanliness, cohesive creature-art language) — this is the aspect the rubric says must not be excused as placeholder, and it is the more serious of the two bar failures.
