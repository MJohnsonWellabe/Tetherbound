# VP9 "World Life" — Round 6 Judge Report

Blind review. No code, diffs, or reports were read — only the rubric, `docs/reference/` (keyart, page-board, five Palworld shots), and the round 5 / round 6 frames themselves, at native 960x540 and, where a shape was ambiguous at native size, in cropped/zoomed detail (noted per frame — the zoom is used only to determine *what* a shape is, not to grade legibility, which is judged at native size as instructed).

Frames reviewed: `round6/00` through `round6/06` (10 files, `-DIAG` ignored), each against its `round5` counterpart.

---

## Per-frame findings

### 00 — village-life-day
**R5→R6:** Camera reworked entirely. R5 showed a wide row of houses with a single figure so colour-blended into the lawn it barely registered as a person. R6 is a closer shot into a house's front yard/garden beds with the trainer approaching two NPCs: a white-haired elder standing in clear daylight (fully legible, no zoom needed) and a second, apron-wearing villager standing inside a stone archway (legible but noticeably dimmer/shadowed — reads as a person, not as clearly as the elder).
**Bodies reading at native size:** 3 (trainer + 2 villagers). Better — this is the strongest population gain in the set, up from effectively 1 (barely).
**Verdict:** Better. The yard finally reads as occupied rather than empty-with-buildings.

### 01 — village-edge-day
**R5→R6:** Unchanged. Same framing, same two figures (trainer + brown-clad villager beside the boulder), both legible at native size.
**Verdict:** No change — was already working, still working.

### 01 — village-edge-night
**R5→R6:** People unchanged (both legible). New in R6: a small pale antlered creature standing in the mid-ground near the fence, absent from R5's frame (confirmed by side-by-side crop — R5 has bare grass and fence in that spot). At native size the creature is a soft light-coloured smudge against dark grass; only on close crop does it resolve into a specific animal.
**Bodies reading at native size:** 2 confidently (trainer, villager) + 1 marginal (creature, present but not confidently identifiable without zooming in).
**Verdict:** Better, but marginal — a creature was added to a night stand that previously had none, which is real progress, but it does not yet "read" the way VP9 asks.

### 02 — mill-pond-banks-day
**R5→R6:** Unchanged. Trainer + turtle-shelled creature at the fence rail, both clearly legible.
**Verdict:** No change — already good.

### 02 — mill-pond-banks-night
**R5→R6:** Unchanged. This is the one night stand where a creature reads cleanly at native size: the turtle creature's shell pattern picks up the moon/water light and stays legible in silhouette next to the trainer.
**Verdict:** No change — remains the model for how night creature-legibility should look elsewhere.

### 03 — band1-open-meadow-day
**R5→R6:** Camera nudged slightly (player a little larger in frame, hill line and tree placement shifted) but the composition is otherwise the same, and — this is the important part — the same pale cluster that reads as flowers/shrubbery sits in the same screen position in both rounds. Zooming into it in R6 reveals it actually is a group of 2-3 antlered creatures (same species as the relay-camp herd), but that is only visible on a tight crop; at native frame size in both R5 and R6 it reads as a flowering shrub, indistinguishable from the actual flower clumps elsewhere in the same frame.
**Bodies reading at native size:** 1 (trainer only). Zero creatures confirmed as creatures without zooming.
**Verdict:** Unchanged. Round 5's "open meadow had no creature" finding still stands in practice — there is geometry there, but it is not staged to read as fauna.

### 04 — relay-camp-day
**R5→R6:** Camera reworked, moving from a shaded grove interior into a brighter clearing at the tree line. R5's single wolf-like creature was mostly hidden behind a tree trunk with only its head and a faded second body visible. R6 shows 2-3 antlered white/tan creatures clustered together in direct sun, fully unoccluded, and legible at native size without any zoom.
**Bodies reading at native size:** 4 (trainer + 2-3 creatures, tightly clustered so an exact count is a little fuzzy, but clearly a *group*, clearly animal, clearly not flowers).
**Verdict:** Better, and by a wide margin — this is the frame that most directly answers "VP9 world life": a legible living group, not just a legible individual.

### 04 — relay-camp-night
**R5→R6:** R5 was functionally a black frame: pitch-dark tree silhouettes, moon, trainer, nothing else readable. R6 adds a lit campfire with visible camp furniture (crates/tent shapes) and, close to the fire, dim animal-shaped silhouettes — a deer/horse-like shape at the frame edge and a curled pale shape near the flame. None of this resolves at native size; it required a crop to confirm the shapes are creatures rather than more camp clutter.
**Bodies reading at native size:** 1 confidently (trainer). Camp structures now read (a genuine atmosphere win). Creature presence: not legible without zooming.
**Verdict:** Better as a *place* (it now looks like an inhabited camp at night rather than a void), but the "relay camp reads at night" ask specifically about a *creature* is still not met — the fire lights the crates, not the animals near it.

### 05 — ridge-camp-day
**R5→R6:** The two blue-plumed bird/dragon creatures are unchanged and remain the clearest, most confident creature read in the whole set — distinct silhouette, wings spread, strong colour contrast against the grass. New in R6: a third, dark furry quadruped near the tree at centre-left, partly obscured by a shrub/tall grass, discernible as an animal but not as crisply as the birds.
**Bodies reading at native size:** 4 (trainer + 2 birds confidently, + 1 furry creature at reduced confidence due to partial occlusion).
**Verdict:** Better — already the strongest frame in R5, now stronger still, though the new addition is staged behind foliage rather than in the open like the birds.

### 06 — starter-beside-trainer-day (pairing frame)
**R5→R6:** This is the largest compositional change in the round. R5 staged the creature as a low-angle hero reveal: camera looking up a hillside, creature filling the upper two-thirds of frame at roughly 2.5-3x the trainer's apparent height, trainer small in a bottom corner — not a pairing shot, a reveal shot. R6 replaces this with a level, over-the-shoulder walking shot: trainer and creature side by side, both moving in the same direction toward the landscape, both fully inside the frame, camera behind and slightly above — directly echoing the keyart's and page-board's "trainer + starter, backs to camera, facing the vista" composition. Scale is closer but not equal: measuring head/shoulder height, the creature is now roughly 1.3-1.5x the trainer's height rather than 2.5-3x — a large improvement, but the creature is still visibly bigger than "comparable," and it is a bulky quadruped where the keyart/page-board pairing shows a slim fox-sized creature roughly half the trainer's height.
**Verdict:** Better, substantially — the composition problem (side-by-side, facing the vista, both in frame) is solved. The scale problem is reduced but not eliminated.

## (A) Pairing frame — side by side, comparable scale, both fully in frame, facing the vista?

**Side by side:** yes. **Both fully in frame:** yes, neither is cropped. **Facing the vista:** yes, both face and move toward the landscape, camera positioned behind them exactly as in the keyart/page-board reference. **Comparable scale:** improved but not achieved — down from ~2.5-3x to ~1.3-1.5x trainer height. Three of four sub-asks are now met; the scale gap is smaller but still visible in a still frame.

## (B) Village-life — how many villagers read, does the village read inhabited?

Two villagers read at native size: an elder in clear daylight (fully legible) and a second apron-wearing figure standing in a shadowed archway (legible but dim). Together with the trainer that is three bodies in one yard — the strongest population count in the set, up from essentially zero in round 5. The village now reads as *occupied*. It does not yet read as *busy* — both NPCs are static, one is backlit into near-silhouette, and there is no second location (street, well, market) showing more people at once, so "inhabited by a couple of people" is fair; "a living settlement" is not yet.

## (C) Night — which night stands show a creature, and does relay-camp-night now read?

Three night stands exist: village-edge-night, mill-pond-night, relay-camp-night.
- **Mill-pond-night**: yes, cleanly — the turtle creature's shell reads clearly against the moonlit water. This is the only night stand where a creature is legible at native size without zooming.
- **Village-edge-night**: a creature is present (new since round 5) but only marginally legible — a pale smudge that resolves into an animal on close inspection, not at a glance.
- **Relay-camp-night**: no. The frame gained real value — a lit campfire and camp structures where round 5 had only blackness — but the creature shapes near the fire do not rise above the ambient darkness enough to read at native size. **Relay-camp-night does not yet read as having a creature**, though it now at least reads as an inhabited camp.

Net: 1 of 3 night stands shows a creature cleanly, 1 shows one marginally, 1 shows none. Night creature-legibility remains the weakest dimension of VP9.

## (D) Open meadow — creatures visible?

No, not at native size. A creature group is present in the scene (confirmed only by cropping) but is staged to look indistinguishable from a flowering shrub — the same shape occupies the same screen position in round 5 and round 6, so nothing about the staging changed even though the surrounding camera angle did. This is the one evidence stand that reads exactly as empty of fauna as it did in round 5.

## Ranked remaining gaps

1. **Open meadow reads as empty of life.** The band1-open-meadow-day stand — the frame most directly comparable to Palworld's open-field shots (`palworld-02`, `palworld-03`), where pals are visible mid-field at a glance — still shows nothing recognizable as an animal at native size. There is a creature group physically present, but it is camouflaged as flowers rather than staged as fauna. This is the single stand that most undercuts "world life" because it is the emptiest, most Palworld-comparable shot in the set.
2. **Night creature-legibility is inconsistent and mostly still absent.** Only the mill-pond turtle clears the bar; village-edge's creature is a coin-flip at native size, and relay-camp's creatures — despite the frame otherwise being fixed (fire, camp structures) — still don't read. Keyart's own night panel (bottom-right of the direction board) shows the trainer-and-creature pairing clearly lit and legible at night; round 6's night stands mostly do not achieve that for anything but the trainer.
3. **Village population is thin and half of it is backlit.** Two static NPCs in one yard is a real improvement over round 5's one camouflaged figure, but one of the two (the apron-wearing figure in the archway) is dim enough that it is the weakest-reading person in the whole survey. There's no second population cluster and no activity (walking, working, gathering) to make it feel lived-in rather than posed.

(Runner-up, not in the top three because the fix already mostly landed: the pairing frame's residual ~1.3-1.5x scale disparity between trainer and starter, versus the keyart/page-board's roughly-half-height starter.)

## Bar questions

### A. Do these frames read as belonging to the world in `tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`?

**No**, on balance — with a clear split by time of day. The day frames (7 of 10) now genuinely carry the keyart's identity: rolling hills, oak groves, a lived-in stone-and-timber settlement, and — critically — frame 06 now reproduces the keyart's own signature composition (trainer and starter, backs to camera, facing the vista together) almost shot-for-shot. If the survey were day-only, this would be a yes. But three of ten stands are night, and the keyart's own night panel is warm and inviting — a lit village glowing across a dark valley, the trainer-and-creature pair still clearly visible by moonlight. Round 6's night stands are, with one exception (mill-pond), just dark: a black or near-black frame with a moon and little else that reads. Relay-camp-night in particular is closer to a silhouette test than to the cozy-but-mysterious night the keyart depicts. Since VP9 explicitly asks for "day and night," and night is the part of the target this round does not deliver, the honest answer for the survey as a whole is no — carried by the day frames, sunk by the night ones.

### B. Shown these frames beside `palworld-0*.jpg`, would someone say these are trying to be the same kind of game?

**No.** The improvements this round move in the right direction — relay-camp-day's creature cluster and ridge-camp's three-creature grouping are the first frames in this program that show *multiple* creatures at once the way every single Palworld reference shot does — but the gap in density and presentation is still wide. Palworld's shots each contain several pals simultaneously, often mid-action (combat FX, a mount being ridden, a pal working a base task) with UI reinforcing that a systemic creature-management game is happening. Round 6's best frames show one clustered group of creatures standing still; its worst frame (open meadow) shows none at all, in a setting where Palworld's equivalent shot is built specifically to show pals roaming the open field. The character/creature model fidelity gap (blocky low-poly, flat toon shading vs Palworld's higher-detail PBR anime style) also remains visible and unaddressed by this round's changes.

**Fixable by changing the scene** (density, staging, lighting — no new art required): open meadow's creature legibility (scale it up, move it out of the grass clump, or give it a distinct silhouette pose); night lighting on creatures at village-edge and relay-camp (the mill-pond turtle proves the engine can do this — apply the same rim/fill treatment elsewhere); adding a second populated village cluster or activity poses; closing the remaining pairing-frame scale gap; increasing per-stand creature counts generally to approach Palworld's density.

**Not fixable by scene changes** (needs art/asset/pipeline work): the blocky low-poly model fidelity and flat toon shading versus Palworld's more detailed, more painterly creature and character models; the total absence of any combat/action framing (every Palworld reference is an active moment — a fight, a mount, a base task — while every round-6 frame is a static "standing near" shot); these are model-detail and animation/staging-system gaps, not camera or lighting gaps.
