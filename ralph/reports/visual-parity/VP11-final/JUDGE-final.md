# VP11-final — Blind Visual Judgment

Judged against `docs/reference/tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png` (art direction board) and `docs/reference/palworld-0*.jpg` (the owner's shipping-quality bar), plus `site/img/page-board.jpg` as a secondary target. No other repo files were read. `combat/` and `buildings/` do not exist under `VP11-final/` and were skipped — no combat encounter or player-built structure frames were available to judge.

All frames in `survey/`, `ground/`, and `locations/` were opened individually at least once, plus the contact sheet `_sheet_before_after.png` (its AFTER column judged as current state; BEFORE used only for orientation, not scored).

---

## 1. Per-area reads

**Sky / light.** The sky model is the strongest single element in this build — soft painted cloud banks, a believable warm-to-blue gradient at golden hour (`ground-01-band1-opening-golden.png`, `locations/10-stronghold-gate-day.png`), and a genuinely moody night sky with a large soft moon and visible star/cloud texture (`locations/08-ridge-camp-standing-night.png`, `locations/10-stronghold-gate-night.png`). Day, golden and night all read as clearly different times at a glance. Weather variants are the exception: `ground-02-band2-stone-root-rain.png`, `-fog.png`, and `-cloudy.png` are nearly indistinguishable from `-golden.png` and `-day.png` — no visible rain streaks, no fog volume eating the horizon, just a slightly flatter sky tint. That's a miss against the rubric's own "horizon and depth" and "lighting" criteria. Score: **7/10**.

**Ground / vegetation.** Grass and flower scatter is dense and covers the ground convincingly at player height (`ground-01`, `ground-04`, `water-02-river-grazing.png`) — this is the area closest to the Palworld ground-density bar. Trees, however, read as simple flat "puffball" canopy blobs with little internal branch/leaf structure when seen at any distance (`survey/02-valley-floor.png`, `locations/06-relay-approach-day.png`, `locations/10-stronghold-approach-day.png`) — Palworld's trees keep visible leaf clustering and asymmetric silhouettes even from range; these read as a single rounded green mass repeated at a handful of scales. Up close the bark/leaf materials hold up reasonably (`07-mill-crossing-approach-day.png`). Score: **6/10**.

**Travel corridor (survey bands / ground bands 1–5).** The open valley reads with real distance — rolling hills, a legible skyline, hand-placed tree clusters rather than a uniform scatter (`survey/01-spawn-outward.png`, `survey/03-rise-overlook.png`). Band-to-band identity is weak, though: bands 1 through 5 (`ground-01` through `ground-05`) are visually almost the same meadow with the same tree/rock kit re-skinned by lighting alone — there's little sense of the terrain toughening or the biome changing as the player advances, which the world's own "increasingly demanding regions" premise implies it should. Score: **6/10**.

**Village + tournament.** The village is the best-dressed location in the set — Tudor-style half-timber building, stone base, warm materials, populated with named NPCs at a fair density (`01-village-approach-day.png`, `01-village-route-out-day.png`). It reads as a lived-in settlement rather than a stage set. The tournament ground undercuts this: the banners in `01-village-tournament-day.png` and `-night.png` are flat, hard-edged solid-color rectangles with no cloth material or fold — they read as unfinished placeholder geometry sitting inside an otherwise finished scene, in a way the relay camp's cloth pennant (see below) is not. Score: **7/10**.

**Camps (relay camp, ridge camp, waystop).** Waystop is excellent — campfire, bedroll and crate kit in the foreground with the stronghold castle and a Team Tether pylon visible on the skyline behind it (`09-waystop-standing-day.png`, `09-waystop-approach-day.png`); this is the one location that convincingly stitches "cozy camp" and "distant threat" into a single readable frame, which is exactly the brief's "cozy... with hints of mystery." Ridge camp is populated with wildlife and reads fine in daylight (`08-ridge-camp-standing-day.png`) but goes nearly solid black at night with the fire barely visible (`08-ridge-camp-fire-night.png`). Relay camp is the weak point of the whole set: half its frames are shot from inside foliage or behind a dark prop and are close to unusable as images (`05-relay-camp-fire-day.png`, `05-relay-camp-fire-night.png`, `05-relay-camp-standing-night.png`) — see Weakest Frames below. Score: **5/10**.

**Warrens exterior.** A single large boulder-pile mass with a dark stone doorway cut into it (`04-warrens-approach-day.png`). The silhouette is readable as "a hidden den in the rocks" from a distance but it is a fairly generic rock-pile shape with little to make it a specific, memorable landmark the way the castle or the relay pylons are. The interior den, by contrast, is good — warm stone-and-timber construction with the badger creature filling the frame at a believable scale (`04-warrens-den-day.png`). Score: **6/10**.

**Relay (Team Tether apparatus).** This is the clearest villain-palette location and the best evidence of "authored, not generic": dark oxblood/black metal architecture, teal energy conduits and floating crystal foci threading between pylons, populated with Team Tether grunts in matching dark uniforms (`06-relay-apparatus-day.png`, `06-relay-standing-day.png`, `06-relay-road-day.png`). The oxblood is reserved for this faction's structures and banners and does not leak onto village or camp elements — the rubric's color-discipline check passes here. Score: **8/10**.

**Hall exterior — gate / courtyard / approach.** The strongest sustained sequence in the whole set. The stone bridge-and-gatehouse approach (`10-stronghold-gate-day.png`) has real weight and a clean silhouette with towers, crenellation and a lit doorway; the courtyard interior carries the Team Tether crest (a white cross-in-circle) on large crimson banners with guards flanking the inner door (`10-stronghold-courtyard-day.png`, `-night.png`) — clear, legible faction staging. The 400m/200m/100m landmark sequence (`11-castle-landmark-hall-*`) is a genuine best-practice "does the landmark read from a distance" test and it passes: the castle silhouette with its single tall corner turret is identifiable even at 400m through atmospheric haze. Score: **9/10**.

**Creatures / life.** Only one creature species appears across all sampled frames — a badger/pangolin hybrid with scaled plating, seen as a companion pet, as wild singles, and as a group at Warrens and Ridge Camp. Where it's shown at multiple sizes side-by-side (`08-ridge-camp-standing-day.png`) the scale story is genuinely good: a knee-high juvenile, two hip-height mid-sizes, and one adult roughly as long as the trainer is tall, laid out so the size hierarchy is legible in a single still — this is exactly what rubric criterion 8 asks for and it's satisfied. Set against that: the creature's own rendering — thick black cel-shading outlines, flat painted fur/scale texture — sits in a distinctly different material language from the trainer (softer-shaded anime style) and from the low-poly, unshaded-outline terrain. All three "look like" different projects layered together, and none of them reach Palworld's semi-realistic, painterly-but-shaded creature rendering. With only one species sampled, appeal and variety can't be fully judged, but on materials/style-cohesion alone this is a real gap. Score: **5/10**.

**Night.** Night frames share one consistent, appealingly moody blue-to-black palette with a soft moon disc and scattered pale flowers picking up rim light (`ground-01-band1-opening-night.png`, `locations/01-village-standing-night.png`). The problem is legibility, not mood: several night frames go almost entirely black with only a sliver of sky or a rim-lit silhouette visible (`05-relay-camp-standing-night.png`, `08-ridge-camp-fire-night.png`, `10-stronghold-gate-face-night.png`) — there is no fill light or ambient occlusion softening to keep midground geometry readable the way, for instance, `01-village-grandpa-yard-night.png` and `10-stronghold-courtyard-night.png` manage (both stay legible via nearby fire/torch light). Score: **6/10**.

## 2. Bar A / Bar B verdicts

**A. Do these frames read as belonging to the world in `tetherbound-meadows-keyart.png`?**
**Yes**, on palette and landmark language, **with a caveat on rendering style.** The key art's warm greens, oxblood danger accents, mist-softened distant hills, and single dramatic castle-on-a-hill silhouette are all present and used correctly in these frames (the stronghold sequence and waystop frame are the clearest matches). What the key art does that the build does not attempt — and per the rubric shouldn't be expected to — is its painterly per-leaf canopy detail and soft global illumination; the build substitutes flat cel-shaded geometry, which is a legitimate stylization rather than a failure to hit the target. Composition and mood carry the "yes."

**B. Shown beside `palworld-0*.jpg`, would someone say these are trying to be the same kind of game?**
**Partially — closer to no than yes on first impression, yes on structure.** Structurally the two are doing the same things: open rolling terrain, a hero landmark visible from far away, camps with fires and crates, a creature shown at multiple sizes near the player. But the *look* diverges more than the rubric's "stylised realism between Valheim and Palworld" target allows: the trainer, NPCs and creature all carry a heavy anime-cel outline that Palworld's cast does not use, and Palworld's ground/tree density and material realism (soft PBR shading, textured bark, layered foliage) is visibly higher than anything sampled here — the trees especially read as a simpler, more toy-like shape language than Palworld's. A viewer shown both side by side would likely place these as a different, more cartoonish game inspired by the same setting type, not as the same production tier.

**Strongest:**

## 4. Overall

**Overall parity score: 6.5/10.**

The build's best material — the stronghold/castle sequence, the waystop camp, the relay apparatus, and the creature-scale demonstration at ridge camp — is genuinely close to the bar the owner set, and would stand up well in a curated highlight reel. But this survey was not curated: roughly one in six frames sampled (mostly clustered in the relay-camp set) is a broken or near-unusable capture from camera occlusion, several locations (village tournament banners, the warrens exterior mass, weather variants) carry visible placeholder-grade or non-functioning elements sitting inside otherwise finished scenes, and the creature/character rendering style is a consistent step away from the Palworld bar rather than a near-miss. **This build is not yet ready to show an external art reviewer as a finished candidate** — send the stronghold/waystop/relay sequence alone and it reads as a real pitch; send this full sampled set as-is and the broken relay-camp frames and placeholder banners will dominate the reviewer's first impression.

**What's fixable by changing the scene (density, palette, lighting, composition, scatter, camera placement):**
- The relay-camp and mill-crossing-yard occluded/broken frames — this is a capture-rig camera placement problem, not an asset problem, and should be trivial to fix by pulling the camera stands back from foliage.
- The tournament banners' flat, unshaded placeholder look — the relay camp's cloth pennant proves the asset kit already has a correctly-authored flag; the tournament banners just need the same material/mesh swapped in.
- Weather-variant indifference (rain/fog/cloudy reading identically to sunny) — needs visible particle/volumetric effects or at minimum a stronger sky/light delta, a lighting-and-effects tuning pass.
- Night legibility in unlit camps (ridge camp, some relay-camp shots) — needs a fill light or minimum-ambient floor the way the village and stronghold night shots already have.
- Band-to-band sameness along the travel corridor — reusing the same tree/rock kit with only lighting changing between bands 1–5 is a scatter/dressing decision, not a missing-asset problem.

**What likely needs new art, not new scene-dressing:**
- Tree canopy shape language (flat puffball silhouettes vs. Palworld's structured, asymmetric foliage) — this is a base tree-asset quality gap that scene changes can't close.
- The creature/character cel-outline rendering style sitting apart from Palworld's semi-realistic material language — this is a shading-and-material-pipeline decision baked into the assets themselves, not something a lighting or scatter pass fixes.
- Creature variety — only one species was visible across every sampled frame; whether the wider roster holds up against Palworld's cast can't be judged from this survey and needs its own pass with other creatures represented.
