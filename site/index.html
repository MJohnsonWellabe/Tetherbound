Tetherbound

Five creatures on your belt. No storage box, no second chances at the roster. Your creature does the fighting — and you are in there with it.

What it is

A third-person survival, crafting and creature-training game built in Godot, aimed at a handheld first. This build is the Meadows vertical slice: wake up in a farmhouse, choose a starter, walk out into the biome, and fight and catch wild creatures yourself.

  • Five, and only fiveYour whole roster is the five creatures on your belt. There is no storage box to park the rest in, so every catch is a decision about what you give up.
  • You pilot the fightCombat is real time and hands you the controls of your creature, inside an arena that forms right where you engaged. There is no dodge button — moving is the dodge, and attacks are aimed and can miss.
  • The throw is aimedTo catch, you take the camera back to your trainer, and a trajectory arc draws where the orb will go. Your creature is left undefended while you line it up, and the opponent does not stop.
  • The human cannot fightYou have no weapon. What you have is a creature, a throw, and where you chose to stand.

How it begins

Not with a menu. You wake in the upstairs bedroom of Grandpa's farmhouse, and three starters are standing by the door. You choose one by walking up to it. The other two stay with Grandpa. Then the village lets you out — a handful of farmsteads around a square, dirt paths running off into the meadows — and by nightfall you have built your first camp and your first day is over.

    Honest state of it

    This is a prototype of one biome, not a finished game. What is here is the Meadows: the opening morning, the village, wild combat, catching, and the first camp. What is not here is everything past the slice. The creatures and characters — all fifteen species, the trainer, Grandpa, the Warden, the Veridian Stag — are original productions, modelled from the project's own concept art through a Meshy-assisted pipeline. The environments are assembled from CC0 kits, credited below. A blind reviewer looks at every build and says what is wrong with it; those reviews are in the repository alongside the code.
